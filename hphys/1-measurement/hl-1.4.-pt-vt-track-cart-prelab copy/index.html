Pre-Lab: Diluting Gravity

Objective

Prepare for the “Diluting Gravity” lab by practicing key calculations and predicting the shapes of the position–time (p–t), velocity–time (v–t), and acceleration–time (a–t) graphs.

Background

In the lab, a cart rolls down a shallow incline so that gravity’s effect along the ramp is “diluted.” The acceleration along the ramp is modeled by $a = a_g\,\sin\theta$, where $a_g$ is the magnitude of the acceleration due to gravity and $\theta$ is the ramp angle. Use $a_g = 9.80\ \text{m/s}^2$ unless otherwise specified.

Part A — Practice the math

Before you collect data, work through this sample scenario in your notebook. The goal is to compare the acceleration predicted by theory to an experimental value from a v–t graph.

  1. Angle setup. Suppose the incline angle is $\theta = 4.00^\circ$.
  2. Theoretical acceleration. Using $a_g = 9.80\ \text{m/s}^2$, compute the theoretical acceleration for this incline:
\[a_{\text{theory}} = a_g\,\sin\theta.\]

Show setup and final answer with correct units.

  1. Experimental acceleration. After running the experiment, the slope of the v–t graph is measured to be $a_{\text{exp}} = 0.675\ \text{m/s}^2$.

  2. Percent error. Using your $a_{\text{theory}}$ and the value above for $a_{\text{exp}}$, compute the percent error:

    \[\%\ \text{error} = \frac{\lvert a_{\text{theory}} - a_{\text{exp}}\rvert}{a_{\text{theory}}}\times 100\%.\]

    Interpret your result: does the experimental measurement reasonably match the theoretical prediction?

Part B — Predict the graphs

Sketch your predictions for a cart released from rest and accelerating smoothly down the ramp. For each, label axes and include units.

  • Position–time (p–t)
    • Sketch: a curve that gets steeper with time (concave up), starting at rest.
    • Justify: with constant positive acceleration, position grows approximately like $t^2$.
  • Velocity–time (v–t)
    • Sketch: a straight line with positive slope starting at $v=0$.
    • Slope meaning: the slope is acceleration (units m/s²).
  • Acceleration–time (a–t)
    • Sketch: approximately a horizontal line at a positive value.
    • Relationship: this average value should match the slope of the v–t graph.

Conceptual prediction. If you increase the angle $\theta$, how does the v–t graph change? Describe how and why the slope changes in terms of $a = a_g\,\sin\theta$.
