Part A: Foundational Skills

These problems focus on basic application of concepts and mathematical relationships.

A.1 Unit Conversions and Dimensional Analysis

1. [Basic unit conversion problem with clear given values]

Example: A cheetah can run at speeds up to $120.0 \text{ km/h}$. Convert this speed to meters per second.

2. [Multi-step conversion problem]

Example: The distance from Earth to the Moon is approximately $3.84 \times 10^8 \text{ m}$. Express this distance in kilometers and in miles. (Use: $1.000 \text{ mile} = 1.609 \text{ km}$)

A.2 Basic Calculations

3. [Direct application of key formula]

Example: Calculate the gravitational potential energy of a $2.5 \text{ kg}$ book placed on a shelf $1.8 \text{ m}$ above the floor. Use $g = 9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$.

4. [Problem requiring rearrangement of formula]

Example: A car accelerates uniformly from rest to $25.0 \text{ m/s}$ in a distance of $150.0 \text{ m}$. Calculate the acceleration.

A.3 Significant Figures Practice

5. [Problem emphasizing proper significant figure use]

Example: Calculate the area of a rectangular garden plot measuring $24.7 \text{ m}$ by $8.23 \text{ m}$. Express your answer with the correct number of significant figures.

Part B: Conceptual Understanding

These problems test deeper understanding of concepts and relationships.

B.1 Relationships and Patterns

6. [Problem exploring relationships between variables]

Example: A ball is dropped from different heights, and the time to hit the ground is measured. If the ball takes $1.4 \text{ s}$ to fall from a height of $9.6 \text{ m}$, how long would it take to fall from a height of $38.4 \text{ m}$? Explain your reasoning.

7. [Proportional reasoning problem]

Example: The gravitational force between two objects depends on their masses and the distance between them. If the distance between two objects is doubled while their masses remain the same, how does the gravitational force change? Support your answer with mathematical reasoning.

B.2 Graphical Analysis

8. [Problem involving graph interpretation]

Example: The graph below shows the velocity of a car over time. Describe the motion during each segment and calculate the total displacement.

[Include appropriate graph or refer to provided graph]

9. [Problem requiring graph construction]

Example: A student drops a ball from rest and measures its position every $0.1 \text{ s}$. Create a position-time graph and a velocity-time graph for the first $0.5 \text{ s}$ of motion.

Part C: Application and Synthesis

These problems require integration of multiple concepts and real-world application.

C.1 Multi-Step Problem Solving

10. [Complex problem requiring multiple steps and concepts]

Example: A projectile is launched at an angle of $35°$ above the horizontal with an initial speed of $42 \text{ m/s}$ from a cliff that is $15 \text{ m}$ above the ground. Calculate: (a) the maximum height reached above the ground, (b) the horizontal distance traveled, and (c) the velocity when it hits the ground.

C.2 Engineering Applications

12. [Problem with engineering or design context]

Example: An engineer is designing a roller coaster loop. If the loop has a radius of $8.0 \text{ m}$, what is the minimum speed the car must have at the top of the loop to maintain contact with the track? Consider the forces acting on a passenger in the car.

13. [Problem requiring optimization or design decisions]

Example: A catapult needs to launch a projectile to hit a target $50 \text{ m}$ away. If the launch point is $2.0 \text{ m}$ above the target, what combinations of launch angle and initial speed would work? Which would require the least initial speed?

Part D: Analysis and Reasoning

These problems emphasize scientific reasoning and critical thinking.

D.1 Error Analysis

14. [Problem involving experimental uncertainty or error analysis]

Example: A student measures the period of a pendulum five times and gets the following results: $2.03 \text{ s}$, $2.01 \text{ s}$, $2.05 \text{ s}$, $1.98 \text{ s}$, $2.02 \text{ s}$. Calculate the average and estimate the uncertainty in the measurement.

15. [Problem identifying assumptions and limitations]

Example: In calculating the range of a projectile, we often ignore air resistance. For a baseball hit at $45°$ with an initial speed of $40 \text{ m/s}$, calculate the range assuming no air resistance. Discuss how air resistance would affect this result and why.

D.2 Conceptual Reasoning

16. [Ranking or comparison problem]

Example: Three balls of different masses are dropped simultaneously from the same height. Ball A has mass $m$, Ball B has mass $2m$, and Ball C has mass $4m$. Rank the balls in order of: (a) time to hit the ground, (b) speed when hitting the ground, (c) kinetic energy when hitting the ground. Explain your reasoning.

17. [Problem requiring conceptual explanation]

Example: Explain why a satellite in orbit around Earth doesn’t fall to the surface, even though gravity is acting on it. Use concepts of force, acceleration, and circular motion in your explanation.
