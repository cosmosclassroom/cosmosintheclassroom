Problem Set 1.2: The Power of Vectors

Complete this problem set on separate paper to be collected by your instructor.

Introduction

In our last lab, we saw how vectors can be added graphically. In our lecture, we learned a more powerful and precise mathematical method: breaking vectors down into their x and y components. This problem set will give you practice with this essential skill. Mastering vector components is the key to creating a truly accurate and professional-looking map of our classroom for your Research Brief.

Apprentice Level: Foundational Skills

Complete the following problems, showing all work and using correct significant figures.

1. Scalar vs. Vector Classification

Identify each quantity as either a scalar or a vector. Explain your reasoning.

a) The speed limit on the highway: 65 mph b) The velocity of a car: 65 mph due north c) The mass of a textbook: 2.1 kg d) The weight of the textbook: 21 N downward e) The temperature in the room: 22°C

2. Basic Vector Components

Problem A: A displacement vector has a magnitude of 50.0 m and points 30.0° north of east. Calculate:

a) The eastward (x) component b) The northward (y) component

Problem B: A force vector of 85.0 N acts at an angle of 45.0° above the horizontal. Find:

a) The horizontal component ($F_x$) b) The vertical component ($F_y$)

Journeyman Level: Applied Problem-Solving

3. Navigation and Bearings

An airplane flies at 180.0 km/h on a bearing of 135° (measured clockwise from north). Calculate:

a) The standard mathematical angle (measured counterclockwise from east) b) The eastward velocity component c) The northward velocity component

4. Vector Addition by Components

Two displacement vectors are given:

Vector A: 75.0 m at 60.0° north of east Vector B: 40.0 m at 120.0° from the positive x-axis

Calculate:

a) The x and y components of vector A b) The x and y components of vector B c) The x and y components of the resultant vector (A + B) d) The magnitude and direction of the resultant vector

Master Level: Complex Analysis & Synthesis

5. The Surveyor’s Challenge

You are creating a vector map of a new park. Your survey drone has provided the locations of three key landmarks relative to a central fountain (the origin):

  • Landmark A (Oak tree): $80.0 \text{ m}$ at $120.0°$ from positive x-axis
  • Landmark B (Picnic area): $100.0 \text{ m}$ at $30.0°$ from positive x-axis
  • Landmark C (Playground): $60.0 \text{ m}$ at $225.0°$ from positive x-axis

Your task is to determine:

a) The coordinates (x,y) of each landmark b) The displacement vector $\overrightarrow{d_{AB}}$ from the oak tree directly to the picnic area c) The displacement vector $\overrightarrow{d_{BC}}$ from the picnic area directly to the playground d) The total displacement $\overrightarrow{d_{total}}$ if someone walks from the oak tree to the picnic area to the playground

6. Multi-Step Navigation Problem

A rescue helicopter must navigate through a complex flight pattern:

Flight segments:

  • Segment 1: $\overrightarrow{d_1} = 25.0 \text{ km}$ on bearing $045°$ (northeast)
  • Segment 2: $\overrightarrow{d_2} = 18.0 \text{ km}$ on bearing $180°$ (due south)
  • Segment 3: $\overrightarrow{d_3} = 32.0 \text{ km}$ on bearing $270°$ (due west)

Calculate:

a) The final position relative to the starting point (x and y coordinates) b) The magnitude and direction of the direct displacement from start to finish c) How much extra distance did the helicopter fly compared to taking the direct route?
