Segment 1: Reading the Launch Data

Estimated Time: 20 minutes
Context & Phenomenon Link: Our primary data source for understanding the launch of the sounding rocket is its velocity-time (v-t) graph. This graph tells the story of the rocket's vertical motion, and our first task is to interpret the initial, most critical phase: the powered ascent.

Core Practice:
  • Plot the two data points for the powered ascent on a graph. Draw a straight line connecting them.
  • Calculate the slope of the line you drew. The formula for slope is m = (y₂ - y₁)/(x₂ - x₁).
  • What physical quantity does the slope of a velocity-time graph represent? What is the rocket's average acceleration during its powered ascent?
Synthesis & Cross-Cutting Lens (Patterns): The straight, upward-sloping line on your graph is a pattern. What does this specific pattern tell you about the force exerted by the rocket's engine during this 10-second period?
Metacognitive Prompt: To find the acceleration, you divided a change in velocity (in m/s) by a change in time (in s). How do the units of your answer (m/s²) confirm that you performed the correct calculation for acceleration?

Segment 2: Motion After Engine Cutoff

Estimated Time: 20 minutes
Context & Phenomenon Link: The rocket's engine provides the initial thrust, but it shuts off at t = 10 seconds. Now, we must analyze the rocket's motion as it coasts upward against the pull of gravity.

Core Practice:
  • How does the acceleration during powered ascent compare to the acceleration after the engine cuts off? Is the rocket still speeding up or is it slowing down?
  • Calculate the additional time to apogee using v_f = v_i + at.
  • What is the total time elapsed from launch to apogee?
Synthesis & Cross-Cutting Lens (Cause and Effect): Explain the cause for the dramatic change in acceleration at t = 10 seconds. What fundamental interaction is now the primary effect governing the rocket's motion?
Metacognitive Prompt: How does rearranging a single kinematic equation allow you to answer both of these very different questions?

Segment 3: Calculating Altitude (Part 1)

Estimated Time: 20 minutes
Context & Phenomenon Link: We have analyzed the rocket's acceleration, but a crucial question remains: how high did it go? We can determine the altitude gained during the powered ascent directly from our graph.

Core Practice:
  • Identify the geometric shape under the v-t graph from t = 0 s to t = 10 s.
  • Calculate the area of that shape. Use A = ½bh.
  • What was the rocket's altitude at MECO? Ensure your answer has the correct units.
Synthesis & Cross-Cutting Lens (Patterns): Compare the area accumulated between t=0 and t=1s with t=9 and t=10s. What does this pattern tell you about how the rocket's speed affects the distance it travels each second?
Metacognitive Prompt: What would multiplying the final velocity by time incorrectly assume about the rocket's motion during the first 10 seconds?

Segment 4: Calculating Altitude (Part 2)

Estimated Time: 20 minutes
Context & Phenomenon Link: To find the rocket's maximum height (apogee), we must add the altitude gained during the powered ascent to the additional height it gained while coasting upward.

Core Practice:
  • Sketch the line representing the rocket's velocity from MECO until apogee. What geometric shape is formed?
  • Calculate the area of this second shape for coasting altitude.
  • Add the altitudes to determine the rocket's maximum altitude.
Synthesis & Cross-Cutting Lens (Cause and Effect): What is the ultimate cause of the rocket stopping at apogee? What is the subsequent effect on its direction?
Metacognitive Prompt: Write a plain-language, step-by-step "recipe" for finding the rocket's maximum height using only the launch data.

Segment 5: Synthesis: The Incident Report

Estimated Time: 20 minutes
Context & Phenomenon Link: Mission control has lost contact with the rocket. The final telemetry data received is an incomplete v-t graph that looks... anomalous. Your team must analyze the graph and write a brief incident report explaining what the data tells you happened to the mission.

Core Practice (The Incident Report):
  • Calculate acceleration during the initial burn.
  • Find the altitude reached when the anomaly occurred at t = 5 s.
  • Conclude what happened to the rocket at t = 5 s.
  • Calculate the maximum height the rocket achieved.
Synthesis & Cross-Cutting Lens (Patterns & Cause and Effect): How did the sudden break in the expected pattern of the graph allow you to infer the cause of the mission failure?
Metacognitive Prompt: How did breaking the successful launch into two stages prepare you to analyze this more complex, multi-stage failure scenario?

Segment 6: Metacognition & Review

Estimated Time: 20 minutes
Context & Phenomenon Link: We have used a simplified v-t graph as a model to understand the complex reality of a rocket launch. This final segment is for reflecting on the connection between our model and the real-world phenomenon.

Core Practice (Conceptual Review):
  • Explain how the slope of the v-t graph relates to acceleration, and how the area under the graph relates to altitude.
  • How would the v-t graph for the coasting phase be different if we included atmospheric drag? Would the rocket reach a higher or lower apogee?
  • What additional information would you need to describe the rocket's trajectory if launched at a 75-degree angle?
Synthesis & Cross-Cutting Lens (Cause and Effect): List two key cause-and-effect relationships that governed the rocket's motion from launchpad to apogee.
Metacognitive Prompt: What aspect of the rocket's story was made clearest by using the velocity-time graph? What important information was invisible in the data we analyzed?
