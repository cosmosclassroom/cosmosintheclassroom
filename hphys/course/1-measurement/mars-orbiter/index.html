  • id: “s1b1” type: “numeric” prompt: | Segment 1: The Root of the Error The Mars Climate Orbiter was lost because one engineering team used English units (pounds) while another used metric units (Newtons). The thrusters on the orbiter were designed to produce a force measured in Newtons (N). The software, however, calculated the required force in pound-force (lbf).

    Convert a force of 1.5 lbf to its equivalent in Newtons. points: 5 answer: 6.675 tolerance: 0.01 units: “N” explanation: “Use the conversion factor 1 lbf ≈ 4.45 N. Calculation: 1.5 lbf * 4.45 N/lbf = 6.675 N.”

Convert this to Newtons: 0.5 lbf * 4.45 N/lbf = 2.225 N.”

    Segment 3: Deconstructing a Trajectory Vector

  • id: “s3b2” type: “numeric” prompt: | Segment 3: Deconstructing a Trajectory Vector Imagine the orbiter is firing its main engine, creating a thrust vector of 500 N at an angle of 20.0° relative to its direction of travel. Calculate the forward component of the thrust (Fx). This is the part of the force that changes the spacecraft’s forward speed. points: 5 answer: 470 tolerance: 1 units: “N” explanation: “Fx = F * cos(θ) = 500 N * cos(20.0°) ≈ 469.8 N”
  • id: “s3b3” type: “numeric” prompt: “Using the same scenario from the previous question, calculate the sideways component of the thrust (Fy).   • id: “s3c” type: “free_response” prompt: “Synthesis (Cause and Effect): Using the vector components you just calculated, explain how a single engine firing (the cause) can produce two different outcomes simultaneously (changing forward speed and moving sideways, the effects).” points: 5 rubric: “Answer should explain that because the force vector has both an x and y component, it simultaneously applies force in both directions, causing acceleration in both.” ngss_alignment: ccc: [“Cause and Effect”]
