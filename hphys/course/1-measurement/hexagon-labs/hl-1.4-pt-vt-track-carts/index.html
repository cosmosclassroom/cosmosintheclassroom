Track Cart: Position-Time & Velocity-Time

Course: HPHYS
Unit: 1

Objective

Measure the constant acceleration of a cart rolling down a gentle incline, then connect the measured value to the theoretical model $a_{\text{theory}} = a_g\,\sin\theta$.

Background — On the Shoulders of Giants

Four centuries ago, Galileo Galilei wanted to study the motion of falling objects, but they fell too quickly for the clocks of his era. His solution was to “dilute” gravity by using a shallow ramp. The component of gravity along the ramp is smaller than $a_g$, so the motion is slower, but it follows the same rules. With modern motion sensors, we can reproduce Galileo’s approach and tie graphs directly to a mathematical model of the acceleration.

Materials

  • Vernier Dynamics Track with low-friction cart
  • Vernier Go Motion (motion) sensor
  • Computer with Vernier Graphical Analysis® software
  • Textbook or block to create an incline
  • Protractor or angle finder
  • Meterstick or ruler (optional)

Setup

  1. Place the track on a stable, level surface. Position the motion sensor at the higher end of the track, pointing down the ramp.
  2. Open Graphical Analysis®.
  3. Configure the display to show three synchronized graphs: position–time (p–t), velocity–time (v–t), and acceleration–time (a–t).
  4. Elevate the sensor end of the track with a book to create a small, measurable angle, e.g., $2^\circ$–$5^\circ$. Measure and record this angle $\theta$ precisely.

Procedure — Data Collection

  1. Place the cart about 15 cm from the sensor.
  2. Click Collect and release the cart from rest so it accelerates down the incline. Do not push.
  3. Catch the cart before it reaches the end stop. Stop collection when you have a clean interval of free roll.

Graphical Analysis

  • On the v–t graph, identify the straight-line region corresponding to constant acceleration during free roll.
  • Use the slope tool to find the slope of this region. Report this as your experimental acceleration $a_{\text{exp}}$ with units of m/s².
  • Inspect the a–t graph over the same time interval. The average value should be approximately constant and should agree with the slope you found on the v–t graph.

Mathematical Analysis

For an object on a frictionless incline, the component of gravity along the ramp produces an acceleration given by

$a_{\text{theory}} = a_g\,\sin\theta,$ where $a_g = 9.80\ \text{m/s}^2$ and $\theta$ is the incline angle.

  1. Using your measured $\theta$, calculate $a_{\text{theory}}$. Show your work and units.
  2. Compare $a_{\text{exp}}$ (from the v–t slope) and $a_{\text{theory}}$ (from the formula) in a small table:

  1. Compute percent error to quantify agreement:

$\%\ \text{error} = \dfrac{\lvert a_{\text{theory}} - a_{\text{exp}}\rvert}{a_{\text{theory}}}\times 100\%.$

Synthesis — Cause and Effect

In a concise paragraph, explain how this lab illustrates the Crosscutting Concept of Cause and Effect. Identify:

  • The cause manipulated in the experiment (the ramp angle $\theta$) and
  • The effect measured (the cart’s acceleration along the ramp).

Describe the mathematical relationship that connects them ($a \propto \sin\theta$ with proportionality constant $a_g$) and explain how this supports Galileo’s idea of “diluting” gravity to make acceleration measurable.
