Historical Measurement

HPHYS Unit 1

Estimated Time:

Lab Type:

Learning Objectives:

Hexagon Lab 1.1: A Shadow of a Doubt

Background

Over 2,200 years ago, Eratosthenes, working at the Library of Alexandria, made a groundbreaking measurement. He learned that in Syene, Egypt, the sun was directly overhead at noon on the summer solstice, while in Alexandria, a tall obelisk cast a shadow at the same time.
Eratosthenes realized that the difference in shadow angles between the two cities was due to Earth’s curvature. Using this observation and the known distance between the cities, he estimated the circumference of the Earth.

Purpose

To use geometric reasoning and proportional relationships to estimate the circumference of the Earth, following Eratosthenes’s method.

Materials

  • Calculator
  • Pencil and paper
  • Research Log

Evidence (Dataset)

Location Shadow Angle (at noon on solstice)
Syene, Egypt 0°
Alexandria, Egypt 7.2°

Known distance between Syene and Alexandria: 800 kilometers

Procedure

Part A: Finding the Angle

  1. Calculate the difference in shadow angles
    • What is the difference in the shadow angles between Alexandria and Syene?
  2. Determine the fraction of Earth’s circle
    • If a full circle is 360°, what fraction of a full circle is this wedge?
    • Hint: Fraction = Angle Difference / 360°

Part B: Calculating Earth’s Circumference

  1. Set up a proportion
    • The 800 km distance between the cities represents the same fraction of Earth’s total circumference as the angle you calculated.
    • Write the proportion:
      \(\frac{\text{Angle Difference}}{360^\circ} = \frac{800\,\text{km}}{\text{Total Circumference}}\)
  2. Solve for Earth’s circumference
    • Rearrange and solve for Total Circumference.
    • Show all work and calculations in your Research Log.

Part C: Accounting for Uncertainty

  1. Consider measurement uncertainty
    • Eratosthenes’s measurement of the distance between the cities was the most uncertain part of his experiment.
  2. Calculate with 10% larger distance
    • What would your result for Earth’s circumference be if the actual distance was 880 km?
  3. Calculate with 10% smaller distance
    • What would your result be if the distance was 720 km?

Analysis & Synthesis

Scientific Lens: Scale, Proportion, and Quantity

  • Eratosthenes used a local measurement (a shadow in his city) and a known proportion to figure out a global quantity (the size of the whole planet).
  • In your Research Log:
    Write 2-3 sentences explaining how this lab demonstrates that understanding scale and proportion allows scientists to make powerful claims about things they can’t directly measure.

Extension (Optional)

  • How would your answer change if the angle measurement was off by 0.5°?
  • What other real-world phenomena could be measured using similar proportional reasoning?
