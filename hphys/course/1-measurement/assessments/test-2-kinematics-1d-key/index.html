Solution Key for Chapter 2 Assessment: The Story of a Sounding Rocket

Segment 1 of 3: The Apprentice’s Telemetry (Core Skills)

B. Core Practice

  1. Average Acceleration Calculation

    A sounding rocket accelerates uniformly from rest on the launch pad. After $4.50 \text{ s}$, its velocity is $125 \text{ m/s}$ upward.

    Coordinate System: Upward is positive.

    Using the formula for average acceleration: \(a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v_f - v_i}{t}\) where ( v_i = 0 \text{ m/s} ), ( v_f = 125 \text{ m/s} ), and ( t = 4.50 \text{ s} ): \(a = \frac{125 \text{ m/s} - 0 \text{ m/s}}{4.50 \text{ s}} = \frac{125}{4.50} = 27.8 \text{ m/s}^2\)

  2. Altitude Calculation

    Coordinate System: Upward is positive, origin at launch pad.

    Parent relation: \(d = v_i t + \tfrac{1}{2} a t^2\) Since $v_i = 0$, rearrange to: \(d = \tfrac{1}{2} a t^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} (v_f - v_i)t = \tfrac{1}{2} v_f t\) Substitute problem values: \(d = \tfrac{1}{2} \left(125 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}\right)(4.50 \,\text{s}) = 281 \,\text{m} = 0.281 \,\text{km}\)

  3. Final Velocity Calculation at 500 m

    Coordinate System: Upward is positive.

    Parent relation: \(v_f^2 = v_i^2 + 2ad\) Solve for $v_f$: \(v_f = \sqrt{v_i^2 + 2ad}\) Substitute problem values (using $a = 27.8 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}$ from question 5): \(v_f = \sqrt{0^2 + 2\!\left(27.8 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}\right)(500. \,\text{m})} = 1.67 \times 10^2 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} = 167 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}\)

Segment 2 of 3: The Journeyman’s Flight Path (Synthesis & Application)

B. Core Practice

Coordinate System: Upward is positive, origin at ground level.

  1. Time to Reach Maximum Altitude

    Parent relation: \(v_f = v_i + at\) Solve for $t$: \(t = \frac{v_f - v_i}{a}\) Substitute problem values: \(t = \frac{0 - 185 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}}{-9.80 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}} = 1.89 \times 10^1 \,\text{s} = 18.9 \,\text{s}\)

  2. Additional Altitude During Coasting

    Parent relation: \(v_f^2 = v_i^2 + 2ad\) Solve for $d$: \(d = \frac{v_f^2 - v_i^2}{2a}\) Substitute problem values: \(d = \frac{0^2 - \left(185 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}\right)^2}{2(-9.80 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2})} = 1.75 \times 10^3 \,\text{m} = 1.75 \,\text{km}\)

    Note: The total maximum altitude from ground would be $2.20 \text{ km} + 1.75 \text{ km} = 3.95 \text{ km}$.

  3. Graphs

    Students should sketch:167

    • Position vs. Time: Parabola opening downward (decreasing slope)
    • Velocity vs. Time: Straight line with negative slope from +185 m/s to 0 m/s

Segment 3 of 3: The Master’s Analysis (Complex Scenarios)

B. Core Practice: The Intercept Problem

Coordinate System: Upward is positive, origin at launch pad.

  1. Equations for Vertical Position

    For Rocket A (starting at $t = 0$): \(\Delta y_A = \frac{1}{2} a_A t^2 = \frac{1}{2}(15.0 \, \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2})t^2 = \left(7.50 \, \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}\right)t^2\)

    For Rocket B (starting at $t = 3.00 \text{ s}$): \(\Delta y_B = \frac{1}{2} a_B (t - 3.00)^2 = \frac{1}{2}(30.0 \, \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2})(t - 3.00 \, \text{s})^2 = \left(15.0 \, \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}\right)(t - 3.00 \, \text{s})^2\)

    where $t$ is the time since Rocket A launched.

  2. Time When Rocket B Overtakes Rocket A

    Parent relations for displacement: \(\Delta y_A = \tfrac{1}{2} a_A t^2, \qquad \Delta y_B = \tfrac{1}{2} a_B (t - 3.00)^2\) Set $\Delta y_A = \Delta y_B$ and simplify: \(7.50t^2 = 15.0(t - 3.00)^2 \quad \Rightarrow \quad t^2 - 12t + 18 = 0\) Solve the quadratic for $t$ (take the root $> 3.00 \,\text{s}$): \(t = \frac{12 + \sqrt{144 - 72}}{2} = 6 + 3\sqrt{2} \approx 1.02 \times 10^1 \,\text{s} = 10.2 \,\text{s}\)

  3. Altitude When Intercept Occurs

    Parent relation for Rocket A: \(\Delta y_A = \tfrac{1}{2} a_A t^2\) Substitute $t = 6 + 3\sqrt{2}$ with units: \(\Delta y_A = \tfrac{1}{2} \left(15.0 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}\right)\!\left(6 + 3\sqrt{2}\right)^2 \approx 7.87 \times 10^2 \,\text{m} = 787 \,\text{m}\) Verification with Rocket B: \(\Delta y_B = \tfrac{1}{2} \left(30.0 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}\right)\!\left(6 + 3\sqrt{2} - 3.00 \,\text{s}\right)^2 \approx 7.87 \times 10^2 \,\text{m} = 787 \,\text{m}\)

    (Small difference due to rounding)

C. Qualitative Critique & Extension

If Rocket A’s engine cuts out at ( t = 8.00 \text{ s} ), the analysis would require:

  1. Finding Rocket A’s state at $t = 8.00 \text{ s}$:
    • Velocity: $v_A = \left(15.0 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}\right)(8.00 \,\text{s}) = 120 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}$
    • Altitude: $y_A = \tfrac{1}{2} \left(15.0 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}\right)(8.00 \,\text{s})^2 = 480 \,\text{m}$

  2. Modeling the coasting phase: After $t = 8.00 \text{ s}$, Rocket A follows $y_A(t) = 480 \,\text{m} + \left(120 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}\right)(t-8.00 \,\text{s}) - \tfrac{1}{2}(9.80 \,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2})(t-8.00 \,\text{s})^2$

  3. Comparing trajectories: Set Rocket B’s equation equal to Rocket A’s new piecewise equation and solve for when (if ever) $y_B = y_A$ for $t > 8.00 \text{ s}$.

The outcome depends on whether Rocket B can catch up before Rocket A reaches apogee and begins descending.
