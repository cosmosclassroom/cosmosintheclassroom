Practice Problem Set: The Physics of a Hollywood Stunt

Anchoring Phenomenon: A stunt coordinator uses physics to safely choreograph a high-fall scene for an action movie.

Instructions:

This is a practice assignment. The goal is to hone your skills before the summative assessment. Work with your peers, check your answers, and focus on understanding the process.

For all calculations, begin with the relevant starting formula, rearrange it algebraically, and then substitute your known values.

Show all work. Final answers must include correct SI units and significant figures.

State your coordinate system (e.g., “up is the positive direction”).

Assume the acceleration due to gravity is \(a_g = 9.80 \, \text{m/s}^2\) and that air resistance is negligible.

Segment 1 of 3: The Test Fall (Apprentice Skills)

A. Context & Phenomenon Link

Before any stunt involving a person, the team performs test drops with sandbags to gather data. You are analyzing the data from a test bag dropped from the top of a building set piece.

B. Core Practice

  1. A sandbag is dropped from rest. It hits the giant inflatable airbag below in \(3.50 \, \text{s}\). What is its velocity just before it makes contact with the airbag?

  2. A second, identical sandbag is dropped from the same height. This time, a faulty sensor measures the impact velocity to be \(-41.65 \, \text{m/s}\). Based on this velocity, how high is the building? (Use the time-independent kinematic equation).

  3. For a different shot, the script requires an object to be thrown downward to increase its impact speed. A prop is thrown downward from the roof with an initial velocity of \(-10.0 \, \text{m/s}\). If the building is \(75.0 \, \text{m}\) tall, what is the prop’s final velocity upon impact?

C. Metacognitive Prompt

In problem #3, how did the non-zero initial velocity affect your choice of kinematic equation compared to problems #1 and #2? Did it change how you solved the problem?

Segment 2 of 3: The Stunt Performer’s Jump (Journeyman Synthesis)

A. Context & Phenomenon Link

Now it’s time to plan the actual stunt. A performer will fall from the building onto a massive airbag. You know the height of the fall, but you must also analyze the motion of the performer while the airbag is compressing to ensure the deceleration is safe.

B. Core Practice

  1. The stunt performer, with a mass identical to the sandbags, falls from rest from a height of \(60.0 \, \text{m}\). What is their velocity the instant they first touch the airbag?

  2. Upon impact, the airbag compresses, bringing the performer to a complete stop (\(v_f = 0\)). What is the performer’s acceleration while the airbag is compressing? (Note: This is a new acceleration, not \(a_g\)).

  3. How much time does it take for the airbag to bring them to a stop?

C. Cross-Cutting Lens (Cause and Effect)

Compare the acceleration of the performer during free-fall to their acceleration while the airbag is compressing. Why is the acceleration provided by the airbag so much larger in magnitude? What would be the “effect” on the stunt performer if this acceleration were too large?

Segment 3 of 3: The Mid-Air Rescue (Master’s Analysis)

A. Context & Phenomenon Link

This is the most complex scene in the movie. A priceless artifact is dropped from a helicopter hovering at an altitude of \(150 \, \text{m}\). Our hero, on a ledge \(20.0 \, \text{m}\) below the helicopter (at an altitude of \(130 \, \text{m}\)), must jump upward to catch it.

B. Core Practice: The Intercept Problem

  1. The artifact is dropped from rest. Exactly \(1.50 \, \text{s}\) after the artifact is dropped, the hero jumps upward with an initial velocity of \(25.0 \, \text{m/s}\).

    Write two distinct equations for the vertical position as a function of time: one for the falling artifact (\(y_{\text{artifact}}\)) and one for the jumping hero (\(y_{\text{hero}}\)). Be sure your equations account for their different initial positions and the time delay.

  2. At what time after the artifact is first dropped do the hero and the artifact meet?

  3. At what altitude does the hero make the catch?

C. Qualitative Critique & Extension

In the scenario you just solved, the hero had to wait \(1.50 \, \text{s}\) before jumping. In your own words, what would happen if the hero jumped with the same initial velocity but waited too long (e.g., \(3.00 \, \text{s}\))? Could they still make the catch? How would you use your physics equations to determine the absolute “last second” the hero could wait before jumping and still be able to save the artifact?
