OVERVIEW SECTION

——————————————————————————

overview: title: | Pre-Lab: Diluting Gravity abstract: | This pre-lab prepares students for the constant acceleration experiment by having them calculate the theoretical acceleration of a cart on an incline and predict the resulting motion graphs. This exercise reinforces the theoretical model $a_{\text{theory}} = a_g \sin\theta$ and the graphical definitions of kinematic variables.

introduction: | Four centuries ago, Galileo Galilei invented a method to study gravity by using a shallow ramp to “dilute” its effects. The ramp slows the motion down, allowing for easier measurement, but the motion still follows the same rules of constant acceleration. Before we reproduce Galileo’s approach, you must practice the math and predict the graphical outcomes to understand the theoretical model.

background_context: | In the main lab, you will measure the incline angle $\theta$ and compare the experimental acceleration ($a_{\text{exp}}$, from the $\text{v-t}$ graph slope) to the theoretical model: $a_{\text{theory}} = a_g \sin\theta$. Use $a_g = 9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$ unless otherwise specified.

INVESTIGATIONS (Data Analysis Sections)

——————————————————————————

investigations:

  • title: | Part A: Practice the Math description: | Work through this sample scenario in your notebook to prepare for data analysis. Instruct students to draw tables and sketches in their Science Notebooks.

    analysis_questions: | # CRITICAL FIX: Applied YAML block scalar (|) for safety and corrected LaTeX

    • Suppose the incline angle is $\theta = 4.00^\circ$. Compute the theoretical acceleration: $a_{\text{theory}} = a_g \sin\theta$. Show setup and final answer with correct units ($\text{m/s}^2$).
    • If the slope of the velocity-time graph is measured to be $a_{\text{exp}} = 0.675 \text{ m/s}^2$, compute the percent error: \(\%\ \text{error} = \frac{\lvert a_{\text{theory}} - a_{\text{exp}}\rvert}{a_{\text{theory}}}\times 100\%.\)
    • Interpret your result: Does the experimental measurement reasonably match the theoretical prediction?

  • title: | Part B: Predict the Graphs description: | Sketch your predictions for a cart released from rest and accelerating smoothly down the ramp. Instruct students to label axes and include units in their Science Notebook sketches.

    analysis_questions: | # CRITICAL FIX: Applied YAML block scalar (|) for safety

    • Sketch the Position–time (p–t) graph. Justify the shape using the kinematic relationship between position and time under constant acceleration.
    • Sketch the Velocity–time (v–t) graph. What does the slope of this graph represent, including units?
    • Sketch the Acceleration–time (a–t) graph. What is the expected numerical relationship between the average value of the a-t graph and the slope of the v-t graph?
    • Conceptual Prediction: If you increase the angle $\theta$, how does the v–t graph change? Describe how and why the slope changes in terms of the relationship $a = a_g \sin\theta$.
