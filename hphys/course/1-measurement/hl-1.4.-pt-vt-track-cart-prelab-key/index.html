This key provides the correct calculations, graph descriptions, and a sample experimental procedure for the “Diluting Gravity” pre-lab.

Part A — Practice the Math

This section checks the student’s ability to perform the core calculations for the lab.

  • Theoretical Acceleration: The calculation requires substituting the given values into the formula.
    • Setup: $a_{theory} = g \sin \theta$
    • $a_{theory} = (9.80 \ m/s^2) \times \sin(4.00^\circ)$
    • Final Answer: $a_{theory} \approx 0.684 \ m/s^2$ (rounded to 3 significant figures).
  • Percent Error: This calculation compares the theoretical value to the given experimental one.
    • Setup: $\% \text{ error} = \left \frac{a_{theory} - a_{exp}}{a_{theory}} \right \times 100\%$
    • $\% \text{ error} = \left \frac{0.684 \ m/s^2 - 0.675 \ m/s^2}{0.684 \ m/s^2} \right \times 100\%$
    • Final Answer: $\% \text{ error} \approx 1.32\%$
  • Interpretation: An error of only 1.32% shows that the experimental measurement very reasonably matches the theoretical prediction. The small difference is likely due to minor experimental factors like friction or measurement uncertainty. 👍

Part B — Predict the Graphs

This section assesses the student’s conceptual understanding of motion with constant acceleration.

  • Position–time (p–t): The sketch should be a parabolic curve starting at the origin and getting steeper, indicating increasing velocity. The justification is that with constant acceleration from rest, position is proportional to time squared ($x = \frac{1}{2}at^2$).

  • Velocity–time (v–t): The sketch should be a straight line with a constant, positive slope, starting from the origin ($v=0$). The slope of this line represents the cart’s acceleration.

  • Acceleration–time (a–t): The sketch should be a horizontal line at a constant positive value. This value is the average acceleration and should match the slope of the v–t graph.

  • Conceptual Prediction: If you increase the angle $\theta$, the value of $\sin\theta$ also increases. According to the model $a = g \sin \theta$, this means the acceleration will be greater. On the v–t graph, a larger acceleration corresponds to a steeper positive slope.

Part D — Develop Your Procedure

The student should write a numbered, step-by-step procedure. A good procedure would look like this:

  1. Set up the ramp. Elevate one end of the track using books or a lab jack. Place the motion detector at the high end, pointing down the track.
  2. Measure the independent variable. Use a protractor to measure the ramp angle, $\theta$. Record this angle in the data table.
  3. Calculate the theoretical acceleration. For the measured angle, calculate $a_{theory} = (9.80 \ m/s^2) \sin \theta$ and record it in the table.
  4. Collect motion data. Place the cart about 15 cm from the motion detector. Start data collection and release the cart from rest, making sure to keep the release method consistent for all trials. Catch the cart at the bottom.
  5. Determine the dependent variable. On the velocity-time graph, find the slope of the linear section where the cart was freely rolling. This slope is the experimental acceleration, $a_{exp}$. Record this in the table.
  6. Analyze results. Calculate the percent error between your theoretical and experimental values and record it in the table.
  7. Ensure reliability. To be confident in the results, repeat steps 2-6 for at least two more different angles. This will show the relationship between angle and acceleration.
