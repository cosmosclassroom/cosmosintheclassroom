Unit 1 Quiz: Measurement and Vectors

50 points 30 minutes

Honors Physics Assessment: Measurement and Vectors

Anchoring Phenomenon: Lunar Measurement & Navigation

Core Concepts: Measurement, Vectors, Precision

Time Limit: 30 minutes

Support Materials Available: Calculator, Formula Sheet, Reference Guide

Assessment Overview

What You'll Demonstrate

By completing this assessment, you'll show your ability to:

  • Convert units and analyze measurement precision
  • Resolve vector components and apply vector notation
  • Model physical situations using vectors

Section 1: Measurement Precision & Unit Analysis

1. Unit Conversion:

Convert 2.45 km to meters, showing unit analysis and proper significant figures.

meters

2. Lunar Circumference Estimation:

Two lunar research stations separated by a surface distance of 12.0 km show a solar shadow angle difference of 2.5° at local lunar noon. What fraction of Luna’s full 360° circle does this represent? Use that fraction to estimate Luna’s circumference. Show proportional reasoning.

Section 2: Vector Components & Representation

3. Vector Sketch:

A displacement vector $\vec{d}$ has magnitude $|\vec{d}| = 15.0$ m at $\theta = 37°$ north of east.
Neatly sketch $\vec{d}$ with labeled horizontal ($d_x$) and vertical ($d_y$) components (include a right triangle and angle).

4. Eastward Component Calculation:

Compute the eastward component $d_x$. Show: formula → substitution → numeric result with units.

meters

5. Northward Component Calculation:

Compute the northward component $d_y$. Justify significant figures.

meters

Section 3: Scientific Modeling & Inquiry

6. Classroom Mapping:

The teacher’s desk is at coordinates $(x, y) = (4.0\ \text{m}, 3.0\ \text{m})$ from your chosen origin.
Determine the displacement vector $\vec{r}$ from the origin to the desk: give (i) component form $\vec{r} = \langle x, y \rangle$, (ii) magnitude $|\vec{r}|$, (iii) direction as an angle north of east (or using $\tan^{-1}$ with quadrant justification).

7. Reliability of Quantitative Mapping:

Explain (3–4 sentences) why a quantitative coordinate system yields higher reliability than a freehand sketch for classroom mapping. Reference precision, reproducibility, and error analysis.

Section 4: Cross-Cutting Concepts

8. Scale & Proportion:

Global Positioning System accuracy requires position resolution within about 1.0 m globally. Explain why expressing motion or position with vector components (rather than only a single magnitude and heading) enables this precision. Cite how component-wise error control works.

9. Patterns & Triangulation:

Connect Eratosthenes’s ancient Earth-size method to modern GPS triangulation. Identify the shared mathematical pattern and how proportional or angular differences translate into large-scale distance determination.

Metacognitive Reflection (Unscored)

Which single step in today’s work most improved your confidence with vectors or measurement, and why?
