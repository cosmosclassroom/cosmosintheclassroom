Problem Set 1.1: The Language of Measurement

HPHYS Unit 1

Difficulty: apprentice, journeyman, master

Submission Format: paper

Estimated Time: 45 minutes

Topics: dimensional-analysis, significant-figures, measurement

Introduction

In our last lecture, we discussed how BIG the goal of measurement is not just to get a number, but to communicate a value with honesty and precision. This problem set is your first opportunity to practice using the formal rules—dimensional analysis and significant figures—that allow scientists to achieve that goal. Complete this problem set on separate paper to be collected by your instructor.

problems:

  • id: “p1” difficulty: “apprentice” question: “The cheetah is the fastest land animal, capable of running at speeds of up to 72 km/hr. What is this speed in meters per second (m/s)?” solution: | \(\left( \frac{72 \text{ km}}{1 \text{ hr}} \right) \left( \frac{1000 \text{ m}}{1 \text{ km}} \right) \left( \frac{1 \text{ hr}}{3600 \text{ s}} \right) = 20 \text{ m/s}\) (The initial measurement has two significant figures, so the final answer must also have two.)
  • id: “p2” difficulty: “apprentice” question: “The official height of a basketball hoop is 10.0 feet. What is this height in meters? (Use the conversion factor: 1.000 inch = 2.540 cm)” solution: | \(\left( \frac{10.0 \text{ ft}}{1} \right) \left( \frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}} \right) \left( \frac{2.540 \text{ cm}}{1 \text{ in}} \right) \left( \frac{1 \text{ m}}{100 \text{ cm}} \right) = 3.05 \text{ m}\) (The initial measurement has three significant figures, so the answer is rounded to three.)
  • id: “p3” difficulty: “apprentice” question: “A marathon is a race that is 26.22 miles long. How many kilometers is this? (Use the conversion factor: 1.000 mi = 1.609 km)” solution: | \((26.22 \text{ mi}) \left( \frac{1.609 \text{ km}}{1 \text{ mi}} \right) = 42.19 \text{ km}\) (Both values have four significant figures, so the result must have four.)
  • id: “p4” difficulty: “apprentice” question: “Calculate the area of a rectangular plot of land that measures 24.5 meters long by 8.2 meters wide.” solution: | \(A = l \times w = (24.5 \text{ m})(8.2 \text{ m}) = 200.9 \text{ m}^2 \approx 2.0 \times 10^2 \text{ m}^2\) (The least precise measurement, 8.2 m, has two significant figures, so the final answer must be rounded to two.)
  • id: “p5” difficulty: “apprentice” question: “A student measures three consecutive displacements: 15.88 m, 2.1 m, and 105 m. What is the total displacement?” solution: | \(15.88 \text{ m} + 2.1 \text{ m} + 105 \text{ m} = 122.98 \text{ m} \approx 123 \text{ m}\) (When adding, the precision is limited by the least precise value. The number 105 m is precise to the ones place, so the final answer must be rounded to the ones place.)
  • id: “p6” difficulty: “apprentice” question: “An experimenter measures a time of 150.0 s. A second experimenter measures 0.55 s. You need to divide the first measurement by the second. What is the result?” solution: | \(\frac{150.0 \text{ s}}{0.55 \text{ s}} = 272.72... \approx 270 \text{ or } 2.7 \times 10^2\) (The least precise measurement, 0.55 s, has two significant figures, so the final answer must be rounded to two.)
  • id: “p7” difficulty: “journeyman” question: “In the Hexagon Lab, we learned that Eratosthenes measured the distance between the cities of Syene and Alexandria. A modern analysis suggests this distance was approximately 5.00e3 stadia. If one stadium is equivalent to 185 meters, what is the distance between the two cities in kilometers? Be sure your final answer is reported with the correct number of significant figures.” context: “This problem synthesizes multiple skills and connects them to our unit’s anchoring phenomenon.” solution: | \((5.00 \times 10^3 \text{ stadia}) \left( \frac{185 \text{ m}}{1 \text{ stadia}} \right) \left( \frac{1 \text{ km}}{1000 \text{ m}} \right) = 925 \text{ km}\) (Both initial measurements have three significant figures, so the final answer must also have three.)
  • id: “p8” difficulty: “master” question: “Why would it be scientifically dishonest for you to report the distance between the cities in the previous problem with seven significant figures (e.g., 925.0000 km), even if that’s what your calculator showed? Connect your answer to the principles of honest and precise measurement we discussed in our last lecture.” context: “This question is not about calculation; it is about thinking like a scientist. Answer in 2-3 complete sentences.” rubric: “A correct answer will state that reporting extra, non-significant digits implies a higher level of precision than was actually measured. It is dishonest because it misleads the reader about the certainty of the result, violating the principle that our reported values must reflect the precision of our original measurements.”
