Unit 3 Test: The Siege of Constantinople

Instructions:
You are an engineer in the army of Sultan Mehmed II. Your task is to apply the principles of physics to the great siege. Show all work for calculations, starting with the base formula(s). Ensure your final answers have the correct units and significant figures.

Part 1: The Apprentice’s Practice (Conceptual Understanding)

Select the best answer for each multiple-choice question.

1. A cannonball is fired at an angle of $30^\circ$ above the horizontal. At the very peak of its trajectory, what is true about its velocity?
a) Both its horizontal and vertical velocity components are zero.
b) Its vertical velocity is zero, but its horizontal velocity is non-zero.
c) Its horizontal velocity is zero, but its vertical velocity is non-zero.
d) Neither velocity component is zero.

2. To achieve the maximum possible horizontal range, a cannoneer should, in theory, set the launch angle of their cannon to:
a) $30^\circ$
b) $45^\circ$
c) $60^\circ$
d) $90^\circ$

3. A scout is walking at $1\,\text{m/s}$ across the deck of a galley ship that is moving at $10\,\text{m/s}$ along the coast. The maximum possible speed of the scout relative to the land is:
a) $9\,\text{m/s}$
b) $10\,\text{m/s}$
c) $11\,\text{m/s}$
d) $101\,\text{m/s}$

Part 2: The Journeyman’s Task (Problem Solving)

Solve the following problems. Show all steps clearly.

4. A trebuchet, a smaller siege engine, launches a large stone with an initial velocity of $45.0\,\text{m/s}$ at an angle of $50.0^\circ$ above the horizontal.

a) Calculate the initial horizontal ($v_{x,i}$) and vertical ($v_{y,i}$) components of the velocity.
b) What is the total time the stone is in the air?
c) What is the maximum height the stone reaches?
d) What is the horizontal range of the stone?

5. A supply skiff must cross the Golden Horn, a waterway that is $300.0\,\text{m}$ wide, to deliver materials to another part of the army. The crew can paddle the skiff at a speed of $2.50\,\text{m/s}$ in still water. The water’s current flows downstream at $1.00\,\text{m/s}$.

a) If the crew aims the skiff directly across the waterway, what is its resultant velocity (magnitude and direction) relative to the land?
b) How long does it take for the skiff to cross?
c) How far downstream does the current carry the skiff by the time it reaches the other side?

Part 3: The Master’s Insight (Synthesis & Analysis)

This problem requires you to synthesize multiple concepts to advise the Sultan himself.

6. The great cannon, “The Basilica,” is mounted on a massive, movable siege tower that is being winched parallel to the city walls at a constant speed of $1.50\,\text{m/s}$. A key defensive tower is located $1600\,\text{m}$ away (perpendicularly) from the siege tower’s path. The Basilica fires a cannonball with a muzzle velocity of $160.0\,\text{m/s}$. The cannoneer must aim the barrel both vertically (to achieve the correct range) and horizontally (to account for the siege tower’s motion).

Assume the cannoneer sets the vertical launch angle to $45.0^\circ$ for maximum range.

a) Ignoring the siege tower’s motion for a moment, calculate the theoretical range of the cannonball if fired from a stationary position. Is this range sufficient to hit a target $1600\,\text{m}$ away?
b) Using the given muzzle velocity and angle, calculate the total time of flight for the cannonball.
c) During this time of flight, the siege tower continues to move. To hit the stationary target, the cannoneer must aim the cannon barrel slightly backward (against the direction of motion). What is the angle (relative to a line aimed directly at the target) at which the cannon must be aimed to compensate for the siege tower’s forward movement?
