Where Are You? Position, Distance, & Displacement

  • Position ($x$): An object’s location relative to a defined origin.
  • Distance: The total path length traveled. It is a scalar.

  • Displacement ($\Delta\vec{x}$): The change in position ($\vec{x}{final} - \vec{x}{initial}$). It is a vector (magnitude and direction).

  • Example: You walk $7.0 \text{ m}$ East, then turn around and walk $3.0 \text{ m}$ West.
    • Distance traveled: $7.0 \text{ m} + 3.0 \text{ m} = 10.0 \text{ m}$
    • Displacement: $4.0 \text{ m, East}$

Position vs. Time Graphs

A position vs. time graph shows an object’s position at every moment.

The most important rule: The slope of a position vs. time graph represents the object’s velocity.

\[\text{slope} = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \text{velocity}\]

Position vs. Time: Accelerated Motion

When an object accelerates, its position vs. time graph becomes curved.

Reading the Story of a P-T Graph

  • Positive Slope: Object is moving in the positive direction (away from the origin).
  • Negative Slope: Object is moving in the negative direction (toward the origin).
  • Zero Slope (Horizontal Line): Object is at rest (velocity is zero).
  • Steeper Slope: Higher speed.
  • Curved Line: The velocity is changing (acceleration is occurring).

Velocity vs. Time Graphs

  1. The slope of the graph represents acceleration. \(\text{slope} = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \text{acceleration}\)
  2. The area under the graph represents displacement. \(\text{area} = \text{velocity} \times \text{time} = \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} \cdot \text{s} = \text{m}\)

Velocity vs. Time: Accelerated Motion

Key observations:

  • Constant slope = constant acceleration
  • Area under the curve = total displacement
  • Steeper slope = greater acceleration

Example: Analyzing the Rocket Launch

Let’s analyze the first 10 seconds of the rocket’s motion from our lab data.

  • Acceleration ($0-4s$): slope = $\frac{40.0 \text{ m/s} - 0.0 \text{ m/s}}{4.0 \text{ s} - 0.0 \text{ s}} = 10.0 \text{ m/s}^2$

  • Displacement ($0-4s$): area = $\frac{1}{2} \cdot \text{base} \cdot \text{height}$ area = $\frac{1}{2} (4.0 \text{ s})(40.0 \text{ m/s}) = 80.0 \text{ m}$

Check for Understanding

Describe the rocket’s motion from t = 4.0 s to t = 8.2 s.

  1. What is its acceleration?
    • It’s the slope. It’s constant and negative ($\approx -9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$).
  2. Is it speeding up or slowing down?
    • It’s slowing down. The velocity is positive but decreasing.
  3. How could we find its displacement during this time?
    • Find the area of the trapezoid between t=4s and t=8.2s.

Answering Our Essential Questions

1. What is the difference between distance and displacement?

  • Distance is the total path length traveled (scalar)
  • Displacement is the change in position with direction (vector)

2. How can we find velocity from a position-time graph?

  • Velocity = slope of the position-time graph
  • Steeper slope = higher speed; negative slope = motion toward origin

3. What do the slope and area of a velocity-time graph represent?

  • Slope = acceleration (rate of velocity change)
  • Area = displacement (total change in position)

Summary and Next Steps

Key Rules:

  • Position-Time:
    • Slope = Velocity
  • Velocity-Time:
    • Slope = Acceleration
    • Area = Displacement
