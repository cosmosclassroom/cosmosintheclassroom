Unit 2 Study Guide: The Art of Observation

Key Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

Mathematical Skills:

  • Select the correct kinematic equation based on the given and unknown variables.
  • Algebraically rearrange any kinematic equation to solve for any of its variables.
  • Correctly apply signs to displacement, velocity, and acceleration based on a chosen coordinate system.
  • Solve multi-stage motion problems where acceleration changes between stages.
  • Calculate displacement, velocity, and acceleration from the slopes and areas of motion graphs.

Scientific Practices:

  • Develop mathematical models (equations and graphs) to represent the motion of an object.
  • Analyze and interpret graphical data to describe an object’s motion in words.
  • Construct explanations for the motion of objects in free fall.

Cross-Cutting Concepts:

  • Patterns: Recognize that motion with constant acceleration exhibits predictable mathematical patterns (e.g., a linear velocity-time graph and a quadratic position-time graph).
  • Cause and Effect: Explain how acceleration (cause) leads to a change in an object’s velocity (effect).

Common Mistakes and Misconceptions

Mathematical Errors:

  • Sign Errors: Forgetting that “down” is negative for displacement, velocity, and acceleration in a standard “up is positive” coordinate system.
  • Vi vs. Vf: Mixing up initial and final velocity in your equations.
  • Forgetting to Square: Forgetting the \(t^2\) term in the displacement equation \(\Delta x = \overrightarrow{v_i} t + \frac{1}{2} a t^2\).
  • Average vs. Instantaneous: Using average velocity in a formula that requires instantaneous velocity.

Conceptual Misunderstandings:

  • Acceleration at the Peak: Thinking that when an object thrown upward reaches its highest point, its acceleration is zero. (False: velocity is momentarily zero, but gravity is still accelerating it downward).
  • Velocity vs. Acceleration: Confusing a negative velocity (moving down) with a negative acceleration (slowing down while moving up, or speeding up while moving down).
  • Graph Interpretation: Confusing the meaning of the slope (rate of change) and the area under the curve for different graphs.

Essential Vocabulary & Formulae

Key Vocabulary:

  • Displacement (\(\Delta \overrightarrow{x}\) or \(\Delta \overrightarrow{y}\)): The change in an object’s position; a vector quantity.
  • Velocity (\(\overrightarrow{v}\)): The rate of change of position; a vector quantity (speed with direction).
  • Speed: The magnitude of velocity; a scalar quantity.
  • Acceleration (\(\overrightarrow{a}\)): The rate of change of velocity; a vector quantity.
  • Free Fall: The motion of an object under the influence of gravity alone, with a constant downward acceleration of \(a_g = 9.80 \, \text{m/s}^2\).

The Kinematic Equations (for constant acceleration):

  • \[\overrightarrow{v_f} = \overrightarrow{v_i} + \overrightarrow{a} t\]
  • \[\Delta \overrightarrow{x} = \frac{1}{2} (\overrightarrow{v_i} + \overrightarrow{v_f}) t\]
  • \[\Delta \overrightarrow{x} = \overrightarrow{v_i} t + \frac{1}{2} \overrightarrow{a} t^2\]
  • \[\overrightarrow{v_f}^2 = \overrightarrow{v_i}^2 + 2\overrightarrow{a} \Delta \overrightarrow{x}\]

Core Concepts & Guiding Questions

The Big Picture: Telling the Story of Motion

Our anchoring phenomenon is the launch of a sounding rocket. Its flight has multiple stages, each with a different acceleration. Your goal is to be able to tell the complete story of its motion using graphs, words, and equations.

  1. The Language of Graphs:
    • How can you look at a position-time graph and know if an object is moving forward, backward, speeding up, or slowing down? (Hint: What does the slope represent?)
    • How can you look at a velocity-time graph and determine the object’s acceleration? How can you determine its displacement? (Hint: What do the slope and the area under the curve represent?)
    • Sketch the position, velocity, and acceleration graphs for the rocket’s powered ascent (constant positive acceleration) and its coasting phase (constant negative acceleration).
  2. Motion with Constant Acceleration (The Kinematic Equations):
    • What condition must be true to use the kinematic equations?
    • How do you decide which of the four equations is the right tool for the job?
    • A ball is thrown straight up. What is its velocity at its highest point? What is its acceleration at its highest point?
  3. Multi-Stage Motion:
    • How do you connect two different stages of a problem? (Hint: What does the final velocity of the first stage become for the second stage?)
    • For a problem like the stunt performer falling into the airbag, why do you need to solve it in two separate parts?
