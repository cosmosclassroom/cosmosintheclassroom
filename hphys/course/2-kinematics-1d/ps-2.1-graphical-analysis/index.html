Problem Set 2.1: Graphical Analysis of Motion

HPHYS Unit 2

Difficulty: apprentice, journeyman, master

Submission Format: paper

Estimated Time: 45 minutes

Topics: kinematics, graphs, velocity, acceleration

Introduction: In the rocket lab, you analyzed velocity-time data to understand different phases of motion. Now you’ll practice interpreting various types of motion graphs and extracting quantitative information from them. Remember: the slope of a position-time graph gives velocity, and the slope of a velocity-time graph gives acceleration.

Mathematical Relationships Reference:

  • Slope of position-time graph = velocity
  • Slope of velocity-time graph = acceleration
  • Area under velocity-time graph = displacement
  • Average velocity = $\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$
  • Average acceleration = $\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$

  • id: “2.1.A.1” difficulty: “apprentice” question: | Problem 1: Walking to School Use the provided dataset to answer the following: a) What is the student’s average velocity between t = 120 s and t = 480 s? b) How far does the student travel in the first 240 s? c) What is the student’s displacement after 600 s? dataset: problemId: “2.1.A.1” title: “Walking to School” description: “Position-time data for a student walking to school at constant velocity” xAxis: {label: “Time (s)”, range: [0, 600]} yAxis: {label: “Position (m)”, range: [0, 900]} dataPoints: - {x: 0, y: 0} - {x: 120, y: 180} - {x: 240, y: 360} - {x: 360, y: 540} - {x: 480, y: 720} - {x: 600, y: 900} graphType: “position_vs_time” solution: | a) Velocity is the slope: $v = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{720 \text{ m} - 180 \text{ m}}{480 \text{ s} - 120 \text{ s}} = \frac{540 \text{ m}}{360 \text{ s}} = 1.5 \text{ m/s}$. b) From the data table, the position at t = 240 s is 360 m. c) The final displacement at t = 600 s is the final position, 900 m.

  • id: “2.1.A.2” difficulty: “apprentice” question: | Problem 2: Bicycle Ride Analysis Use the provided dataset to answer the following: a) Calculate the bicycle’s acceleration between t = 4.0 s and t = 10.0 s. b) What is the bicycle’s displacement during the first 8.0 s? (Hint: Find the area under the curve) c) If this acceleration continues, what will the bicycle’s velocity be at t = 16.0 s? dataset: problemId: “2.1.A.2” title: “Bicycle Velocity Analysis” description: “Velocity-time data for a bicycle rider accelerating from rest” xAxis: {label: “Time (s)”, range: [0, 12]} yAxis: {label: “Velocity (m/s)”, range: [0, 18]} dataPoints: - {x: 0, y: 0} - {x: 2, y: 3.0} - {x: 4, y: 6.0} - {x: 6, y: 9.0} - {x: 8, y: 12.0} - {x: 10, y: 15.0} - {x: 12, y: 18.0} graphType: “velocity_vs_time” solution: | a) Acceleration is the slope: $a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{15.0 \text{ m/s} - 6.0 \text{ m/s}}{10.0 \text{ s} - 4.0 \text{ s}} = \frac{9.0 \text{ m/s}}{6.0 \text{ s}} = 1.5 \text{ m/s}^2$. b) Displacement is the area (triangle): $\Delta x = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} (8.0 \text{ s})(12.0 \text{ m/s}) = 48 \text{ m}$. c) Using $v = v_0 + at$: $v = 0 + (1.5 \text{ m/s}^2)(16.0 \text{ s}) = 24 \text{ m/s}$.

  • id: “2.1.A.3” difficulty: “apprentice” question: | Problem 3: Elevator Motion Use the provided dataset to answer the following: a) During which time intervals is the elevator accelerating? b) What is the elevator’s acceleration between t = 10.0 s and t = 14.0 s? c) Calculate the elevator’s total displacement from t = 0 to t = 20.0 s. dataset: problemId: “2.1.A.3” title: “Elevator Velocity Profile” description: “Velocity-time data for an elevator moving between floors” xAxis: {label: “Time (s)”, range: [0, 20]} yAxis: {label: “Velocity (m/s)”, range: [-3, 5]} dataPoints: - {x: 0, y: 0} - {x: 2, y: 4.0} - {x: 6, y: 4.0} - {x: 10, y: 4.0} - {x: 14, y: 0} - {x: 16, y: 0} - {x: 18, y: -2.0} - {x: 20, y: -2.0} graphType: “velocity_vs_time” solution: | a) The elevator is accelerating when the velocity graph has a non-zero slope. This occurs during the intervals 0-2 s, 10-14 s, and 16-18 s. b) $a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{0 \text{ m/s} - 4.0 \text{ m/s}}{14.0 \text{ s} - 10.0 \text{ s}} = \frac{-4.0 \text{ m/s}}{4.0 \text{ s}} = -1.0 \text{ m/s}^2$. c) Total displacement is the total area under the curve: Area (0-14s) = Area(trapezoid) = $\frac{1}{2}(14+8)(4.0) = 44$ m. Area (16-20s) = Area(trapezoid) = $\frac{1}{2}(4+2)(-2.0) = -6$ m. Total Displacement = $44 \text{ m} - 6 \text{ m} = 38 \text{ m}$.

  • id: “2.1.B.4” difficulty: “journeyman” question: | Problem 4: Rocket Telemetry Analysis Building on your lab experience, analyze this velocity-time data from a different sounding rocket launch. a) Determine the rocket’s acceleration during the engine burn phase. b) Determine the rocket’s acceleration during the ballistic (engine-off) phase. c) At what time does the engine cut off? Provide evidence from the data. d) Calculate when this rocket will reach apogee (maximum altitude). dataset: problemId: “2.1.B.4” title: “Alternative Rocket Launch Profile” description: “Velocity-time data from sounding rocket with different engine burn time” xAxis: {label: “Time (s)”, range: [0, 50]} yAxis: {label: “Velocity (m/s)”, range: [0, 600]} dataPoints: - {x: 0, y: 0} - {x: 5, y: 120} - {x: 10, y: 240} - {x: 15, y: 360} - {x: 25, y: 480} - {x: 30, y: 431} - {x: 35, y: 382} - {x: 40, y: 333} - {x: 45, y: 284} - {x: 50, y: 235} graphType: “velocity_vs_time” solution: | a) The burn phase appears to last until t=25 s, when velocity is maximum. The acceleration is not constant. From 0-15s, $a = \frac{360 \text{ m/s}}{15 \text{ s}} = 24 \text{ m/s}^2$. From 15-25s, $a = \frac{480-360 \text{ m/s}}{10 \text{ s}} = 12 \text{ m/s}^2$. The average acceleration is $\frac{480 \text{ m/s}}{25 \text{ s}} = 19.2 \text{ m/s}^2$. b) The ballistic phase begins after t=25 s. $a = \frac{235 \text{ m/s} - 480 \text{ m/s}}{50 \text{ s} - 25 \text{ s}} = \frac{-245 \text{ m/s}}{25 \text{ s}} = -9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$. c) The engine cuts off at t = 25 s. The evidence is the abrupt change in acceleration from positive (thrust) to a constant -9.8 m/s² (gravity alone). d) Apogee occurs when v=0. Using the ballistic phase data with $v_0 = 480$ m/s at $t_0 = 25$ s: $0 = 480 + (-9.8)(t - 25)$. Solving for t gives $t \approx 74$ s.
