SECTION 1: PROBLEM SET OVERVIEW

——————————————————————————

overview: introduction: | A sounding rocket claws away from the pad—engines thunder, velocity climbs, fuel exhausts, silence; only Earth’s steady pull remains. With nothing more than a handful of symbols we can now reconstruct its entire story: when it coasts, how high it soars, when it reverses course.

expectation: | Show algebra first, substitute numbers second, include units and correct significant figures. Choose a consistent positive direction (state it). Use $g = 9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$ (magnitude) so $\vec{a}_g = -9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$ when up is positive.

key_formulae: - “$v_f = v_i + a t$” - “$\Delta x = v_i t + \tfrac{1}{2} a t^2$” - “$v_f^2 = v_i^2 + 2 a \Delta x$” - “$v_{avg} = \dfrac{v_i + v_f}{2}$ (constant $a$ only)” - “Freefall: $a = -9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$ (if up is +)”

SECTION 2: PROBLEMS

——————————————————————————

problems: # ========================= TIER 1: APPRENTICE =============================

  • problem_number: “A.1.1” tier: level: 1 title: “Apprentice Level: Building the Toolbox” section: “Equation Identification & Unit Skills” problem_statement: “Convert $108.0 \text{ km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$. Show dimensional analysis.” solution: final_answer: “30.0 m/s”

  • problem_number: “A.1.2” tier: level: 1 title: “Apprentice Level: Building the Toolbox” section: “Equation Identification & Unit Skills” problem_statement: | A sprinter accelerates uniformly from rest to $11.2 \text{ m/s}$ in $4.00 \text{ s}$. a) Which kinematic equation directly relates $v_i, v_f, a, t$? b) Compute the acceleration. solution: final_answer: a: “Eqn: $v_f = v_i + at$” b: “$a = 2.80\ \text{m/s}^2$”

  • problem_number: “A.1.3” tier: level: 1 title: “Apprentice Level: Building the Toolbox” section: “Equation Identification & Unit Skills” problem_statement: | A bicycle traveling $6.50 \text{ m/s}$ accelerates uniformly at $1.20 \text{ m/s}^2$ for $3.00 \text{ s}$. Find (a) its final speed, (b) displacement during this interval (two methods: (i) Eq. 2, (ii) average velocity). Show agreement. solution: final_answer: “$v_f = 10.1\ \text{m/s}$, $\Delta x = 24.9\ \text{m}$”

  • problem_number: “A.1.4” tier: level: 1 title: “Apprentice Level: Building the Toolbox” section: “Equation Identification & Unit Skills” problem_statement: | An object is dropped from rest and falls $2.00 \text{ m}$. a) Which equation omits time and lets you solve directly for $v_f$? b) Compute $v_f$ on impact. solution: final_answer: “$|v_f| = 6.26\ \text{m/s}$ downward”

  • problem_number: “A.1.5” tier: level: 1 title: “Apprentice Level: Building the Toolbox” section: “Equation Identification & Unit Skills” problem_statement: | Solve algebraically for $t$ from $\Delta x = v_i t + \tfrac{1}{2} a t^2$ (quadratic form). Present the plus/minus expression and explain why only one root is physical in typical motion problems. solution: final_answer: “$t = \dfrac{-v_i \pm \sqrt{v_i^2 + 2 a \Delta x}}{a}$ (choose positive root)”

  • problem_number: “A.2.6” tier: level: 1 title: “Apprentice Level: Building the Toolbox” section: “Significant Figures Focus” problem_statement: | A lab cart accelerates at $0.845 \text{ m/s}^2$ from $0.00 \text{ m/s}$ for $2.60 \text{ s}$. Report $\Delta x$ with proper significant figures. Justify your significant digit choice. solution: final_answer: “$\Delta x = 2.86\ \text{m}$”

# ========================= TIER 2: JOURNEYMAN =============================

  • problem_number: “J.7” tier: level: 2 title: “Journeyman Level: Telling the Story” problem_statement: | A ball thrown straight up has $v=0$ only at the very top. Explain why its acceleration is not zero there (2–3 sentences). solution: final_answer: “Acceleration still $-9.80\ \text{m/s}^2$ (gravity acts; velocity zero only instantaneously)”

  • problem_number: “J.8” tier: level: 2 title: “Journeyman Level: Telling the Story” problem_statement: | Two objects are dropped: one from $5.0 \text{ m}$, another from $20.0 \text{ m}$. Which has the larger (a) impact speed? (b) average acceleration? Justify with equations. solution: final_answer: a: “20 m drop faster” b: “same avg acceleration $-9.80\ \text{m/s}^2$”

  • problem_number: “J.9” tier: level: 2 title: “Journeyman Level: Telling the Story” problem_statement: | For motion under constant negative acceleration, sketch (qualitatively) velocity–time graphs for: a) Object thrown upward, caught at same height. b) Object simply dropped. Label slopes and intercepts; explain one key difference. solution: final_answer: “Up-then-down line (crosses zero) vs. starting at zero and decreasing linearly”

  • problem_number: “J.10” tier: level: 2 title: “Journeyman Level: Telling the Story” problem_statement: | A drone descends vertically at $-3.00 \text{ m/s}$ (downward) then accelerates uniformly at $+2.00 \text{ m/s}^2$ (upward direction positive) for $1.80 \text{ s}$ to slow. a) Final velocity? b) Displacement over the interval? State if drone net moved up or down. solution: final_answer: “$v_f = +0.60\ \text{m/s}$ (slight up), $\Delta x = -2.16\ \text{m}$”

  • problem_number: “J.11” tier: level: 2 title: “Journeyman Level: Telling the Story” problem_statement: | A rock is thrown upward at $18.0 \text{ m/s}$ from a cliff $45.0 \text{ m}$ above the ground. a) Time to reach maximum height. b) Maximum height above the ground. c) Total time until it hits the ground. (Break into up + down; or solve with quadratic—show method.) d) Impact speed (magnitude). solution: final_answer: “$t_{tot}=5.39\ \text{s}$, $h_{max}=61.5\ \text{m}$, impact speed $34.8\ \text{m/s}$”

  • problem_number: “J.12” tier: level: 2 title: “Journeyman Level: Telling the Story” problem_statement: | A car accelerates uniformly from $15.0 \text{ m/s}$ to $25.0 \text{ m/s}$ over $120 \text{ m}$. a) Find acceleration using the time‑independent equation. b) Time for this change. c) Average velocity (two methods: displacement/time and $(v_i+v_f)/2$). solution: final_answer: “$a=1.67\ \text{m/s}^2$, $t=5.99\ \text{s}$, $v_{avg}=20.0\ \text{m/s}$”

  • problem_number: “J.13” tier: level: 2 title: “Journeyman Level: Telling the Story” problem_statement: | A ball is thrown downward from a balcony with initial speed $5.00 \text{ m/s}$. It hits the ground $1.10 \text{ s}$ later. a) Displacement (include sign). b) Height of balcony above ground (magnitude). c) Final speed just before impact. solution: final_answer: “Height $=10.4\ \text{m}$, $v_f=15.8\ \text{m/s}$ downward”

# ========================= TIER 3: MASTER =================================

  • problem_number: “M.14” tier: level: 3 title: “Master Level: Extending the Model” problem_statement: | A student measures reaction time using a dropped meter stick. If the stick falls $21.0 \text{ cm}$ before being caught, compute reaction time (assume $v_i=0$). Show equation choice and algebra. solution: final_answer: “$t = 0.207\ \text{s}$”

  • problem_number: “M.15” tier: level: 3 title: “Master Level: Extending the Model” problem_statement: | Two-phase motion: A cart starts from rest and accelerates at $1.50 \text{ m/s}^2$ for $6.00 \text{ s}$, then coasts at constant velocity for $4.00 \text{ s}$. a) Velocity at end of acceleration phase. b) Displacement during acceleration. c) Displacement during coasting. d) Total displacement and average velocity over the entire 10.0 s. solution: final_answer: “$\Delta x_{total}=63.0\ \text{m}$, $v_{avg}=6.30\ \text{m/s}$”

  • problem_number: “M.16” tier: level: 3 title: “Master Level: Extending the Model” problem_statement: | Error propagation concept (qualitative): In Problem 14, if the distance measurement has a ±1 cm uncertainty, explain (2–3 sentences) how that affects the time result (reference functional dependence $t = \sqrt{2\Delta x / |a|}$). solution: final_answer: “Fractional time uncertainty ≈ half fractional distance uncertainty”

  • problem_number: “M.17” tier: level: 3 title: “Master Level: Extending the Model” problem_statement: | You have four equations but five variables. Explain why this is sufficient to solve any uniformly accelerated motion problem with three knowns by citing degrees of freedom (2–4 sentences). solution: final_answer: “3 knowns → one equation with one unknown always reachable (variable elimination)”

  • problem_number: “M.18” tier: level: 3 title: “Master Level: Extending the Model” problem_statement: | A probe near Earth’s surface (vertical motion only) must limit impact speed to $< 12.0 \text{ m/s}$. It retro-thrusts upward with constant acceleration starting when its downward speed is $35.0 \text{ m/s}$ and altitude is $160 \text{ m}$. a) Minimum constant upward acceleration required (take up as positive; acceleration will be +). b) Time the burn lasts. c) Altitude lost during burn. d) Is altitude margin sufficient? Justify numerically. solution: final_answer: “$a_{min}=3.38\ \text{m/s}^2$, $t=6.80\ \text{s}$, altitude margin = fully used (0 buffer)”

  • problem_number: “M.19” tier: level: 3 title: “Master Level: Extending the Model” problem_statement: | Design: You must time a fast process (~0.050 s) but only possess a meter stick and your reflexes. Propose a feasible measurement strategy using freefall (4–5 sentences) and identify two main error sources + mitigation. solution: final_answer: “Freefall timing via drop distance; errors: distance read & release/catch reaction (mitigate: repeated trials, fixed height)”

  • problem_number: “M.20” tier: level: 3 title: “Master Level: Extending the Model” problem_statement: | Model limits: List two real-world effects NOT included in our constant acceleration model for a falling object and describe the direction each would change predicted impact time or speed. solution: final_answer: “Missing effects: air resistance (↑ time, ↓ speed); varying $g$ (↑ time slightly)”
