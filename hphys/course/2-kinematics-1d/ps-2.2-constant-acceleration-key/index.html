Solution Key: Problem Set 2.2 - Kinematics with Constant Acceleration

HPHYS Unit 2

Last Updated: October 29, 2025

Topics: kinematics, solutions, constant-acceleration, solution-key, teacher-resource

📚 Pedagogical Note: When presenting solutions to students, show algebraic work step-by-step rather than distributing this document directly. Model the problem-solving process.

Instructor Use Only – Do not distribute directly. Show algebra first when presenting solutions to students.

Conventions: Upward (or stated positive) direction taken as + unless noted. $g=9.80\ \text{m/s}^2$.

Part A

  1. $108.0\ \text{km/h}\rightarrow \text{m/s}$
    $108.0\ \text{km/h}\left(\dfrac{1000\ \text{m}}{1\ \text{km}}\right)\left(\dfrac{1\ \text{h}}{3600\ \text{s}}\right)=30.0\ \text{m/s}$ (3 sig figs)

  2. Sprinter: $v_i=0,\ v_f=11.2,\ t=4.00$ s
    (a) Use $v_f=v_i+at$ → $a=\dfrac{v_f-v_i}{t}=\dfrac{11.2}{4.00}=2.80\ \text{m/s}^2$

  3. Bicycle: $v_i=6.50,\ a=1.20,\ t=3.00$
    (a) $v_f=v_i+at=6.50+1.20(3.00)=10.1\ \text{m/s}$
    (b-i) $\Delta x=v_i t+\tfrac12 a t^2=6.50(3.00)+0.5(1.20)(9.00)=19.5+5.40=24.9\ \text{m}$
    (b-ii) $v_{avg}=\dfrac{v_i+v_f}{2}=\dfrac{6.50+10.1}{2}=8.30\ \text{m/s}$ → $\Delta x=v_{avg}t=8.30(3.00)=24.9\ \text{m}$ (agreement)

  4. Drop: $v_i=0,\ \Delta x=-2.00\ \text{m},\ a=-9.80$
    (a) Use time‑independent: $v_f^2=v_i^2+2a\Delta x$
    (b) $v_f=\sqrt{2(-9.80)(-2.00)}=\sqrt{39.2}=6.26\ \text{m/s}$ downward → $v_f=-6.26\ \text{m/s}$ (sign shows direction)

  5. Solve $\Delta x=v_i t+\tfrac12 a t^2$ for $t$:
    $\tfrac12 a t^2+v_i t-\Delta x=0$ → $t=\dfrac{-v_i\pm\sqrt{v_i^2+2a\Delta x}}{a}$
    Physical root: choose sign giving $t>0$ (depends on sign of $a$ and $v_i$).

  6. Cart: $v_i=0,\ a=0.845,\ t=2.60$
    $\Delta x=\tfrac12 a t^2=0.5(0.845)(2.60^2)=0.4225(6.76)=2.86\ \text{m}$ (3 sig figs)

Part B

  1. At peak: $v=0$ but acceleration remains constant $-9.80\ \text{m/s}^2$ because gravity acts continuously; zero velocity does not imply zero net force.

  2. Heights 5.0 m vs 20.0 m (drop).
    Impact speed from $v_f=\sqrt{2g h}$. Larger $h$ → larger $v_f$; thus 20.0 m object faster.
    Average acceleration both: $-9.80\ \text{m/s}^2$ (same).

  3. Graphs qualitative:
    (a) Throw up: positive $v$ decreasing linearly → crosses zero at top → negative $v$ of equal magnitude on descent (straight line slope $-9.80$).
    (b) Drop: starts at $v=0$ then linear negative slope $-9.80$. Difference: (a) has positive segment before zero crossing.

Part C

  1. Drone: Take up positive. $v_i=-3.00,\ a=+2.00,\ t=1.80$
    (a) $v_f=v_i+at=-3.00+2.00(1.80)=0.60\ \text{m/s}$ (now slight upward)
    (b) $\Delta x=v_i t+\tfrac12 a t^2= (-3.00)(1.80)+0.5(2.00)(1.80^2)= -5.40+ (1.00)(3.24)= -2.16\ \text{m}$ (net still downward)

  2. Rock: $v_i=+18.0,\ y_0=45.0\ \text{m},\ a=-9.80$
    (a) $t_{up}= \dfrac{-v_i}{a}=\dfrac{-18.0}{-9.80}=1.84\ \text{s}$
    (b) $\Delta y_{up}=v_i t_{up}+\tfrac12 a t_{up}^2=18.0(1.84)+0.5(-9.80)(1.84^2)=33.1-16.6=16.5\ \text{m}$ → $y_{max}=45.0+16.5=61.5\ \text{m}$
    (c) Descent distance: $61.5\ \text{m}$ to ground → solve $0=61.5+0\cdot t+\tfrac12(-9.80)t^2$ → $t_{down}=\sqrt{\dfrac{2(61.5)}{9.80}}=\sqrt{12.6}=3.55\ \text{s}$
    Total time $t_{total}=1.84+3.55=5.39\ \text{s}$
    (d) Impact speed: $v_f= v_i + a t_{total}=18.0 + (-9.80)(5.39)=18.0-52.8=-34.8\ \text{m/s}$ (speed 34.8 m/s downward)

  3. Car: $v_i=15.0,\ v_f=25.0,\ \Delta x=120$
    (a) $v_f^2=v_i^2+2a\Delta x \Rightarrow a=\dfrac{v_f^2-v_i^2}{2\Delta x}=\dfrac{625-225}{240}= \dfrac{400}{240}=1.67\ \text{m/s}^2$
    (b) $t=\dfrac{v_f-v_i}{a}=\dfrac{10.0}{1.67}=5.99\ \text{s}$
    (c) $v_{avg}=\dfrac{v_i+v_f}{2}=20.0\ \text{m/s}$; $\Delta x/t=120/5.99=20.0\ \text{m/s}$ (agreement)

  4. Ball thrown downward: define down positive so $v_i=+5.00,\ t=1.10,\ a=+9.80$
    (a) $\Delta x= v_i t + \tfrac12 a t^2=5.00(1.10)+0.5(9.80)(1.10^2)=5.50+4.90=10.4\ \text{m}$
    (b) Height = 10.4 m
    (c) $v_f= v_i + a t=5.00+9.80(1.10)=15.8\ \text{m/s}$ downward

Part D

  1. Reaction time drop: $\Delta x=0.210\ \text{m},\ v_i=0,\ a=9.80$
    $t=\sqrt{\dfrac{2\Delta x}{a}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{0.420}{9.80}}=\sqrt{0.0429}=0.207\ \text{s}$

  2. Two-phase cart: Phase 1: $a=1.50,\ t_1=6.00,\ v_i=0$
    (a) $v_1= a t_1=9.00\ \text{m/s}$
    (b) $\Delta x_1= \tfrac12 a t_1^2=0.5(1.50)(36.0)=27.0\ \text{m}$
    Phase 2: constant $v=9.00,\ t_2=4.00$
    (c) $\Delta x_2=9.00(4.00)=36.0\ \text{m}$
    (d) Total $\Delta x=63.0\ \text{m}$; total $t=10.0\ \text{s}$ → $v_{avg}=6.30\ \text{m/s}$

  3. Uncertainty propagation qualitative: $t\propto\sqrt{\Delta x}$ so fractional uncertainty halves: $\dfrac{\delta t}{t}=\tfrac12 \dfrac{\delta (\Delta x)}{\Delta x}$. A ±1 cm on 21.0 cm (≈4.8%) gives time uncertainty ≈2.4%.

  4. Four equations each exclude one variable. Knowing any 3 of the 5 variables yields one equation directly or a two‑equation system; degrees of freedom reduced so system solvable without overconstraint (one unknown after selections).

Part E

  1. Probe retro-thrust (taking up positive). Given initial downward speed $v_i=-35.0\ \text{m/s}$, target $ v_f <12.0\ \text{m/s}$ (choose $v_f=-12.0$ for minimum required decel), $\Delta x$ available = -160 m (will lose altitude). Need minimum constant upward acceleration $a$ over same distance OR solve via two unknowns approach:

First solve with velocity constraint minimizing required $a$ if stopping just before exceeding altitude: Using $v_f^2=v_i^2+2a\Delta x$. We do not know $\Delta x$ consumed yet; instead better to ensure speed reduction before ground: Use $v_f = v_i + a t$ with displacement $\Delta x= v_i t + \tfrac12 a t^2$; two equations, unknown $t,a$. Eliminate $t$: $t=\dfrac{v_f-v_i}{a}$. Substitute:

$\Delta x = v_i \dfrac{v_f-v_i}{a} + \tfrac12 a \left(\dfrac{v_f - v_i}{a}\right)^2 = \dfrac{v_i(v_f-v_i)}{a} + \dfrac{(v_f - v_i)^2}{2a}$

We require $\Delta x \ge -160\ \text{m}$ (negative). Compute numerator:

$v_f - v_i = (-12) - (-35)=23\ \text{m/s}$; $v_i(v_f - v_i)=(-35)(23)=-805$; $(v_f - v_i)^2=529$.

$\Delta x = \dfrac{-805 + 529/2}{a} = \dfrac{-805 + 264.5}{a} = \dfrac{-540.5}{a}$

Set $\Delta x = -160$ (use full altitude → minimum $a$):

$-160= -540.5/a \Rightarrow a= \dfrac{540.5}{160}=3.38\ \text{m/s}^2$ upward (net acceleration).
(a) $a=+3.38\ \text{m/s}^2$
(b) $t=(v_f - v_i)/a=23/3.38=6.80\ \text{s}$
(c) Altitude lost: $160\ \text{m}$ (by construction). Distance consumed equals margin, so just meets constraint.
(d) Margin? None (zero buffer). To add safety reduce $|v_f|$ further or start burn earlier.

  1. Strategy: Drop an object, measure average fall distance corresponding to reaction catch; use $t=\sqrt{2\Delta x/g}$ to estimate reaction time. Repeat many trials; average reduces random error. Major errors: (1) distance reading parallax—mitigate by aligning eye perpendicular / using camera still; (2) inconsistent release height—use a clamp or fixed top reference.

  2. Model omissions: air resistance (would increase fall time, reduce impact speed); variation of $g$ with altitude (for tall distances would slightly decrease $ a $, increasing time). Surface compliance / energy losses upon impact not predicted by ideal equations (affects post-impact not fall).
