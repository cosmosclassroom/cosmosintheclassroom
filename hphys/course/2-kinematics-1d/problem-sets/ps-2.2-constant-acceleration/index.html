Problem Set 2.2: Kinematics with Constant Acceleration

A sounding rocket claws away from the pad—engines thunder, velocity climbs, fuel exhausts, silence; only Earth’s steady pull remains. With nothing more than a handful of symbols we can now reconstruct its entire story: when it coasts, how high it soars, when it reverses course.

Expectation: Show algebra first, substitute numbers second, include units and correct significant figures. Choose a consistent positive direction (state it). Use $g = 9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$ (magnitude) so $\vec{a}_g = -9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$ when up is positive.

Apprentice Level: Building the Toolbox (Problems 1–6)

A.1 Equation Identification & Unit Skills

  1. Convert $108.0 \text{ km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$. Show dimensional analysis.
  2. A sprinter accelerates uniformly from rest to $11.2 \text{ m/s}$ in $4.00 \text{ s}$.
    a) Which kinematic equation directly relates $v_i, v_f, a, t$?
    b) Compute the acceleration.
  3. A bicycle traveling $6.50 \text{ m/s}$ accelerates uniformly at $1.20 \text{ m/s}^2$ for $3.00 \text{ s}$. Find (a) its final speed, (b) displacement during this interval (two methods: (i) Eq. 2, (ii) average velocity). Show agreement.
  4. An object is dropped from rest and falls $2.00 \text{ m}$.
    a) Which equation omits time and lets you solve directly for $v_f$?
    b) Compute $v_f$ on impact.
  5. Solve algebraically for $t$ from $\Delta x = v_i t + \tfrac{1}{2} a t^2$ (quadratic form). Present the plus/minus expression and explain why only one root is physical in typical motion problems.

A.2 Significant Figures Focus

  1. A lab cart accelerates at $0.845 \text{ m/s}^2$ from $0.00 \text{ m/s}$ for $2.60 \text{ s}$. Report $\Delta x$ with proper significant figures. Justify your significant digit choice.

Journeyman Level: Telling the Story (Problems 7–13)

  1. A ball thrown straight up has $v=0$ only at the very top. Explain why its acceleration is not zero there (2–3 sentences).
  2. Two objects are dropped: one from $5.0 \text{ m}$, another from $20.0 \text{ m}$. Which has the larger (a) impact speed? (b) average acceleration? Justify with equations.

  3. For motion under constant negative acceleration, sketch (qualitatively) velocity–time graphs for:
    a) Object thrown upward, caught at same height.
    b) Object simply dropped.
    Label slopes and intercepts; explain one key difference.

  4. A drone descends vertically at $-3.00 \text{ m/s}$ (downward) then accelerates uniformly at $+2.00 \text{ m/s}^2$ (upward direction positive) for $1.80 \text{ s}$ to slow.
    a) Final velocity?
    b) Displacement over the interval? State if drone net moved up or down.
  5. A rock is thrown upward at $18.0 \text{ m/s}$ from a cliff $45.0 \text{ m}$ above the ground.
    a) Time to reach maximum height.
    b) Maximum height above the ground.
    c) Total time until it hits the ground. (Break into up + down; or solve with quadratic—show method.)
    d) Impact speed (magnitude).
  6. A car accelerates uniformly from $15.0 \text{ m/s}$ to $25.0 \text{ m/s}$ over $120 \text{ m}$.
    a) Find acceleration using the time‑independent equation.
    b) Time for this change.
    c) Average velocity (two methods: displacement/time and $(v_i+v_f)/2$).
  7. A ball is thrown downward from a balcony with initial speed $5.00 \text{ m/s}$. It hits the ground $1.10 \text{ s}$ later.
    a) Displacement (include sign).
    b) Height of balcony above ground (magnitude).
    c) Final speed just before impact.

Master Level: Extending the Model (Problems 14–20)

  1. A student measures reaction time using a dropped meter stick. If the stick falls $21.0 \text{ cm}$ before being caught, compute reaction time (assume $v_i=0$). Show equation choice and algebra.
  2. Two-phase motion: A cart starts from rest and accelerates at $1.50 \text{ m/s}^2$ for $6.00 \text{ s}$, then coasts at constant velocity for $4.00 \text{ s}$.
    a) Velocity at end of acceleration phase.
    b) Displacement during acceleration.
    c) Displacement during coasting.
    d) Total displacement and average velocity over the entire 10.0 s.
  3. Error propagation concept (qualitative): In Problem 14, if the distance measurement has a ±1 cm uncertainty, explain (2–3 sentences) how that affects the time result (reference functional dependence $t = \sqrt{2\Delta x / a }$).
  4. You have four equations but five variables. Explain why this is sufficient to solve any uniformly accelerated motion problem with three knowns by citing degrees of freedom (2–4 sentences).
  5. A probe near Earth’s surface (vertical motion only) must limit impact speed to $< 12.0 \text{ m/s}$. It retro-thrusts upward with constant acceleration starting when its downward speed is $35.0 \text{ m/s}$ and altitude is $160 \text{ m}$.
    a) Minimum constant upward acceleration required (take up as positive; acceleration will be +).
    b) Time the burn lasts.
    c) Altitude lost during burn.
    d) Is altitude margin sufficient? Justify numerically.
  6. Design: You must time a fast process (~0.050 s) but only possess a meter stick and your reflexes. Propose a feasible measurement strategy using freefall (4–5 sentences) and identify two main error sources + mitigation.
  7. Model limits: List two real-world effects NOT included in our constant acceleration model for a falling object and describe the direction each would change predicted impact time or speed.

Reflection (Metacognitive)

Prompt: Which single algebraic manipulation or conceptual idea today most improved your confidence solving constant acceleration problems, and why?
 

Compact Formula Reference (For Derivation Cross‑Check Only)

  • $v_f = v_i + a t$
  • $\Delta x = v_i t + \tfrac{1}{2} a t^2$
  • $v_f^2 = v_i^2 + 2 a \Delta x$
  • $v_{avg} = \dfrac{v_i + v_f}{2}$ (constant $a$ only)
  • Freefall: $a = -9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$ (if up is +)
