Part 1: The Forecast The Mission Planner’s Challenge: You are a flight dynamics engineer preparing for a launch. Before the rocket leaves the pad, you must forecast its behavior based on its design. This allows you to set expectations and identify what “normal” flight looks like.

Your Task: In your notebook, answer the following questions to create a qualitative model of the rocket’s flight.

Sketch a qualitative velocity vs. time graph for the entire mission. The graph should have at least three distinct phases:

Phase 1: The engine is firing, providing a strong, constant upward acceleration.

Phase 2: The engine has cut off, and the rocket is coasting upwards against gravity.

Phase 3: The rocket has reached its peak altitude (apogee) and is falling back to Earth.

On your sketch, label the point where the rocket’s acceleration is greatest. Label the point where its velocity is momentarily zero. Label the region where its acceleration is approximately −9.80 m/s 2 .

What is the fundamental physics principle that allows you to analyze the rocket’s flight as a series of distinct stages, each with its own set of rules?

Part 2: The Blueprint Modeling the Trajectory: The launch has occurred, and the telemetry data is streaming in. Your team has generated a velocity-time graph of the initial phase of the flight. Your mission is to use this data to develop a quantitative model and predict the rocket’s maximum altitude.

Your Task: In your notebook, use the provided velocity-time graph (or one supplied by your instructor) to complete the following calculations. Show all steps, starting with the base concepts or formulae.

Part 3: The Reflection The Mission Debrief: The flight director needs a report that not only presents the results but also explains the physics behind them. They need to understand why this modeling approach is reliable.

Your Task: Write a formal “Mission Debrief” explaining the physics behind your calculations. Your report must:

Begin by clearly stating the calculated acceleration during burn, the altitude at engine cutoff, and the final predicted apogee.

Explain the powerful connection between the graphical model and the mathematical model. How does the slope of the graph physically represent the a in the kinematic equations? How does the area under the graph physically represent the displacement, Δx?

Analyze the flight using the thinking lens of Patterns. Describe the distinct patterns of motion in Phase 1 (engine burn) and Phase 2 (coasting). What is the underlying cause of each pattern?

Conclude by summarizing how these analytical models allow us to move from simply observing motion to precisely predicting it, which is the fundamental skill required for all aerospace exploration.
