Introduction: The Story of a Journey

Motion is the most fundamental story in the universe. It is the story of a planet orbiting a star, an apple falling from a tree, a car driving down the road. But how do we tell this story with precision? How do we capture the nuance of a journey—the speeding up, the slowing down, the moments of rest? The language of words is often not enough. To truly understand motion, we must learn to see it. We will discover that a simple drawing, a graph, can tell the complete story of an object’s journey through space and time. This is the story of how we learned to translate the dynamic, flowing reality of motion into a static, elegant picture that reveals all its secrets.

Phenomenon: The Ascent of a Rocket

Three… two… one… ignition. A suborbital sounding rocket tears away from the launchpad in a controlled explosion of fire and sound, a testament to human ingenuity. For a few brief, violent moments, its engines burn with furious power, pushing it ever faster against the relentless pull of gravity. Then, silence. The engines cut out, but the rocket’s journey is far from over. It coasts silently upward, trading its incredible speed for altitude, until it reaches its highest point—its apogee. For a fleeting instant, it hangs motionless at the edge of space before beginning its long fall back to Earth. This complex, multi-stage journey, full of dramatic changes in speed and direction, presents a puzzle: how can we describe this entire dynamic event in a single, coherent picture? How can we map out its velocity at every single moment in time?

Investigation: Finding the Patterns in the Data

A rocket’s ascent data can be presented as a table of numbers: at 1 second, the velocity is this; at 2 seconds, it’s that. But the human brain is a pattern-finding machine, and it’s much easier to see the story in a picture than in a list. What patterns emerge when we graph a rocket’s velocity against time?
