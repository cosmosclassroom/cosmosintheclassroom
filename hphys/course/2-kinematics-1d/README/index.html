Unit 2 - “The Art of Observation”

Central Question

How can we quantitatively describe and predict the motion of an object?

Anchoring Phenomenon

The multi-stage launch and trajectory of a suborbital sounding rocket.

Unit Overview

This unit transitions from the language of physics (Unit 1) to describing motion itself. Students will:

  • Analyze rocket ascent data to understand position, velocity, and acceleration relationships
  • Master graphical analysis of motion (slope = rate of change, area = accumulation)
  • Develop kinematic equations for constant acceleration
  • Investigate freefall as a special case of constant acceleration

Key Mathematical Concepts

  • Position, displacement ($\Delta \overrightarrow{r}$), and distance
  • Average and instantaneous velocity ($\overrightarrow{v} = \frac{d\overrightarrow{r}}{dt}$)
  • Acceleration ($\overrightarrow{a} = \frac{d\overrightarrow{v}}{dt}$)
  • Kinematic equations for constant acceleration
  • Graphical relationships (slope of $x$-$t$ graph = velocity, area under $v$-$t$ graph = displacement)

Unit 2 Sequence

  1. Hexagon Lab 2.1: Analyzing Rocket Ascent Data (velocity-time graphs)
  2. Lecture 2.1: The Language of Motion (position, velocity, acceleration)
  3. Problem Set 2.1: Graphical Analysis of Motion
  4. Hexagon Lab 2.2: Freefall Simulations (Feather & Bowling Ball)
  5. Lecture 2.2: The Rules of Falling (kinematic equations)
  6. Problem Set 2.2: Kinematics with Constant Acceleration
  7. Research Brief 2: Telling the complete story of rocket motion using graphs

Cross-Cutting Concept Focus

Patterns - Students will identify patterns in motion graphs and mathematical relationships

Assessment Preparation

The unit builds toward understanding that complex motion can be described by simple mathematical relationships, preparing students for Unit 3’s two-dimensional projectile motion.
