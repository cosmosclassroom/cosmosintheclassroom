SECTION 1: OVERVIEW & CONTEXT

——————————————————————————

abstract: | This investigation requires students to interpret a velocity-time graph of a suborbital sounding rocket launch, identifying three distinct phases of motion: powered ascent, coasting, and freefall. By calculating the slope of the graph during each phase, students will quantitatively determine the rocket’s acceleration and then use graphical integration (area under the curve) to estimate the maximum altitude (apogee).

introduction: | The multi-stage launch and trajectory of a suborbital sounding rocket is the phenomenon anchoring this unit. Rockets undergo rapid and dramatic changes in velocity as they burn fuel, coast upwards, and eventually fall back to Earth. Your job is to act as a mission analyst, extracting the complete narrative of the flight—from thrust to zero gravity—using only the data captured by its onboard telemetry system. The elegant patterns in the velocity data hold the key to understanding the forces at work.

claim_prompt: | Claim: Write a single, clear, and specific sentence that states the maximum acceleration and maximum altitude achieved by the rocket. evidence_prompt: | Evidence: Provide a bulleted list of specific, quantitative data points from your analysis that support your claim. - (From Investigation 1) The velocity changed from $0 \, \mathrm{m/s}$ to $100 \, \mathrm{m/s}$ over the first 5 seconds. - (From Investigation 1) The velocity reached $0 \, \mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=18$ seconds. - (From Investigation 2) The actual descent acceleration was $-7.50 \, \mathrm{m/s}^2$, which is less than the theoretical freefall acceleration of $|\vec{a}_g| = 9.80 \, \mathrm{m/s}^2$.

reasoning_prompt: | Reasoning: In a paragraph, explain how the principles of kinematics and the Cause and Effect of external forces (thrust, gravity, and air resistance) translate into the Patterns observed on the v-t graph. Why are the varying slopes of the graph (acceleration) sufficient to explain the entire journey? (Example): The steep positive slope during powered flight proves the presence of a strong net upward force (thrust > gravity + drag). The shallower negative slope during descent, compared to theoretical freefall, provides quantitative evidence of the cause—air resistance—which reduces the magnitude of the downward acceleration, affecting the total flight time and terminal velocity.
