Hexagon Lab: Analyzing Rocket Ascent Data

HPHYS | Hexagon lab | Unit 2
Section 1

Overview

Abstract

This investigation requires students to interpret a velocity-time graph of a suborbital sounding rocket launch, identifying three distinct phases of motion: powered ascent, coasting, and freefall. By calculating the slope of the graph during each phase, students will quantitatively determine the rocket’s acceleration and then use graphical integration (area under the curve) to estimate the maximum altitude (apogee).

Introduction

The multi-stage launch and trajectory of a suborbital sounding rocket is the phenomenon anchoring this unit. Rockets undergo rapid and dramatic changes in velocity as they burn fuel, coast upwards, and eventually fall back to Earth. Your job is to act as a mission analyst, extracting the complete narrative of the flight—from thrust to zero gravity—using only the data captured by its onboard telemetry system. The elegant patterns in the velocity data hold the key to understanding the forces at work.
