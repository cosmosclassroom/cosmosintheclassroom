# ————————- TIER 2: JOURNEYMAN —————————

  • problem_number: 2 tier: level: 2 title: “The Journeyman’s Task: Synthesis & Application” phenomenon_connection: “This problem models a more realistic scenario for the pyramid builders by including the opposing force of friction. The workers pull horizontally with a combined force of 9.00 x 10³ N. The opposing force of kinetic friction between the block and the sand is 3.50 x 10³ N. First, draw a complete free-body diagram for the block. Then, calculate the net force on the block and its resulting acceleration.” diagram_placeholder: “” solution: givens: - | $m = 2.50 \times 10^3 \, kg$ - | $\vec{F}{pull} = +9.00 \times 10^3 \, N$ - | $\vec{F}{f} = -3.50 \times 10^3 \, N$ unknowns: - | $\Sigma\vec{F}x = ?$ - | $\vec{a}_x = ?$ steps: - “1. Draw Free-Body Diagram: The diagram should show four forces: $\vec{F}{pull}$ to the right, $\vec{F}f$ to the left, the force of gravity ($\vec{F}_g$) downward, and the normal force ($\vec{F}_N$) upward.” - “2. Calculate Net Force: Since the block does not accelerate vertically, we only need to sum the horizontal forces. $\Sigma\vec{F}_x = \vec{F}{pull} + \vec{F}_f$.” - | $\Sigma\vec{F}_x = (+9.00 \times 10^3 \, N) + (-3.50 \times 10^3 \, N) = +5.50 \times 10^3 \, N$ - “3. Select Parent Equation: Use Newton’s Second Law for the horizontal dimension: $\Sigma\vec{F}_x = m\vec{a}_x$.” - “4. Algebraic Rearrangement: Solve for acceleration: $\vec{a}_x = \frac{\Sigma\vec{F}_x}{m}$.” - “5. Substitute Values and Track Units:” - | $\vec{a}_x = \frac{+5.50 \times 10^3 \, N}{2.50 \times 10^3 \, kg} = +2.20 \, m/s^2$ final_answer: | The net force on the block is +5.50 x 10³ N and its acceleration is +2.20 m/s².

# ————————- TIER 3: MASTER ——————————-

  • problem_number: 3 tier: level: 3 title: “The Master’s Insight: Design & Optimization” phenomenon_connection: “This question addresses a common and profound conceptual hurdle related to Newton’s Third Law. A true understanding of this law is required to correctly model the system of forces needed to construct the pyramids.” problem_statement: “An engineer overseeing the pyramid construction says: ‘I am confused. According to Newton’s Third Law, if the workers pull on the rope attached to the block, the rope and block pull back on the workers with an equal and opposite force. Since these forces are equal and opposite, they must cancel out. How can the block ever accelerate?’ Using the principles of Newton’s Laws, write a clear explanation that resolves the engineer’s confusion.” diagram_placeholder: “” solution: givens: [] unknowns: [] steps: - “1. Identify the Flaw: The engineer’s fundamental error is applying two forces from an action-reaction pair to the same object. Action-reaction pairs always act on different objects.” - “2. Analyze Forces on the Block: To determine the acceleration of the block, we only consider the forces acting on the block. In this case, that’s the pull from the rope and the friction from the ground. The block accelerates if the pull force is greater than the friction force.” - “3. Analyze Forces on the Workers: The ‘reaction’ force (the rope pulling back on the workers) acts on the workers, not the block. This force is what the workers feel in their hands. For the workers to not be pulled backward, they must push on the ground with their feet with a force greater than the pull from the rope.” - “4. Conclusion: The forces don’t cancel because they act on different objects within the system. The motion of the block is determined only by the net force on the block. The motion of the workers is determined only by the net force on the workers.” final_answer: | The engineer’s reasoning is flawed because action-reaction forces act on different objects and therefore cannot cancel each other out. The acceleration of the block depends only on the net force exerted on the block. The reaction force acts back on the workers, not the block.
