Quiz 3.1: Motion in Two Dimensions

Quiz 3.1: Motion in Two Dimensions

Instructions: Answer all questions. Show all work for calculations, including base formulas, units, and significant figures. You may use a calculator and reference sheet.

Part 1: Conceptual Understanding (Multiple Choice)

  1. Which statement best describes the independence of horizontal and vertical motion for a projectile? a) Both motions depend on each other. b) The horizontal motion is independent of the vertical motion. c) The vertical motion is independent of the horizontal motion. d) Both motions are independent of each other.

  2. A ball is launched horizontally from a cliff. What is its initial vertical velocity? a) Zero b) Equal to its horizontal velocity c) Greater than its horizontal velocity d) Cannot be determined

  3. Which vector operation is used to break a projectile’s initial velocity into components? a) Addition b) Subtraction c) Multiplication d) Trigonometric functions

Part 2: Short Answer

  1. Briefly explain why the time of flight for a projectile depends only on its initial vertical velocity and height, not its horizontal velocity.

  2. Draw a labeled diagram showing the velocity components of a projectile launched at an angle. Indicate the direction and label $v_{0x}$ and $v_{0y}$.

Part 3: Problem Solving

  1. A soccer ball is kicked with an initial velocity of 18.0 m/s at an angle of 35.0° above the horizontal. a) Calculate the initial horizontal and vertical velocity components. b) How long is the ball in the air? c) What is the maximum height reached? d) What is the horizontal range?

  2. A student throws a rock horizontally from a 12.0 m high balcony with a speed of 6.0 m/s. a) How long does it take to hit the ground? b) How far from the base of the balcony does the rock land?

Part 4: Synthesis

  1. Describe a real-world scenario (other than sports) where analyzing motion in two dimensions is essential. Explain how you would break the motion into components to solve a practical problem.
