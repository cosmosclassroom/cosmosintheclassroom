HPHYS Unit 3

Estimated Time: 35 minutes

Tags: projectile motion, vectors, 2D kinematics, Constantinople, physics

The World in Two Dimensions: The Siege at Constantinople

Phenomenon: The first effective use of large-scale artillery in the 1453 Siege of Constantinople.

Forecast: The Artillery Commander’s Dilemma

Prompt: You are an artillery commander at the Siege of Constantinople. How do you determine the correct angle to launch your cannonball to hit the city walls?

Blueprint: Modeling Projectile Trajectories

Prompt: Develop a mathematical model that predicts the range of a projectile based on its initial velocity and launch angle. How can you test the accuracy and limitations of your model against a simulation?

  • Break the motion into horizontal and vertical components using vector analysis.
  • Use the kinematic equations for projectile motion:
    • $x = v_{0x} t$
    • $y = v_{0y} t - \frac{1}{2} g t^2$
  • Calculate the time of flight and range for a given launch angle and initial velocity.
  • Test your model using a simulation or provided data. What launch angle yields the maximum range?

Reflection: Systems and the Independence of Motion

Prompt: Answer your initial question: what is the correct angle to hit the walls? Reflect on how analyzing a complex, two-dimensional problem as two independent, simpler one-dimensional problems made a solution possible.

  • Discuss the independence of horizontal and vertical motion.
  • Explain why a $45^\circ$ launch angle yields the maximum range (in the absence of air resistance).
  • Reflect on the value of breaking complex systems into simpler parts for analysis in physics.
