Overview

Abstract

In this investigation, you will use an interactive simulation to explore the fundamental principles of projectile motion. By systematically changing variables like launch angle and initial speed, you will discover the optimal conditions for maximizing a projectile’s range and develop a deeper understanding of the physics that governs its trajectory.

Introduction

During the 1453 Siege of Constantinople, artillery commanders like Orban faced a critical challenge: how to aim their massive cannons to be effective. Should they aim high to clear the city’s formidable walls, or aim lower for maximum distance? Every launch was a high-stakes physics experiment. The difference between a successful hit and a wasted shot depended entirely on understanding the relationship between launch angle and range. Your task is to discover these rules for yourself, not with a 1,200-pound stone ball, but with a powerful digital simulation.

Connection to Unit Phenomenon

The patterns you discovered are the exact principles that revolutionized warfare and engineering. An artillery commander at the Siege of Constantinople would need to know that a 45° angle provides the maximum range for bombarding a distant target. However, to clear a tall defensive wall, they might choose a higher angle like 60°. Though the range would be shorter (the same as a 30° launch), the higher trajectory (apex) would be necessary. This lab demonstrates that understanding the physics of projectile motion is not just an academic exercise—it’s about controlling outcomes and making precise, effective decisions.
