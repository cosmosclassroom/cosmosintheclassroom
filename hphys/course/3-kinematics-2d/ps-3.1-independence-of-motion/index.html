Problem Set 3.1: Independence of Motion

HPHYS | Problem set | Unit 3
▶ Instructions

In this problem set, we begin to truly explore the complexity of two-dimensional motion. The core insight—the Independence of Motion—allows us to break one difficult problem into two simpler, one-dimensional problems. Just as the artillery commanders at Constantinople had to consider how far and how long their cannonballs would fly, you will use kinematic equations to predict the trajectory of horizontally launched projectiles.

▶ Key Formulae
  • Horizontal motion (constant velocity): $\Delta x = v_x t$
  • Vertical motion (constant acceleration): $\Delta y = v_{y,i} t + \frac{1}{2} a_g t^2$
  • Vertical motion (constant acceleration): $v_{y,f}^2 = v_{y,i}^2 + 2 a_g \Delta y$
  • Vertical motion (constant acceleration): $v_{y,f} = v_{y,i} + a_g t$
▶ Standards for All Problems
  • For all problems, assume the acceleration due to gravity is $\vec{a}_g = -9.80 \, m/s^2$.
  • Use downward as the negative direction.
  • Follow the systematic problem-solving process: identify knowns, choose equation, rearrange algebraically, then substitute values.
  • Present all final answers with the correct units and number of significant figures.

Part A: Foundational Skills

Problem 1 Apprentice

A marble rolls off a horizontal table with a speed of $1.5\,\text{m/s}$. The table is $0.80\,\text{m}$ high.

a) How long does it take the marble to hit the floor? b) How far from the base of the table does the marble land?

Solution:
(a)

$0.40\,\text{s}$

(b)

$0.60\,\text{m}$

Problem 2 Apprentice

A spy at Constantinople drops a message from a wall that is $35.0\,\text{m}$ high. At the exact same moment, a cannonball is fired horizontally from the top of the wall with an initial speed of $120.\,\text{m/s}$.

a) How long is each object in the air? b) How far horizontally does the cannonball travel before hitting the ground?

Solution:
(a)

$2.67\,\text{s}$ (both objects - same time)

(b)

$320\,\text{m}$

Problem 3 Apprentice

A ball is thrown horizontally from a cliff $45.0\,\text{m}$ high with an initial speed of $20.0\,\text{m/s}$.

a) How long is the ball in the air? b) What is the horizontal distance traveled? c) What is the ball’s vertical velocity when it hits the ground?

Solution:
(a)

$3.03\,\text{s}$

(b)

$60.6\,\text{m}$

(c)

$-29.7\,\text{m/s}$ (downward)

Problem 4 Apprentice

A package is dropped from a helicopter flying horizontally at $85.0\,\text{m/s}$ at an altitude of $120.0\,\text{m}$.

a) How long does it take the package to reach the ground? b) How far horizontally does the package travel?

$4.95\,\text{s}$

$421\,\text{m}$

Part B: Synthesis & Application

Problem 5 Journeyman

Two stones are thrown horizontally from the same cliff. Stone A is thrown at $10.0\,\text{m/s}$, and Stone B is thrown at $20.0\,\text{m/s}$.

a) Which stone hits the ground first? b) Explain your reasoning using the principle of independence. c) Stone B lands twice as far from the cliff as Stone A. Explain why this makes sense mathematically.

Solution:
(a)

They hit at the same time

(b)

Vertical motion is independent of horizontal velocity; both experience same downward acceleration and same initial vertical velocity (0 m/s)

(c)

Since time is the same and distance = velocity × time, doubling the horizontal velocity doubles the horizontal distance

Problem 6 Journeyman

A physics student drops one marble straight down from a height of $1.20\,\text{m}$. At the exact same instant, another student throws a second marble horizontally from the same height with a speed of $3.0\,\text{m/s}$.

a) Which marble hits the ground first? b) Calculate the time it takes for each marble to hit the ground. c) How far horizontally does the thrown marble travel?

Solution:
(a)

They hit at the same time

(b)

$0.49\,\text{s}$

(c)

$1.5\,\text{m}$

Problem 7 Journeyman

A plane flying at $250.0\,\text{m/s}$ at an altitude of $2000.0\,\text{m}$ needs to drop a supply crate to land in a designated zone.

a) How long does it take for the crate to fall? b) How far before the target zone should the pilot release the crate? c) What assumptions did you make in your calculation?

Solution:
(a)

$20.2\,\text{s}$

(b)

$5050\,\text{m}$ (5.05 km before target)

(c)

No air resistance, flat ground, no wind, Earth’s rotation negligible, constant gravity

$4.0\,\text{s}$

$78\,\text{m}$

Problem 9 Master

A stunt driver drives a car horizontally off a cliff at $25.0\,\text{m/s}$. The car lands in a lake $50.0\,\text{m}$ from the base of the cliff.

a) How high is the cliff? b) What is the car’s impact velocity (magnitude and direction)? c) If the driver wants to land $75.0\,\text{m}$ from the cliff instead, what should the new launch speed be (assuming the same cliff height)?

$19.6\,\text{m}$

(c)

$37.5\,\text{m/s}$
