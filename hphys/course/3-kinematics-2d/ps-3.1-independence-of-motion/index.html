problems: # ==== TIER 1: APPRENTICE —————————

  • id: p1 difficulty: apprentice tier_title: | The Apprentice’s Practice: Conceptual Understanding

    phenomenon_connection: | This problem explores the fundamental principle that horizontal and vertical motions are independent during projectile motion.

    prompt: | An attacker fires a small cannon (Cannonball A) horizontally from a siege tower. The cannonball leaves the barrel at the exact same height as a defender on the city wall, who drops a heavy stone (Stone B) at the same instant.

    (a) Which projectile (the cannonball or the stone) strikes the ground first?

    (b) Briefly (1-2 sentences) explain your reasoning using the principle of the independence of motion.

    solution: | (a) Answer: They strike at the same time.

    (b) Explanation: The horizontal motion ($\vec{v}x$) of Cannonball A does not affect its vertical motion. Both projectiles start with an initial vertical velocity ($\vec{v}{0y}$) of $0 \text{ m/s}$ and accelerate downwards at $\vec{a}_g = 9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$ from the same height. Because their vertical motions are identical, they must land at the same time. This is the principle of the independence of motion.

# ==== TIER 2: JOURNEYMAN —————————

  • id: p2 difficulty: journeyman tier_title: | The Journeyman’s Task: Horizontal Projectile

    phenomenon_connection: | This problem models the trajectory of a cannonball fired horizontally from a siege tower, demonstrating how vertical and horizontal motions combine.

    prompt: | A small cannon is fired horizontally from the top of a $78.4 \text{ m}$ tall siege tower. The cannonball’s initial horizontal velocity is $90.0 \text{ m/s}$.

    (a) How long does it take for the cannonball to hit the ground?

    (b) What is the horizontal distance (the range) the cannonball travels before it lands?

    solution: | Given:

    • $\Delta y = -78.4 \text{ m}$ (negative because downward)
    • $\vec{v}_{0x} = 90.0 \text{ m/s}$
    • $\vec{v}_{0y} = 0 \text{ m/s}$ (fired horizontally)
    • $\vec{a}_y = -9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$

    (a) Time to hit ground:

    Step 1: Select equation for vertical motion \(\Delta y = \vec{v}_{0y}t + \frac{1}{2}\vec{a}_y t^2\)

    Step 2: Simplify (since $\vec{v}_{0y} = 0$) \(\Delta y = \frac{1}{2}\vec{a}_y t^2\)

    Step 3: Rearrange for time \(t = \sqrt{\frac{2\Delta y}{\vec{a}_y}}\)

    Step 4: Substitute values \(t = \sqrt{\frac{2(-78.4 \text{ m})}{-9.80 \text{ m/s}^2}} = \sqrt{16.0 \text{ s}^2} = 4.00 \text{ s}\)

    (b) Horizontal range:

    Step 1: Use horizontal motion equation \(\Delta x = \vec{v}_{0x} t\)

    Step 2: Substitute values \(\Delta x = (90.0 \text{ m/s})(4.00 \text{ s}) = 360. \text{ m}\)

    Final Answers:

    • (a) Time = $4.00 \text{ s}$
    • (b) Range = $360. \text{ m}$

# ==== TIER 3: MASTER ——————————-

  • id: p3 difficulty: master tier_title: | The Master’s Insight: Projectile at an Angle

    phenomenon_connection: | This problem challenges you to analyze the complete trajectory of Orban’s great bombard, requiring component analysis and understanding of symmetric projectile motion.

    prompt: | One of Orban’s “great bombards” fires a massive stone cannonball from ground level with an initial velocity of $160. \text{ m/s}$ at an angle of $30.0°$ above the horizontal, aiming for the Theodosian Walls.

    (a) Calculate the initial horizontal ($\vec{v}{0x}$) and vertical ($\vec{v}{0y}$) components of the velocity.

    (b) What is the maximum height the cannonball reaches?

    (c) What is the total time the cannonball is in the air (its “hang time”)?

    solution: | Given:

    • $\vec{v}_0 = 160. \text{ m/s}$
    • $\theta = 30.0°$
    • $\vec{a}_y = -9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$

    (a) Velocity components:

    \[\vec{v}_{0x} = \vec{v}_0 \cos\theta = (160. \text{ m/s})(\cos 30.0°) = (160. \text{ m/s})(0.866) = 139 \text{ m/s}\] \[\vec{v}_{0y} = \vec{v}_0 \sin\theta = (160. \text{ m/s})(\sin 30.0°) = (160. \text{ m/s})(0.500) = 80.0 \text{ m/s}\]

    (b) Maximum height:

    At maximum height, $\vec{v}_y = 0$

    Step 1: Use kinematic equation \(\vec{v}_y^2 = \vec{v}_{0y}^2 + 2\vec{a}_y\Delta y\)

    Step 2: Rearrange (with $\vec{v}_y = 0$) \(\Delta y = \frac{-\vec{v}_{0y}^2}{2\vec{a}_y}\)

    Step 3: Substitute values \(\Delta y = \frac{-(80.0 \text{ m/s})^2}{2(-9.80 \text{ m/s}^2)} = \frac{6400 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}^2}{19.6 \text{ m/s}^2} = 327 \text{ m}\)

    (c) Total hang time:

    Step 1: Find time to peak \(\vec{v}_y = \vec{v}_{0y} + \vec{a}_y t_{peak}\)

    Step 2: Rearrange (with $\vec{v}_y = 0$) \(t_{peak} = \frac{-\vec{v}_{0y}}{\vec{a}_y} = \frac{-(80.0 \text{ m/s})}{-9.80 \text{ m/s}^2} = 8.16 \text{ s}\)

    Step 3: Total time (symmetry of projectile motion) \(t_{total} = 2 \times t_{peak} = 2 \times (8.16 \text{ s}) = 16.3 \text{ s}\)

    Final Answers:

    • (a) $\vec{v}{0x} = 139 \text{ m/s}$, $\vec{v}{0y} = 80.0 \text{ m/s}$
    • (b) Maximum height = $327 \text{ m}$
    • (c) Hang time = $16.3 \text{ s}$
