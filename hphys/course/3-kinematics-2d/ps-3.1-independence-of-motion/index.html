Problem Set 3.1: Independence of Motion

HPHYS | Problem set | Unit 3
▶ Instructions

In this problem set, we begin to truly explore the complexity of two-dimensional motion. The core insight—the Independence of Motion—allows us to break one difficult problem into two simpler, one-dimensional problems. Just as the artillery commanders at Constantinople had to consider how far and how long their cannonballs would fly, you will use kinematic equations to predict the trajectory of horizontally launched projectiles.

▶ Key Formulae
  • Horizontal motion (constant velocity): $\Delta x = v_x t$
  • Vertical motion (constant acceleration): $\Delta y = v_{y,i} t + \frac{1}{2} a_g t^2$
  • Vertical motion (constant acceleration): $v_{y,f}^2 = v_{y,i}^2 + 2 a_g \Delta y$
  • Vertical motion (constant acceleration): $v_{y,f} = v_{y,i} + a_g t$
▶ Standards for All Problems
  • For all problems, assume the acceleration due to gravity is $\vec{a}_g = -9.80 \, m/s^2$.
  • Use downward as the negative direction.
  • Follow the systematic problem-solving process: identify knowns, choose equation, rearrange algebraically, then substitute values.
  • Present all final answers with the correct units and number of significant figures.
