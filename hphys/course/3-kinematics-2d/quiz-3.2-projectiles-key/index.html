Quiz 3.2: Relative Motion & Real-World Applications — Answer Key

Part 1: Conceptual Understanding

  1. d) $\sqrt{20}$ m/s = 4.47 m/s [ v_{rel} = \sqrt{(4.0~\text{m/s})^2 + (2.0~\text{m/s})^2} = \sqrt{16 + 4} = \sqrt{20} = 4.47~\text{m/s} ]

  2. c) 8.0 m/s [ v_{rel} = 5.0~\text{m/s} + 3.0~\text{m/s} = 8.0~\text{m/s} ]

  3. c) Vector addition

Part 2: Short Answer

  1. Reference frames determine the observed velocity. For example, a person walking inside a train moving at 20 m/s: to an observer on the train, the person’s speed is their walking speed; to an observer on the ground, it’s the sum of the train’s and the person’s speed (if in the same direction).

  2. Relative velocity is crucial when crossing a street with moving traffic: you must consider both your speed and the speed of the cars to avoid a collision.

Part 3: Problem Solving

  1. Swimmer: $v_{swim} = 1.5~\text{m/s}$, $v_{river} = 1.0~\text{m/s}$, $d = 60.0~\text{m}$ [ t = \frac{d}{v_{swim}} = \frac{60.0~\text{m}}{1.5~\text{m/s}} = 40.0~\text{s} ] [ x = v_{river} \cdot t = 1.0~\text{m/s} \times 40.0~\text{s} = 40.0~\text{m} ]
  1. Car: $v_{car} = 20.0~\text{m/s}$ north, Motorcycle: $v_{moto} = 15.0~\text{m/s}$ east [ v_{rel} = \sqrt{(20.0~\text{m/s})^2 + (15.0~\text{m/s})^2} = \sqrt{400 + 225} = \sqrt{625} = 25.0~\text{m/s} ] Direction: [ \theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{15.0}{20.0}\right) = \tan^{-1}(0.75) = 36.9^\circ \text{ east of north} ]
  1. Drone: $v_{ship} = 8.0~\text{m/s}$ east, $v_{drone} = 12.0~\text{m/s}$ [ v_{drone, east} = v_{ship} = 8.0~\text{m/s} ] [ v_{drone, north} = \sqrt{(12.0~\text{m/s})^2 - (8.0~\text{m/s})^2} = \sqrt{144 - 64} = \sqrt{80} = 8.94~\text{m/s} ] Heading: [ \theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{8.94}{8.0}\right) = 48.4^\circ \text{ north of east} ] Velocity relative to ground: [ v_{rel} = \sqrt{(8.0~\text{m/s})^2 + (8.94~\text{m/s})^2} = 12.0~\text{m/s} ]

Part 4: Synthesis

  1. Example: In air traffic control, analyzing the relative motion of airplanes is essential to prevent collisions. Controllers use vector addition to determine the velocity of one plane relative to another and ensure safe separation.
