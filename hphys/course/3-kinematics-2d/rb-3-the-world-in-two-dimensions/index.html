SECTION 1: RESEARCH BRIEF OVERVIEW

——————————————————————————

overview: phenomenon: “The first effective use of large-scale artillery in the 1453 Siege of Constantinople.”

historical_context: | The Siege of Constantinople in 1453 marked a turning point in military history. The Ottoman forces, under Sultan Mehmed II, deployed massive bronze cannons designed by the Hungarian engineer Orban. These cannons could hurl stone projectiles weighing up to 600 kg over distances exceeding 1.5 km. The success of this artillery bombardment depended critically on understanding the trajectory of projectiles—a problem that requires analyzing motion in two dimensions simultaneously.

central_question: “How do you determine the correct angle to launch a projectile to hit a target at a specific distance?”

key_concepts: - “Independence of horizontal and vertical motion” - “Vector decomposition of velocity” - “Projectile motion equations” - “Range optimization”

SECTION 2: THREE-PHASE STRUCTURE

——————————————————————————

========================= FORECAST PHASE ===================================

forecast: phase_title: “Forecast: The Artillery Commander’s Dilemma” timing: “Beginning of Unit”

prompt: | You are an artillery commander at the Siege of Constantinople in 1453. Your Sultan has commissioned the construction of massive cannons to breach the legendary walls of the city. However, the cannons are heavy, difficult to aim, and expensive to fire (each shot requires significant time and gunpowder to reload).

**Your Challenge:** Before wasting precious ammunition, you must predict where a cannonball will land based on the angle at which you aim the cannon and the velocity at which the ball leaves the barrel.

guiding_questions: - “What variables do you think affect where a projectile lands?” - “Draw a sketch showing the path you expect a cannonball to follow. What shape is it?” - “If you could only choose one launch angle to test, which would you choose and why?” - “What makes this problem more complex than the one-dimensional motion problems you’ve solved before?”

deliverables: - “Sketch of expected projectile path” - “List of variables that affect range” - “Initial prediction for optimal launch angle” - “Written explanation of reasoning (3-5 sentences)”

========================= BLUEPRINT PHASE ==================================

blueprint: phase_title: “Blueprint: Modeling Projectile Trajectories” timing: “Mid-Unit”

prompt: | Now that you’ve learned about vector decomposition and the independence of motion in perpendicular directions, it’s time to build a mathematical model that can predict projectile motion.

**Your Task:** Develop a complete mathematical framework for calculating the range of a projectile launched at angle θ with initial velocity $v_0$.

mathematical_framework: - step: 1 description: “Decompose the initial velocity into horizontal and vertical components” equations: - “$v_{0x} = v_0 \cos(\theta)$” - “$v_{0y} = v_0 \sin(\theta)$”

- step: 2
  description: "Apply kinematic equations separately to each dimension"
  horizontal:
    - "$x = v_{0x} t$ (constant velocity)"
  vertical:
    - "$y = v_{0y} t - \\frac{1}{2} g t^2$ (constant acceleration)"
    - "$v_y = v_{0y} - gt$"

- step: 3
  description: "Find time of flight by setting y = 0 at landing"
  equation: "$t_{flight} = \\frac{2v_0 \\sin(\\theta)}{g}$"

- step: 4
  description: "Calculate range using horizontal motion equation"
  equation: "$R = v_{0x} \\cdot t_{flight} = \\frac{v_0^2 \\sin(2\\theta)}{g}$"

investigation_prompts: - “Using your model, calculate the range for launch angles of 15°, 30°, 45°, 60°, and 75° (assume $v_0 = 100$ m/s).” - “Create a graph showing Range vs. Launch Angle.” - “At what angle is the range maximized? Does this match your initial prediction?” - “Test your model using a projectile motion simulation. How well does it predict actual trajectories?” - “What assumptions does your model make? When might it fail in the real world?”

deliverables: - “Complete derivation of range equation” - “Data table with calculated ranges for different angles” - “Graph of Range vs. Launch Angle” - “Comparison between model predictions and simulation results” - “Discussion of model limitations (air resistance, rotation, etc.)”

========================= REFLECTION PHASE =================================

reflection: phase_title: “Reflection: Systems and the Independence of Motion” timing: “End of Unit”

prompt: | Return to your initial question: What is the correct angle to launch a projectile to hit a target at a specific distance?

You now have a complete mathematical model and have tested it against simulations. Synthesize your learning by answering this question with full justification.

synthesis_questions: - question: “What is the optimal launch angle for maximum range, and why?” guidance: “Explain both mathematically (referencing the $\sin(2\theta)$ term) and conceptually (balancing time aloft with horizontal speed).”

- question: "How does the principle of independence of motion make this problem solvable?"
  guidance: "Reflect on how treating a complex 2D problem as two simpler 1D problems is a powerful problem-solving strategy. Where else in physics might this approach be useful?"

- question: "Why is 45° the optimal angle only in idealized conditions?"
  guidance: "Discuss how air resistance, launch height differences, and other real-world factors change the optimal angle."

- question: "How did this investigation demonstrate the CCC 'Systems and System Models'?"
  guidance: "Consider how breaking the projectile motion system into horizontal and vertical subsystems allowed for analysis that would be impossible if treated as a single system."

real_world_connections: - “Sports: Why do athletes release projectiles (shot put, javelin) at angles less than 45°?” - “Engineering: How do engineers account for air resistance when designing projectiles?” - “Space Exploration: How does the absence of air resistance on the Moon change projectile motion?” - “Military History: How accurate was Ottoman artillery, and what factors limited its effectiveness?”

deliverables: - “Final answer to the central question with complete justification” - “Reflection essay (2-3 paragraphs) on the power of the independence of motion principle” - “Diagram showing how your understanding evolved from Forecast to Blueprint to Reflection” - “Application example: Choose one real-world scenario and explain how projectile motion analysis applies”
