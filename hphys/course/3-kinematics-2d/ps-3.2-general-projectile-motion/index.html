————————- TIER 1: APPRENTICE —————————

  • problem_number: 1 tier: level: 1 title: “The Apprentice’s Practice: Foundational Skills” phenomenon_connection: “This problem models the initial ascent of a projectile—the first phase of the cannonball’s journey—by finding the time to reach its highest point.” problem_statement: “A stone is launched from the ground with an initial velocity of $28.0 \, m/s$ at an angle of $40.0^{\circ}$ above the horizontal. Assuming it lands at the same height from which it was launched, what is the total time of flight?” diagram_placeholder: “” solution: givens: - | $v_0 = 28.0 \, m/s$ - | $\theta = 40.0^{\circ}$ - | $v_y(\text{peak}) = 0 \, m/s$ - | $a_y = -9.80 \, m/s^2$ unknowns: - | $t_{\text{total}} = ?$ steps: - “1. Find Initial Vertical Velocity ($v_{0y}$): $v_{0y} = v_0 \sin(\theta)$.” - | $v_{0y} = (28.0 \, m/s) \sin(40.0^{\circ}) = 17.998 \, m/s$ - “2. Find Time to Peak ($t_{\text{peak}}$): Use $v_{y,f} = v_{0y} + a_y t_{\text{peak}}$. Rearrange for $t_{\text{peak}}$ (where $v_{y,f} = 0$). $\implies t_{\text{peak}} = -v_{0y} / a_y$.” - “3. Substitute Values and Track Units ($t_{\text{peak}}$):” - | $t_{\text{peak}} = \frac{-17.998 \, m/s}{-9.80 \, m/s^2} = 1.8365 \, s$ - “4. Find Total Time of Flight ($t_{\text{total}}$): Due to symmetry, $t_{\text{total}} = 2 \cdot t_{\text{peak}}$.” - | $t_{\text{total}} = 2 (1.8365 \, s) = 3.673 \, s$ final_answer: | The total time of flight is $3.67 \, s$.

————————- TIER 3: MASTER ——————————-

  • problem_number: 3 tier: level: 3 title: “The Master’s Insight: Design & Optimization” phenomenon_connection: “This problem directly relates to the concept of Optimizing Launch Angle for Range, a critical discovery for the artillery commander.” problem_statement: “You conducted an experiment (Hexagon Lab: Optimizing Launch Angle for Range) and confirmed that for maximum range, a projectile should be launched at $45^{\circ}$ (assuming no air resistance). Use the kinematic equations and principles of vector components to explain why $45^{\circ}$ yields the maximum range.” prompt: “Your explanation must be based on the trade-off between the horizontal velocity component ($v_{0x}$) and the time of flight ($t$), both of which are required to determine the range ($d_x = v_{0x} t$). Discuss how the $45^{\circ}$ angle provides the optimal balance.” diagram_placeholder: “[Image showing three projectile trajectories launched at the same speed but different angles: $20^{\circ}$, $45^{\circ}$, and $70^{\circ}$]” solution: reflection: >- The range ($d_x$) is determined by $d_x = v_{0x} t$. The choice of launch angle ($\theta$) is a trade-off because it determines both $v_{0x}$ and $t$:

      1.  **Horizontal Component ($v_{0x}$):** $v_{0x}$ is proportional to $\\cos(\\theta)$. As $\\theta$ increases toward $90^{\\circ}$ (a straight up shot), $v_{0x}$ decreases. An angle of $0^{\\circ}$ maximizes $v_{0x}$, but results in zero time of flight.
  2.  **Time of Flight ($t$):** The total flight time is determined by $t_{\\text{total}} \\propto v_{0y}$, and $v_{0y}$ is proportional to $\\sin(\\theta)$. As $\\theta$ increases, $v_{0y}$ increases, leading to more time in the air. An angle of $90^{\\circ}$ maximizes $t$, but results in zero horizontal distance.

  The total range $d_x$ is proportional to $(\\cos\\theta)(\\sin\\theta)$. The mathematical **Pattern** is that the product of $\\sin(\\theta)$ and $\\cos(\\theta)$ is maximized when $\\theta = 45^{\\circ}$. At $45^{\\circ}$, the initial velocity is perfectly split, providing the optimal **balance** between enough forward speed ($v_{0x}$) and enough hang time ($t$) to achieve the greatest horizontal displacement.
