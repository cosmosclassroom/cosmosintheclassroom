Hexagon Lab 3.1: Analyzing Projectile Motion Data

Background

The first effective use of large-scale artillery in the 1453 Siege of Constantinople required an unprecedented understanding of projectile motion. Before this, aiming was an art based on experience and guesswork. With massive cannons, the cost of a missed shot became enormous, making the science of aiming essential. This lab explores the fundamental rules connecting a projectile’s initial velocity and launch angle to its trajectory, maximum height, and range, using idealized conditions (no air resistance, ground-level launches).

Analysis

  1. What is the qualitative relationship between initial speed and the projectile’s range and height? Does doubling the speed double the range?
  2. At what launch angle is the maximum possible range achieved? What is the mathematical relationship between paired angles that yield the same range?
  3. How could the concept of paired angles be tactically useful for a cannoneer?
