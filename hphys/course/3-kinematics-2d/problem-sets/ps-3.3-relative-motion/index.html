title: ‘Problem Set 3.3: A Matter of Perspective’ layout: problem_set date: ‘2025-10-19’ course: hphys content_type: problem_set unit: 3 Introduction In this final problem set for Unit 3, we will put all the pieces together. The world is rarely stationary. To accurately predict the motion of an object, we must often account for the motion of the medium it travels through—the current of a river or the wind in the sky. These problems will challenge you to shift your frame of reference and combine vector analysis with the rules of projectile motion.

Tier 1: The Apprentice’s Practice These problems focus on the foundational skill of adding and subtracting velocity vectors in one and two dimensions.

Problem 1: The Cannon on the Deck A warship is sailing due east at a speed of 8.50 m/s. A sailor walks across the deck from the starboard (right) side to the port (left) side at a speed of 1.20 m/s. The ship is 20.0 m wide. a) From the perspective of a stationary observer on a nearby island, what is the sailor’s velocity (magnitude and direction)? b) How long does it take the sailor to cross the deck?

Problem 2: Upstream, Downstream A motorboat has a top speed of 6.00 m/s in still water. The boat is on a river that flows downstream at a constant rate of 2.50 m/s. a) What is the boat’s maximum possible velocity relative to the riverbank? b) What is the boat’s velocity relative to the riverbank if it travels directly upstream?

Tier 2: The Journeyman’s Task This problem requires you to apply 2D vector addition to solve the classic “river crossing” scenario.

Problem 3: Crossing the River

You want to paddle your canoe straight across a river that is 150.0 m wide. You can paddle at a speed of 3.00 m/s relative to the water. The river, however, is flowing downstream at 1.80 m/s. a) If you point your canoe straight across the river (due north), what will be your actual velocity (magnitude and direction) relative to the ground? b) How long will it take you to cross the river? c) When you reach the other side, how far downstream from your starting point will you be? d) Challenge: If you want to land at a point directly across the river from where you started, at what angle upstream must you point your canoe?

Tier 3: The Master’s Insight This problem requires a full synthesis of relative velocity and projectile motion. It is a multi-step analysis that reflects a real-world engineering challenge.

Problem 4: The Supply Drop An airplane is flying on a humanitarian mission to drop a package of medical supplies to a remote village. The plane’s airspeed (its speed relative to the air) is a constant 115 m/s, and it is flying at a constant altitude of 1250 m above the ground. However, there is a steady crosswind blowing at 22.0 m/s in a direction perpendicular to the plane’s intended flight path. The pilot must account for both the wind’s effect on the plane’s path and the projectile motion of the package once it is released.

a) Calculate the plane’s actual velocity (magnitude and direction) relative to the ground. b) The package is released from the plane. Calculate how long it will take for the package to hit the ground. (Remember, once released, its horizontal motion is independent of its vertical motion). c) How far horizontally will the package travel during its fall? (This distance is known as the “range” in this context). d) To ensure the package lands on the target, the pilot must release it before the plane is directly overhead. How far, horizontally, from the target must the plane be when the package is released?
