\ Rules of the Game: Work and Energy"\n content: |\n Your investigation
\ revealed the rule for "doing work": $W = Fd\cos(\theta)$. Work is the
\ product of the force and the distance over which it’s applied, but only the
\ component of the force in the direction of motion counts (that’s what the $\
cos\theta$ is for). If you push a box horizontally, you do work. If you just
\ hold it still, no matter how tired you get, you are doing zero work because
\ the distance is zero.\n\n So where does that transferred energy go? It
\ often becomes Kinetic Energy ($KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$), the energy an object
\ has because it’s moving. The link between these is the Work-Energy Theorem:
\ the net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy ($W_{net}
\ = \Delta KE$). This is a powerful idea. It tells us that the total of all pushes
\ and pulls on an object, accounted for over a distance, precisely determines
\ its change in speed.\n\n But energy can also be stored. When you lift an
\ object, you do work against gravity. That energy isn’t lost; it’s stored as
\ Gravitational Potential Energy ($PE_g = mgh$). It’s the energy of position—the
\ higher an object is, the more potential it has to be converted back into motion.
\ This is the secret of the Hoover Dam: storing energy by putting water in a high
\ place.\n\n - type: image\n # A simple diagram showing a roller coaster car
