Prologue: The Most Complex Machine

An Introduction to Physics

Today’s Core Idea

The same fundamental rules that govern the cosmos also govern the most complex and familiar object you will ever encounter: your own body.

Our goal this year is to learn the “user’s guide” to this machine.

The Ingredients of You

Every physical experience you have is a story written with three ingredients:

  • Matter: The “stuff” you’re made of (mass, inertia)
  • Force: The pushes and pulls you interact with (gravity, muscles)
  • Energy: The “currency” of change that powers you (chemical, kinetic)

Check for Understanding

Consider the simple act of catching a basketball.

With your table, identify:

  • The Matter involved
  • The Forces at play
  • The Energy transformations that occur

Be ready to share.

The User’s Guide to Your Brain

To make sense of the world, your brain uses a powerful “Scientist’s Toolkit.” Our goal is to make your use of these tools deliberate and precise.

The toolkit has two main parts:

  • The 8 Practices: The things scientists DO
  • The 7 Concepts: The “lenses” scientists USE TO SEE

The 8 Practices: The “Do” of Science

These are the actions scientists take to build knowledge:

  • Asking Questions & Defining Problems
  • Developing & Using Models
  • Planning & Carrying Out Investigations
  • Analyzing & Interpreting Data
  • Using Mathematics & Computational Thinking
  • Constructing Explanations & Designing Solutions
  • Engaging in Argument from Evidence
  • Obtaining, Evaluating, & Communicating Information

The 7 Concepts: The “Lenses” of Science

These are big ideas that help us find patterns in a complex world. They are the lenses we use to see more clearly:

  • Patterns
  • Cause and Effect
  • Scale, Proportion, & Quantity
  • Systems & System Models
  • Energy & Matter
  • Structure & Function
  • Stability & Change

Adolescence: Stability and Change

Adolescence is the story of Stability and Change. Your body is an unstable system, and you are constantly adapting to maintain your balance.

Thinking Lens: Systems

Let’s put on the Systems and System Models lens.

You treat your body as a system when you realize that not getting enough sleep (a change in one part of the system) affects your athletic performance (another part of the system).

Question: What is another example of how your body acts like an interconnected system?

Essential Questions

  • In my own words, what are Matter, Force, and Energy?
  • Which of the 8 Practices feels most familiar to me? Which feels newest?
  • How can using a “lens” like “Cause and Effect” help me understand the world better?

Summary Prompt

Explain how the simple act of walking across the room involves Matter, a Force, and Energy, and identify one Scientific Practice or Concept involved.
