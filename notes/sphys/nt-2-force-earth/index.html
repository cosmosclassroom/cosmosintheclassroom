  • “Execute a systematic, algebraic problem-solving strategy for multi-step kinematics problems.” keyVocabulary:
  • term: “Freefall” definition: “Motion under the influence of gravity alone; acceleration is constant, $\vec{a} = -9.80 \, m/s^2$.”
  • term: “Apogee” definition: “The highest point reached by an object in projectile or freefall motion, where its instantaneous vertical velocity is zero.”
  • term: “Systematic Strategy” definition: “A structured process for solving physics problems: List knowns/unknowns, select formula, rearrange algebraically, then substitute with units.” keyFormulae:
  • formula: “\vec{v}_f = \vec{v}_i + \vec{a}t” description: “Final velocity from initial velocity, acceleration, and time.”
  • formula: “\Delta\vec{x} = \vec{v}_i t + \frac{1}{2}\vec{a}t^2” description: “Displacement from initial velocity, acceleration, and time.”
  • formula: “\vec{v}_f^2 = \vec{v}_i^2 + 2\vec{a}\Delta\vec{x}” description: “Final velocity from initial velocity, acceleration, and displacement (time-independent).”

instructional_flow: # ————————————————————————– # SECTION 1: Introduction to Freefall # ————————————————————————–

  • type: reading title: “The Universal Accelerator: Gravity” content: | One of the most common applications of constant acceleration is freefall, which is the motion of any object subject only to the force of gravity. On Earth’s surface, this means the acceleration is always $\vec{a}_g = -9.80 \, m/s^2$. This is a constant value and its direction is always down.

    Understanding this one value is the key to solving a massive class of kinematics problems, from a simple drop to the complex trajectory of a sounding rocket.

  • type: callout style: “tip” content: | The Freefall Key: The term freefall or dropped means two things:
    1. Initial Velocity: $\vec{v}_i = 0 \, m/s$ (if dropped from rest).
    2. Acceleration: $\vec{a} = -9.80 \, m/s^2$ (always, near Earth).

  • type: example title: “Example 1: A Simple Drop” content: | Problem: A wrench is accidentally dropped from a platform. How fast is it traveling after $4.0 \, s$?

    Solution:

    1. Knowns/Unknowns: $\vec{v}_i = 0 \, m/s$, $\vec{a} = -9.8 \, m/s^2$, $t = 4.0 \, s$. Unknown is $\vec{v}_f$.
    2. Select Formula: Use the equation that excludes displacement: $\vec{v}_f = \vec{v}_i + \vec{a}t$.
    3. Substitute & Solve: \(\\vec{v}_f = 0 + (-9.8 \\, m/s^2)(4.0 \\, s)\) \(\\vec{v}_f = -39 \\, m/s\)

    Answer: The wrench is traveling at $39 \, m/s$ downwards.

    1. If you throw a ball straight up, what is its acceleration at its highest point (apogee)?

# ————————————————————————– # SECTION 2: Multi-Step Problems # ————————————————————————–

  • type: reading title: “Breaking Down Complex Motion” content: | Many real-world motions, like a rocket launch, are multi-stage problems. The rocket’s motion when the engine is firing (high positive $\vec{a}$) is one stage, and its motion after the engine cuts out (freefall, $\vec{a} = -9.80 \, m/s^2$) is a second, distinct stage.

    The final velocity of Stage 1 becomes the initial velocity of Stage 2. This linking step is critical for a systematic solution.
