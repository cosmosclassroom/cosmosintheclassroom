# Cornell Notes: Unit 9 – Electricity

| **Topic/Questions** | **Notes** |
|---------------------|-----------|
| **Key Concepts** | - Electric charge (Coulomb, C) <br> - Electric fields: region where charges experience force <br> - Electric potential energy (PE), voltage (V) <br> - Electric current (I): flow of charge, measured in amperes (A) <br> - Ohm’s Law: $V = IR$ <br> - Resistance (Ω), resistivity ($\rho$) <br> - Series and parallel circuits <br> - Electric power: $P = VI$ |
| **How do charges move?** | - Charges move due to electric fields and potential difference (voltage) <br> - Batteries provide EMF (voltage) to push charges through circuits |
| **Historical Figures** | - Galvani: "life force" and frog leg experiment <br> - Volta: invented the battery (Volta pile), showed electricity comes from metals, not just living things |
| **Circuit Diagrams** | - Use standard symbols for batteries, resistors, etc. <br> - Show current flow from + to - (conventional current) <br> - Keep diagrams neat and labeled |
| **Current & Analogy** | - Current is not the flow of electrons, but the flow of charge <br> - Water analogy: charge flows like water in pipes <br> - Current direction: positive to negative terminal |
| **Ohm’s Law** | - $V = IR$ <br> - Resistance opposes current <br> - Units: $1~\Omega = 1~V/1~A$ |
| **Resistivity** | - $R = \rho \frac{L}{A}$ <br> - Resistance depends on material, length, and area <br> - Conductors (copper, aluminum) have low resistivity; insulators (wood, rubber) have high resistivity |
| **Series Circuits** | - One path for current <br> - Total resistance: $R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2 + ...$ <br> - Same current through all components <br> - Voltage divides across components |
| **Parallel Circuits** | - Multiple paths for current <br> - Total resistance: $\frac{1}{R_{eq}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + ...$ <br> - Same voltage across branches <br> - Current divides between branches |
| **Electric Power** | - $P = VI$, $P = I^2R$, $P = \frac{V^2}{R}$ <br> - Power is the rate of energy transfer (watts, W) <br> - $1~W = 1~J/s$ |
| **Sample Problems** | - Use Ohm’s Law and power equations to solve for current, voltage, resistance, and power in circuits <br> - Analyze series and parallel resistor combinations |

---

**Summary/Main Ideas:**  
- Electricity involves the movement of charge due to electric fields and potential differences.
- Circuits can be analyzed using Ohm’s Law and rules for series/parallel combinations.
- Power in circuits is calculated using voltage and current.
- Understanding diagrams and units is essential for solving circuit problems.

**Key Terms:**  
Charge, current, voltage, resistance, Ohm’s Law, series circuit, parallel circuit, power, EMF, resistivity.
