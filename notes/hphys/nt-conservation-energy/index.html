notes:

  • section: “Introduction” content: | The Law of Conservation of Energy is a fundamental principle in physics. It states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another within an isolated system. The total energy of the system remains constant.

  • section: “Key Concepts” items:
    • “Total Energy Conservation: $E_{total} = constant$ in isolated systems”
    • “Energy Transformation: Energy changes forms but total amount remains constant”
    • “Mechanical Energy: Sum of kinetic and potential energies ($E_{mech} = KE + PE$)”
    • “Non-Conservative Forces: Friction converts mechanical energy to thermal energy”

  • section: “Mathematical Expression” content: | The total energy at the initial state must equal the total energy at the final state:

    \[E_{initial} = E_{final}\]

    When expanded to include common forms of mechanical and non-mechanical energy:

    \[KE_i + PE_i + E_{other,i} = KE_f + PE_f + E_{other,f}\]

    For mechanical systems without friction:

    \[\frac{1}{2}mv_i^2 + mgh_i = \frac{1}{2}mv_f^2 + mgh_f\]
  • section: “Examples & Demonstrations” examples:
    • title: “Roller Coaster Design” description: “Engineers use conservation of energy to determine minimum height required for the coaster to reach subsequent hills, transforming $PE_g$ into $KE$ and back.”
    • title: “Pendulum Motion” description: “A pendulum continuously converts $PE_g$ (at highest points) into $KE$ (at lowest point), illustrating predictable energy transformation patterns.”
    • title: “Hydroelectric Power” description: “Water transforms $PE_g$ (stored behind dam) into $KE$ as it falls, then into electrical energy via turbine.”
  • section: “Summary & Reflection” content: | Key Takeaways:
    • Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed
    • In isolated systems, total energy remains constant
    • Mechanical energy (KE + PE) is conserved when no non-conservative forces act
    • Real systems include thermal energy from friction and air resistance

    Problem-Solving Strategy:

    1. Identify the system and check if isolated
    2. Choose initial and final states
    3. List all energy forms at each state
    4. Apply conservation: $E_i = E_f$
    5. Solve for unknowns
