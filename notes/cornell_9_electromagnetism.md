# Cornell Notes: Unit 9.1 – Electrostatics

| **Topic/Questions** | **Notes** |
|---------------------|-----------|
| **What is electrostatics?** | Study of stationary electric charges and the forces, fields, and phenomena they produce. |
| **Historical Background** | - Ancient Greeks observed attractive properties of amber ("elektron") when rubbed with fur.<br>- William Gilbert coined "electric" and compared electricity to magnetism; discovered Earth is a magnet.<br>- Benjamin Franklin: showed lightning is static electricity, defined positive/negative charge, and introduced the law of charges. |
| **Law of Charges** | - Two types: positive and negative.<br>- Like charges repel, opposites attract.<br>- Charge is conserved in all processes. |
| **Atomic Structure and Charge** | - Atoms are neutral by default (equal protons and electrons).<br>- Electrons can transfer between atoms, creating charged objects. |
| **Quantization of Charge** | - Charge exists in discrete units (elementary charge, $e$).<br>- $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C.<br>- Protons: $+e$, electrons: $-e$. |
| **Conductors, Insulators, Semiconductors** | - Conductors: allow free movement of charge (e.g., metals).<br>- Insulators: do not allow charge to move freely (e.g., glass, rubber).<br>- Semiconductors: intermediate behavior.<br>- Superconductors: zero resistance at low temperatures. |
| **Charging Methods** | - By contact: direct transfer of electrons.<br>- By induction: rearrangement of charges due to a nearby charged object, often with grounding.<br>- Grounding: connecting to Earth to neutralize excess charge. |
| **Coulomb’s Law** | - Describes electric force between two point charges:<br> $F = k_C \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}$<br>- $k_C$ (Coulomb's constant) ≈ $8.99 \times 10^9$ N·m²/C².<br>- Force is attractive or repulsive depending on charge signs.<br>- Inverse square law, similar to gravity but can repel or attract. |
| **Units of Charge** | - SI unit: Coulomb (C).<br>- $1$ C = charge of $6.2 \times 10^{18}$ electrons.<br>- Common subunits: mC ($10^{-3}$ C), μC ($10^{-6}$ C), nC ($10^{-9}$ C). |
| **Electric Fields** | - Region around a charge where other charges experience a force.<br>- Field strength: $E = \frac{F}{q}$ (N/C).<br>- Field lines show direction (away from $+$, toward $-$) and strength (closer lines = stronger field).<br>- Fields interact and can be visualized with diagrams. |
| **Forces in Fields** | - A charge in a field experiences a force: $F = qE$.<br>- Acceleration depends on charge and mass: $a = F/m$. |
| **Sample Problems** | - Calculating force between charges (Coulomb’s Law).<br>- Determining electric field strength at a point.<br>- Finding equilibrium points for multiple charges. |

---

**Summary/Main Ideas:**  
- Electrostatics deals with stationary charges, their interactions, and the fields they create.
- Charges are quantized, conserved, and interact via Coulomb’s Law.
- Conductors, insulators, and semiconductors differ in how they allow charge movement.
- Electric fields explain action-at-a-distance forces.
- Understanding units and methods of charging is essential for solving electrostatics problems.

**Key Terms:**  
Charge, conductor, insulator, induction, grounding, Coulomb’s Law, electric field, elementary charge, quantization, field lines.
