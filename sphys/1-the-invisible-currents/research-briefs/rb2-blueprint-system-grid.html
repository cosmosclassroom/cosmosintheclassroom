Part 2: BLUEPRINT (During Learning)

New Information: So far, we have learned that an electrical grid is a complex system that must be perfectly balanced. The amount of electricity supplied by power plants must match the demand from communities in real-time. During the Texas crisis, this balance was broken. Now, we have investigated the data to understand the bigger questions of why it broke.

Update Your Model:

Look back at your initial model from the "Forecast" section. It's time to create a new, much more detailed system model that explains the Texas grid failure. Your new model should be a diagram that shows not just the parts, but how they are all interconnected and affect one another.

Use the data and guiding questions below to build your model. A strong model will help explain: * The Imbalance: How did freezing temperatures affect both the supply of electricity (from different sources) and the demand for it? * Reliability: Why did some energy sources fail while others kept working? * The Storage Challenge: Why couldn't Texas use stored electricity to prevent the blackouts? * Equity: How did the design of the system lead to some communities being without power for much longer than others?
