P3L1-L4 Study Guide: Historical Data and Motion Analysis

Assessment Overview

  • Time: 45 minutes
  • Format: Selected response + calculations
  • Topics: Historical safety data + Position-time graphs + Velocity-time graphs + Collision forces

What Students Should Know

Historical Safety Data (P3L1)

  • How has vehicle safety changed over the last 100 years?
  • What are some important reasons for those changes?
  • Why are fatality rates per vehicle better than total fatalities for comparing safety?
  • What factors have made driving safer since the 1920s?

Position-Time Graphs (P3L2)

  • What is reaction distance? What is braking distance?
  • How does distraction affect stopping distance?
  • What does the slope of a P-T graph represent?
  • What do straight lines vs curved lines mean on P-T graphs?

Velocity-Time Graphs (P3L3)

  • What does the area under a V-T graph represent?
  • How do you calculate distance from a V-T graph?
  • What is the two-second rule for following distance?
  • Why do higher speeds require longer stopping distances?
  • What do horizontal vs sloped lines mean on V-T graphs?

Collision Forces (P3L4)

  • What is Newton’s Third Law and how does it apply to collisions?
  • How do collision forces compare between two objects?
  • What is the mathematical relationship between mass and velocity changes?
  • How does mass affect velocity changes in collisions?
  • What is the difference between force magnitude and direction?

What Students Should Be Able to Do

Graph Skills

  • Read coordinates accurately from P-T and V-T graphs
  • Calculate velocity from slope of P-T graphs
  • Calculate distance from area under V-T graphs
  • Identify trends in historical data over time

Calculations

  • Calculate reaction distance using speed and time
  • Find total stopping distance from graph areas
  • Calculate safe following distances
  • Apply mass-velocity change relationship in collisions
  • Determine collision force magnitudes and directions

Problem Solving

  • Explain why distracted driving increases stopping distance
  • Predict collision outcomes based on mass ratios
  • Apply Newton’s Third Law to collision scenarios
  • Connect physics concepts to real driving safety situations
  • Evaluate following distance rules using physics principles
