Standard Physics

Welcome to Standard Physics, an applied physics course that connects fundamental physical principles to real-world systems, with special emphasis on energy technologies and Earth science phenomena.

Course Overview

This course emphasizes: - Applied Physics: Real-world applications of physical principles - Energy Systems: Understanding modern energy technologies and grids - Earth Sciences: Physics of geological and atmospheric processes - Problem-Solving: Practical approaches to complex systems - Mathematical Modeling: Quantitative analysis of physical systems

Course Units

Unit 1: Energy and Grids

  • Core Topics: Energy transformations, electrical systems, power grids
  • Key Skills: Energy analysis, circuit fundamentals, efficiency calculations
  • Real-World Focus: Modern electrical infrastructure and renewable energy
  • Content Types: Lectures | Flexbook | Labs | Problem Sets | Research Briefs

Unit 2: Earth Forces

  • Core Topics: Gravitational fields, tectonic forces, atmospheric dynamics
  • Key Skills: Force analysis, field concepts, pressure and fluid mechanics
  • Real-World Focus: Geological processes and weather systems
  • Content Types: Lectures | Flexbook | Labs | Problem Sets | Research Briefs

Course Philosophy

Standard Physics bridges the gap between theoretical physics and practical applications by: - Connecting Concepts: Linking fundamental physics to everyday technologies - Systems Thinking: Understanding complex real-world systems through physics principles - Mathematical Reasoning: Using quantitative methods to analyze practical problems - Environmental Awareness: Exploring physics connections to energy and environmental issues

Assessment Approach

Assessment focuses on applied understanding through: - Practical Problem-Solving: Real-world scenarios requiring physics analysis - System Analysis: Understanding complex technological and natural systems - Mathematical Application: Using physics equations to solve practical problems - Communication: Explaining physics concepts in applied contexts
