LAB OVERVIEW

——————————————————————————

overview: abstract: | This investigation uses data from the famous “photoelectric effect” experiment. Students will analyze data tables to discover that light, which we’ve modeled as a wave, must also behave like a particle (a “photon”) when it interacts with matter.

introduction: | In our last lab, we proved that light behaves like a wave. But that’s not the whole story. At the beginning of the 20th century, scientists ran an experiment (the photoelectric effect) that could not be explained by the wave model. They shined light on a piece of metal, and under certain conditions, electrons were ejected. Your task is to analyze the data from this experiment to find the flaw in the wave model and see the evidence for the “photon” model of light.

background_context: | Before starting this lab, you should understand: - Wave Model of Light: In this model, the “brightness” or intensity of light is related to its amplitude (energy). A brighter light should carry more energy. - Particle Model of Light (Photon): In this model, light energy comes in discrete packets called photons. The energy of one photon is determined by its frequency ($E=hf$), not its intensity. - Electron Ejection: To eject an electron from a metal, a certain minimum energy (the “work function”) is required.

SYNTHESIS: CLAIM-EVIDENCE-REASONING

——————————————————————————

synthesis: research_question: | How does this experiment demonstrate ‘Cause and Effect’ at the quantum level, and what evidence supports the particle (photon) model of light?

claim_prompt: | Claim: Based on the research question, write a single, clear, and specific sentence that states your main conclusion about the nature of light.

**Example Format:** The photoelectric effect demonstrates that light 
interacts with matter as a particle (a photon), where the *cause* of 
electron ejection is a single photon having sufficient *frequency*, not 
the light's overall intensity.

evidence_prompt: | Evidence: Provide a bulleted list of specific, quantitative data points from your investigation that support your claim.

- (From Table 1) High-intensity (100%) but low-frequency (4.0 x 10¹⁴ Hz) 
  light failed to eject any electrons.
- (From Table 2) Low-intensity (50%) but high-frequency (7.0 x 10¹⁴ Hz) 
  light *did* eject electrons, with a max energy of 1.2 eV.
- (From Table 3) Doubling the intensity (to 100%) of the high-frequency 
  light *did not change* the max energy of a single electron (still 1.2 eV).

reasoning_prompt: | Reasoning: In a paragraph, explain the scientific principles that connect your evidence to your claim. Why is this evidence sufficient to support your conclusion?

**Guidance:** Your reasoning should:
- Reference the **Cross-Cutting Concept of 'Cause and Effect'**.
- Explain *why* the wave model fails. The wave model predicts that 
  high-intensity light (Table 1) should have more energy and *should* have 
  ejected electrons, but it didn't.
- Explain *how* the particle model ($E=hf$) succeeds. The *cause* of 
  ejection is a single photon. The energy of that photon depends only 
  on $f$.
- In Table 1, the red photons had $f$ that was too low, so $E$ was too low. 
  No ejection, no matter how many photons (high intensity) there were.
- In Tables 2 & 3, the blue photons had a high enough $f$ to have enough 
  energy ($E=hf$) to eject an electron. Increasing the intensity (Table 3) 
  just sent *more photons*, but the energy *per photon* was the same, 
  so the max electron energy was the same.
