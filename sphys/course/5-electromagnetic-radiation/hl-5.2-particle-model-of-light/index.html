Abstract

In this investigation, you will analyze data from a simulated photoelectric effect experiment. By comparing how a metal surface reacts to light of different frequencies and intensities, you will find evidence that contradicts a simple wave model of light and strongly supports the existence of light “particles,” or photons.

Introduction

For centuries, light was understood as a wave. However, at the turn of the 20th century, an experiment called the photoelectric effect produced results that the wave model simply could not explain. The experiment involved shining light on a metal plate to see if it would knock electrons off the surface. The bizarre results of this experiment led Albert Einstein to propose a revolutionary idea: that light itself is “quantized” into particle-like packets of energy. Your task is to analyze the data and see the evidence for yourself. [Image of the photoelectric effect]

Connection to Unit Phenomenon

This lab reveals a deep, quantum-level truth about the electromagnetic radiation in our microwave oven. The energy is not delivered as a continuous, gentle wave, but as a barrage of trillions of individual energy packets—photons. Each microwave photon has a specific amount of energy, determined by its frequency, that is perfectly tuned to be absorbed by a water molecule and cause it to rotate. The photoelectric effect was the first experiment to reveal this strange, particle-like nature of light, a concept that is fundamental to all modern technology, from solar panels to digital cameras.
