Problem

This problem models a simple mechanical wave, like the seismic P-waves we studied in Unit 2, to practice the basic wave equation.

A seismologist observes a seismic P-wave traveling through granite. The wave has a frequency of $f = 20.0 \, \text{Hz}$ and a wavelength of $\lambda = 275 \, \text{m}$.

What is the speed ($v$) of the P-wave in granite?

Given:

  • $f = 20.0 \, \text{Hz}$
  • $\lambda = 275 \, \text{m}$

Find: $v$

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

  • $f = 20.0 \, \text{s}^{-1}$ (since $1 \, \text{Hz} = 1/\text{s}$)
  • $\lambda = 275 \, \text{m}$
  • Unknown: $v = ?$

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

\[v = f \lambda\]

Step 3: Substitute Values

\[v = (20.0 \, \text{s}^{-1}) \times (275 \, \text{m})\] \[v = 5500 \, \text{m/s}\]

Final Answer: The speed of the P-wave is $5.50 \times 10^3 \, \text{m/s}$ (or 5,500 m/s).

Problem

This problem connects to our anchoring phenomenon. You will calculate the frequency of the electromagnetic waves that heat food in a microwave oven.

A standard microwave oven operates using electromagnetic radiation that has a wavelength of $\lambda = 12.2 \, \text{cm}$.

What is the frequency ($f$) of this radiation?

Hint: This is an electromagnetic wave, so its speed is $c$. Watch your units!

Given:

  • $\lambda = 12.2 \, \text{cm}$
  • $v = c = 3.00 \times 10^8 \, \text{m/s}$

Find: $f$

📝 Solution Workspace

Show all work and include units in your final answer.

Complete this problem in your notebook or on separate paper.

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

  • $\lambda = 12.2 \, \text{cm} = 0.122 \, \text{m}$ (Critical Unit Conversion)
  • $v = c = 3.00 \times 10^8 \, \text{m/s}$
  • Unknown: $f = ?$

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

\[c = f \lambda\]

Step 3: Algebraic Rearrangement

\[f = \frac{c}{\lambda}\]

Step 4: Substitute Values

\[f = \frac{3.00 \times 10^8 \, \text{m/s}}{0.122 \, \text{m}}\] \[f = 2,459,016,393... \, \text{Hz}\] \[f = 2.46 \times 10^9 \, \text{Hz}\]

Final Answer: The frequency of the microwave radiation is $2.46 \times 10^9 \, \text{Hz}$ (or 2.46 GHz, which is a standard frequency for Wi-Fi and microwaves).

Problem

This problem applies the same formula to a different part of the EM spectrum: the radio waves that carry information.

A local FM radio station broadcasts at a frequency of $f = 91.7 \, \text{MHz}$ (MegaHertz).

What is the wavelength ($\lambda$) of these radio waves?

Hint: $1 \, \text{MHz} = 1 \times 10^6 \, \text{Hz}$.

Given:

  • $f = 91.7 \, \text{MHz} = 91.7 \times 10^6 \, \text{Hz}$
  • $v = c = 3.00 \times 10^8 \, \text{m/s}$

Find: $\lambda$

📝 Solution Workspace

Show all work and include units in your final answer.

Complete this problem in your notebook or on separate paper.

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

  • $f = 9.17 \times 10^7 \, \text{Hz}$ (or $\text{s}^{-1}$)
  • $v = c = 3.00 \times 10^8 \, \text{m/s}$
  • Unknown: $\lambda = ?$

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

\[c = f \lambda\]

Step 3: Algebraic Rearrangement

\[\lambda = \frac{c}{f}\]

Step 4: Substitute Values

\[\lambda = \frac{3.00 \times 10^8 \, \text{m/s}}{9.17 \times 10^7 \, \text{s}^{-1}}\] \[\lambda = 3.2715... \, \text{m}\]

Final Answer: The wavelength of the radio waves is $3.27 \, \text{m}$.

Problem

This problem connects the wave properties of frequency and period to a measurable time. This is the principle behind radar and echolocation.

A boat uses a sonar system (sound waves) to map the ocean floor. The sonar emits a sound pulse with a wavelength of $\lambda = 0.0500 \, \text{m}$. The speed of sound in seawater is $v = 1530 \, \text{m/s}$.

a) What is the period ($T$) of a single wave? b) The boat receives an echo (the pulse bouncing off the seafloor) $t = 0.600 \, \text{s}$ after it was sent. How deep is the ocean at this point?

Find: $T$ and depth $d$

📝 Solution Workspace

Show all work and include units in your final answer.

Complete this problem in your notebook or on separate paper.

🔍 View Solution

— Part (a): Period (T) —

Step 1: Find Frequency (f) We first need the frequency to find the period. \(v = f \lambda \implies f = \frac{v}{\lambda}\) \(f = \frac{1530 \, \text{m/s}}{0.0500 \, \text{m}} = 30,600 \, \text{Hz}\)

Step 2: Find Period (T) \(T = \frac{1}{f}\) \(T = \frac{1}{30,600 \, \text{Hz}} = 0.00003267... \, \text{s}\)

Answer (a): The period of the wave is $3.27 \times 10^{-5} \, \text{s}$ (or $32.7 \, \mu\text{s}$).

— Part (b): Depth (d) —

Step 1: Identify Principle The total time $t = 0.600 \, \text{s}$ is the time for the pulse to travel down and back. The time to travel one-way to the bottom is half of the total time.

\[t_{one-way} = \frac{0.600 \, \text{s}}{2} = 0.300 \, \text{s}\]

Step 2: Calculate Distance The sound travels at a constant speed, so we use $distance = speed \times time$. \(d = v \times t_{one-way}\) \(d = (1530 \, \text{m/s}) \times (0.300 \, \text{s})\) \(d = 459 \, \text{m}\)

Answer (b): The ocean depth is $459 \, \text{m}$.
