Problem Set 1: Circuits & Power - Answer Key

Key Formulae

  • Ohm’s Law: $V = IR$
  • Power Equation: $P = VI$
  • Power and Energy: $E = P \times t$

Part 1: Conceptual Questions

  1. The Water Analogy:
    • The water pressure from the pump: Voltage (V)
    • The flow rate of the water (gallons per second): Current (I)
    • A narrow, rocky section of the pipe: Resistance (R)
  2. Joules vs. Watts: Energy (Joules) is the total amount of work done or energy transferred, while power (Watts) is the rate at which energy is transferred or work is done. Yes, a device can use many Joules but have low power - for example, a small LED light left on for many hours uses a lot of total energy but has very low power consumption.

Part 2: Ohm’s Law Practice (V = IR)

  1. LED Problem: $R = \frac{V}{I} = \frac{3 \text{ V}}{0.02 \text{ A}} = 150 \text{ Ω}$

  2. Electric Motor: $V = IR = (0.8 \text{ A})(15 \text{ Ω}) = 12 \text{ V}$

  3. Smartphone Charger: $I = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{120 \text{ V}}{48 \text{ Ω}} = 2.5 \text{ A}$

Part 3: Power Calculations Practice (P = VI)

  1. Television: $P = VI = (120 \text{ V})(2.5 \text{ A}) = 300 \text{ W}$

  2. Microwave: $I = \frac{P}{V} = \frac{1,100 \text{ W}}{120 \text{ V}} = 9.17 \text{ A}$

  3. Electric Car Charging: $V = \frac{P}{I} = \frac{150,000 \text{ W}}{375 \text{ A}} = 400 \text{ V}$

Part 4: Synthesis Problems

  1. Electric Toaster:
    • a) $I = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{120 \text{ V}}{12 \text{ Ω}} = 10 \text{ A}$
    • b) $P = VI = (120 \text{ V})(10 \text{ A}) = 1,200 \text{ W}$
  2. Light Bulb:
    • a) $I = \frac{P}{V} = \frac{60 \text{ W}}{120 \text{ V}} = 0.5 \text{ A}$
    • b) $R = \frac{V}{I} = \frac{120 \text{ V}}{0.5 \text{ A}} = 240 \text{ Ω}$
  3. Energy Calculation:
    • a) $3 \text{ hours} = 3 \times 3600 = 10,800 \text{ seconds}$
    • b) $E = P \times t = (100 \text{ W})(10,800 \text{ s}) = 1,080,000 \text{ J}$

Part 5: Challenge Problem

  1. Space Heater Analysis:

    • a) $I = \frac{P}{V} = \frac{1,500 \text{ W}}{120 \text{ V}} = 12.5 \text{ A}$

    • b) When millions of homes simultaneously draw 12.5 A each, the total current demand on the electrical grid becomes enormous. The power grid has limited capacity to generate and transmit electricity. This sudden, massive increase in current demand can overload power plants, transmission lines, and distribution systems, leading to equipment failures, blackouts, and cascading grid failures as the system cannot supply the required current to all consumers simultaneously.
