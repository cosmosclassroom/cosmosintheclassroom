Circuit Calculations (Mathematical Thinking)

Key Formulas:

  • Ohm’s Law: V = IR (Voltage = Current × Resistance)
  • Power equations: P = VI
  • Energy equation: E = P × Δt (Energy = Power × Time)

Note: Units will be provided for each variable on the assessment

What You Should Be Able to Do:

  • Solve for any variable in V = IR when given the other two
  • Calculate electrical power using P = VI
  • Calculate energy consumption in kilowatt-hours
  • Show your work clearly with units in every step
  • Choose the right equation for each type of problem

Study Prompts:

  • Practice problems from Problem Set 1 - redo any you got wrong
  • Make sure you can solve for V, I, or R in any V = IR problem
  • Practice converting between W and kW, and between hours and other time units
  • Check: Can you calculate your monthly energy bill for a specific appliance?

Connecting Models to Real Systems (Explanation & Modeling)

What You Should Know:

  • How parallel circuits work (if one part fails, others keep working)
  • How the electrical grid is like a giant circuit with power plants, transmission lines, and homes
  • Why grid operators sometimes shut off power to prevent total system failure

What You Should Be Able to Do:

  • Explain the analogy between home electrical circuits and the power grid
  • Connect circuit models to real-world grid behavior during the crisis
  • Describe specific impacts of the blackouts on real people’s lives
  • Use circuit concepts to explain why rolling blackouts prevent total system collapse

Study Prompts:

  • Review your power strip lab: What happened when you turned off the main switch?
  • Think about: How is your house’s electrical system like the Texas grid?
  • Practice explaining: Why didn’t the whole grid just shut down permanently?
