Unit P.1 Assessment: Analyzing the Texas Power Crisis

Time Limit: 50 minutes
Materials: formula_sheet, calculator
Started:

Student Information

Anchoring Phenomenon: The Texas power crisis of February 2021, where a severe winter storm caused a massive failure of the state’s electrical grid.

Core Concepts: Electrical Circuits, Energy Consumption, Systems & System Models, Source Credibility

Segment 1: Foundation Understanding (Estimated Time: 15 minutes)

Part A: Mathematical Application

1. Guided Calculation: Power

A family uses a small gasoline generator that provides 120 V to power a 60 W electric heater.

Given: Voltage (V) = 120 V, Power (P) = 60 W
Find: The current (I) drawn by the heater in amps (A).

a) Identify the correct formula from your sheet.

b) Show your substitution of values.

c) Calculate the result with correct units.

2. Guided Calculation: Energy & Cost

A different family powers a 1,500 W space heater for 4 hours using a generator. The cost of gasoline to run the generator is about $0.50 per kilowatt-hour (kWh).

Given: Power (P) = 1,500 W, Time (Δt) = 4 hours, Cost = $0.50/kWh
Find: The total cost to run the heater.

a) Convert Power from W to kW. (Hint: 1000 W = 1 kW)

b) Calculate Energy in kWh using the formula E = P × Δt.

c) Calculate the total cost to run the heater for 4 hours.

Segment 2: Application and Analysis (Estimated Time: 20 minutes)

Part B: Systems Thinking & Data Analysis

3. Evaluating Conflicting Sources

After the crisis, two news headlines were published:

Headline A: “Single Point of Failure: Frozen Wind Turbines Were Sole Cause of Texas Grid Collapse.”

Headline B: “A System Cascade: How Frozen Natural Gas Plants, High Demand, and Grid Mismanagement All Contributed to Texas Blackouts.”

Based on the evidence you analyzed in class (from the Hexagon Labs and readings), which headline is a more credible, scientific explanation? Explain your reasoning.

4. Connecting Models to Real Systems

During the crisis, grid operators used “rolling blackouts” to intentionally cut power to some neighborhoods for short periods. They did this to avoid a permanent, state-wide grid collapse.

Explain how this is similar to the parallel circuits in a house. Why didn’t turning off power to one neighborhood (like unplugging one light) cause the whole grid to shut down?

Segment 3: Extended Application & Synthesis (Estimated Time: 15 minutes)

Part C: Constructing a Scientific Explanation

5. Synthesizing Your Understanding

Write a comprehensive explanation of what caused the Texas power crisis. Your answer should be a short paragraph that synthesizes information from the entire unit.

Your explanation MUST include:

  • The initial cause that started the chain of events.
  • An explanation of what happened to both energy supply and energy demand.
  • A description of how the failure of one part of the system affected the other parts of the system.
  • At least two specific types of evidence you saw in class (e.g., “The supply vs. demand graph showed…”, “The data on frozen equipment indicated…”).
