Hexagon Lab P.1.3: Engineering Design Tradeoffs

Today’s Question: How do we make data-driven decisions about the best energy sources for a community, considering all the competing priorities?

Complete this lab in your Science Notebook.

What You Need:

  • Your Science Notebook
  • Calculator
  • Provided “Energy Source Data Table” (see below)

Background:

Imagine you are part of a team tasked with designing a new energy plan for your community. There’s no single “perfect” energy source; each has its strengths and weaknesses. Engineers often use tools like a decision matrix to systematically compare options and make the best choices based on specific goals and limitations. This lab will help you practice that process.

Part 1: Setting Your Priorities

Step 1: In your Science Notebook, create a list of at least four key criteria that you think are most important for a successful community energy plan. Think about what matters most to people and the environment. Example criteria: Cost, Reliability, Environmental Impact, Land Use.

Step 2: Assign a weight to each criterion from 1 to 5, where 5 is “extremely important” and 1 is “least important.” The sum of your weights doesn’t need to equal a specific number, but higher numbers should reflect higher priorities for your community.

Part 2: Scoring the Energy Sources

Step 3: Now, you will score each energy source (Solar, Wind, Natural Gas) against each of your chosen criteria. Use a scale of 1 to 10, where 10 is “excellent” and 1 is “poor.” You’ll need to use the “Energy Source Data Table” above to help you.

  • Cost: Lower cost = higher score.
  • Land Use: Less land used = higher score.
  • Reliability: Higher reliability percentage = higher score.
  • CO2 Emissions: Lower emissions = higher score.

Part 3: Calculating and Recommending

Step 4: Calculate the weighted score for each energy source for each criterion. To do this, multiply the “Weight” by the “Score” you gave it. Example: If Cost has a weight of 4 and Solar scored 8 for Cost, the weighted score is 4 * 8 = 32.

Step 5: Sum up all the weighted scores for each energy source to get a Total Weighted Score.

Part 4: Reflect and Connect

Step 7: How does this activity model the engineering practice of ‘Designing Solutions’ by explicitly considering criteria, constraints, and trade-offs? In your Science Notebook, write a paragraph explaining how this lab helped you understand the process of engineering design.

Step 8: Imagine a scenario where your community’s priorities change (e.g., a new law requires zero CO2 emissions, or a major economic downturn makes cost the absolute top priority).
