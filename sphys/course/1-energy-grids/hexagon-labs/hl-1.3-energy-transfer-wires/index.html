Hexagon Lab 1.3: Modeling Energy Transfer in Wires

Complete the following investigations in your Research Log following the format of a scientific paper.

Abstract

In this investigation, you will analyze a pre-generated dataset to determine how variables like wire material, length, and thickness affect electrical resistance and energy loss. This analysis will help explain why power grids experience significant energy losses as electricity travels from power plants to communities.

Introduction

Energy is lost as electricity travels from power plants to homes through transmission and distribution lines. Understanding these losses is critical for designing efficient power grids and explaining why some communities experienced extended outages during the Texas crisis when generation capacity was already limited.

Central Research Question: How do the physical properties of transmission wires affect electrical resistance and energy loss in power grids?

Background: Electrical resistance opposes the flow of current, converting electrical energy into thermal energy (heat). This “lost” energy reduces grid efficiency and can contribute to system instability during peak demand periods.

Dataset Description

Navigate to the provided webpage displaying wire resistance data. The dataset contains experimental results from testing different wire configurations in a laboratory setting that simulates power transmission conditions.

Variables in Dataset:

  • Wire Material (categorical: Copper, Aluminum, Steel)
  • Wire Length (continuous: measured in kilometers)
  • Wire Cross-sectional Area (continuous: measured in mm²)
  • Measured Resistance (continuous: measured in Ohms)
  • Current Carrying Capacity (continuous: measured in Amperes)

Dataset Generation Notes for Instructors:

Variables:

  • WireMaterial: [“Copper”, “Aluminum”, “Steel”]
  • WireLength_km: [25, 50, 75, 100, 125, 150, 175, 200]
  • CrossSectionalArea_mm2: [100, 150, 200, 250, 300]
  • Resistance_Ohms: (calculated using resistivity values)
  • CurrentCapacity_A: (related to cross-sectional area and material properties)

Measurement Specifications:

  • Copper resistivity: 1.7 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m
  • Aluminum resistivity: 2.8 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m
  • Steel resistivity: 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ Ω·m
  • Resistance = (resistivity × length) / cross-sectional area
  • Data should show clear linear relationship between length and resistance
  • Data should show clear inverse relationship between area and resistance
  • Include 8-10 data points per material for statistical reliability

Data Presentation:

  • Interactive table allowing filtering by material and wire specifications
  • Separate scatter plot showing Length vs. Resistance for each material
  • Bar chart comparing materials at standard specifications
  • All plots should be clearly labeled with units and appropriate scales
