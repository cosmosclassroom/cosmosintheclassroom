Tracing the Outcome

Compare your final recommendations from Part 3. If your group members arrived at different energy source recommendations, work backward through your decision matrices.

  1. Can you pinpoint the single biggest difference in either the weights (Part 1) or the scores (Part 2) that led to the different final outcomes? Explain how that one choice created a ripple effect.

The “Best” and the “Worst”

Look at the energy source that scored the highest for your most important criterion.

  1. Why might a community not choose that option?

  2. Identify the most significant trade-off or sacrifice a community would have to make to adopt that energy source.

Beyond the Numbers

Our data table included four key criteria (Cost, Land Use, Reliability, CO2 Emissions).

  1. What are at least two other important factors not included in the data that a real-world engineering team would have to consider? (e.g., public opinion, job creation, construction time, aesthetics)

The Hybrid Solution

  1. The lab asked you to recommend a single energy source. Propose a hybrid solution that combines two energy sources from the table. Justify your choice by explaining how the two sources complement each other to create a more balanced and realistic energy plan.
