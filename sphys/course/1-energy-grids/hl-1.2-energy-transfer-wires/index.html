Overview

Abstract

In this investigation, you will analyze a pre-generated dataset from a series of simulated experiments to determine how physical properties of a wire—its length, thickness, and material—affect its electrical resistance. By identifying these patterns, you will develop a model to explain how and why energy is lost as electricity is transmitted through the power grid.

Introduction

A significant amount of the electrical energy generated at a power plant never reaches our homes. It is lost as heat in the thousands of miles of transmission lines that make up the grid. This energy loss is a direct consequence of a property called resistance. An efficient grid is one that minimizes this resistance. But what makes a wire more or less resistant? Your task is to analyze the data from several experiments to discover the rules that govern resistance.

Connection to Unit Phenomenon

This lab directly explains a key vulnerability of power grids. The energy lost in transmission lines is given by the formula $P_{loss} = I^2R$. Your investigation shows that the resistance, ‘R’, is an inherent property of the thousands of miles of wire that make up the grid. During the Texas crisis, the massive surge in current (‘I’) meant that this unavoidable resistance caused a huge increase in energy lost as heat ($I^2$ is a powerful factor!). This loss of energy further strained the failing power plants, making the supply-demand deficit even worse and contributing to the cascading blackouts. Engineers must use highly conductive, thick wires to minimize ‘R’ and make the grid as efficient as possible.
