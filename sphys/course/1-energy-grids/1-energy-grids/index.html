Phase 1: Forecast

Our unit begins with a real-world catastrophe: the 2021 Texas power crisis. Before we investigate the physics, your first task is to document your initial understanding of the problem and your first ideas about how our electrical system works.

Summarize the Gist

In your own words, summarize the core problem presented by the 2021 Texas power crisis. What was the main issue and who was affected?

My First Model of the Grid

Draw a simple diagram showing all the parts you think are involved in getting electricity from a power plant to a light bulb in a house. Label as many parts as you can and show the direction you think energy flows.

Phase 3: Reflection

Our investigation into the Texas power crisis is complete. The final step is to construct your definitive, evidence-based explanation of the event, and then to reflect on your own learning process.

Final Synthesis: Explaining the System Failure

This is your final, complete explanation of the 2021 Texas power crisis. Your response should be a comprehensive narrative that explains the event as a complex system failure. Your explanation must be explicitly grounded in evidence from the Hexagon Labs (e.g., specific data on supply/demand, properties of resistance) and incorporate key concepts from the Flexbook (e.g., Ohm's Law, power, energy, criteria and constraints).

Final Model: A Resilient Grid

Accompany your synthesis with a final, revised model. This should not just be a diagram of *how* the grid failed, but a proposal for a *more resilient* grid. Draw a system diagram that includes at least one engineering solution (e.g., battery storage, weatherized power plants) and explain how it would help prevent a future crisis.
