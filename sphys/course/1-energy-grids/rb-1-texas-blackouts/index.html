SPHYS Unit 1

Metacognitive Journal: Your Thinking Journey

Throughout this unit, you’ll keep a journal to help you track your thinking, connect your learning, and reflect on your growth as a scientist, engineer, policymaker, and citizen. Use the prompts below to guide your entries at each stage.

(a) Forecast: Your Initial Ideas & Predictions

  • What do you currently believe caused the Texas power crisis of February 2021?
    • Write down your ideas—even if you’re unsure or think they might be wrong.

  • Make a prediction:
    Which energy source (natural gas, wind, solar, etc.) do you think is most reliable for your community? Why?

  • What do you expect will be the biggest challenge for a community facing a large-scale blackout?
    • Explain your reasoning.

(b) Blueprint: Connecting Your Experiences to Big Ideas

Choose at least two activities (labs, problem sets, or readings) from your progress tracker. For each activity:

  • Briefly describe what you did.
  • What did you learn about energy transfer, grid stability, or engineering design?
  • Did this experience change or confirm your initial predictions? How?

Connect your learning to the unit’s central question:
How can we design more reliable systems to meet our communities’ energy needs?

Use the systems thinking framework:

  • Interactions:
    How did human and natural systems interact in your activities?

  • Interconnections:
    Did you notice any connections between local actions and broader impacts (e.g., equity, environmental justice)?

  • Implications:
    What consequences did you anticipate or observe from different engineering or policy choices?

(c) Reflection: How Has Your Thinking Grown?

  • How has your thinking about energy systems and community resilience changed since the start of the unit?
  • In what ways have your abilities to use math and science to understand and solve energy infrastructure problems improved?
  • As a deliberate citizen, what responsibilities do you have in understanding, evaluating, and acting on scientific, engineering, and policy information?
  • What ethical or societal considerations now seem most important to you when planning for energy reliability and equity?
