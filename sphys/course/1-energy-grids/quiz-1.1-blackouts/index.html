# Standard Physics Assessment: Analyzing the Texas Power Crisis
## Anchoring Phenomenon: The Texas power crisis of February 2021, where a severe winter storm caused a massive failure of the state's electrical grid. ## Core Concepts: Electrical Circuits, Energy Transfer, Systems & System Models, Engineering Trade-offs ### Time Limit: 50 minutes ### Support Materials Available: Formula Sheet, Calculator

Assessment Overview

What You'll Demonstrate

By completing this assessment, you'll show your ability to:

  • Analyze simple circuits using Ohm's Law.
  • Explain the causes of a large-scale system failure using data.
  • Evaluate competing engineering solutions based on criteria and constraints.

How This Assessment Works

  • Real-World Focus: All questions connect to the Texas power crisis.
  • Scaffolded Support: Each section builds from foundational knowledge to complex application.
  • Multiple Formats: You will answer multiple-choice, short answer, and data analysis questions.

Segment 1: Foundation Understanding (Estimated Time: 15 minutes)

Part A: Conceptual Understanding

1. Visual Analysis (5 points)

The diagram below shows a simplified model of a home's lighting circuit connected to the main power grid.

a) What does the voltage provided by the Grid represent in this system? (2 points)

b) If the switch is opened, what happens to the current, and why? (3 points)

Part B: Mathematical Application

2. Guided Calculation (6 points)

During the crisis, a family uses a small gasoline generator that provides 120 V to power a 60 W electric heater.

Given: Voltage (V) = 120 V, Power (P) = 60 W
Find: The current (I) drawn by the heater in amps (A).

a) Identify which formula from your sheet relates Power, Voltage, and Current. (1 point)

b) Show your substitution of values into the formula. (2 points)

c) Calculate the result with the correct units. (2 points)

d) Is this a large or small amount of current for a household appliance? (1 point)

Segment 2: Application and Analysis (Estimated Time: 20 minutes)

Real-World Problem Solving

3. Data Interpretation (10 points)

The chart below shows simplified data for the Texas power grid on a day during the 2021 crisis.

Part 1: Analyze the Graph (4 points)

At what point on the graph did the blackouts begin? Explain how you know.

What two factors contributed to the massive gap between supply and demand?

Part 2: Explain the System Failure (6 points)

Using the concept of Systems and System Models, explain why the failure of one part of the grid (like natural gas plants freezing) led to a collapse of the entire system.

How does this event demonstrate the Cross-Cutting Concept of Stability and Change?

Segment 3: Extended Application (Estimated Time: 15 minutes)

Engineering a Solution

4. Evaluating Trade-offs (12 points total)

A Texas town is using the Decision Matrix below to choose a new backup power source. They have assigned weights based on their priorities.

Criteria Weight Solar Score (1-5) Wind Score (1-5) Gas Score (1-5)
Cost 4 3 5 4
Reliability 5 3 2 5
Environment 2 4 5 1
Part 1: Calculate the Best Option (6 points)

In your notebook, calculate the total weighted score for each of the three energy sources.

Based on your calculation, which energy source is the best choice for this specific town?

Part 2: Justify Your Recommendation (6 points)

Write a short, persuasive argument (2-3 sentences) to the town council.

Use the Claim-Evidence-Reasoning framework to explain why your chosen source is the best option, referencing both the data and the town's weighted priorities.
