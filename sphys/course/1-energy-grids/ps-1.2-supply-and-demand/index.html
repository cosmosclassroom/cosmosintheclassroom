Key Formulas and Concepts

Energy vs. Power

  • Power (P): How fast energy is used (measured in watts, W)
  • Energy (E): The total amount of work done (measured in joules, J or kilowatt-hours, kWh)
  • Relationship: $E = P \times \Delta t$ (Energy = Power × Time)

Energy Conservation

  • Total Energy: $E_{total} = E_1 + E_2 + E_3 + …$ (Energy is additive)
  • System Efficiency: $Efficiency = \frac{E_{out}}{E_{in}} \times 100\%$

Cost Analysis

  • Energy Cost: $Cost = Energy \times Rate$ where rate is in $/kWh

Part A: Foundation - Worked Examples

Example 1: Energy vs. Power

Scenario: A Texas household uses a 1,500 W space heater during the February 2021 crisis.

Question: How much energy does the heater use if it runs for 8 hours?

Solution:

Given: P = 1,500 W = 1.5 kW, Δt = 8 hours
Find: E = ?
Formula: E = P × Δt
Substitute: E = 1.5 kW × 8 h
Calculate: E = 12 kWh

Check: 12 kWh means the heater uses 12 kilowatt-hours of energy.

Example 2: System Efficiency

Scenario: A power plant generates 1,000 kWh of electrical energy, but only 800 kWh reaches homes due to transmission losses.

Question: What is the efficiency of the transmission system?

Solution:

Given: E_in = 1,000 kWh, E_out = 800 kWh
Find: Efficiency = ?
Formula: Efficiency = (E_out / E_in) × 100%
Substitute: Efficiency = (800 kWh / 1,000 kWh) × 100%
Calculate: Efficiency = 80%

Check: 80% efficiency means 20% of the energy is lost during transmission.

Problem 2: Energy Mix Analysis

During the Texas crisis, the state’s energy mix changed dramatically:

Before Crisis (Normal conditions):

  • Natural Gas: 20,000 MW
  • Wind: 15,000 MW
  • Coal: 8,000 MW
  • Nuclear: 5,000 MW
  • Total: 48,000 MW

During Crisis (Extreme cold):

  • Natural Gas: 12,000 MW (froze)
  • Wind: 9,000 MW (iced over)
  • Coal: 8,000 MW (stayed stable)
  • Nuclear: 5,000 MW (stayed stable)
  • Total: 34,000 MW

a) What percentage of total power generation was lost during the crisis?

Power lost = Normal total - Crisis total = _____ MW
Percentage lost = (Power lost / Normal total) × 100% = _____%

b) If electricity costs $0.12 per kWh, how much money did Texans save per hour during the blackouts? (Assume demand would have been 48,000 MW without blackouts)

Energy not used per hour = _____ MW = _____ kW
Cost saved per hour = _____ kW × $0.12/kWh = $______

Problem 3: Storage System Design

A community wants to add battery storage to prepare for future emergencies.

Given Information:

  • Community peak demand: 100 MW
  • Want to store 4 hours of emergency power
  • Battery efficiency: 85%
  • Cost: $300 per kWh of storage capacity

a) How much energy storage capacity is needed?

Power needed: _____ MW = _____ kW
Time needed: _____ hours
Energy to store: E = P × Δt = _____ kWh

b) Accounting for efficiency losses, how much battery capacity must be installed?

Required capacity = Energy needed / Efficiency
Required capacity = _____ kWh / 0.85 = _____ kWh

c) What will this storage system cost?

Cost = Capacity × Cost per kWh
Cost = _____ kWh × $300/kWh = $______

Part C: Extension - Challenge Problems

Problem 4: Regional Grid Analysis

The Texas grid (ERCOT) serves 26 million people across 75% of the state.

Given:

  • Average household power: 1.2 kW
  • Average household size: 2.5 people
  • Industrial/commercial adds 50% more demand
  • Grid efficiency: 92%

a) Calculate the total power generation needed.

Number of households = _____ people ÷ 2.5 people/household = _____ households
Residential demand = _____ households × 1.2 kW = _____ kW
Total demand = Residential × 1.5 = _____ kW
Generation needed = Total demand ÷ 0.92 = _____ kW = _____ MW

b) Compare your answer to the actual Texas peak demand of about 75,000 MW. What might explain any differences?

Problem 5: Renewable Energy Trade-offs

A Texas city is choosing between energy options:

Option A: Natural Gas Plant

  • Capacity: 500 MW
  • Efficiency: 55%
  • Fuel cost: $40/MWh
  • Construction cost: $800 million
  • Lifespan: 30 years

Option B: Wind + Battery System

  • Wind capacity: 600 MW (average output 200 MW)
  • Battery capacity: 300 MW for 4 hours
  • Fuel cost: $0/MWh
  • Construction cost: $1.2 billion
  • Lifespan: 25 years

a) Calculate the cost per MW of capacity for each option.

Option A: $_____ million ÷ _____ MW = $_____ million per MW
Option B: $_____ million ÷ _____ MW = $_____ million per MW

b) Which option would you recommend and why? Consider reliability, cost, and environmental impact.
