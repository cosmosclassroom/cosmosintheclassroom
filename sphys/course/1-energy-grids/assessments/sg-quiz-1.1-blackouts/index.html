Unit P.1 Assessment Study Guide: The Invisible Currents Focus: This assessment tests your ability to think like a scientist and engineer. It focuses on applying concepts to the Texas power crisis, not just memorizing facts.

Segment 1: Foundation Skills (Concepts & Calculations) What You Need to Know & Do: Solve Circuit Problems: You must be fluent with the two key formulas for this unit. Show your work clearly and include units in every step.

Ohm’s Law: $V = IR$ (Voltage = Current × Resistance)

Practice solving for V, I, or R when given the other two values.

Power Equation: $P = VI$ (Power = Voltage × Current)

Practice solving for P, V, or I when given the other two values.

Analyze Circuit Diagrams: You need to explain what happens in a simple circuit.

What does voltage represent?

What happens to the current when a switch is opened or closed?

Study Prompts: Redo any problems you got wrong from Problem Set 1.

Look at a simple circuit diagram. Explain the role of the power source, the switch, and the load (e.g., a light bulb).

A 120 V outlet powers a device that draws 2 A of current. What is the power of the device?

Segment 2: Application and Analysis (Systems & Data) What You Need to Know & Do: Analyze a Supply vs. Demand Graph: You will be given a line graph showing energy supply and energy demand over time during the crisis.

Be able to identify the point where demand exceeded supply.

Be able to explain what the graph tells you about the causes of the blackouts.

Explain System Failure: Use systems thinking to explain how the failure of one part of the grid led to a large-scale collapse.

Connect the cause (extreme weather) to the effects (equipment failure, supply drop, blackouts).

How does this crisis demonstrate the concept of Stability and Change in a complex system?

Study Prompts: Review your notes and the data from the Hexagon Lab where you analyzed the blackout data.

Practice explaining aloud: “The Texas power crisis shows how a complex system fails because…”

How would you explain the idea of a “cascading failure” to someone who wasn’t in our class?

Segment 3: Extended Application (Engineering a Solution) What You Need to Know & Do: Use a Decision Matrix: You will be given a decision matrix with different energy sources, criteria (like Cost, Reliability), and community weights.

You must be able to calculate the total weighted score for each energy source.

*Formula: Total Score = (Score1 × Weight1) + (Score2 × Weight2) + … *

Justify a Recommendation (CER): You will need to make a recommendation and support it with data from the matrix.

Claim: State which option is best for the specific community.

Evidence: Use the calculated total scores and specific criteria to support your claim.

Reasoning: Explain why the evidence supports the claim, linking it back to the community’s priorities (the weights).

Study Prompts: Review your work from the Hexagon Lab 1.4: Engineering Design Tradeoffs. Make sure you understand how you calculated the final scores.

Choose a community from that lab and practice writing a one-sentence Claim, a one-sentence Evidence statement, and a one-sentence Reasoning statement.
