# Q2: Quantitative Problem Solving (P = IV, E = P * t, Efficiency)

  • id: q2_a type: numeric prompt: “An electric heater draws $10\,\text{A}$ of current from a $240\,\text{V}$ line. Calculate the heater’s Power ($P$) output in kilowatts ($\text{kW}$). (Conversion: $1\,\text{kW} = 1000\,\text{W}$).” points: 4 answer: 2.4 units: “kW” step: 0.1 [cite_start]explanation: “Use $P = IV$[cite: 10]. $P = (10\,\text{A})(240\,\text{V}) = 2400\,\text{W}$. Converting to $\text{kW}$: $2400\,\text{W} / 1000 = 2.4\,\text{kW}$[cite: 23, 24].”

  • id: q2_b type: numeric [cite_start]prompt: “If the heater is used for 5 hours, calculate the total Energy Consumed ($E$) in kilowatt-hours ($\text{kWh}$)[cite: 16].” points: 3 answer: 12.0 units: “kWh” step: 0.1 [cite_start]explanation: “Use $E = P \cdot \Delta t$[cite: 12]. $E = (2.4\,\text{kW}) \times (5\,\text{h}) = 12.0\,\text{kWh}$[cite: 23].”

  • id: q3_a type: short_answer prompt: “Using the ‘Wire Trade-off Analysis’ table (Table 2), analyze the Power Loss column. How does this data demonstrate the Circuit & Wire Properties concept? Since the current ($I$) is constant, the formula $P=I^2R$ dictates that power loss ($P$) is directly proportional to resistance ($R$). Copper has the lowest $R$, resulting in the lowest $P$ loss, while steel has the highest $R$ and highest $P$ loss.”

  • id: q4_b type: essay prompt: “Write a short paragraph explaining why cost is considered a Constraint in this engineering problem, even though Copper offers the lowest energy loss. Your explanation must include the term Trade-off[cite: 6].” points: 6 rows: 5 explanation: “Cost is a constraint because any real-world solution must be financially feasible; an engineer cannot simply choose the best-performing material if it makes the project prohibitively expensive for the utility or the public. The choice of Aluminum over Copper exemplifies a necessary trade-off[cite: 27]. Engineers accept a slightly higher power loss (a sacrifice in efficiency) in exchange for a significantly lower material cost, thereby ensuring the solution meets the economic constraints of the project.”
