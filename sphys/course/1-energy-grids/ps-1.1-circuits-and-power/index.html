  # ————————- TIER 3: MASTER ——————————-   - problem_number: 9     tier:       level: 3       title: “The Master’s Insight: Connecting to the Phenomenon”     phenomenon_connection: “During the Texas power crisis, a primary cause of increased demand was the use of electric heaters to combat the cold. This problem models the impact of a single such device.”     problem_statement: “A common portable electric space heater is rated at 1500. W. a) Calculate the current a single heater draws from a 120. V outlet. b) Explain why millions of homes simultaneously drawing this extra current would strain an electrical grid, connecting to the Texas blackout.”     hints: []     answer_format:       - part: “a”         type: “numeric_with_units”         units: [“A”]       - part: “b”         type: “essay”         rows: 6     solution:       givens:         - “$P = 1500. \, \text{W}$”         - “$V = 120. \, \text{V}$”       unknowns:         - “$I = ?$”         - “Conceptual explanation.”       steps:         - “— Part (a): Calculate current —”         - “1. Select Parent Equation: $P = IV$.”         - “2. Algebraic Rearrangement: $I = \frac{P}{V}$.”         - “3. Substitute Values:”         - |           \(I = \\frac{1500. \\, \\text{W}}{120. \\, \\text{V}} = 12.5 \\, \\text{A}\)         - “— Part (b): Explanation —”         - |           A single heater drawing 12.5 A is a very large load, close to the 15 A limit of a typical household circuit. When millions of homes turn on devices like this simultaneously, the total current drawn from the grid increases enormously. This massive surge in current has several effects:           1.  Overloads Transformers: Local and regional transformers have thermal limits. The immense current heats them up ($P_{loss} = I^2R$), risking damage or failure.           2.  Strains Transmission Lines: Similarly, the high-voltage lines that carry power across the state heat up and lose a significant amount of energy as heat, reducing the grid’s efficiency.           3.  Exceeds Generation Capacity: Most importantly, the total power demand ($P_{total} = \sum P_{individual}$) can exceed the power plants’ maximum ability to generate it, especially if some plants fail due to weather, as happened in Texas. This supply-demand imbalance forces operators to implement rolling blackouts to prevent a total grid collapse.       final_answer: “The heater draws 12.5 A. This large individual load, multiplied by millions of households, creates a massive surge in demand that can overload grid components and exceed the total power generation capacity, leading to systemic failures.”

  - problem_number: 11     tier:       level: 3       title: “The Master’s Insight: Circuit Design and Safety”     phenomenon_connection: “Household circuits are protected by circuit breakers (or fuses) that prevent excessive current, which can cause fires.
