LAB OVERVIEW

——————————————————————————

overview: abstract: | This investigation explores one of the biggest puzzles in modern cosmology: dark matter. Students will analyze a “galaxy rotation curve,” comparing the expected velocity of stars (based on visible matter) with their observed velocity. The clear discrepancy provides the primary evidence for dark matter.

introduction: | When we observe how galaxies spin, we find a major puzzle. The stars on the outer edges are moving so fast that the gravity from the visible matter (stars, gas, and dust) shouldn’t be enough to hold them in orbit. They should fly off into space. There must be something else there—something we can’t see. This is the primary evidence for dark matter.

background_context: | Before starting this lab, you should understand: - Galaxy Rotation Curve: A graph that plots the orbital speed of stars (y-axis) against their distance from the galaxy’s center (x-axis). - Expected Curve (from Visible Matter): Based on Newton’s law of gravitation and counting only the matter we can see, scientists build a model that predicts star speeds. This model predicts that speeds should decrease at large distances. - Observed Curve (from Measurements): The actual speeds of stars, measured using redshift. Astronomers observed that these speeds do not decrease; they stay flat at high values.

SYNTHESIS: CLAIM-EVIDENCE-REASONING

——————————————————————————

synthesis: research_question: | How does this lab demonstrate the scientific process of identifying a conflict between a model (the expected curve) and observation (the observed curve)? How does the concept of dark matter represent a modification to our System Model of a galaxy to account for this new evidence?

claim_prompt: | Claim: Based on the research question, write a single, clear, and specific sentence that states your main conclusion.

**Example Format:** The observed rotation curve of a galaxy conflicts 
with the model based on visible matter, which is evidence that our 
**System Model** of a galaxy is incomplete and must be modified to 
include a new component: dark matter.

evidence_prompt: | Evidence: Provide a bulleted list of specific, quantitative data points from your investigation that support your claim.

- (From Graph/Table) The `ExpectedVelocity_km_s` (our model) peaked 
  at 160 km/s (at 6 kpc) and then *decreased* to 95 km/s by 16 kpc.
- (From Graph/Table) The `ObservedVelocity_km_s` (our observation) 
  *did not decrease*, but instead flattened out, reaching 230 km/s 
  at 16 kpc.
- (From Graph) The discrepancy (the conflict) between the model and 
  the observation is largest at the outer edges of the galaxy. At 
  16 kpc, the difference is $230 - 95 = 135 \, \text{km/s}$.

reasoning_prompt: | Reasoning: In a paragraph, explain the scientific principles that connect your evidence to your claim. Why is this evidence sufficient to support your conclusion?

**Guidance:** Your reasoning should:
- Reference the **Cross-Cutting Concept of 'Systems and System Models'**.
- Explain that the `Expected` curve *is* our 'System Model' based on 
  visible matter and the laws of gravity.
- Explain that the `Observed` curve is the real-world data.
- The *conflict* between the two (the evidence) proves our initial 
  model is wrong or incomplete. The observed high velocities require 
  *more gravitational force* than the visible matter can provide.
- Therefore, the scientific process requires us to *modify the model*. 
  The concept of "dark matter" is this modification. We add an 
  invisible, massive halo to our **System Model** to provide the 
  "missing" gravity needed to make the model's prediction match 
  the observation (the flat curve).
