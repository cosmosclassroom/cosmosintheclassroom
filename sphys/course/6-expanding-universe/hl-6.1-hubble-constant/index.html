Overview

Abstract

This investigation uses a modern dataset of distant galaxies to recreate Edwin Hubble’s landmark discovery. Students will plot the recessional velocity of galaxies against their distance, graphically deriving Hubble’s Law ($v = H_0 d$) and finding a value for the Hubble Constant.

Introduction

In the 1920s, Edwin Hubble made a discovery that changed our understanding of the universe forever. By plotting the velocity of galaxies against their distance, he uncovered a simple, shocking pattern. This pattern is the primary piece of evidence for the expansion of the universe and the Big Bang theory. In this lab, you will recreate his discovery using a modern dataset.

Investigation 1: Deriving Hubble's Law

Objective: Analyze a dataset of galaxies to graphically derive the mathematical relationship between a galaxy’s distance and its speed (Hubble’s Law).

Data Source: Provided dataset of galaxies. The velocity has been calculated for you from redshift data.

Procedure:

  1. Review the “Galaxy Distance and Velocity Data” (Table 1).
  2. Create a graph in your log with RecessionalVelocity_km_s on the y-axis and Distance_Mpc on the x-axis.
  3. Plot all the data points from Table 1.
  4. Draw a single best-fit line that goes through the origin (0,0) and passes as close as possible to all the data points.
  5. Use your best-fit line to answer the analysis questions.

Question 1:

After plotting your data, what is the shape of the graph? What does this shape imply about the relationship between a galaxy’s distance and its velocity?

Answer this question in your lab notebook.

Question 2:

Calculate the slope of your best-fit line ($\text{slope} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$). This value is your estimate of the Hubble Constant ($H_0$). What are the units of your slope?

Answer this question in your lab notebook.

Question 3:

Write the equation for your line in the form $y = mx$. Now, replace $y$ with $v$ (velocity) and $x$ with $d$ (distance), and $m$ with your calculated $H_0$. You have just derived Hubble’s Law.

Answer this question in your lab notebook.
