Problem

This problem quantifies the energy of the Chelyabinsk meteor, explaining why it exploded with the force of a nuclear weapon.

Let’s calculate the kinetic energy of the Chelyabinsk meteor just before it entered the atmosphere.

Given:

  • Estimated mass ($m$): $1.0 \times 10^7 \, \text{kg}$ (10,000 tonnes)
  • Estimated velocity ($v$): $1.9 \times 10^4 \, \text{m/s}$ (19 km/s)

Find: Kinetic Energy ($KE$) in Joules

📝 Solution Workspace

Show all work and include units in your final answer.

Complete this problem in your notebook or on separate paper.

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

  • $m = 1.0 \times 10^7 \, \text{kg}$
  • $v = 1.9 \times 10^4 \, \text{m/s}$
  • Unknown: $KE = ?$

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

\[KE = \frac{1}{2} mv^2\]

Step 3: Substitute Values

\[KE = 0.5 \times (1.0 \times 10^7 \, \text{kg}) \times (1.9 \times 10^4 \, \text{m/s})^2\] \[KE = 0.5 \times (1.0 \times 10^7) \times (3.61 \times 10^8)\] \[KE = 1.805 \times 10^{15} \, \text{J}\]

Final Answer: The meteor’s kinetic energy was $1.8 \times 10^{15} \, \text{J}$. (This is $\approx 430$ kilotons of TNT, about 30 times the Hiroshima bomb).

Problem

When assessing NEO risks, is it more important for an asteroid to be massive or to be fast? This problem explores the $v^2$ relationship.

You are analyzing data for two Near-Earth Objects (NEOs).

  • Asteroid A: $m_A = 1.0 \times 10^6 \, \text{kg}$, $v_A = 1.0 \times 10^4 \, \text{m/s}$
  • Asteroid B: $m_B = 1.0 \times 10^4 \, \text{kg}$, $v_B = 1.5 \times 10^5 \, \text{m/s}$

Which asteroid has more kinetic energy, and is therefore more dangerous?

Find: $KE_A$ and $KE_B$

📝 Solution Workspace

Show all work and include units in your final answer.

Complete this problem in your notebook or on separate paper.

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Calculate $KE_A$

\[KE_A = \frac{1}{2} m_A v_A^2 = 0.5 \times (1.0 \times 10^6 \, \text{kg}) \times (1.0 \times 10^4 \, \text{m/s})^2\] \[KE_A = 0.5 \times (1.0 \times 10^6) \times (1.0 \times 10^8) = 5.0 \times 10^{13} \, \text{J}\]

Step 2: Calculate $KE_B$

\[KE_B = \frac{1}{2} m_B v_B^2 = 0.5 \times (1.0 \times 10^4 \, \text{kg}) \times (1.5 \times 10^5 \, \text{m/s})^2\] \[KE_B = 0.5 \times (1.0 \times 10^4) \times (2.25 \times 10^{10}) = 1.125 \times 10^{14} \, \text{J}\]

Step 3: Compare

$KE_A = 5.0 \times 10^{13} \, \text{J}$ $KE_B = 1.125 \times 10^{14} \, \text{J}$ (which is $11.25 \times 10^{13} \, \text{J}$)

Final Answer: Asteroid B is more dangerous. Even though it is 100 times less massive, its 15-times-higher velocity is squared, making its kinetic energy $2.25$ times greater than Asteroid A’s.

Problem

This problem models the final "plunge" of a meteor fragment, converting its gravitational potential energy into kinetic energy.

A meteor fragment (mass $m$) is stopped by air resistance at a very high altitude $h = 50,000 \, \text{m}$. From this point, it is momentarily at rest ($v_i = 0$) before it begins to fall straight down.

Ignoring air resistance for this final part of its fall, what is its impact velocity ($v_f$) when it hits the ground ($h=0$)?

Hint: The fragment’s initial potential energy is converted into final kinetic energy.

Given:

  • $PE_{initial} = mgh$
  • $KE_{final} = \frac{1}{2} mv_f^2$
  • $h = 5.0 \times 10^4 \, \text{m}$
  • $g = 9.80 \, \text{m/s}^2$

Find: $v_f$

📝 Solution Workspace

Show all work and include units in your final answer.

Complete this problem in your notebook or on separate paper.

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Principle We will use the Law of Conservation of Energy. The initial energy (all potential) equals the final energy (all kinetic).

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

\(PE_{initial} = KE_{final}\) \(mgh = \frac{1}{2} m v_f^2\)

Step 3: Algebraic Rearrangement

Notice that the mass $m$ of the fragment cancels out from both sides:

\[gh = \frac{1}{2} v_f^2\]

Solve for $v_f$:

\(v_f^2 = 2gh\) \(v_f = \sqrt{2gh}\)

Step 4: Substitute Values

\[v_f = \sqrt{2 \times (9.80 \, \text{m/s}^2) \times (5.0 \times 10^4 \, \text{m})}\] \[v_f = \sqrt{980,000}\] \[v_f = 989.9... \, \text{m/s}\]

Final Answer: The fragment’s impact velocity would be $990 \, \text{m/s}$ (or $9.90 \times 10^2 \, \text{m/s}$). (This is its “terminal velocity” without air resistance).
