Overview

Abstract

This investigation uses a dataset of orbital data for planets in our solar system. Students will manipulate and graph this data to recreate one of the most profound discoveries in astronomy: Kepler’s Third Law. They will discover the simple mathematical pattern (T² ∝ R³) that governs all orbiting bodies.

Introduction

Long before Newton explained why planets move, Johannes Kepler spent decades meticulously analyzing observational data to discover how they move. He found a simple, shocking pattern hidden in the complex motions of the planets. Your task is to follow in his footsteps. You will use a provided dataset to analyze the relationship between how long a planet’s year is (its period) and how far it is from the Sun (its radius).

Investigation 1: Finding the Pattern in the Planets

Objective: Analyze a dataset of planetary orbits to derive the mathematical relationship between a planet’s orbital period (T) and its distance from the Sun (R).

Data Source: Provided orbital data for our solar system.

Procedure:

  1. Review the “Planetary Orbital Data” (Table 1).
  2. In your log, create a new table (Table 2) with five columns: ObjectName, OrbitalRadius_AU (R), OrbitalPeriod_years (T), R_cubed (R³), and T_squared (T²).
  3. For each object in Table 1, calculate the values for $R³$ and $T²$ and record them in your new Table 2.
  4. Create a graph with T_squared (T²) on the y-axis and R_cubed (R³) on the x-axis.
  5. Plot the data for all 8 planets from your Table 2.
  6. Draw a single best-fit line through your 8 data points.
