Overview

Abstract

In this investigation, you will follow in the footsteps of the great astronomer Johannes Kepler. By analyzing a dataset containing the orbital periods and distances for various planets, you will manipulate the data and create a graph to discover the fundamental mathematical relationship that governs all orbits—Kepler’s Third Law.

Introduction

Before Newton, Johannes Kepler spent decades analyzing the meticulous astronomical observations of his predecessor, Tycho Brahe. He was searching for a hidden pattern in the seemingly complex motions of the planets. He believed the cosmos was governed by an underlying mathematical harmony. He was right. Your task is to use a modern dataset to recreate his most profound discovery, revealing the simple, elegant rule that keeps our solar system in order.

Connection to Unit Phenomenon

The Chelyabinsk meteor, like every other object orbiting the sun, obeyed Kepler’s Laws for its entire existence. Its orbit was a predictable, elliptical path defined by the same mathematical rules you have just derived. Astronomers use these very laws to model the paths of all known Near-Earth Objects. By calculating an object’s orbit, they can predict its position years or even decades into the future to determine if its predictable path will ever intersect with Earth’s. This lab reveals the underlying order that makes such predictions possible, turning the chaos of the cosmos into a system of understandable clockwork.
