Overview

Abstract

In this investigation, you will act as a planetary defense scientist tasked with assessing the threat level of several newly discovered Near-Earth Objects (NEOs). By analyzing a dataset of their estimated size, velocity, and proximity to Earth’s orbit, you will identify the objects of greatest concern and develop a preliminary model for quantifying impact risk.

Introduction

Astronomers are constantly monitoring the skies, cataloging thousands of asteroids and comets whose orbits bring them close to Earth. While the chances of a major impact are small, the consequences could be catastrophic, as the Chelyabinsk event demonstrated. An effective defense strategy begins with good data. Your task is to analyze the initial orbital data for a set of NEOs and determine which ones warrant a closer look. [Image of an asteroid in space]

Connection to Unit Phenomenon

The Chelyabinsk meteor was a perfect example of why this work is critical. It was a relatively small object (only about 0.02 km in diameter) and was not detected before it entered the atmosphere. While it was not large enough to cause a crater, its high velocity gave it enough kinetic energy to create a massive atmospheric explosion, injuring over 1,500 people. This lab demonstrates the process that astronomers use to identify potentially hazardous objects with enough lead time to develop a response, ensuring that a future Chelyabinsk-like event does not come as a surprise.
