Problem

This problem has you calculate the fundamental force that kept the Chelyabinsk meteor on its orbital path before it encountered Earth.

Calculate the magnitude of the gravitational force ($F_g$) between the Sun and the Earth.

Given:

  • Mass of Sun ($m_1$): $1.99 \times 10^{30} \, \text{kg}$
  • Mass of Earth ($m_2$): $5.97 \times 10^{24} \, \text{kg}$
  • Distance $r$ (Sun to Earth): $1.50 \times 10^{11} \, \text{m}$

Find: $F_g$

Complete this problem in your notebook or on separate paper.

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

  • $G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11} \, \text{N} \cdot \text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$
  • $m_1 = 1.99 \times 10^{30} \, \text{kg}$
  • $m_2 = 5.97 \times 10^{24} \, \text{kg}$
  • $r = 1.50 \times 10^{11} \, \text{m}$
  • Unknown: $F_g = ?$

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

\[F_g = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}\]

Step 3: Substitute Values

\[F_g = (6.67 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{(1.99 \times 10^{30})(5.97 \times 10^{24})}{(1.50 \times 10^{11})^2}\] \[F_g = (6.67 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{1.19 \times 10^{55}}{2.25 \times 10^{22}}\] \[F_g = (6.67 \times 10^{-11}) (5.28 \times 10^{32})\] \[F_g = 3.52 \times 10^{22} \, \text{N}\]

Final Answer: The gravitational force between the Sun and Earth is $3.52 \times 10^{22} \, \text{N}$.

Problem

An asteroid's path is highly sensitive to distance. This problem explores how the force pulling on it changes dramatically as its distance from the Sun or a planet changes. This is key to understanding "gravity assists".

An asteroid experiences a gravitational force of $F = 1000 \, \text{N}$ when it is at a distance $r$ from a planet.

What will the new gravitational force be if the asteroid’s distance from the planet triples (i.e., its new distance is $3r$)?

Hint: You don’t need the masses or the value of $r$. Set up a ratio to see how the force scales.

Given:

  • $F_{initial} = 1000 \, \text{N}$ at $r_{initial} = r$
  • $r_{final} = 3r$

Find: $F_{final}$

📝 Solution Workspace

Show all work and include units in your final answer.

Complete this problem in your notebook or on separate paper.

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Principle The force of gravity is inversely proportional to the square of the distance ($F \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$). This is the Inverse Square Law.

Step 2: Set up a Proportionality

\[F_{final} \propto \frac{1}{(r_{final})^2}\]

Step 3: Substitute the New Distance

Since $r_{final} = 3r$:

\[F_{final} \propto \frac{1}{(3r)^2} = \frac{1}{9r^2}\]

Step 4: Compare to Initial Force

\[F_{final} = \frac{1}{9} \left( \frac{1}{r^2} \right)\]

Since $F_{initial} \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$, the new force is $\frac{1}{9}$ of the original force.

\[F_{final} = \frac{1}{9} F_{initial} = \frac{1}{9} (1000 \, \text{N}) = 111.1... \, \text{N}\]

Final Answer: The new force is $111 \, \text{N}$. Tripling the distance reduces the force by a factor of $3^2$, or 9.

Problem

The Chelyabinsk meteor was pulled in by Earth's gravitational field. But what is the field strength ($g$) on other bodies, like Mars, that we also monitor for impacts?

Calculate the gravitational field strength ($g$), also known as the acceleration due to gravity, on the surface of Mars.

Given:

  • Mass of Mars ($M$): $6.42 \times 10^{23} \, \text{kg}$
  • Radius of Mars ($r$): $3.39 \times 10^{6} \, \text{m}$

Find: $g$ on Mars

📝 Solution Workspace

Show all work and include units in your final answer.

Complete this problem in your notebook or on separate paper.

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Principle We need to combine the two key formulae. The force on an object (mass $m$) on the surface of Mars (mass $M$) is: $F_g = G \frac{M m}{r^2}$

The definition of gravitational field strength is: $g = \frac{F_g}{m}$

Step 2: Derive the Parent Equation Substitute the first equation into the second:

\[g = \frac{(G \frac{M m}{r^2})}{m}\]

The mass of the object $m$ cancels out:

\[g = G \frac{M}{r^2}\]

Step 3: Substitute Values

\[g = (6.67 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{(6.42 \times 10^{23})}{(3.39 \times 10^{6})^2}\] \[g = (6.67 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{(6.42 \times 10^{23})}{(1.15 \times 10^{13})}\] \[g = (6.67 \times 10^{-11}) (5.58 \times 10^{10})\] \[g = 3.72 \, \text{N/kg} \text{ (or m/s}^2\text{)}\]

Final Answer: The gravitational field strength on the surface of Mars is $3.72 \, \text{N/kg}$ (or $3.72 \, \text{m/s}^2$).
