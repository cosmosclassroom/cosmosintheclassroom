Problem 1
The Apprentice's Practice: Calculating Momentum

The danger of a vehicle in motion is best described by its momentum. This problem compares the 'unstoppability' of two different vehicles.

A 1,200. kg car is traveling East at 25.0 m/s. A 4,500. kg truck is traveling East at 10.0 m/s. Which vehicle has more momentum?

Given:

  • $m_{car} = 1,200. \, \text{kg}$, $\vec{v}_{car} = +25.0 \, \text{m/s}$
  • $m_{truck} = 4,500. \, \text{kg}$, $\vec{v}_{truck} = +10.0 \, \text{m/s}$

Find:

  • $\vec{p}{car} = ?$ , $\vec{p}{truck} = ?$

Solution:

    1. Select Parent Equation: $\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$
    1. Calculate Momentum for the Car:
  3. \[\\vec{p}_{car} = (1,200. \\, kg)(+25.0 \\, m/s) = +30,000 \\, kg \\cdot m/s\]
    1. Calculate Momentum for the Truck:
  5. \[\\vec{p}_{truck} = (4,500. \\, kg)(+10.0 \\, m/s) = +45,000 \\, kg \\cdot m/s\]

Final Answer:

The truck has more momentum (4.50e4 kg·m/s) than the car (3.00e4 kg·m/s).

Problem 2
The Apprentice's Practice: Perfectly Inelastic Collision

This models a common rear-end collision where the two vehicles become entangled and move as a single object after the crash.

A 1,500. kg car traveling at 20.0 m/s strikes a stationary 1,200. kg car from behind. The two cars lock together after the collision. What is their combined final velocity?

Solution:
Solution not available.
🔍 View Solution

Given:

  • $m_1 = 1,500. \, \text{kg}$, $\vec{v}_{1i} = +20.0 \, \text{m/s}$
  • $m_2 = 1,200. \, \text{kg}$, $\vec{v}_{2i} = 0 \, \text{m/s}$

Find:

  • $\vec{v}_f = ?$

Solution:

    1. Select Parent Equation: Conservation of momentum for a perfectly inelastic collision: $m_1\vec{v}{1i} + m_2\vec{v}{2i} = (m_1 + m_2)\vec{v}_f$.
    1. Algebraic Rearrangement: Solve for $\vec{v}_f$: \(\vec{v}_f = \frac{m_1\vec{v}_{1i} + m_2\vec{v}_{2i}}{m_1 + m_2}\)
    1. Substitute Values:
  4. \[\\vec{v}_f = \\frac{(1,500. \\, kg)(20.0 \\, m/s) + (1,200. \\, kg)(0 \\, m/s)}{1,500. \\, kg + 1,200. \\, kg}\]
  5. \[\\vec{v}_f = \\frac{30,000 \\, kg \\cdot m/s}{2,700. \\, kg} = +11.1 \\, m/s\]

Final Answer:

The combined final velocity of the two cars is +11.1 m/s.

Problem 3
The Journeyman's Task: Elastic Collision

This models a collision where vehicles bounce off each other. By measuring the final speed of one car, an investigator can calculate the final speed of the other.

A 2,000. kg SUV traveling at 15.0 m/s rear-ends a 1,000. kg compact car traveling in the same direction at 5.00 m/s. After the collision, the compact car is measured to be moving at 17.0 m/s. What is the final velocity of the SUV?

Given:

  • $m_1 = 2,000. \, \text{kg}$ (SUV), $\vec{v}_{1i} = +15.0 \, \text{m/s}$
  • $m_2 = 1,000. \, \text{kg}$ (Car), $\vec{v}{2i} = +5.00 \, \text{m/s}$, $\vec{v}{2f} = +17.0 \, \text{m/s}$

Find:

  • $\vec{v}_{1f} = ?$

Solution:

    1. Select Parent Equation: Conservation of momentum: $m_1\vec{v}{1i} + m_2\vec{v}{2i} = m_1\vec{v}{1f} + m_2\vec{v}{2f}$.
    1. Algebraic Rearrangement: Isolate the term $m_1\vec{v}{1f}$: $m_1\vec{v}{1f} = m_1\vec{v}{1i} + m_2\vec{v}{2i} - m_2\vec{v}_{2f}$. Then divide by $m_1$.
    1. Substitute Values:
  4. \[p_{initial} = (2000)(15) + (1000)(5) = 30000 + 5000 = 35000 \\, kg \\cdot m/s\]
  5. \[p_{final} = (2000)\\vec{v}_{1f} + (1000)(17) = 2000\\vec{v}_{1f} + 17000\]
    1. Set initial = final: $35000 = 2000\vec{v}_{1f} + 17000$.
  7. $18000 = 2000\vec{v}_{1f}$
  8. \[\vec{v}_{1f} = \frac{18000}{2000} = +9.00 \, m/s\]

Final Answer:

The final velocity of the SUV is +9.00 m/s.

Problem 4
The Master's Insight: 2D Collision at an Intersection

This models a 'T-bone' collision, a common and dangerous type of crash. Momentum is conserved independently in both the North-South and East-West directions, allowing investigators to reconstruct the event.

A 1,800. kg minivan traveling East at 20.0 m/s collides with a 1,400. kg sedan traveling North at 25.0 m/s. The vehicles lock together. What is the magnitude of their final velocity?

Solution:
Solution not available.
🔍 View Solution

Given:

  • $m_v = 1,800. \, \text{kg}$, $\vec{v}_{vi,x} = +20.0 \, \text{m/s}$
  • $m_s = 1,400. \, \text{kg}$, $\vec{v}_{si,y} = +25.0 \, \text{m/s}$
  • $M_{total} = 1800 + 1400 = 3,200. \, \text{kg}$

Find:

  • $ \vec{v}_f = ?$

Solution:

  1. Strategy: Conserve momentum in the x and y directions separately to find the components of the final velocity, then combine them using the Pythagorean theorem.
  2. — X-Direction (East-West) —
  3. $p_{ix} = m_v v_{vi,x} + m_s v_{si,x} = (1800)(20) + (1400)(0) = 36,000 \, kg \cdot m/s$.
  4. $p_{fx} = M_{total} v_{fx} = (3200)v_{fx}$.
  5. $p_{ix} = p_{fx} \implies 36000 = 3200 v_{fx} \implies v_{fx} = 11.25 \, m/s$.
  6. — Y-Direction (North-South) —
  7. $p_{iy} = m_v v_{vi,y} + m_s v_{si,y} = (1800)(0) + (1400)(25) = 35,000 \, kg \cdot m/s$.
  8. $p_{fy} = M_{total} v_{fy} = (3200)v_{fy}$.
  9. $p_{iy} = p_{fy} \implies 35000 = 3200 v_{fy} \implies v_{fy} = 10.94 \, m/s$.
  10. — Combine Components —
  11. $v_f = \sqrt{v_{fx}^2 + v_{fy}^2} = \sqrt{(11.25)^2 + (10.94)^2}$
  12. $v_f = \sqrt{126.56 + 119.68} = \sqrt{246.24} = 15.69 \, m/s$

Final Answer:

The magnitude of their final velocity is 15.7 m/s.
