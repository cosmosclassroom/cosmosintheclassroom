Problem

Before a driver reacts to a hazard, their car is often moving at a constant velocity. Understanding this simple case is the first step in analyzing a full collision event.

The position-time graph below shows the motion of a car. What is the car’s velocity?

(Note: A graph would be displayed here showing a straight line passing through the points (0.00 s, 10.0 m) and (5.00 s, 60.0 m).)

Given:

  • From graph: $\vec{x}_i = 10.0 \, \text{m}$ at $t_i = 0.00 \, \text{s}$
  • From graph: $\vec{x}_f = 60.0 \, \text{m}$ at $t_f = 5.00 \, \text{s}$

Find: $\vec{v} = ?$

Complete this problem in your notebook or on separate paper.

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Principle The velocity is the slope of the position-time graph.

Step 2: Select Parent Equation \(\vec{v} = \frac{\Delta \vec{x}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\vec{x}_f - \vec{x}_i}{t_f - t_i}\)

Step 3: Substitute Values \(\vec{v} = \frac{60.0 \, m - 10.0 \, m}{5.00 \, s - 0.00 \, s} = \frac{50.0 \, m}{5.00 \, s}\)

\[\vec{v} = +10.0 \, m/s\]

Final Answer: The car’s velocity is +10.0 m/s.

Problem

This graph represents the full story of a driver perceiving a hazard, braking, and coming to a stop.

The velocity-time graph below shows a car’s motion.

(Note: A graph would be displayed here showing a horizontal line at v = +20.0 m/s from t = 0 to t = 2.0 s, followed by a straight line sloping down to v = 0 m/s at t = 5.0 s.)

a) What is the car’s acceleration from t=2.0 s to t=5.0 s? b) How far did the car travel during these 5.0 seconds (i.e., what is its total displacement)?

Given:

  • From graph (0-2s): $\vec{v} = +20.0 \, \text{m/s}$
  • From graph (2-5s): $\vec{v}_i = +20.0 \, \text{m/s}$, $\vec{v}_f = 0 \, \text{m/s}$

Find:

  • $\vec{a}$ for t=2-5s = ?
  • $\Delta \vec{x}$ for t=0-5s = ?
📝 Solution Workspace

Show all work and include units in your final answer.

Complete this problem in your notebook or on separate paper.

🔍 View Solution

— Part (a): Acceleration —

Step 1: Identify Principle Acceleration is the slope of the velocity-time graph.

Step 2: Select Parent Equation \(\vec{a} = \frac{\Delta \vec{v}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\vec{v}_f - \vec{v}_i}{t_f - t_i}\)

Step 3: Substitute Values (for the braking interval) \(\vec{a} = \frac{0 \, m/s - 20.0 \, m/s}{5.00 \, s - 2.00 \, s} = \frac{-20.0 \, m/s}{3.00 \, s} = -6.67 \, m/s^2\)

— Part (b): Total Displacement —

Step 1: Identify Principle Displacement is the total area under the velocity-time graph.

Step 2: Calculate Area of Rectangle (0-2s) Area = base $\times$ height = $(2.00 \, s)(20.0 \, m/s) = 40.0 \, m$.

Step 3: Calculate Area of Triangle (2-5s) Area = 1/2 $\times$ base $\times$ height = $0.5 \times (5.00 \, s - 2.00 \, s)(20.0 \, m/s) = 0.5 \times (3.00 \, s)(20.0 \, m/s) = 30.0 \, m$.

Step 4: Sum the areas $\Delta \vec{x}_{total} = 40.0 \, m + 30.0 \, m = 70.0 \, m$.

Final Answer: The car’s acceleration during braking is -6.67 m/s². It traveled a total distance of 70.0 m.

Problem

The puzzling rise in collisions may be linked to driver distraction. This problem quantifies how a small delay in reaction time dramatically increases the total stopping distance, making a collision more likely.

A driver traveling at 25.0 m/s sees a hazard. Their braking system can provide an acceleration of -5.00 m/s².

a) If the driver’s reaction time is 0.800 s, what is the total stopping distance (reaction distance + braking distance)? b) If the driver is distracted and their reaction time increases to 2.20 s, what is the new total stopping distance?

Given:

  • $\vec{v}_i = +25.0 \, \text{m/s}$
  • $\vec{a} = -5.00 \, \text{m/s}^2$
  • $t_{react, a} = 0.800 \, s$ ; $t_{react, b} = 2.20 \, s$

Find:

  • Total stopping distance for both cases.

Complete this problem in your notebook or on separate paper.

🔍 View Solution

Strategy: Total Distance = (Distance traveled during reaction) + (Distance traveled during braking).

Step 1: Calculate Reaction Distance ($d_{react}$) During reaction, velocity is constant. $\Delta\vec{x} = \vec{v}t$.

$d_{react, a} = (25.0 \, m/s)(0.800 \, s) = 20.0 \, m$.

$d_{react, b} = (25.0 \, m/s)(2.20 \, s) = 55.0 \, m$.

Step 2: Calculate Braking Distance ($d_{brake}$) Use the kinematic equation that relates distance, velocity, and acceleration: $\vec{v}_f^2 = \vec{v}_i^2 + 2\vec{a}\Delta\vec{x}$.

Rearrange for $\Delta\vec{x}$: $\Delta\vec{x} = \frac{\vec{v}_f^2 - \vec{v}_i^2}{2\vec{a}}$.

Substitute: $\Delta\vec{x} = \frac{(0)^2 - (25.0 \, m/s)^2}{2(-5.00 \, m/s^2)} = \frac{-625 \, m^2/s^2}{-10.0 \, m/s^2} = 62.5 \, m$. (This is the same for both parts).

Step 3: Calculate Total Stopping Distance

$d_{total, a} = d_{react, a} + d_{brake} = 20.0 \, m + 62.5 \, m = 82.5 \, m$.

$d_{total, b} = d_{react, b} + d_{brake} = 55.0 \, m + 62.5 \, m = 117.5 \, m$.

Final Answer: a) The total stopping distance for the attentive driver is 82.5 m. b) The total stopping distance for the distracted driver is 118 m (rounding to 3 sig figs). The 1.40-second delay added 35.0 meters to the stopping distance.
