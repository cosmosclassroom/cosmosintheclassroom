Overview

Abstract

In this investigation, you will act as a data scientist for a national transportation safety board. Your task is to analyze a dataset of U.S. traffic incidents from the last few decades. By visualizing and interpreting this data, you will uncover a surprising and puzzling paradox: vehicle fatalities are decreasing, while the total number of collisions and injuries is increasing. This paradox will form the central question for our entire unit.

Introduction

Over the past 30 years, cars have become engineering marvels of safety, equipped with airbags, crumple zones, and advanced braking systems. We would expect our roads to be safer than ever. But is that the full story? Data often reveals complex patterns that challenge our assumptions. Your job is to analyze the historical data and determine what story it really tells about traffic safety in the modern era.

Connection to Unit Phenomenon

This lab is our anchoring phenomenon. You have discovered the central mystery for this unit: we are getting better at surviving crashes, but worse at avoiding them. This frames our two essential questions. First, what is the physics that makes safety features like airbags work so well? Second, what physics principles can help us understand and prevent collisions from happening in the first place? Answering these questions will require us to master the physics of motion, momentum, and impulse.
