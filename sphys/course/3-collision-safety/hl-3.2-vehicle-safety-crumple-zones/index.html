Overview

Abstract

This investigation uses data from simulated car crashes to explore the physics of crumple zones. Students will analyze data comparing a rigid car to a car with a crumple zone, focusing on the key variables of collision time and peak impact force.

Introduction

It seems counter-intuitive, but a car that crumples during a collision is much safer than one that stays rigid. This key safety feature, the “crumple zone,” is a direct application of the impulse-momentum theorem. Your task is to analyze data from two simulated crashes to discover the physics of why crumpling saves lives.

Investigation 1: Analyzing Simulated Crash Data

Objective: Analyze a dataset to determine how crumple zones are designed to increase collision time and reduce the force experienced by occupants.

Data Source: Provided dataset from two simulated crashes. Both vehicles have the same mass and the same initial velocity ($15.0 \text{ m/s}$) and are brought to a complete stop.
