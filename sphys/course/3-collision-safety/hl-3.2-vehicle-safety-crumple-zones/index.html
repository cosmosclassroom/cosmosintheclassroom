Overview

Abstract

In this investigation, you will analyze data from two simulated crash tests: one involving a vehicle with a rigid frame and another with a modern crumple zone. By comparing the peak impact force and the duration of the collision for each, you will discover the fundamental physics principle that allows crumple zones to protect occupants and save lives.

Introduction

It seems counterintuitive, but a car that is destroyed in a crash is often safer than one that remains intact. The mangled front end of a car after a collision is not a sign of failure; it is the sign of a critical safety system working perfectly. This system is the crumple zone. Its job is to absorb and dissipate the energy of a crash before it reaches the passenger compartment. Your task is to analyze crash test data to figure out exactly how it accomplishes this feat.

Connection to Unit Phenomenon

This lab provides the direct physical explanation for one half of our unit’s anchoring paradox. The reason traffic fatalities have decreased, as you saw in the first lab, is a direct result of this engineered application of the Impulse-Momentum Theorem. Crumple zones, along with airbags and seatbelts which also work by extending the time of impact, have made it possible to survive crashes that would have been fatal decades ago. This is a perfect example of how a deep understanding of a fundamental physics principle leads to engineering designs that save millions of lives.
