▶ Instructions

The law of conservation of momentum is a fundamental rule of the universe: in any collision, the total momentum before the crash must equal the total momentum after. This principle allows accident investigators to work backward and determine how fast vehicles were going before a crash. In this problem set, you will be the investigator, applying this law to solve for unknown velocities in various collision scenarios.

▶ Key Formulae
  • Momentum: \(\vec{p} = m\vec{v}\)
  • Impulse-Momentum Theorem: \(F_{net} \cdot \Delta t = \Delta \vec{p}\)
  • Conservation of Momentum: \(\Sigma \vec{p}_{initial} = \Sigma \vec{p}_{final}\)
  • \[m_1\vec{v}_{1i} + m_2\vec{v}_{2i} = m_1\vec{v}_{1f} + m_2\vec{v}_{2f}\]
▶ Standards for All Problems
  • Assume all collisions are ‘closed systems’ where external forces like friction are negligible during the brief impact.
  • Present all final answers with the correct units and to three significant figures.
