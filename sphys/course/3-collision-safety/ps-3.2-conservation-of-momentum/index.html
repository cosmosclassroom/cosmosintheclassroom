overview: follows_lecture: “Lecture 3.3: The Rules of Interaction” introduction: “The law of conservation of momentum is a fundamental rule of the universe: in any collision, the total momentum before the crash must equal the total momentum after. This principle allows accident investigators to work backward and determine how fast vehicles were going before a crash. In this problem set, you will be the investigator, applying this law to solve for unknown velocities in various collision scenarios.” key_formulae: - “Momentum: \(\\vec{p} = m\\vec{v}\)” - “Impulse-Momentum Theorem: \(F_{net} \\cdot \\Delta t = \\Delta \\vec{p}\)” - “Conservation of Momentum: \(\\Sigma \\vec{p}_{initial} = \\Sigma \\vec{p}_{final}\)” - “\(m_1\\vec{v}_{1i} + m_2\\vec{v}_{2i} = m_1\\vec{v}_{1f} + m_2\\vec{v}_{2f}\)” global_standards: - “Assume all collisions are ‘closed systems’ where external forces like friction are negligible during the brief impact.” - “Present all final answers with the correct units and to three significant figures.”

problems: # ————————- TIER 1: APPRENTICE —————————

  • problem_number: 1 tier: level: 1 title: “The Apprentice’s Practice: Calculating Momentum” phenomenon_connection: “The danger of a vehicle in motion is best described by its momentum. This problem compares the ‘unstoppability’ of two different vehicles.” problem_statement: “A 1,200. kg car is traveling East at 25.0 m/s. A 4,500. kg truck is traveling East at 10.0 m/s. Which vehicle has more momentum?” solution: givens: - “$m_{car} = 1,200. \, \text{kg}$, $\vec{v}{car} = +25.0 \, \text{m/s}$” - “$m{truck} = 4,500. \, \text{kg}$, $\vec{v}{truck} = +10.0 \, \text{m/s}$” unknowns: - “$\vec{p}{car} = ?$ , $\vec{p}_{truck} = ?$” steps: - “1. Select Parent Equation: $\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$” - “2. Calculate Momentum for the Car:” - | \(\\vec{p}_{car} = (1,200. \\, kg)(+25.0 \\, m/s) = +30,000 \\, kg \\cdot m/s\) - “3. Calculate Momentum for the Truck:” - | \(\\vec{p}_{truck} = (4,500. \\, kg)(+10.0 \\, m/s) = +45,000 \\, kg \\cdot m/s\) final_answer: “The truck has more momentum (4.50e4 kg·m/s) than the car (3.00e4 kg·m/s).”

  • problem_number: 2 tier: level: 1 title: “The Apprentice’s Practice: Perfectly Inelastic Collision” phenomenon_connection: “This models a common rear-end collision where the two vehicles become entangled and move as a single object after the crash.” problem_statement: “A 1,500. kg car traveling at 20.0 m/s strikes a stationary 1,200. kg car from behind. The two cars lock together after the collision. What is their combined final velocity?” solution: givens: - “$m_1 = 1,500. \, \text{kg}$, $\vec{v}{1i} = +20.0 \, \text{m/s}$” - “$m_2 = 1,200. \, \text{kg}$, $\vec{v}{2i} = 0 \, \text{m/s}$” unknowns: - “$\vec{v}f = ?$” steps: - “1. Select Parent Equation: Conservation of momentum for a perfectly inelastic collision: $m_1\vec{v}{1i} + m_2\vec{v}_{2i} = (m_1 + m_2)\vec{v}_f$.” - “2. Algebraic Rearrangement: Solve for $\vec{v}_f$: \(\\vec{v}_f = \\frac{m_1\\vec{v}_{1i} + m_2\\vec{v}_{2i}}{m_1 + m_2}\)” - “3. Substitute Values:” - | \(\\vec{v}_f = \\frac{(1,500. \\, kg)(20.0 \\, m/s) + (1,200. \\, kg)(0 \\, m/s)}{1,500. \\, kg + 1,200. \\, kg}\) - | \(\\vec{v}_f = \\frac{30,000 \\, kg \\cdot m/s}{2,700. \\, kg} = +11.1 \\, m/s\) final_answer: “The combined final velocity of the two cars is +11.1 m/s.”

# ————————- TIER 2: JOURNEYMAN —————————

  • problem_number: 3 tier: level: 2 title: “The Journeyman’s Task: Elastic Collision” phenomenon_connection: “This models a collision where vehicles bounce off each other. By measuring the final speed of one car, an investigator can calculate the final speed of the other.” problem_statement: “A 2,000. kg SUV traveling at 15.0 m/s rear-ends a 1,000. kg compact car traveling in the same direction at 5.00 m/s. After the collision, the compact car is measured to be moving at 17.0 m/s. What is the final velocity of the SUV?” solution: givens: - “$m_1 = 2,000. \, \text{kg}$ (SUV), $\vec{v}{1i} = +15.0 \, \text{m/s}$” - “$m_2 = 1,000. \, \text{kg}$ (Car), $\vec{v}{2i} = +5.00 \, \text{m/s}$, $\vec{v}{2f} = +17.0 \, \text{m/s}$” unknowns: - “$\vec{v}{1f} = ?$” steps: - “1. Select Parent Equation: Conservation of momentum: $m_1\vec{v}{1i} + m_2\vec{v}{2i} = m_1\vec{v}{1f} + m_2\vec{v}{2f}$.” - “2. Algebraic Rearrangement: Isolate the term $m_1\vec{v}{1f}$: $m_1\vec{v}{1f} = m_1\vec{v}{1i} + m_2\vec{v}{2i} - m_2\vec{v}{2f}$. Then divide by $m_1$.” - “3. Substitute Values:” - | \(p_{initial} = (2000)(15) + (1000)(5) = 30000 + 5000 = 35000 \\, kg \\cdot m/s\) - | \(p_{final} = (2000)\\vec{v}_{1f} + (1000)(17) = 2000\\vec{v}_{1f} + 17000\) - “4. Set initial = final: $35000 = 2000\vec{v}{1f} + 17000$.” - “$18000 = 2000\vec{v}_{1f}$” - “\(\\vec{v}_{1f} = \\frac{18000}{2000} = +9.00 \\, m/s\)” final_answer: “The final velocity of the SUV is +9.00 m/s.”

# ————————- TIER 3: MASTER ——————————-

  • problem_number: 4 tier: level: 3 title: “The Master’s Insight: 2D Collision at an Intersection” phenomenon_connection: “This models a ‘T-bone’ collision, a common and dangerous type of crash. Momentum is conserved independently in both the North-South and East-West directions, allowing investigators to reconstruct the event.” problem_statement: “A 1,800. kg minivan traveling East at 20.0 m/s collides with a 1,400. kg sedan traveling North at 25.0 m/s. The vehicles lock together. What is the magnitude of their final velocity?” diagram_placeholder: “” solution: givens: - “$m_v = 1,800. \, \text{kg}$, $\vec{v}{vi,x} = +20.0 \, \text{m/s}$” - “$m_s = 1,400. \, \text{kg}$, $\vec{v}{si,y} = +25.0 \, \text{m/s}$” - “$M_{total} = 1800 + 1400 = 3,200. \, \text{kg}$” unknowns: - “$|\vec{v}f| = ?$” steps: - “Strategy: Conserve momentum in the x and y directions separately to find the components of the final velocity, then combine them using the Pythagorean theorem.” - “— X-Direction (East-West) —” - “$p{ix} = m_v v_{vi,x} + m_s v_{si,x} = (1800)(20) + (1400)(0) = 36,000 \, kg \cdot m/s$.” - “$p_{fx} = M_{total} v_{fx} = (3200)v_{fx}$.” - “$p_{ix} = p_{fx} \implies 36000 = 3200 v_{fx} \implies v_{fx} = 11.25 \, m/s$.” - “— Y-Direction (North-South) —” - “$p_{iy} = m_v v_{vi,y} + m_s v_{si,y} = (1800)(0) + (1400)(25) = 35,000 \, kg \cdot m/s$.” - “$p_{fy} = M_{total} v_{fy} = (3200)v_{fy}$.” - “$p_{iy} = p_{fy} \implies 35000 = 3200 v_{fy} \implies v_{fy} = 10.94 \, m/s$.” - “— Combine Components —” - “$v_f = \sqrt{v_{fx}^2 + v_{fy}^2} = \sqrt{(11.25)^2 + (10.94)^2}$” - “$v_f = \sqrt{126.56 + 119.68} = \sqrt{246.24} = 15.69 \, m/s$” final_answer: “The magnitude of their final velocity is 15.7 m/s.”
