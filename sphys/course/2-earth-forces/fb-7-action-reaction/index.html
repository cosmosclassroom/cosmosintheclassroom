Chapter 7: The Earth Pushes Back: Action, Reaction, and the Forces Within

Central Question: How can the fluid motion of magma deep within the Earth exert enough force to tear solid rock at the surface? Narrative Arc: The rift wasn’t a one-way event; the Earth’s crust didn’t just move, it was pushed and pulled by forces from within. Here we explore the elegant symmetry of Newton’s Third Law—that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. We will journey deep into the planet’s interior to find the immense, balanced forces that hold a planet together, and the imbalances that can tear it apart.

7.1 The Symmetric Universe

Imagine a rocket, silent on its launchpad. To defy gravity and hurl itself into space, it must push. But what does it push against? The rocket’s engines blast hot gas downwards with incredible force. This is the action. In response, the universe provides a perfect and equal push in the opposite direction, propelling the rocket upwards. This is the reaction.

This is a profound insight. Forces never exist in isolation. They always come in pairs. You cannot push on a wall without the wall pushing back on you. To find the force that tore Africa apart, we must look for its equal and opposite partner.

When you walk, your feet push backward on the ground. What is the ‘reaction’ force that moves you forward?

A cannon fires a cannonball. The cannon exerts a force on the ball. What is the reaction force? Why does the cannon move much less than the cannonball? (Hint: Think about F=ma).

7.2 Newton's Third Law of Motion

This principle of paired forces was codified by Isaac Newton as his Third Law of Motion. It is often stated as: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. This means that if Object A exerts a force on Object B, then Object B simultaneously exerts a force on Object A that is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. It is critical to understand that these two forces act on different objects. They never cancel each other out because they are not acting on the same thing. The rocket pushes the gas, and the gas pushes the rocket. It is the force on the rocket that causes the rocket to accelerate.

7.3 The Engine of the Earth: Convection and Tension

The inside of the Earth is not a solid, static ball. The mantle, a thick layer of rock beneath the crust, churns with incredible slowness in a process called mantle convection. Think of it like a giant pot of thick soup simmering on a stove.

  1. Heating: Rock near the Earth’s hot core heats up, expands, and becomes less dense. 2. Rising: This hotter, less dense rock slowly rises. 3. Spreading & Cooling: When it reaches the underside of the rigid tectonic plates, it spreads out, transferring heat and beginning to cool. 4. Sinking: As it cools, the rock becomes denser and sinks back down toward the core.

This massive, cyclical movement is the engine of our planet. As the rising magma spreads, it drags on the bottom of the tectonic plates. This is the action: the mantle pushes and drags the plate sideways. According to Newton’s Third Law, the plate must exert an equal and opposite pull on the mantle—the reaction. The relentless drag of the mantle on the crust creates a pulling force within the plate itself, known as tension.

7.4 When the Earth Pulls Itself Apart

We can now see the whole system. The constant churning of the mantle creates a steady tensional force, trying to rip the African plate in two. For eons, this force has been in a balanced tug-of-war with the strength of the rock and the friction holding the plate together. The 2005 Afar Rift was a catastrophic moment of change. The tensional force, built up over millions of years, finally overwhelmed the forces holding the crust together. The action force (the mantle’s drag) became greater than the resisting forces, resulting in a sudden, unbalanced net force and the creation of a 35-mile-long wound in the Earth’s skin. We have found the culprit. The force did not come from the outside; it came from the symmetric push and pull of the Earth’s own internal engine.
