Overview

Abstract

In this investigation, you will act as a seismologist to locate the epicenter of a simulated earthquake. By analyzing the arrival times of P-waves and S-waves at three different monitoring stations, you will calculate the S-P time interval. You will then use a standard travel-time graph to convert these time intervals into distances, and finally, use the geometric method of triangulation to pinpoint the earthquake’s origin on a map.

Introduction

We cannot see inside the Earth, but we can listen to it. Earthquakes release enormous amounts of energy that travel through the planet as seismic waves. There are two main types of these waves: fast-moving P-waves (Primary) and slower S-waves (Secondary). Because they travel at different speeds, the time delay between their arrivals at a seismic station can tell us how far away the earthquake occurred. By combining data from at least three stations, we can find the exact location of the epicenter.

Connection to Unit Phenomenon

While the Afar Rift was caused by the slow pulling apart of tectonic plates (a tensional force), that process does not happen smoothly. It is accompanied by thousands of earthquakes. By using the triangulation method from this lab, seismologists can map the precise locations of these earthquakes over time. This mapping reveals the exact location and geometry of the developing fault line deep underground, allowing them to model the forces at play and predict how the rift might continue to evolve. This lab demonstrates the primary tool scientists use to ‘see’ the underground processes that shape our world.
