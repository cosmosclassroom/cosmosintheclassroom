Overview

Abstract

This investigation uses real-world data from global GPS stations to explore the motion of Earth’s tectonic plates. Students will analyze a data table of velocity vectors (speed and direction) to identify plate boundaries and evaluate the claim that the Atlantic Ocean is widening.

Introduction

The surface of the Earth is in constant, slow motion, but this motion is too slow to see with our eyes. By analyzing precise data from GPS stations across the globe, we can measure this motion and understand the forces shaping our planet. Your job is to act as a geologist, interpreting this data to see the “patterns” of a moving planet.

Investigation 1: Measuring a Moving Planet

Objective: Analyze global GPS data to determine the velocity of tectonic plates and identify the types of boundaries between them.

Data Source: Provided data table from a network of scientific GPS stations.
