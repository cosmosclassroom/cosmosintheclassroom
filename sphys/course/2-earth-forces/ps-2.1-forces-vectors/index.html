Problem

This problem models the forces at a convergent boundary, like the one creating the Andes mountains.

The Nazca plate is subducting under the South American plate. Two major forces act on the Nazca plate:

  • Slab Pull: A force of $3.0 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}$ pulling the plate east (downward into the mantle).
  • Ridge Push: A force of $1.5 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}$ pushing the plate east (from the mid-ocean ridge).

Forces on the Nazca Plate: 3.0×10¹³ N slab pull + 1.5×10¹³ N ridge push = 4.5×10¹³ N net force eastward

What is the net force ($\vec{F}_{net}$) acting on the plate in the east-west direction?

Given:

  • $\vec{F}_{pull} = +3.0 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}$ (East)
  • $\vec{F}_{push} = +1.5 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}$ (East)

Find: Net Force $\vec{F}_{net}$

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

  • $\vec{F}_1 = +3.0 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}$
  • $\vec{F}_2 = +1.5 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}$
  • Unknown: $\vec{F}_{net} = ?$

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

Since both forces are in the same dimension, we use 1D vector addition:

\[\vec{F}_{net} = \vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2\]

Step 3: Substitute Values

\[\vec{F}_{net} = (+3.0 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}) + (+1.5 \times 10^{13} \text{ N})\] \[\vec{F}_{net} = +4.5 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}\]

Final Answer: The net force on the plate is $4.5 \times 10^{13} \text{ N, East}$.

Problem

This models the motion of a plate (like the Pacific plate) that is interacting with two different plate boundaries at a perpendicular angle.

GPS data from the Hexagon Lab: Analyzing Plate Motion Data shows a plate moving North-East. Geologists model the forces on this plate as:

  • Force A: A push of $4.0 \times 10^{12} \text{ N}$ acting due North.
  • Force B: A pull of $3.0 \times 10^{12} \text{ N}$ acting due East.

Perpendicular forces: 4.0×10¹² N north + 3.0×10¹² N east = 5.0×10¹² N northeast (3-4-5 triangle)

Pythagorean theorem: √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5.0×10¹² N

What is the magnitude of the net force acting on the plate?

Given:

  • $\vec{F}_y = 4.0 \times 10^{12} \text{ N}$ (North)
  • $\vec{F}_x = 3.0 \times 10^{12} \text{ N}$ (East)

Find: Magnitude of $\vec{F}_{net}$

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

  • $\vec{F}_y = 4.0 \times 10^{12} \text{ N}$
  • $\vec{F}_x = 3.0 \times 10^{12} \text{ N}$
  • Unknown: $F_{net}$ (magnitude)

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

The forces are perpendicular, so we use the Pythagorean theorem for 2D vectors:

\[F_{net} = \sqrt{F_x^2 + F_y^2}\]

Step 3: Substitute Values

\[F_{net} = \sqrt{(3.0 \times 10^{12})^2 + (4.0 \times 10^{12})^2}\] \[F_{net} = \sqrt{(9.0 \times 10^{24}) + (16.0 \times 10^{24})}\] \[F_{net} = \sqrt{25.0 \times 10^{24}}\] \[F_{net} = 5.0 \times 10^{12} \text{ N}\]

Final Answer: The magnitude of the net force is $5.0 \times 10^{12} \text{ N}$. (This is a 3-4-5 right triangle!)

Problem

This problem models the work of a geophysicist. GPS data (like in Lab 2.1) shows the *effect* (the net force and motion), and known models (like slab pull) provide one *cause*. You must find the unknown *cause*.

Scientists observe a tectonic plate moving due West with a net force of $-5.0 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}$. They know there is a strong mantle drag force pulling the plate East (resisting the motion) with a magnitude of $+2.0 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}$.

Working backwards: Net force + known drag force = unknown force

Force balance analysis: Unknown force (7.0×10¹³ N west) + Drag (2.0×10¹³ N east) = Net (5.0×10¹³ N west)

There must be another force (like slab pull) acting on the plate. What is the magnitude and direction of this unknown force ($\vec{F}_{unknown}$)?

Given:

  • $\vec{F}_{net} = -5.0 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}$ (West)
  • $\vec{F}_{drag} = +2.0 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}$ (East)

Find: $\vec{F}_{unknown}$

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

  • $\vec{F}_{net} = -5.0 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}$
  • $\vec{F}_{drag} = +2.0 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}$
  • Unknown: $\vec{F}_{unknown} = ?$

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

The net force is the sum of all forces:

\[\vec{F}_{net} = \vec{F}_{drag} + \vec{F}_{unknown}\]

Step 3: Algebraic Rearrangement

We need to solve for $\vec{F}_{unknown}$:

\[\vec{F}_{unknown} = \vec{F}_{net} - \vec{F}_{drag}\]

Step 4: Substitute Values

\[\vec{F}_{unknown} = (-5.0 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}) - (+2.0 \times 10^{13} \text{ N})\] \[\vec{F}_{unknown} = -7.0 \times 10^{13} \text{ N}\]

Final Answer: The unknown force is $7.0 \times 10^{13} \text{ N, West}$. This strong westward pull (likely slab pull) overcomes the mantle drag to produce the observed net motion.
