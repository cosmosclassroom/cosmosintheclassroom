LAB OVERVIEW

========================================================

overview: abstract: | This investigation uses seismic wave data to probe Earth’s structure. Students will first act as seismologists, using P-wave and S-wave arrival times from three stations to triangulate the epicenter of an earthquake. They will then analyze the properties of these waves to make inferences about the hidden layers of Earth’s interior.

introduction: | We cannot see inside the Earth, but we can use the waves from earthquakes to “see” for us. An earthquake releases massive amounts of energy in the form of seismic waves that travel through the planet. By recording these waves at different locations, scientists can pinpoint the earthquake’s origin and even create a model of Earth’s core.

background_context: | Before starting this lab, you should understand: - P-waves (Primary): Longitudinal (compression) waves. They are fast and can travel through solids and liquids. - S-waves (Secondary): Transverse (shear) waves. They are slower than P-waves and can only travel through solids. - S-P Time Interval: The time difference between the arrival of the fast P-wave and the slower S-wave. The farther a station is from the epicenter, the larger this time interval will be.
