Scenario Description

A driver traveling at 20 m/s sees an obstacle ahead and must perform emergency braking. The scenario includes:

  • Phase 1 (0-1.5s): Reaction time - constant velocity at 20 m/s
  • Phase 2 (1.5-4.0s): Braking phase - decreasing velocity with constant deceleration of 8 m/s²

Key Analysis Points

Critical Positions

  • Position A (t=0s, x=0m): Driver sees obstacle
  • Position B (t=1.5s, x=30m): Driver begins braking
  • Position C (t=2.6s, x=42.1m): Car comes to complete stop

Distance Calculations

  • Reaction Distance: 30.0 meters (constant velocity phase)
  • Braking Distance: 12.1 meters (deceleration phase)
  • Total Stopping Distance: 42.1 meters

Physics Equations Used

Reaction Phase (0 ≤ t ≤ 1.5s):

  • Position: x = v₀t = 20t
  • Velocity: v = 20 m/s (constant)

Braking Phase (1.5s ≤ t ≤ 2.6s):

  • Position: x = x₀ + v₀(t-t₀) + ½a(t-t₀)²
  • Where: x₀ = 30m, v₀ = 20 m/s, a = -8 m/s², t₀ = 1.5s
  • Velocity: v = v₀ + a(t-t₀) = 20 - 8(t-1.5)

Stopped Phase (t ≥ 2.6s):

  • Position: x = 42.1m (constant)
  • Velocity: v = 0 m/s

Student Activities

Graphing Exercise

  1. Plot the 50 data points on a position-time graph
  2. Identify the three distinct phases of motion
  3. Calculate the slope of each phase to find velocity
  4. Analyze how the graph shape relates to the physics of the situation

Velocity Calculations

Students can calculate instantaneous velocity by finding the slope between consecutive points:

  • Slope = Δx/Δt = (x₂ - x₁)/(t₂ - t₁)

Real-World Connections

  • Compare stopping distances at different speeds
  • Analyze how reaction time affects total stopping distance
  • Discuss implications for safe following distances

Assessment Questions Based on Data

  1. What is the vehicle’s velocity during the reaction phase? Answer: 20 m/s (slope of line from t=0 to t=1.5s)

  2. How far does the car travel during reaction time? Answer: 30 meters (position at t=1.5s minus position at t=0s)

  3. What is the deceleration during braking? Answer: -8 m/s² (can be calculated from velocity change over time)

  4. At what time does the car come to a complete stop? Answer: t = 2.6 seconds

  5. What would happen to stopping distance if reaction time increased to 2.0 seconds? Answer: Total stopping distance would increase by 10 meters (additional 0.5s × 20 m/s)
