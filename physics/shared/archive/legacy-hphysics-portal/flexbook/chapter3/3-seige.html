Unit 3: The World in Two Dimensions

Phenomenon: The First Use of a Cannon at the Siege of Constantinople

Segment 1

20 min

Deconstructing the Cannon's Launch

A. Context: To predict a projectile's path, we must break its launch velocity into horizontal (vx) and vertical (viy) vector components.

B. Core Practice: A cannon fires at 120 m/s at a 35° angle.

  • Sketch the launch vector triangle.
  • Calculate the horizontal component (vx).
  • Calculate the initial vertical component (viy).

C. Synthesis (Systems): How does this vector component model simplify the analysis of a complex trajectory?

D. Metacognition: What would happen physically if you swapped sine and cosine in your calculations?

Segment 2

20 min

The Horizontal Journey

A. Context: Assuming no air resistance, the cannonball's horizontal motion is a simple, constant-velocity problem.

B. Core Practice: Using vx from Segment 1:

  • Find the horizontal distance after 1s.
  • Find the horizontal distance after 5s.
  • Does the cannonball cover the same horizontal distance each second?

C. Synthesis (Cause/Effect): What is the cause for the effect of constant horizontal velocity in our model?

D. Metacognition: Why can we use d = v*t for horizontal motion but not for vertical motion?

Segment 3

20 min

The Vertical Battle

A. Context: The cannonball's vertical motion is a battle against gravity, identical to an object thrown straight up.

B. Core Practice: Use viy and g = -9.8 m/s²:

  • Calculate the time to reach peak height (where vfy = 0).
  • Calculate the maximum height reached.

C. Synthesis (Patterns): Describe the symmetrical pattern of the cannonball's vertical velocity throughout its flight.

D. Metacognition: What does the negative sign on the acceleration of gravity physically represent?

Segment 4

20 min

Time of Flight & Range

A. Context: To find the cannonball's range, we must synchronize the horizontal and vertical motions using the total time of flight.

B. Core Practice: Assume the cannonball lands at the same height it was fired from.

  • Calculate the total time of flight.
  • Calculate the total horizontal range.
  • How would a higher launch angle affect time of flight and range?

C. Synthesis (Systems): How does "time of flight" connect the horizontal and vertical system models?

D. Metacognition: Write a plain-language recipe for finding a projectile's range from its initial speed and angle.

Segment 5

20 min

Synthesis: Engineering Report

A. Context: Ottoman engineers needed to hit the city walls. Your task is to analyze a specific launch to determine its effectiveness.

B. Core Practice: The walls are 950m away. Your cannon fires at 120 m/s at a 40° angle. Write a 3-5 sentence report including:

  • Calculated time of flight.
  • Calculated range.
  • Conclusion: Does it hit, fall short, or fly over?
  • Recommendation to adjust the angle.

C. Synthesis (Cause/Effect): Describe the cause-and-effect link between launch angle, time of flight, and range.

D. Metacognition: How does having a specific goal (hitting a target) change your problem-solving approach?

Segment 6

20 min

Metacognition & The Real World

A. Context: Our ideal physics model is a powerful tool, but the real world of 15th-century warfare was far more chaotic.

B. Core Practice: Conceptual Review

  • Explain why separating motion into H/V components is a useful model.
  • How would air resistance affect the actual range and height?
  • How would a tailwind change your horizontal motion calculations?

C. Synthesis (Systems): How does adding air resistance change our system model?

D. Metacognition: What underlying physical principle is the same for both a rocket and a cannonball's vertical motion?
