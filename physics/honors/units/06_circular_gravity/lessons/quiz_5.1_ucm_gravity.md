# Quiz | Uniform Circular Motion and Gravitation

#### Quiz - Spring 2025

I. Uniform Circular Motion Concepts (Multiple Choice - 2 points each)

The word "centripetal" describes a force that is directed:
(A) toward the center of a circle
(B) toward the radius of a circle
(C) away from the center of a circle
(D) there is no such force

While driving quickly around a sharp right turn, you find yourself pressed against the door of your car. Why is this so?
(A) Centrifugal force is pushing you into the door.
(B) The door is exerting a rightward force on you.
(C) Both of the above.
(D) Neither of the above.   

A stone attached to a string is swung in a vertical circle such that the speed it has at the top of the circle is just enough to keep it moving in a perfectly circular path. At the top of the circle, which force (or forces) is/are primarily responsible for maintaining the circular motion?
(A) The weight of the stone
(B) The tension in the string
(C) The weight of the stone and the tension in the string
(D) Air resistance   

A 12 g Ping-Pong ball is shot into a circular tube that is lying flat (horizontal) on a tabletop. When the Ping-Pong ball exits the tube at 4.1 m/s, which path will it follow?
(A) A path curving inwards.
(B) A path curving outwards.
(C) A straight path tangent to the circle at the exit point.
(D) A path directly opposite to the direction it was moving inside the tube.   

II. Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation Concepts (Short Answer - 3 points each)

Describe two ways to increase the gravitational force between two objects. Be specific about which properties of the objects are changing.

If the distance between the centers of two planets is tripled, how will the gravitational force between them change?

III. Combined Concepts & Quantitative Problems

(Short Answer - 4 points) Explain why a satellite, once launched into the correct orbit around the Earth, continues to orbit without constantly needing to burn fuel to stay in that orbit. Your explanation should connect the concepts of gravity and circular motion.

(Symbolic Answer - 4 points) A satellite of mass m orbits the Earth (mass M) at a distance r from the center of the Earth. Write an expression for the orbital speed (v) of the satellite, starting with the principle that the gravitational force provides the centripetal force. (Do not solve numerically, just provide the algebraic expression).

IV. Quantitative Problems (Show your work for full credit)

(Circular Motion - 6 points) An athlete whirls a 7.0 kg hammer in a circle with a radius of 1.80 m. If the hammer makes 1 revolution in 0.990 s:
(a) Calculate the centripetal acceleration of the hammer. (3 points)
(b) What is the tension in the chain (assuming the chain is horizontal)? (3 points)

(Newton's Law of Gravitation - 6 points) Calculate the gravitational force between two students sitting 1.5 meters apart. Assume each student has a mass of 70 kg. (The gravitational constant, G, is 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N⋅m²/kg²).

(Optional - Bonus - 3 points)

Planet X has twice the mass of Planet Y, and the distance between their centers is also twice as large as the distance between the centers of Planet A and Planet B (which have equal masses). How does the gravitational force between Planet X and Planet Y compare to the gravitational force between Planet A and Planet B? Explain your reasoning.
