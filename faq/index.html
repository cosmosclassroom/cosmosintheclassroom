Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers on what we do, how it helps, and how we start—designed to respect your time.

What is "bespoke AI"?

My mother was a seamstress. She taught me to appreciate the craft—the deep, specialized skill of it.

Fabric is a difficult medium. It's not like wood or stone. It must conform to a living body that moves and changes. A well-made garment must, all at once, keep its essential shape while also making deliberate room to flex.

A bespoke tailor understands this. They start with the fabric that matches the environment. They learn your ranges of motion. They start with you. They take your measure. They learn how you move. You speak for the fabric to be crafted to you.

This is exactly what we mean by "bespoke AI".

The large, public AI models (like ChatGPT, Claude, and others) are "off-the-shelf." They are like a one-size-fits-all suit. To make that suit, they have been "trained" on the entire internet—the good, the bad, the brilliant, the unhinged, the often-incorrect.

An off-the-shelf model wasn't built for your 9th-grade biology curriculum. It wasn't designed to align with your district's specific learning standards. Like that cheap suit, it's baggy in some places (pulling in irrelevant information) and dangerously tight in others (making up facts, or "hallucinating," to fill a gap). It just doesn't fit.

Our bespoke approach is the tailor's approach.

We don't start with the public internet. We start with you.

We take your measure: We begin with your curriculum, your source materials, your textbooks, your pedagogical standards and institutional wisdom.

We cut the fabric: We build a "walled garden" AI environment that is trained only on that vetted, trusted content. Its core universe of knowledge is the "cosmos" you define, and you don’t have to tell it each time.

We tailor the fit: The result is a tool that fits your team perfectly. It can't be distracted. It can be asked higher-order tasks because its knowledge is grounded in your knowledgebase.

It's an AI that keeps its shape—providing safe, consistent, and accurate support. But it also makes room to flex, allowing your teachers, curriculum teams, and administrators to ask "what if" questions, innovate on new lessons, or analyze policy... all within the safe, creative, and purposeful boundaries of your own institutional DNA.

What do you actually do? Are you selling software?

We are partners, not a platform. We don't sell a pre-made curriculum or a student login.

We are a consulting service that helps you codify your school's unique, hard-won institutional wisdom. We facilitate workshops to help your team capture its own expertise and build a private, staff-facing AI tool—a Custom Codex—that thinks like your best teachers and understands your curriculum.

Why not just use a generic, free AI tool?

A generic AI is a powerful calculator. Your Custom Codex is a colleague.

A generic tool doesn't know how your veteran teachers connect core concepts to their proven strategies. Your Codex does. Because it's trained exclusively on your curriculum and the "lived wisdom" we codify from your team, it handles the "machine work" (alignment, differentiation) so your teachers can focus on the "human work" of instruction.

How does this help my students?

In three ways:

  • Immediately: They get a better-rested, more creative teacher who has more time for the human work of instruction.
  • Systemically: They get a more consistent, thoughtfully scaffolded learning experience. When alignment is automated, students can focus their cognitive energy on the content, not on a confusing structure.
  • Strategically: They get a teacher who is actually AI literate. Teachers cannot teach AI literacy if they are not literate themselves. Our partnership is the seed a prudent district plants. It builds the groundwork for real AI literacy to enter your school—from the roots up.
Our Non-Negotiable Commitment: Privacy First

This is simple, and it's everything to us.

  • We never collect, store, or analyze student PII. Period. Our model is designed to avoid student data entirely. There is no student-facing portal.
  • We never evaluate teachers. Our process is designed to preserve expertise and reduce burnout, not to monitor or judge humans.
What does this process require from my team?

Our "Custom Codex Implementation" is a collaborative partnership. The most critical phase is Step 1: the "DNA Sequencing".

This isn't a passive interview. We facilitate hands-on "codification workshops" where we partner with your expert teachers to capture and structure their most effective strategies and "lived wisdom". Your team's time is the essential ingredient.

How can we start without committing to a massive project?

That's what The DNA Discovery Audit is for.

It's a focused, one-time project. We codify the "DNA" of a single domain (like a course or policy area), analyze its gaps and strengths, and deliver a comprehensive report and action plan. It's the perfect way to build a case for a larger initiative and see exactly how our partnership works.

What kinds of prompts can I actually ask my Custom Codex?

This is where things get exciting. Unlike generic AI, your Custom Codex knows your curriculum, pacing, standards, and teaching strategies.

Here are real examples—organized by role—of prompts you can only give your Custom Codex:

For Teachers: The "Course Sculptor" Codex

  • "Here is an old tried-and-true lab I used to do for 1-dimensional motion. Adapt this lab into our current curriculum. Draft 2 versions with pre-lab questions at the (a) intermediate and (b) advanced level for my 11th-grade physics students. Aim for 2 class periods in duration so we don't lose pace."
  • "I'm swamped. Generate three standards-aligned exit tickets for today's lesson on The Great Gatsby, making sure they check for understanding on 'motif' vs. 'theme' before the quiz on Friday."
  • "I'm new to teaching biology. How did a veteran colleague introduce the rainforest ecology unit? Generate a 3-day lesson skeleton based on their 'jungle school' stories, ensuring it hits our 9th-grade curriculum standards."
  • "I have three students with IEPs for reading comprehension and two ELL students (Spanish-speaking). Generate a 1-page resource summary, a bilingual vocabulary list, and 3 scaffolding questions for this week's Gatsby unit, all aligned with our 11th-grade ELA curriculum."

For Curriculum Teams: The "Cohesion & Creativity" Codex

  • "Show me the full K-12 scaffold for 'argumentative writing' as defined in our district's curriculum. Generate a report on all gaps between the 8th-grade ELA standards and the 9th-grade content."
  • "We are considering a new textbook for 11th-grade US History. Analyze its table of contents against our existing curriculum and generate an Integration Report, identifying gaps and redundancies."
  • "Where did the 7th-grade math team leave off on their vertical alignment work last spring? Summarize the last three unresolved issues from their changelog."

For Administrators: The "Mission-Anchored" Codex

  • "Analyze this draft of the new 'AI in the Classroom' policy against our district's core 'whole-child' mission statement. Identify three areas of misalignment and suggest revisions."
  • "I just finalized the new grading policy. Draft three versions of the announcement: one for principals (focused on implementation), one for teachers (focused on pedagogy and the curriculum), and one for the parent portal (focused on student impact)."
  • "Review all PLC notes regarding the new 'PBL' initiative. Summarize the main feedback themes and identify the biggest resource gaps or curriculum alignment challenges that teams are reporting."

For Student & Staff Support Teams: The "Consistency & Support" Codex

  • Counselor: "A new 10th-grade student is transferring in. Generate a 'quick-glance' summary of the 10th-grade curriculum for ELA, Math, and History so I can build their schedule and identify potential gaps from their old school."
  • Librarian: "The 9th-grade ELA team is starting Romeo and Juliet. Cross-reference our library's digital catalog with their course plan and generate a curated list of 10 supplemental resources (articles, videos, modern adaptations) that are already available."
  • Paraprofessional: "I'm supporting a student in 10th-grade World History. Give me a 1-page summary of the key concepts for this week's 'Industrial Revolution' unit, including the 5 key vocabulary terms the teacher will be using, based on the class curriculum."
