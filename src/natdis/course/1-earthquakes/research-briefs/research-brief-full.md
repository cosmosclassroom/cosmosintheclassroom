# Earthquakes Unit: Summative Briefs Overview

This document outlines the structure for the three summative briefs that conclude each chapter of the Earthquakes unit.

---

## Brief 1: The Scientist's Risk Report

**To be completed at the end of Chapter 1**

**Objective:** Synthesize data and scientific principles to formally assess the seismic risk to a specific community.

**Title:** Seismic Risk Assessment for [Local Town, e.g., East Haven, CT]

### Hazard Analysis

- **Geologic Setting:** Briefly explain the tectonic setting (e.g., plate boundaries, local fault lines like the Moodus Seismic Area).
- **Hazard Level (Magnitude):** Based on historical data, what is the likely magnitude? Assign a qualitative level: Low, Moderate, or High.
- **Expected Intensity:** Describe expected effects using the Mercalli Intensity Scale (e.g., "VI: Felt by all; many frightened. Some heavy furniture moved.").

### Vulnerability Assessment

- **Infrastructure:** Identify at least two specific vulnerabilities in the built environment (e.g., "Many older buildings are unreinforced brick," "A key bridge was built before modern seismic codes.").
- **Geographic Factors:** Identify one geographic vulnerability (e.g., "Many homes are built on soft, sandy soil near the coastline, which is prone to liquefaction.").

### Overall Risk Statement

- **Calculation:** Use the core formula:

	- `Risk = Hazard × Vulnerability`
	- [Calculated Risk Level] = [Hazard Level] × [Vulnerability Level]

- **Synthesis:** Write a 2–3 sentence summary statement that clearly articulates the overall risk, justifying your conclusion with specific hazards and vulnerabilities.

## Brief 2: The Engineer's Proposal

**To be completed at the end of Chapter 2**

**Objective:** Use experimental data from the shake-table lab to propose a specific engineering solution.

**Title:** An Engineering Proposal for Seismic Retrofitting

### Problem Statement

- Summarize the risk identified in the Scientist's Risk Report as an engineering challenge (1–2 sentences).

### Experimental Analysis (Hexagon Lab 1.2)

- **Initial Design:** Describe the performance of your first tower and why it failed (e.g., "The initial tower failed due to a lack of resistance to lateral forces, causing it to shear at the base.").
- **Redesigned Solution:** Describe the specific change made to your tower.
- **Results:** Present stability rating data, comparing the initial and redesigned towers. State which design was more resilient.

### Proposed Technical Solution

- **Principle:** Identify the specific engineering principle used (e.g., cross-bracing), referencing "Engineering Research Brief 1.2."
- **Mechanism:** Explain how the principle works (1–2 sentences).
- **Recommendation:** Based on lab data, recommend the city investigate the use of [Identified Principle] for reinforcing vulnerable structures.

## Brief 3: The Policymaker's Brief

**To be completed at the end of Chapter 3**

**Objective:** Analyze the engineering solution through a social and economic lens, weighing tradeoffs to propose a viable public policy.

**Title:** Policy Brief: A Recommendation on Seismic Safety Mandates

### Executive Summary

- Summarize the issue, proposed solution, and final recommendation (2–3 sentences; written last, placed first).

### Background: The Risk and the Solution

- Briefly summarize the conclusions of the Scientist's Risk Report and the Engineer's Proposal.

### Analysis of Tradeoffs

- **Costs:** Identify at least two costs (e.g., financial cost of retrofitting, economic disruption, political challenges).
- **Benefits:** Identify at least two benefits (e.g., lives saved, reduced damage, protection of critical infrastructure).
- **Equity Consideration:** Who bears the greatest burden? Who benefits most? Is the solution equitable for all?

### Policy Recommendation

- Choose and defend one specific, actionable policy:

	- **Option A:** Mandatory Retrofit Law (highest safety, highest cost)
	- **Option B:** Voluntary Incentive Program (e.g., tax breaks; lower cost, lower compliance)
	- **Option C:** Phased-In Approach (e.g., mandate for critical buildings now, incentives for others)

- **Justification:** Defend your chosen policy, explaining why its benefits outweigh its costs and how it addresses key tradeoffs (reference cost-benefit analysis).
