# Problem Set 1.1: Forces & Waves - Answer Key

## Part 1: Wave Calculations

**1.** A P-wave travels through rock at a constant speed of **6 km/s**. It takes the wave **30 s** to reach a city.  
*How far away was the earthquake's epicenter?*

**Solution:**
Using: Distance = Speed × Time

Distance = 6 km/s × 30 s = **180 km**

**Work:**
- Given: Speed = 6 km/s, Time = 30 s
- Distance = 6 × 30 = 180 km

---

**2.** A seismograph at a research station detects a P-wave arriving at **10:05:15 AM**. It detects the slower S-wave from the same earthquake arriving at **10:05:40 AM**.  
*What is the S−P interval in seconds?*

**Solution:**
Using: Δt<sub>S-P</sub> = t<sub>S-wave</sub> − t<sub>P-wave</sub>

Δt<sub>S-P</sub> = 10:05:40 − 10:05:15 = **25 seconds**

**Work:**
- P-wave arrival: 10:05:15 AM
- S-wave arrival: 10:05:40 AM  
- Time difference: 40 s − 15 s = 25 s

---

**3.** An earthquake happens **1,200 km** away from a seismic station. The P-wave from the quake travels at a constant speed of **8 km/s**.  
*How long will it take for the P-wave to reach the station?*

**Solution:**
Rearranging Distance = Speed × Time to solve for Time:
Time = Distance ÷ Speed

Time = 1,200 km ÷ 8 km/s = **150 seconds** (or 2 minutes 30 seconds)

**Work:**
- Given: Distance = 1,200 km, Speed = 8 km/s
- Time = 1,200 ÷ 8 = 150 s

## Part 2: Applying Magnitude and Intensity

**4.** Looking only at the **Magnitude** column, which earthquake was stronger (released more energy)?

**Answer:** **Quake Beta** (Magnitude 7.0)

*Explanation: Magnitude measures the actual energy released at the source. The higher the magnitude number, the stronger the earthquake.*

---

**5.** Based on the **Observed Effects**, which earthquake produced a higher **Intensity** where people lived?

**Answer:**
- **Quake Alpha Intensity:** High (VII-VIII) - widespread damage, cracked walls, 500 buildings damaged
- **Quake Beta Intensity:** Low (I-II) - barely felt, no damage  
- **Quake Gamma Intensity:** Moderate (IV-V) - felt by most, minor items fell, no structural damage

**Quake Alpha** produced the highest intensity in populated areas despite having a lower magnitude.

---

**6.** Explain your answer to the question above using the terms **Magnitude**, **Intensity**, and **Vulnerability**.

**Sample Answer:**
**Magnitude** measures the energy released at the earthquake's source, but **Intensity** measures the actual effects people experience. Quake Alpha had a lower magnitude than Quake Beta, but it occurred in a densely populated city with high **vulnerability** (many buildings and people). Quake Beta, despite its higher magnitude, occurred in a remote desert with low vulnerability (few people or structures), resulting in lower intensity effects where people actually lived.

## Common Student Errors to Watch For:

1. **Unit confusion** - mixing seconds with minutes in time calculations
2. **Formula rearrangement** - difficulty solving for time when given distance and speed
3. **Magnitude vs. Intensity confusion** - thinking bigger magnitude always means worse effects
4. **Missing the vulnerability concept** - not understanding why location matters for earthquake impacts
