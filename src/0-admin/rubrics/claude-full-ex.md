# Assessment Rubrics for Three-Course Physics Curriculum

## Overview
This document provides comprehensive assessment rubrics for Honors Physics, Standard Physics, and Natural Disasters courses, aligned with NGSS Science and Engineering Practices and adapted for each course's unique rigor level and student population.

## Course-Specific Adaptations

### Honors Physics
- Uses all four performance levels with emphasis on "Exceeds Expectations"
- Focuses on mathematical rigor, derivations, and advanced reasoning
- Expects independent research and sophisticated argumentation

### Standard Physics
- Primarily uses "Developing," "Approaching," and "Meets Expectations"
- Emphasizes conceptual understanding with appropriate mathematical support
- Includes scaffolding and collaborative learning indicators

### Natural Disasters
- Uses all levels but emphasizes civic engagement and geo-literacy
- Focuses on real-world applications and community connections
- Integrates multi-role perspectives (Scientist, Engineer, Policymaker)

## Research Brief Rubrics

### Honors Physics Research Brief Rubric

| Science Practice | Exceeds Expectations (4) | Meets Expectations (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|------------------|------------------------|----------------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Asking Questions** | Formulates sophisticated research questions that challenge existing models or push beyond standard curriculum, demonstrating deep scientific curiosity | Asks questions that arise from data analysis and require empirical evidence to answer, showing clear understanding of scientific inquiry | Asks questions that identify relationships between variables and can be investigated through controlled experiments | Asks basic testable questions about cause-and-effect relationships |
| **Developing Models** | Creates comprehensive models that synthesize multiple physics concepts and can predict outcomes under novel conditions | Develops and refines models that accurately represent complex physics phenomena and can generate testable predictions | Develops models that represent key physics relationships and can be used to test ideas about phenomena | Creates simple models that describe basic physics relationships |
| **Mathematical Thinking** | Applies advanced mathematical analysis, derives relationships from first principles, and uses computational methods to solve complex problems | Uses mathematical representations effectively to describe phenomena and solve multi-step problems with proper significant figures | Applies mathematical concepts correctly to solve physics problems with appropriate units and reasonable answers | Uses basic mathematical operations to solve simple physics problems |
| **Constructing Explanations** | Constructs sophisticated, evidence-based explanations that connect multiple physics principles and address potential counterarguments | Constructs clear explanations based on valid evidence, connecting cause and effect through established physics principles | Constructs explanations that include quantitative relationships and support claims with appropriate evidence | Constructs basic explanations using evidence to support simple claims |

### Standard Physics Research Brief Rubric

| Science Practice | Meets Expectations (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) | Needs Support (0) |
|------------------|----------------------|-----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Asking Questions** | Asks clear questions that can be investigated through lab activities and connect to the anchoring phenomenon | Asks questions that identify cause-and-effect relationships relevant to the unit | Asks basic questions about what happens when variables change | Questions are unclear or not related to physics concepts |
| **Using Evidence** | Uses specific data from labs and readings to support claims, citing sources appropriately | Uses some data from activities to support claims with minimal citation | Uses general observations from activities but lacks specific evidence | Makes claims without supporting evidence |
| **Making Connections** | Clearly connects physics concepts to real-world examples and personal experiences | Makes some connections between physics concepts and everyday situations | Makes basic connections between physics and familiar experiences | Struggles to connect physics to real-world situations |
| **Communication** | Writes clearly with appropriate physics vocabulary, proper grammar, and organized structure | Writes mostly clearly with some physics vocabulary and generally organized thoughts | Basic writing with some physics terms but may lack organization or clarity | Writing is unclear, lacks physics vocabulary, or is poorly organized |

### Natural Disasters Research Brief Rubric

| Role Integration | Exceeds Expectations (4) | Meets Expectations (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|------------------|------------------------|----------------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Scientist Report** | Synthesizes multiple data sources to create comprehensive hazard analysis with clear understanding of physical processes | Uses scientific data effectively to explain natural phenomena and assess local hazards | Explains basic scientific concepts behind natural hazards using provided data | Identifies basic facts about natural hazards with limited scientific reasoning |
| **Engineer Proposal** | Designs innovative, multi-faceted solutions that address complex trade-offs and optimize across multiple constraints | Proposes practical engineering solutions with clear consideration of criteria, constraints, and trade-offs | Suggests reasonable engineering solutions with some consideration of limitations | Proposes basic solutions with minimal consideration of practical constraints |
| **Policymaker Brief** | Develops sophisticated policy recommendations that balance multiple stakeholder perspectives and anticipate unintended consequences | Creates policy recommendations based on scientific and engineering evidence, considering community values and economic factors | Makes policy suggestions that consider some stakeholder perspectives and basic costs/benefits | Suggests simple policy ideas with limited consideration of broader impacts |
| **Geo-Literacy** | Demonstrates sophisticated understanding of interactions, interconnections, and implications across multiple scales | Shows clear understanding of how natural and human systems interact and affect each other | Identifies some connections between natural hazards and human communities | Recognizes basic relationships between natural events and human impacts |

## Hexagon Lab Rubrics

### Lab Investigation Process (All Courses)

| Component | Honors Physics | Standard Physics | Natural Disasters |
|-----------|----------------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Data Analysis** | Performs advanced statistical analysis, calculates uncertainties, and interprets complex datasets | Identifies clear patterns in data, makes appropriate calculations, and draws reasonable conclusions | Uses data to support claims about hazards and solutions, comparing different scenarios effectively |
| **Scientific Reasoning** | Connects observations to underlying physics principles through sophisticated reasoning chains | Explains observations using physics concepts with appropriate scientific vocabulary | Explains natural phenomena using scientific principles in accessible language |
| **Problem Solving** | Tackles complex, multi-step problems requiring synthesis of multiple physics concepts | Solves structured problems using appropriate formulas and showing clear work | Applies scientific understanding to real-world hazard scenarios and community problems |
| **Collaboration** | Leads collaborative efforts, teaches peers, and integrates diverse perspectives effectively | Participates actively in group work, shares ideas respectfully, and learns from others | Works effectively in role-based teams, considering multiple stakeholder perspectives |

### Lab Report Quality

| Element | Exceeds (4) | Meets (3) | Approaching (2) | Developing (1) |
|---------|-------------|-----------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Claim-Evidence-Reasoning** | Sophisticated claim with comprehensive evidence and nuanced reasoning connecting multiple physics principles | Clear claim supported by specific evidence with sound reasoning using appropriate physics concepts | Basic claim with some supporting evidence and simple reasoning | Unclear claim with minimal evidence or weak reasoning |
| **Data Presentation** | Uses multiple, sophisticated representations (graphs, diagrams, equations) with proper formatting and error analysis | Creates clear, accurate graphs and tables with proper labels, units, and formatting | Creates basic graphs or tables with mostly correct labels and units | Data presentation is unclear, inaccurate, or improperly formatted |
| **Synthesis** | Connects findings to broader physics principles and real-world applications with sophisticated insight | Relates findings to physics concepts and explains real-world connections | Makes basic connections between findings and physics concepts | Struggles to connect findings to broader concepts or applications |

## Problem Set Rubrics

### Mathematical Problem Solving

| Course Level | Problem Setup | Calculation Process | Final Answer | Conceptual Understanding |
|--------------|---------------|-------------------|--------------|-------------------------|
| **Honors Physics** | Sets up complex problems independently, chooses appropriate methods, shows mathematical reasoning | Shows all algebraic steps, applies calculus when appropriate, includes uncertainty analysis | Provides answer with correct significant figures, appropriate units, and error bounds | Explains physical meaning of results and connects to broader principles |
| **Standard Physics** | Sets up problems with minimal guidance, identifies given/unknown variables | Shows calculation steps clearly with appropriate formula substitution | Provides answer with reasonable significant figures and correct units | Explains what the answer means in physical terms |
| **Natural Disasters** | Identifies relevant information for real-world scenarios | Shows calculation steps for practical applications (risk assessment, cost-benefit analysis) | Provides answer that makes sense in the real-world context | Explains how the calculation relates to community decision-making |

### Problem Set Reflection Questions

| Quality | Exemplary Response | Proficient Response | Developing Response |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Learning Recognition** | Identifies specific concepts mastered and areas for growth with sophisticated self-awareness | Recognizes what was learned and what was challenging with clear examples | Shows basic awareness of learning and challenges |
| **Strategy Analysis** | Analyzes problem-solving strategies critically and suggests improvements for future learning | Explains problem-solving approach and identifies effective strategies | Describes basic problem-solving steps taken |
| **Real-World Connection** | Makes sophisticated connections between physics principles and complex real-world situations | Connects physics concepts to everyday experiences or current events | Makes simple connections between physics and familiar situations |

## Presentation and Communication Rubrics

### Final Portfolio Presentations

| Component | Natural Disasters Focus | Standard Physics Adaptation | Honors Physics Enhancement |
|-----------|------------------------|---------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Content Accuracy** | Accurately represents natural hazards and mitigation strategies with appropriate scientific vocabulary | Demonstrates solid understanding of physics concepts with mostly correct explanations | Shows deep understanding with sophisticated explanations and connections to advanced concepts |
| **Audience Awareness** | Effectively communicates to community stakeholders (city council simulation) using appropriate language and persuasive techniques | Presents clearly to classmates and teacher using appropriate scientific language | Presents sophisticated analysis appropriate for scientific audience |
| **Use of Evidence** | Integrates data from multiple sources to support policy recommendations | Uses lab data and calculations to support physics conclusions | Synthesizes complex data sets and research to support advanced conclusions |
| **Visual Design** | Creates clear, professional visual aids appropriate for civic presentation | Uses appropriate graphs, diagrams, and visual organizers to support explanations | Develops sophisticated visual representations including original modeling or analysis |

### Scientific Writing (Research Briefs and Lab Reports)

| Criterion | Exceeds Expectations | Meets Expectations | Approaching | Developing |
|-----------|---------------------|-------------------|-------------|------------|
| **Scientific Accuracy** | Uses scientific concepts precisely with sophisticated understanding of limitations and uncertainties | Uses scientific concepts accurately with clear understanding of key principles | Uses scientific concepts mostly correctly with basic understanding | Uses scientific concepts with some errors or misconceptions |
| **Evidence Integration** | Seamlessly integrates multiple types of evidence (quantitative, qualitative, observational) to build compelling arguments | Uses appropriate evidence effectively to support claims with clear citations | Uses some evidence to support claims but may lack variety or proper citation | Uses minimal evidence or evidence that doesn't clearly support claims |
| **Organization and Flow** | Creates sophisticated logical structure that guides reader through complex reasoning | Organizes ideas clearly with smooth transitions and logical progression | Generally well-organized with mostly clear connections between ideas | Basic organization with some unclear transitions or logical gaps |
| **Language and Style** | Uses precise scientific vocabulary appropriately while maintaining accessibility for intended audience | Uses scientific vocabulary correctly with clear, engaging writing style | Uses some scientific vocabulary with generally clear writing | Limited use of scientific vocabulary or unclear writing style |

## Self-Assessment and Reflection Rubrics

### Metacognitive Reflection

| Focus Area | Advanced Reflection | Proficient Reflection | Basic Reflection |
|------------|-------------------|---------------------|------------------|
| **Learning Process** | Analyzes learning strategies critically, identifies effective approaches, and plans specific improvements | Describes learning process clearly and identifies what worked well or was challenging | Basic description of what was learned or found difficult |
| **Conceptual Growth** | Traces sophisticated development of understanding, identifies misconceptions overcome, and connects to broader learning | Explains how understanding changed through the unit with specific examples | Shows some awareness of learning or changing understanding |
| **Application Transfer** | Identifies sophisticated connections between course content and other subjects, future goals, or complex real-world situations | Makes clear connections between course content and other areas of learning or personal interests | Makes basic connections between course content and familiar experiences |
| **Goal Setting** | Sets specific, ambitious learning goals with detailed action plans and success criteria | Sets clear learning goals with general plans for improvement | Sets basic goals for future learning |

### Collaborative Learning Assessment

| Skill | Exemplary | Proficient | Developing | Beginning |
|-------|-----------|------------|------------|-----------|
| **Contribution to Group Work** | Consistently provides valuable insights, helps organize group efforts, and ensures all voices are heard | Actively participates, shares ideas, and supports group goals | Participates somewhat but may need encouragement to contribute | Minimal participation or contribution to group efforts |
| **Peer Support** | Actively helps peers understand concepts, asks thoughtful questions, and provides constructive feedback | Offers help when appropriate and responds positively to others' ideas | Sometimes helps or supports peers but inconsistently | Rarely offers support or may be disruptive to others' learning |
| **Perspective Taking** | Seeks out and thoughtfully considers multiple viewpoints, especially in role-based activities | Shows awareness of different perspectives and can represent viewpoints other than their own | Limited awareness of different perspectives or difficulty stepping into other roles | Struggles to see beyond own perspective |

## Implementation Guidelines

### For Teachers
1. **Calibration**: Use exemplar student work to calibrate scoring across different teachers
2. **Feedback Timing**: Provide formative feedback throughout units, not just summative scores
3. **Student Involvement**: Include students in understanding rubric criteria and self-assessment
4. **Growth Focus**: Emphasize improvement over time rather than just final performance levels

### For Students
1. **Rubric Familiarity**: Review rubrics before starting major assignments
2. **Self-Assessment**: Use rubrics for self-evaluation before submitting work
3. **Peer Review**: Apply rubrics when providing feedback to classmates
4. **Goal Setting**: Use rubric levels to set specific learning targets

### For AI Content Generation
1. **Alignment**: Ensure all generated activities and assessments align with these performance expectations
2. **Scaffolding**: Build in supports to help students reach higher performance levels
3. **Differentiation**: Adapt expectations appropriately for each course level while maintaining rigor
4. **Evidence**: Provide clear opportunities for students to demonstrate each performance criterion

---

*These rubrics should be adapted based on specific unit content and learning objectives while maintaining consistency with the overall course goals and student population needs.*
