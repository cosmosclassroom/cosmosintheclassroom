Teaching & Learning Approach

This is a college-preparation course building a foundational understanding of classical physics through algebra.

Course Aims

  • Studentship: Ownership of learning (habits, organization, reflection)
  • Thinking: Analytical reasoning about natural phenomena
  • Analysis: Interpreting and evaluating data

Why Physics?

To foster inquiry into nature and develop durable problem-solving and logical reasoning skills.

Curriculum: Part I

Learning the Language of Nature

Guiding Question: How can we describe the world in a way that is true for everyone, everywhere?

Focus: Establish the “grammar” of science—measurement, observation, mathematical description of motion.

Part I Topics

  • Units & Measurement
  • 1D Kinematics
  • 2D Kinematics & Vectors

Curriculum: Part II

The Great Unification

Guiding Question: What universal laws connect a falling apple to the orbit of the moon?

Focus: Causes of motion and conservation laws as nature’s accounting principles.

Part II Topics

  • Newton’s Laws & Forces
  • Impulse & Momentum
  • Work, Energy & Power
  • Universal Gravitation

Curriculum: Part III

The Unseen World

Guiding Question: How do invisible forces and waves shape reality and technology?

Focus: Waves and fundamental interactions that structure matter & information transfer.

Part III Topics

  • Mechanical Waves & Sound
  • Optics & EM Spectrum
  • Electrostatics

Assessment & Grading

Grades use a total-points system (percentage → letter grade).

Point Ranges:

  • Classwork / Homework: 4–8 pts
  • Quiz: 15–20 pts
  • Test: ~50 pts
  • Exam: ~100 pts

Assessment Notes

  • No extra credit
  • Safety violations → removal & possible zero
  • Emphasis on process quality and scientific communication

Homework Expectations

Purpose: Practice physics + build studentship habits.

  • Not graded solely on correctness (keys for self-check)
  • Show diagrams, formulas, units, clear steps
  • Incomplete work not accepted

Late Work Policy

Accepted only if an extension request is submitted in writing before the due date, including an expected completion date.

How You Can Help at Home

  • Environment: Quiet, organized study space
  • Mindset: Normalize mistakes as part of learning
  • Engagement: Ask them to explain concepts—teaching strengthens mastery

Required Daily Materials

  • Charged Chromebook
  • Pencils
  • Laboratory notebook
  • Scientific or graphing calculator (student-operated; no sharing on assessments)
