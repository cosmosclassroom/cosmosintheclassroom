Interdisciplinary Science

Throughout the year, students will wear three different hats:

    • The Scientist: Asking questions and decoding data to understand why a disaster happens.
    • The Engineer: Using that knowledge to design solutions for a safer world.
    • The Policymaker: Facing the ethical and financial challenges of making decisions for a community.

How Grading Works: A Focus on Skills

In this class, students learn and practice the 8 key skills of scientists and engineers. Holistic, mastery-level grades are reported for each practice (plus studentship)

The 8 Scientific & Engineering Practices

  • Asking Questions & Defining Problems
  • Developing & Using Models
  • Planning & Carrying Out Investigations
  • Analyzing & Interpreting Data
  • Using Mathematics & Computational Thinking
  • Constructing Explanations & Designing Solutions
  • Engaging in Argument from Evidence
  • Obtaining, Evaluating & Communicating Information

Example: Constructing Explanations & Designing Solutions

  • Developing: Identifies a problem or proposes a simple solution.
    • “We should build a wall to stop the flood.”

Example: Constructing Explanations & Designing Solutions

  • Meets Expectations: Uses specific evidence to explain or design.
    • “The flood happened because the river rose 5 feet. Based on that data, a 6-foot seawall would protect the neighborhood.”

Example: Constructing Explanations & Designing Solutions

  • Exceeds Expectations: Evaluates trade-offs and proposes alternatives.
    • “A 6-foot wall would work, but it would be expensive and could harm the local salt marsh. A better long-term solution might be…”

Units of Study

  • Unit 1: Earthquakes
  • Unit 2: Tsunamis & Volcanoes
  • Unit 3: Wildfires
  • Unit 4: Flooding & Landslides

Unit Example: Earthquakes

  • The Scientist: Analyze seismic data to understand the forces and patterns.
  • The Engineer: Design and test structures to withstand seismic waves.
  • The Policymaker: Debate the costs and benefits of new building codes and emergency preparedness plans.

The Final Project: Branford, CT

The course culminates in a final portfolio where students apply everything they have learned to our own community.

Students will analyze Branford’s specific vulnerabilities to natural hazards (like flooding and storms) and develop a policy brief with their own evidence-based recommendations for making our community more resilient.

How You Can Help at Home

Your perspective is a valuable part of this journey.

  • Talk about the roles: Ask, “This week, are you being a scientist, an engineer, or a policymaker?”
  • Connect to our community: Discuss local news about storm preparedness, coastal erosion, or new construction in Branford.
  • Encourage the process: Ask, “What’s your evidence for that?” to help them build their arguments.
