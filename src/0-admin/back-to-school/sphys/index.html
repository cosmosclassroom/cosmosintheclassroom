Teaching & Learning Approach

This course is built on a simple idea: science should be about figuring things out, not just memorizing facts. Each unit starts with a real-world problem or question. Students work like scientists and engineers to investigate, collect data, and build explanations or solutions.

Course Aims

  • Develop scientific thinking and problem-solving skills
  • Practice evidence-based reasoning and communication
  • Build confidence in applying physics to real-world situations

Why Physics?

To foster curiosity about the natural world and develop skills that are valuable everywhere: critical thinking, data analysis, and logical reasoning.

Curriculum: Part I

Electricity & Power Grids

Guiding Question: How can we design more reliable energy systems?

Focus: Investigate how electrical energy is transferred by analyzing real-world failures (e.g., the 2021 Texas power crisis) and how such systems can be improved.

Part I Topics

  • Electricity basics
  • Circuits & power grids
  • Energy transfer & reliability

Curriculum: Part II

Forces & Earth’s Interior

Guiding Question: How do forces inside Earth change its surface?

Focus: Model the immense forces and energy transfers that drive plate tectonics, leading to earthquakes and volcanic activity.

Part II Topics

  • Forces & motion
  • Plate tectonics
  • Earthquakes seismic activity

Curriculum: Part III

Momentum & Vehicle Safety

Guiding Question: What can we do to make driving safer?

Focus: Explore the physics of collisions by analyzing traffic data and applying concepts of momentum and impulse to engineer safer vehicles.

Part III Topics

  • Momentum & impulse
  • Collisions
  • Vehicle safety engineering

Curriculum: Part IV

Gravity & Orbital Motion

Guiding Question: How do collisions with space objects affect Earth?

Focus: Examine the laws of gravity and orbits by investigating meteor impacts and planetary defense.

Part IV Topics

  • Gravity
  • Orbits
  • Meteor impacts

Curriculum: Part V

Waves & Modern Technology

Guiding Question: How do we use radiation, and is it safe?

Focus: Study electromagnetic waves and their interactions with matter by exploring how everyday devices like microwave ovens and cell phones work.

Part V Topics

  • Mechanical & electromagnetic waves
  • Radiation & safety
  • Technology applications

Assessment & Grading

Grades are based on a total-points system (percentage → letter grade).

Point Ranges:

  • Homework/Classwork: 5–10 pts
  • Quizzes/Activities: 20–50 pts
  • Major Assessments: 100 pts

Assessment Notes

  • No extra credit is offered
  • Lab safety violations may result in removal from the activity and a zero for the lab
  • Emphasis on process, communication, and scientific habits

Homework & Late Work

Homework is for practice and building good habits. It is not graded solely on accuracy; answer keys are available for self-assessment. Late work is accepted only if a student provides written notice before the due date.

How You Can Help at Home

  • Ask about the “big question” in the current unit
  • Encourage the process: “What have you figured out so far?”
  • Reinforce that science values questions and learning from mistakes
  • Have your student shrae what research or investigations we are currently working on

Required Daily Materials

  • Charged Chromebook
  • Pencils
  • Science notebook
  • Scientific calculator (class set available if needed)
