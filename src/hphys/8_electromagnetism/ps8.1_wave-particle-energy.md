## Problems

1. The energy of a photon is given by E = hf, where h is Planck's constant (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s) and f is the frequency of light.
   a) Calculate the energy of a photon of blue light with frequency 7.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz.
   b) Compare this to the energy of a photon of red light with frequency 4.3 × 10¹⁴ Hz.

2. A radio station broadcasts at a frequency of 98.6 MHz with a power of 50 kW.
   a) Calculate the wavelength of these radio waves.
   b) How many photons per second does this station emit?
   c) Compare the energy of one radio wave photon to that of a visible light photon.

3. In a double-slit experiment, coherent light of wavelength 630 nm passes through two slits separated by 0.300 mm.
   If a screen is placed 2.0 m from the slits, what is the distance between the central maximum and the first bright fringe?

4. An electron with kinetic energy of 50 eV has a de Broglie wavelength given by λ = h/p.
   a) Calculate this wavelength.
   b) Would this electron show significant diffraction effects passing through an opening of 1.0 nm? Explain.

5. A sensitive light detector can register individual photons. If the detector is exposed to green light (wavelength 520 nm) with an intensity of 1.0 × 10⁻¹² W/m², and has a surface area of 1.0 mm²:
   a) How many photons per second will hit the detector?
   b) If the detector can register a signal when at least 100 photons arrive within a 1.0 ms interval, will this light be detectable?

6. A microwave oven operates at 2.45 GHz.
   a) Calculate the wavelength of these microwaves.
   b) Many microwave ovens have a wavelength that matches the resonant absorption of water molecules. How does this relate to the cooking process?
   c) If the microwave delivers 900 W of power to the food, how many photons per second are absorbed?

7. The human eye can detect as few as 5-10 photons of light. If the wavelength of light is 550 nm (green light):
   a) Calculate the energy carried by 7 photons.
   b) If this energy were converted to thermal energy, by how much would it raise the temperature of 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁵ g of water?

8. X-rays used in medical imaging typically have wavelengths around 0.1 nm.
   a) Calculate the frequency and energy (in eV) of these X-ray photons.
   b) How many of these X-ray photons would carry a total energy of 1.0 J?
   c) Compare the energy of one X-ray photon to that of a visible light photon (550 nm). What implications does this have for biological safety?

9. An electron microscope accelerates electrons through a potential difference of 50 kV.
   a) Calculate the kinetic energy of these electrons in joules.
   b) Calculate the momentum and de Broglie wavelength of these electrons.
   c) Why can electron microscopes achieve much higher resolution than light microscopes?

10. The photoelectric effect demonstrates the particle nature of light. When light with a frequency of 1.0 × 10¹⁵ Hz strikes a cesium surface (work function 1.9 eV):
    a) Will electrons be emitted? If so, calculate their maximum kinetic energy in eV.
    b) What is the threshold frequency for electron emission from this surface?
    c) If the intensity of the light is doubled but the frequency remains the same, how does this affect the maximum kinetic energy and the number of emitted electrons?

## Answers

1. a) 4.97 × 10⁻¹⁹ J (3.11 eV)
   b) 2.85 × 10⁻¹⁹ J (1.78 eV); blue photons have about 1.75 times the energy of red photons

2. a) 3.04 m
   b) 1.52 × 10²⁸ photons/s
   c) A radio photon has approximately 10⁻⁶ times the energy of a visible photon

3. 4.2 mm

4. a) 1.7 × 10⁻¹⁰ m (0.17 nm)
   b) Yes, significant diffraction would occur because the wavelength is on the same order of magnitude as the opening size

5. a) 2.6 × 10⁴ photons/s
   b) No, only about 26 photons would arrive in 1 ms, below the detection threshold

6. a) 0.122 m (12.2 cm)
   b) The wavelength creates standing waves in the oven; water molecules rotate in response to the oscillating electric field, generating heat through molecular friction
   c) 1.8 × 10²⁶ photons/s

7. a) 2.52 × 10⁻¹⁸ J
   b) Approximately 6.0 × 10⁻⁴ K (negligible temperature increase)

8. a) f = 3.0 × 10¹⁸ Hz; E = 12.4 keV
   b) Approximately 5.0 × 10¹⁴ photons
   c) An X-ray photon has about 22,000 times more energy than a visible light photon, which is why X-rays can cause ionization and damage to biological tissues

9. a) 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁵ J (50 keV)
   b) p = 1.2 × 10⁻²² kg·m/s; λ = 5.5 × 10⁻¹² m (0.0055 nm)
   c) Electron microscopes can achieve higher resolution because the de Broglie wavelength of accelerated electrons is much smaller than the wavelength of visible light, allowing smaller structures to be resolved

10. a) Yes; KE_max = 4.1 - 1.9 = 2.2 eV
    b) $f_{threshold}$ = 4.6 × 10¹⁴ Hz
    c) The maximum kinetic energy remains unchanged; the number of emitted electrons doubles (demonstrates that energy depends on frequency, not intensity)
