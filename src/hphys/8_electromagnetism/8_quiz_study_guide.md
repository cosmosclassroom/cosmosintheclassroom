# Unit 8 Study Guide: Electric Potential Energy and Capacitance

## Learning Outcomes

### Students should know:

- The definition of electric potential energy as energy stored in a system of electric charges due to their positions
- That electric potential energy is analogous to gravitational potential energy
- That electric forces are conservative and charge is conserved
- Charging by induction/friction
- The units of electric potential energy (joules, J)
- That like charges (+/+ or -/-) have positive potential energy (repulsion)
- That unlike charges (+/-) have negative potential energy (attraction)
- The definition of electric potential as electric potential energy per unit charge
  - That electric potential describes the electric "pressure" at a point in space
  - That electric potential is independent of test charge/charge in field
- The units of electric potential (volts, V, where 1 V = 1 J/C)
- That positive charges naturally move from high potential to low potential
- That electric field points in the direction of force exerted on a positive test charge
- The definition of a capacitor as a device that stores electric charge
- That a basic capacitor consists of two conductors separated by an insulator
- The units of capacitance (farads, F, where 1 F = 1 C/V)


### Students should be able to:

- Calculate the force between two charges
- Calculate the strength of an electric field generated by a point charge
- Calculate electric potential energy between two point charges
- Determine the work done by electric forces
- Calculate electric potential due to a point charge
- Determine the potential at any point due to multiple charges using superposition
- Calculate work done moving a charge in a uniform electric field
- Determine potential difference in a uniform field
- Calculate capacitance
- Calculate energy stored in a capacitor
- Calculate electric field between capacitor plates



## Key Terms to Know
- Electric potential energy
- Electric potential (voltage)
- Volt
- Coulomb
- Equipotential surface
- Capacitor
- Capacitance
- Farad
