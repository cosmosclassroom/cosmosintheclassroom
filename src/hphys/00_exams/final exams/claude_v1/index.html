Physics Practice Exam

Based on Giancoli Physics 7th Edition - Difficulty Level (I)

Name: __________ Date: ______

Instructions: Show all work for calculation problems. Circle your final answers.

Section 1: Dynamics (Chapter 4)

  1. A 5.0-kg object is subject to a net force of 15 N to the east. What is the magnitude and direction of its acceleration?

  2. A 75-kg skier is going down a slope that makes an angle of 20° with the horizontal. If the coefficient of kinetic friction between the skis and the snow is 0.12, determine the acceleration of the skier down the slope.

  3. A 2.0-kg block is at rest on a rough horizontal surface. The coefficient of static friction between the block and the surface is 0.30. What is the minimum horizontal force needed to start the block moving?

  4. Which of Newton’s laws explains why passengers tend to lean to the right when a car makes a sharp left turn? a) First law b) Second law c) Third law d) None of Newton’s laws explain this phenomenon

  5. A 50-kg crate rests on a horizontal floor. If the coefficient of static friction is 0.40, what horizontal force is required to start moving the crate?

Section 2: Circular Motion and Gravitation (Chapter 5)

  1. A satellite orbits Earth at a height of 3600 km above the surface. Given that Earth’s radius is 6400 km and its mass is 6.0 × 10²⁴ kg, calculate the orbital speed of the satellite. (G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²)

  2. A car travels around a circular track with a radius of 50 m at a constant speed of 15 m/s. What is the centripetal acceleration experienced by the car?

  3. Which of Kepler’s laws states that planets sweep out equal areas in equal times? a) First law b) Second law c) Third law d) None of Kepler’s laws state this

  4. A 0.15-kg ball at the end of a string is rotated in a horizontal circle of radius 1.2 m. If the ball makes 2.0 revolutions per second, what is the tension in the string?

  5. The Moon orbits Earth at an average distance of 3.84 × 10⁸ m with a period of 27.3 days. Using this information, estimate the mass of Earth. (G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²)

Section 3: Work and Energy (Chapter 6)

  1. A 2.0-kg object initially at rest is pushed 5.0 m along a frictionless horizontal surface by a constant 8.0-N force. What is the final kinetic energy of the object?

  2. A 60-kg hiker climbs a mountain, increasing her elevation by 400 m. How much gravitational potential energy does she gain? (g = 9.8 m/s²)

  3. A spring with spring constant 200 N/m is compressed 15 cm from its equilibrium position. How much elastic potential energy is stored in the spring?

  4. A 1500-kg car accelerates from rest to 25 m/s in 10 seconds. What is the average power delivered by the engine during this time?

  5. When is the work done on an object negative? a) When force and displacement are in opposite directions b) When force and displacement are in the same direction c) When the force is perpendicular to the displacement d) When the object is not moving

Section 4: Harmonics (Chapter 11)

  1. A simple pendulum has a period of 2.0 s. What is its length? (g = 9.8 m/s²)

  2. A 0.50-kg mass attached to a spring oscillates with a frequency of 2.0 Hz. If the total energy of the system is 6.0 J, what is the amplitude of the oscillation?

  3. Two waves with the same frequency, wavelength, and amplitude travel in the same direction. If they have a phase difference of 180°, what type of interference occurs? a) Constructive interference b) Destructive interference c) No interference d) Resonance

  4. A wave travels at 340 m/s with a frequency of 680 Hz. What is its wavelength?

  5. A mass-spring system oscillates with an amplitude of 10 cm. At what position(s) is the speed of the mass maximum? a) At the equilibrium position b) At the maximum displacement c) At one-half the maximum displacement d) At one-quarter the maximum displacement

Section 5: Electromagnetism (Chapters 16 and 17)

  1. Two point charges, +5.0 μC and -3.0 μC, are separated by 40 cm. What is the magnitude of the electric force between them? (k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²)

  2. An electron enters a uniform electric field of 4.0 × 10³ N/C. What is the magnitude of the electric force on the electron? (Charge of electron = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C)

  3. Which of the following is NOT a method of charging an object? a) Friction b) Contact c) Induction d) Nuclear decay

  4. A point charge creates an electric potential of 600 V at a distance of 0.20 m. What is the electric potential at a distance of 0.40 m from the charge?

  5. A parallel plate capacitor has plates of area 4.0 × 10⁻² m² separated by 2.0 mm. If the capacitance is 1.77 × 10⁻¹⁰ F, what dielectric material fills the space between the plates? (ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m)

Section 6: Electricity (Chapters 18 and 19)

  1. A wire with a resistance of 5.0 Ω carries a current of 3.0 A. What is the voltage across the wire?

  2. A toaster draws 8.0 A when connected to a 120-V outlet. What is the resistance of the toaster?

  3. Three resistors with resistances of 3.0 Ω, 6.0 Ω, and 9.0 Ω are connected in series. What is their equivalent resistance?

  4. The same three resistors from the previous problem (3.0 Ω, 6.0 Ω, and 9.0 Ω) are now connected in parallel. What is their equivalent resistance?

  5. A battery with an emf of 12 V and internal resistance of 0.50 Ω is connected to a 5.5 Ω resistor. What is the terminal voltage of the battery?

Answer Key

Section 1: Dynamics

  1. a = F/m = 15 N / 5.0 kg = 3.0 m/s² to the east

  2. Forces along the slope: Fₓ = mg sin(20°) - μₖmg cos(20°) a = g[sin(20°) - μₖcos(20°)] a = 9.8[sin(20°) - 0.12cos(20°)] a = 9.8[0.342 - 0.12(0.940)] a = 9.8[0.342 - 0.113] a = 9.8(0.229) = 2.24 m/s²

  3. Minimum force = μₛN = μₛmg = 0.30 × 2.0 kg × 9.8 m/s² = 5.88 N

  4. a) First law (inertia)

  5. F = μₛN = μₛmg = 0.40 × 50 kg × 9.8 m/s² = 196 N

Section 2: Circular Motion and Gravitation

  1. v = √(GM/r) where r = Earth radius + height = 6400 km + 3600 km = 10000 km = 1.0 × 10⁷ m v = √[(6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²)(6.0 × 10²⁴ kg)/(1.0 × 10⁷ m)] v = √[(4.0 × 10¹⁴)] = 6.3 × 10⁶ m/s

  2. aₑ = v²/r = (15 m/s)²/50 m = 4.5 m/s²

  3. b) Second law

  4. T = m × aₑ = m × ω²r ω = 2πf = 2π × 2.0 rev/s = 4π rad/s T = 0.15 kg × (4π rad/s)² × 1.2 m T = 0.15 kg × 16π² rad²/s² × 1.2 m T = 0.15 kg × 16 × 9.87 × 1.2 m/s² T = 28.4 N

  5. M = 4π²r³/(GT²) T = 27.3 days = 27.3 × 24 × 3600 s = 2.36 × 10⁶ s M = 4π²(3.84 × 10⁸ m)³/[(6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²)(2.36 × 10⁶ s)²] M = 4π²(5.66 × 10²⁵)/(6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.57 × 10¹²) M = 6.0 × 10²⁴ kg

Section 3: Work and Energy

  1. W = F × d = 8.0 N × 5.0 m = 40 J KE = W = 40 J

  2. PE = mgh = 60 kg × 9.8 m/s² × 400 m = 235,200 J = 2.35 × 10⁵ J

  3. PE = ½kx² = ½ × 200 N/m × (0.15 m)² = ½ × 200 × 0.0225 = 2.25 J

  4. P = ΔKE/Δt = ½mv²/t = ½ × 1500 kg × (25 m/s)²/10 s = 46,875 W = 4.7 × 10⁴ W

  5. a) When force and displacement are in opposite directions

Section 4: Harmonics

  1. T = 2π√(L/g) L = g(T/2π)² = 9.8 m/s² × (2.0 s/2π)² = 9.8 × (0.318)² = 9.8 × 0.101 = 0.99 m

  2. E = ½kA² = 6.0 J For a simple harmonic oscillator, k = m(2πf)² k = 0.50 kg × (2π × 2.0 Hz)² = 0.50 kg × (4π)² Hz² = 0.50 × 16π² = 79 N/m A² = 2E/k = 2 × 6.0 J / 79 N/m = 0.152 m² A = 0.39 m

  3. b) Destructive interference

  4. λ = v/f = 340 m/s / 680 Hz = 0.50 m

  5. a) At the equilibrium position

Section 5: Electromagnetism

  1. F = k|q₁q₂|/r² = (9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²) × |5.0 × 10⁻⁶ C × (-3.0 × 10⁻⁶ C)|/(0.40 m)² F = 9.0 × 10⁹ × 15 × 10⁻¹² / 0.16 F = 9.0 × 15 × 10⁻³ / 0.16 = 0.84 N

  2. F = qE = (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) × (4.0 × 10³ N/C) = 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁶ N

  3. d) Nuclear decay

  4. V ∝ 1/r, so V(0.40 m) = V(0.20 m) × (0.20 m/0.40 m) = 600 V × 0.5 = 300 V

  5. C = κε₀A/d where κ is the dielectric constant κ = Cd/(ε₀A) = (1.77 × 10⁻¹⁰ F)(2.0 × 10⁻³ m)/[(8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m)(4.0 × 10⁻² m²)] κ = 3.54 × 10⁻¹³ / 3.54 × 10⁻¹³ = 1.0 (vacuum or air)

Section 6: Electricity

  1. V = IR = 3.0 A × 5.0 Ω = 15 V

  2. R = V/I = 120 V / 8.0 A = 15 Ω

  3. Rₑq = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ = 3.0 Ω + 6.0 Ω + 9.0 Ω = 18 Ω

  4. 1/Rₑq = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ = 1/3.0 + 1/6.0 + 1/9.0 = 0.333 + 0.167 + 0.111 = 0.611 Rₑq = 1/0.611 = 1.64 Ω

  5. Vₜ = ε - Ir = 12 V - (I × 0.50 Ω) I = ε/(R + r) = 12 V / (5.5 Ω + 0.50 Ω) = 12 V / 6.0 Ω = 2.0 A Vₜ = 12 V - (2.0 A × 0.50 Ω) = 12 V - 1.0 V = 11 V
