### Important Equations by Topic

#### 8 Electromagnetism
- Wave Mechanics: $c = \lambda f$
- Photon Energy: $E = hf$
- Photon Energy (wavelength): $E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$
- Electric Field: $E = \frac{q}{r^2}$
- Coulomb's Law: $F = \frac{q_1q_2}{r^2}$

#### 9 Electricity
- Ohm's Law: $V = IR$
- Power: $P = IV$
- Power (resistor): $P = I^2R$, $P = \frac{V^2}{R}$
- Series Resistance: $R_{total} = R_1 + R_2$
- Parallel Resistance: $\frac{1}{R_{total}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}$

#### 10 Optics
- Snell's Law: $n_1\sin\theta_1 = n_2\sin\theta_2$
- Thin Lens: $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i}$
- Magnification: $m = -\frac{d_i}{d_o}$

#### Problem Set 8.1: SHM in Electromagnetism
**Formulas:**
- Period of mass-spring: $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}$
- Period of pendulum: $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$
- Frequency: $f = \frac{1}{T}$
- Energy in SHM: $E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2$

1. Define simple harmonic motion (SHM) and give a real-world example.
2. Describe the relationship between SHM and electromagnetic waves.
3. Calculate the period of a mass-spring system given mass and spring constant.
4. Find the frequency of oscillation for a pendulum of given length.
5. Explain how SHM applies to the oscillation of charges in an antenna.
6. Calculate the maximum speed of an object in SHM.
7. Determine the energy stored in a mass-spring system at maximum displacement.
8. Describe how energy is transferred in SHM.
9. Explain the connection between SHM and resonance in circuits.
10. Give an example of SHM in a physical or electrical system and explain its significance.

#### Problem Set 8.2: Charges and EM Fields
**Formulas:**
- Electric field (point charge): $E = \frac{q}{r^2}$
- Coulomb's Law: $F = \frac{q_1q_2}{r^2}$
- Superposition principle for fields: $E_{net} = E_1 + E_2 + ...$

1. Define electric field and electric force. How are they different?
2. Calculate the electric field strength at a given distance from a point charge.
3. Use Coulomb's law to find the force between two charges.
4. Draw the electric field lines for a single positive charge and a dipole.
5. Explain the principle of superposition for electric fields.
6. Calculate the net electric field at a point due to multiple charges.
7. Describe how electric fields are used in real-world technology (e.g., photocopiers, TVs).
8. Find the force on a charge placed in a known electric field.
9. Explain the difference between conductors and insulators in terms of electric fields.
10. Describe a real-life scenario where electric field concepts are important.

#### Problem Set 9.2: Simple Circuit Diagrams
**Formulas:**
- Series resistance: $R_{total} = R_1 + R_2 + ...$
- Parallel resistance: $\frac{1}{R_{total}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + ...$
- Ohm's Law: $V = IR$

1. Draw a simple series circuit with a battery and two resistors.
2. Draw a parallel circuit with a battery and two resistors.
3. Calculate total resistance in a series circuit.
4. Calculate total resistance in a parallel circuit.
5. Find the current through each resistor in a parallel circuit.
6. Find the voltage across each resistor in a series circuit.
7. Explain the advantages of parallel circuits in household wiring.
8. Identify circuit symbols for battery, resistor, and switch.
9. Draw a circuit diagram for a flashlight.
10. Troubleshoot a simple circuit that does not work and suggest possible causes.

#### Problem Set 9.3: Electrical Power
**Formulas:**
- Power: $P = IV$
- Power (resistor): $P = I^2R$, $P = \frac{V^2}{R}$
- Energy: $E = Pt$
- Cost: $\text{Cost} = \text{Energy (kWh)} \times \text{rate}$
- Efficiency: $\text{Efficiency} = \frac{\text{Output Power}}{\text{Input Power}} \times 100\%$

1. Define electrical power and its unit.
2. Calculate power consumed by a device given current and voltage.
3. Use $P = I^2R$ and $P = \frac{V^2}{R}$ to find power in a resistor.
4. Calculate energy used by a device over a given time.
5. Calculate the cost of running an appliance for a month.
6. Explain the difference between AC and DC power.
7. Describe how fuses and circuit breakers protect circuits.
8. Calculate the efficiency of a device given input and output power.
9. Explain why power lines are high voltage for long-distance transmission.
10. Give a real-world example of electrical power in use (e.g., toaster, microwave).

3. Calculate the angle of reflection given the angle of incidence.

5. Describe how a periscope works using plane mirrors.
6. Draw a ray diagram for a concave mirror.
7. Find the focal point of a concave mirror given its radius of curvature.
8. Calculate the image distance for an object in front of a plane mirror.
9. Explain why your image in a mirror appears reversed.
10. Give a real-world example of reflection in technology.

#### Problem Set 10.2: Refraction and Optics
**Formulas:**
- Snell's Law: $n_1\sin\theta_1 = n_2\sin\theta_2$
- Thin lens equation: $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i}$
- Magnification: $m = -\frac{d_i}{d_o}$

1. Define refraction and state Snell's law.
2. Calculate the angle of refraction for light passing from air into water.
3. Explain why objects appear bent in water.
4. Draw a ray diagram for a converging lens.
5. Calculate the focal length of a lens using the thin lens equation.
6. Find the image distance for an object placed in front of a lens.
7. Calculate magnification for a lens system.
8. Explain total internal reflection and its applications.
9. Describe how fiber optic cables use total internal reflection.
10. Give a real-world example of refraction (e.g., eyeglasses, prisms).

2. Draw a ray diagram for a diverging lens.
3. Calculate the image position for an object in front of a diverging lens.
4. Find the magnification of an image produced by a lens.
5. Explain the difference between real and virtual images for lenses.
6. Calculate the image distance for a convex mirror.
7. Draw a ray diagram for a convex mirror.
8. Explain how corrective lenses help people see clearly.
9. Describe the use of lenses in microscopes and telescopes.
10. Give a real-world example of geometric optics in technology.
