# Wave Functions & Phenomena

A Lecture on the Mathematical Description of Waves

---

## Wave Functions: The Language of Motion

*   A wave function, denoted by *ψ(x, t)*, describes the amplitude of a wave at a specific position *x* and time *t*.
*   For a sinusoidal wave traveling in the positive x-direction:

    *ψ(x, t) = A sin(kx - ωt + φ)*

    Where:
    *   *A* = Amplitude
    *   *k* = Wave number (2π/λ)
    *   *ω* = Angular frequency (2πf)
    *   *φ* = Phase constant

---

## Superposition and Interference

*   The principle of superposition states that when two or more waves overlap, the resultant displacement is the algebraic sum of the individual displacements.
*   **Constructive Interference:**  Occurs when waves are in phase, resulting in a larger amplitude.
*   **Destructive Interference:** Occurs when waves are out of phase, resulting in a smaller amplitude (or cancellation).

    *Example: Two waves with the same amplitude and wavelength, but with a phase difference of π/2 will result in destructive interference.*

---

## Diffraction: Bending Around Obstacles

*   Diffraction is the spreading of waves as they pass through an opening or around an obstacle.
*   The amount of diffraction depends on the wavelength of the wave and the size of the opening/obstacle.
*   *Larger wavelength relative to the obstacle size = greater diffraction.*

---

## The Doppler Effect: A Change in Frequency

*   The Doppler effect is the apparent change in frequency of a wave due to the relative motion between the source and the observer.
*   **Moving Source:**  The observed frequency is lower than the emitted frequency.
*   **Moving Observer:** The observed frequency is higher than the emitted frequency.
*   
---
## Calculating the Doppler Effect
*   *Formula (for sound): f' = f (v ± vo) / (v ± vs)*
    *   *f'* = observed frequency
    *   *f* = emitted frequency
    *   *v* = speed of sound
    *   *vo* = observer's velocity
    *   *vs* = source's velocity

---

## Intensity of Waves

*   Intensity (I) is the power per unit area carried by a wave.
*   For a sinusoidal wave: *I ∝ A²*
*   In simpler terms, the intensity of a wave is proportional to the square of its amplitude.
*   *Higher amplitude = higher intensity.*

---

## Summary

*   Wave functions provide a mathematical description of wave behavior.
*   Superposition leads to interference (constructive and destructive).
*   Diffraction explains the bending of waves.
*   The Doppler effect alters the perceived frequency.
*   Intensity reflects the wave's power.
