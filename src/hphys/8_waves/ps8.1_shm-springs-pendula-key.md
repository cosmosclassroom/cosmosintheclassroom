### A child swings on a playground swing with a 2.5 m long chain.  

| 2 | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{a_g}}$ |
|---|---|
| $L = 2.5 \text{ m}$ <br> $a_g = 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$ | $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2.5 \text{ m}}{9.8 \text{ m/s}^2}}$ <br> $T = 2\pi\sqrt{0.2551 \text{ s}^2}$ <br> $T = 2\pi (0.5050 \text{ s})$ <br> $T = 3.1416 (0.5050 \text{ s})$ <br> $T = 1.590 \text{ s}$ <br> Round to three significant figures: $T = 1.59 \text{ s}$ |

| 2 | $f = \frac{1}{T}$ |
|---|---|
| $T = 1.59 \text{ s}$ | $f = \frac{1}{1.59 \text{ s}}$ <br> $f = 0.629 \text{ Hz}$ <br> Round to three significant figures: $f = 0.629 \text{ Hz}$ |

5.  Two identical waves traveling in opposite directions have wavelength 2.0 m and amplitude 3.0 cm. What is:
   Okay, here's the solution to the problem, formatted according to the specified style.

**1. Distance between adjacent nodes in the resulting standing wave**

The distance between adjacent nodes in a standing wave is equal to one-half the wavelength of the individual waves.

| Significant Digits | Calculation |
|---|---|
| 1 | $\lambda = 2.0 \ m$ <br>
| 1 | $d = \frac{\lambda}{2}$ <br>
| 1 | $d = \frac{2.0 \ m}{2}$ <br>
| 1 | $d = 1.0 \ m$ |

Therefore, the distance between adjacent nodes is 1.0 m.

**2. Maximum amplitude that can occur**

When two waves with the same amplitude travel in opposite directions, the maximum amplitude in the resulting standing wave is the sum of the amplitudes of the individual waves.

| Significant Digits | Calculation |
|---|---|
| 1 | $A_1 = 3.0 \ cm$ <br>
| 1 | $A_2 = 3.0 \ cm$ <br>
| 1 | $A_{max} = A_1 + A_2$ <br>
| 1 | $A_{max} = 3.0 \ cm + 3.0 \ cm$ <br>
| 1 | $A_{max} = 6.0 \ cm$ |

Therefore, the maximum amplitude that can occur is 6.0 cm.
