# Day 1: Introduction to Light Waves

## Wave Properties of Light

- Light as an electromagnetic wave
- Wavelength, frequency, and amplitude
- Speed of light: $c = 3.00 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$
- Wave relation: $c = \lambda f$

## Beyond What We Can See

<div class="columns">
<div>

- Visible light is a small part of the EM spectrum

- All travel at speed of light in vacuum

# Light Energy

- Light carries energy in discrete packets called **photons**
- Photon energy depends on frequency: $E = hf = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$
- Planck's constant: $h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J·s}$
- Intensity follows inverse square law: $I \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$

# Wave-Particle Duality

- Light exhibits both wave and particle properties
- Wave model explains interference and diffraction
- Particle model explains photoelectric effect
- Neither model alone is complete!

# Day 2: Reflection of Light

## Law of Reflection

- Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection
- $\theta_i = \theta_r$
- Incident ray, reflected ray, and normal all lie in same plane
- Applies to all waves (light, sound, water)

<div class="image-container">
<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Law_of_reflection.svg/640px-Law_of_reflection.svg.png">
</div>

<p class="reference">Image: Law of reflection (Wikimedia Commons)</p>

<!-- Slide 8 -->
# Types of Reflection

<div class="columns">
<div>

### Specular Reflection
- Smooth surfaces (mirrors)
- Parallel rays remain parallel
- Creates clear images

### Diffuse Reflection
- Rough surfaces
- Rays scattered in various directions
- No clear image formation

</div>

# Plane Mirrors

- Virtual images: appear to be behind mirror
- Same distance behind mirror as object is in front
- Laterally inverted (left becomes right)
- Same size as object (magnification = 1)

# Day 3: Refraction of Light

## Snell's Law

- Light bends when passing between media
- $n_1 \sin \theta_1 = n_2 \sin \theta_2$
- $n$ = index of refraction = $\frac{c}{v}$
- Caused by change in wave speed

<div class="image-container">
<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/Snells_law2.svg/640px-Snells_law2.svg.png">

<!-- Slide 12 -->
# Total Internal Reflection

- Occurs when light travels from higher to lower index medium
- Critical angle: $\sin \theta_c = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$ (where $n_1 > n_2$)
- If angle of incidence exceeds critical angle, all light is reflected
- Applications: fiber optics, prism binoculars

# Day 5: Interference of Light

## Wave Superposition

- When waves meet, their displacements add algebraically
- Constructive interference: waves in phase, amplitudes add
- Destructive interference: waves out of phase, amplitudes subtract
- Proof of wave nature of light

<div class="image-container">
<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Wave_interference.svg/640px-Wave_interference.svg.png">
</div>

<p class="reference">Image: Wave interference (Wikimedia Commons)</p>

<!-- Slide 15 -->
# Young's Double-Slit Experiment

- 1801: Thomas Young proved light behaves as a wave
- Coherent light through two narrow slits
- Interference pattern of bright and dark bands
- Spacing depends on wavelength, slit separation, and screen distance

<div class="image-container">
<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Young%27s_experiment_with_single_slit.svg/640px-Young%27s_experiment_with_single_slit.svg.png">
</div>

<p class="reference">Image: Young's double-slit experiment (Wikimedia Commons)</p>

---

<!-- Slide 16 -->
# Double-Slit Interference Formulas

- Bright fringes (constructive): $d \sin \theta = m\lambda$ (m = 0, ±1, ±2, ...)
- Dark fringes (destructive): $d \sin \theta = (m+\frac{1}{2})\lambda$ (m = 0, ±1, ±2, ...)
- Fringe spacing: $\Delta y = \frac{L\lambda}{d}$

# Day 6: Diffraction of Light

## What is Diffraction?

- Bending of waves around obstacles or through apertures
- Occurs with all waves (water, sound, light)
- More pronounced when obstacle/aperture size ≈ wavelength
- Another confirmation of wave nature of light

<div class="image-container">
<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Diffraction_pattern_of_a_small_circular_aperture.jpg/640px-Diffraction_pattern_of_a_small_circular_aperture.jpg">
</div>

<p class="reference">Image: Diffraction pattern of circular aperture (Wikimedia Commons)</p>

---

<!-- Slide 18 -->
# Single-Slit Diffraction

- Light spreads out after passing through narrow slit
- Pattern of bright and dark bands
- Central bright maximum is twice as wide as others
- Minima occur at: $a \sin \theta = m\lambda$ (m = ±1, ±2, ...)

<div class="image-container">
<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Single_slit_and_intensity.svg/640px-Single_slit_and_intensity.svg.png">
</div>

<p class="reference">Image: Single-slit diffraction pattern (Wikimedia Commons)</p>

<!-- Slide 19 -->
# Resolution Limits

- Diffraction limits the resolution of optical instruments
- Rayleigh criterion: $\sin \theta = 1.22 \frac{\lambda}{D}$
- Minimum angle to resolve two points
- Explains why telescopes need large apertures for detail

# Day 9: Thin Lenses - Part 2

## Lens Equation and Magnification

- Thin lens equation: $\frac{1}{s} + \frac{1}{s'} = \frac{1}{f}$
- Magnification: $m = \frac{h'}{h} = -\frac{s'}{s}$
- Sign conventions are crucial!
- Real image: inverted, can project on screen
- Virtual image: upright, cannot project on screen

# Day 10: Color and Dispersion

## Color as Wavelength

- Visible spectrum: ~380-750 nm
- Violet (~400 nm) to red (~700 nm)
- Objects appear colored by reflecting/transmitting certain wavelengths
- White light contains all visible wavelengths

<div class="image-container">
<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Linear_visible_spectrum.svg/640px-Linear_visible_spectrum.svg.png">
</div>

<p class="reference">Image: Visible light spectrum (Wikimedia Commons)</p>

---

<!-- Slide 26 -->
# Dispersion

- Refractive index varies with wavelength
- Short wavelengths (blue) refract more than long wavelengths (red)
- Explains prism splitting white light into spectrum
- Causes chromatic aberration in lenses

<div class="image-container">
<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Dispersive_prism.svg/640px-Dispersive_prism.svg.png">

<!-- Slide 27 -->
# Rainbow Formation

- Natural example of dispersion
- Primary rainbow: single reflection inside droplet
- Secondary rainbow: double reflection inside droplet
- Always appears in direction opposite the sun

# Vision Defects and Corrections

<div class="columns">
<div>

### Myopia (Nearsightedness)
- Images focus in front of retina
- Corrected with diverging lenses

### Hyperopia (Farsightedness)
- Images focus behind retina
- Corrected with converging lenses
