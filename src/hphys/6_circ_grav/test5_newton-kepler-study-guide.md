# Review Guide: Kepler's Laws and Newton's Universal Gravitation

### What Students Should Know

Define centripetal force and its direction.

Explain why objects in orbit appear to float (free-fall).

Understand the net force acting on objects in orbit.

Understand the factors affecting gravitational force and their mathematical relationships.

Know the equation for gravitational force and what each variable represents.

Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion

State and explain Kepler's Laws.

Describe orbits using Kepler's Laws (shape, location of the central body).

### What Students Should Be Able to Do

Use Kepler's Third Law to calculate orbital distances and periods.

Calculate the gravitational force between two objects.

Calculate the velocity of an object in orbit.

Calculate the altitude required for a geosynchronous orbit (assuming a circular orbit)*


* Note that while this assessment will not focus on UCM, orbits that are perfectly circular can be modeled by the equations of UCM, so you will want them on your index card
