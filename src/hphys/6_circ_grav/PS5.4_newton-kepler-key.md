15. Problem 1: The orbital period of Saturn is 29.46 years. Calculate its average distance to the sun.

4 sf

T^2 = s^3

T_{Saturn} = 29.46 , \text{years}

s_{Saturn} = \sqrt[3]{T_{Saturn}^2}  s_{Saturn} = \sqrt[3]{(29.46 , \text{years})^2}  s_{Saturn} = \sqrt[3]{867.8916 , \text{years}^2}  s_{Saturn} = 9.539 , \text{AU}

Problem 2: The average distance between the Sun and Neptune is 30.11 AU. Calculate the orbital period of Neptune in years. What is this value in days?

4 sf

T^2 = s^3

Conversion to Days

s_{Neptune} = 30.11 , \text{AU}

T_{Neptune} = \sqrt{s_{Neptune}^3}  T_{Neptune} = \sqrt{(30.11 , \text{AU})^3}  T_{Neptune} = \sqrt{27324.33331 , \text{AU}^3}  T_{Neptune} = 165.3 , \text{years}

T_{Neptune} = 165.3 , \text{years} \times \frac{365.25 , \text{days}}{1 , \text{year}}  T_{Neptune} = 60390 , \text{days}

Problem 4: The average distance between Mercury and the sun is 57.9 million km, find the orbital period of Mercury.

3 sf

T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM_{sun}} s^3

Conversion to Days

s_{Mercury} = 5.79 \times 10^{10} , \text{m}  G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} , \text{N m}^2/\text{kg}^2  M_{sun} = 1.989 \times 10^{30} , \text{kg}

T_{Mercury}^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{(6.674 \times 10^{-11})(1.989 \times 10^{30})} (5.79 \times 10^{10})^3  T_{Mercury}^2 = \frac{39.478}{(1.3274 \times 10^{20})} (1.933 \times 10^{32})  T_{Mercury}^2 = 5.729 \times 10^{15} , \text{s}^2  T_{Mercury} = \sqrt{5.729 \times 10^{15}} , \text{s}  T_{Mercury} = 7.569 \times 10^7 , \text{s}

T_{Mercury} = 7.569 \times 10^7 , \text{s} \times \frac{1 , \text{hour}}{3600 , \text{s}} \times \frac{1 , \text{day}}{24 , \text{hours}}  T_{Mercury} = 87.6 , \text{days}

Problem 5: Calculate the force of attraction between Earth and the sun when Earth is at periapsis.

3 sf

F = G \frac{M_{sun} m_{Earth}}{r^2}

G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} , \text{N m}^2/\text{kg}^2  M_{sun} = 1.989 \times 10^{30} , \text{kg}  m_{Earth} = 5.972 \times 10^{24} , \text{kg}  r_{periapsis} = 1.471 \times 10^{11} , \text{m}

F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{(1.989 \times 10^{30})(5.972 \times 10^{24})}{(1.471 \times 10^{11})^2}  F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{1.188 \times 10^{55}}{2.164 \times 10^{22}}  F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) (5.490 \times 10^{32})  F = 3.66 \times 10^{22} , \text{N}

Problem 6: Calculate the force of attraction on a 70 kg human standing on the surface of the sun.

3 sf

F = G \frac{M_{sun} m_{human}}{r_{sun}^2}

G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} , \text{N m}^2/\text{kg}^2  M_{sun} = 1.989 \times 10^{30} , \text{kg}  m_{human} = 70 , \text{kg}  r_{sun} = 6.957 \times 10^8 , \text{m}

F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{(1.989 \times 10^{30})(70)}{(6.957 \times 10^8)^2}  F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{1.3923 \times 10^{32}}{4.840 \times 10^{17}}  F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) (2.876 \times 10^{14})  F = 1.92 \times 10^4 , \text{N}

Problem 7: Jupiter and Saturn are an average of 646,270,000 km apart. Calculate the attractive force between the two bodies.

3 sf

F = G \frac{m_{Jupiter} m_{Saturn}}{r^2}

G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} , \text{N m}^2/\text{kg}^2  m_{Jupiter} = 1.898 \times 10^{27} , \text{kg}  m_{Saturn} = 5.683 \times 10^{26} , \text{kg}  r = 6.4627 \times 10^{11} , \text{m}

F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{(1.898 \times 10^{27})(5.683 \times 10^{26})}{(6.4627 \times 10^{11})^2}  F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{1.0786 \times 10^{54}}{4.1767 \times 10^{23}}  F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) (2.5825 \times 10^{30})  F = 1.72 \times 10^{20} , \text{N}

Problem 8: The gravitational pull of the moon is responsible for Earth’s tides. Calculate the attractive force exerted by the moon when it is at periapsis.

3 sf

F = G \frac{m_{Earth} m_{moon}}{r_{periapsis}^2}

G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} , \text{N m}^2/\text{kg}^2  m_{Earth} = 5.972 \times 10^{24} , \text{kg}  m_{moon} = 7.348 \times 10^{22} , \text{kg}  r_{periapsis} = 3.631 \times 10^8 , \text{m}

F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{(5.972 \times 10^{24})(7.348 \times 10^{22})}{(3.631 \times 10^8)^2}  F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{4.388 \times 10^{47}}{1.318 \times 10^{17}}  F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) (3.329 \times 10^{30})  F = 2.22 \times 10^{20} , \text{N}

Problem 9: The approximate orbital height of the international space station (ISS) is 400 km above the surface of the Earth. If the mass of the assembled station is 417,289 kg, calculate how much weaker the attractive force on the station is in orbit than on the ground. Hint: sketch the two situations and note the distances

3 sf (for ratio)

Force on the Ground

Force in Orbit

Ratio (Force on Ground / Force in Orbit)

Percentage Weaker



F_{ground} = G \frac{M_{Earth} m_{ISS}}{r_{Earth}^2}  r_{Earth} = 6.371 \times 10^6 , \text{m}  F_{ground} = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{(5.972 \times 10^{24})(417289)}{(6.371 \times 10^6)^2}  F_{ground} = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{2.490 \times 10^{30}}{4.059 \times 10^{13}}  F_{ground} = 4.10 \times 10^6 , \text{N}

F_{orbit} = G \frac{M_{Earth} m_{ISS}}{(r_{Earth} + h)^2}  h = 4.00 \times 10^5 , \text{m}  r_{Earth} + h = 6.771 \times 10^6 , \text{m}  F_{orbit} = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{(5.972 \times 10^{24})(417289)}{(6.771 \times 10^6)^2}  F_{orbit} = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{2.490 \times 10^{30}}{4.584 \times 10^{13}}  F_{orbit} = 3.63 \times 10^6 , \text{N}

\frac{F_{ground}}{F_{orbit}} = \frac{4.10 \times 10^6}{3.63 \times 10^6} = 1.13

(1 - \frac{1}{1.13}) \times 100% = (1 - 0.885) \times 100% = 11.5%

The attractive force on the ISS in orbit is approximately 11.5% weaker than on the ground.

Problem 10: Suppose that two objects attract each other with a gravitational force of 16 units. If the distance between the two objects is doubled, what is the new force of attraction between the two objects?



Original Force

New Force (distance doubled)



F_1 = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r_1^2} = 16 , \text{units}

F_2 = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{(2r_1)^2} = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{4r_1^2} = \frac{1}{4} \left( G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r_1^2} \right)  F_2 = \frac{1}{4} (16 , \text{units}) = 4 , \text{units}

The new force of attraction is 4 units.

Problem 11: Suppose that two objects attract each other with a gravitational force of 16 units. If the distance between the two objects is reduced in half, then what is the new force of attraction between the two objects?



Original Force

New Force (distance halved)



F_1 = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r_1^2} = 16 , \text{units}

F_2 = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{(\frac{1}{2}r_1)^2} = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{\frac{1}{4}r_1^2} = 4 \left( G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r_1^2} \right)  F_2 = 4 (16 , \text{units}) = 64 , \text{units}

The new force of attraction is 64 units.
