Newton’s and Kepler’s Laws Problem Set

—

In this problem set, use the mathematical relationships described by Kepler and Newton to answer questions about the forces and orbits of bodies in space. This sheet is designed to be accompanied by the Planetary Fact Sheet Metric handout.

  1. The orbital period of Saturn is 29.46 years. Calculate its average distance to the sun.

Since both objects orbit the same parent, 1 AU and 1 Year can be substituted for Earth’s values, allowing use of the simple form of Kepler:

4 sf $T^2 = s^3$
$T_{Saturn} = 29.46 \,y$ $s_{Saturn} = \sqrt[3]{T_{Saturn}^2}$
$s_{Saturn} = \sqrt[3]{29.46 \, years}$
$s_{Saturn} = 9.539 AU$

  1. The average distance between the Sun and Neptune is 30.11 AU. Calculate the orbital period of Neptune in years. What is this value in days?

    Again, both objects orbit the same parent so the simpler form of Kepler can be used:

    4 sf $T^2 = s^3$
    $D_{Neptune} = 30.11 \,AU$ $s_{Neptune} = \sqrt[3]{T_{Neptune}^2}$
    $s_{Saturn} = \sqrt[3]{29.46 \, years}$
    $s_{Saturn} = 9.539 AU$

  3. The average distance between Mercury and the sun is 57.9 million km, find the orbital period of Mercury.

3 sf

[ T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM_{\text{sun}}} s^3 ]

Conversion to Days:

[ s_{\text{Mercury}} = 5.79 \times 10^{10} \, \text{m}, \, G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \, \text{N m}^2/\text{kg}^2, \, M_{\text{sun}} = 1.989 \times 10^{30} \, \text{kg} ]

[ T_{\text{Mercury}}^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{(6.674 \times 10^{-11})(1.989 \times 10^{30})} (5.79 \times 10^{10})^3 ]

[ T_{\text{Mercury}}^2 = \frac{39.478}{1.3274 \times 10^{20}} (1.933 \times 10^{32}) ]

[ T_{\text{Mercury}}^2 = 5.729 \times 10^{15} \, \text{s}^2, \, T_{\text{Mercury}} = \sqrt{5.729 \times 10^{15}} \, \text{s} ]

[ T_{\text{Mercury}} = 7.569 \times 10^7 \, \text{s} ]

Convert to days:

[ T_{\text{Mercury}} = 7.569 \times 10^7 \, \text{s} \times \frac{1 \, \text{hour}}{3600 \, \text{s}} \times \frac{1 \, \text{day}}{24 \, \text{hours}} ]

[ T_{\text{Mercury}} = 87.6 \, \text{days} ]

  1. Calculate the force of attraction between Earth and the sun when Earth is at periapsis.

3 sf

[ F = G \frac{M_{\text{sun}} m_{\text{Earth}}}{r^2} ]

[ G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \, \text{N m}^2/\text{kg}^2, \, M_{\text{sun}} = 1.989 \times 10^{30} \, \text{kg}, \, m_{\text{Earth}} = 5.972 \times 10^{24} \, \text{kg}, \, r = 1.471 \times 10^{11} \, \text{m} ]

[ F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{(1.989 \times 10^{30})(5.972 \times 10^{24})}{(1.471 \times 10^{11})^2} ]

[ F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{1.188 \times 10^{55}}{2.164 \times 10^{22}} ]

[ F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) (5.490 \times 10^{32}) ]

[ F = 3.66 \times 10^{22} \, \text{N} ]

  1. Calculate the force of attraction on a 70 kg human standing on the surface of the sun.

3 sf

[ F = G \frac{M_{\text{sun}} m_{\text{human}}}{r_{\text{sun}}^2} ]

[ G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \, \text{N m}^2/\text{kg}^2, \, M_{\text{sun}} = 1.989 \times 10^{30} \, \text{kg}, \, m_{\text{human}} = 70 \, \text{kg}, \, r_{\text{sun}} = 6.957 \times 10^8 \, \text{m} ]

[ F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{(1.989 \times 10^{30})(70)}{(6.957 \times 10^8)^2} ]

[ F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{1.3923 \times 10^{32}}{4.840 \times 10^{17}} ]

[ F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) (2.876 \times 10^{14}) ]

[ F = 1.92 \times 10^4 \, \text{N} ]

  4. Suppose that two objects attract each other with a gravitational force of 16 units. If the distance between the two objects is doubled, what is the new force of attraction between the two objects?

  5. Suppose that two objects attract each other with a gravitational force of 16 units. If the distance between the two objects is reduced in half, then what is the new force of attraction between the two objects?

Original Force:

[ F_1 = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r_1^2} = 16 \, \text{units} ]

New Force (distance halved):

[ F_2 = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{\left(\frac{1}{2}r_1\right)^2} = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{\frac{1}{4}r_1^2} = 4 \left( G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r_1^2} \right) ]

[ F_2 = 4 (16 \, \text{units}) = 64 \, \text{units} ]

The new force of attraction is 64 units.
