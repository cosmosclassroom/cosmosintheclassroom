👨‍🍳 FAQ: From the Kitchen
"Will AI replace me as the teacher?"
About as much as a sous chef replaces Gordon Ramsay.

AI handles the prep work (detailed activities, timing, materials). You handle the artistry (questioning, adaptation, student relationships). You're still the head chef—now you just have world-class kitchen support.

"What if my students need something different mid-lesson?"
That's when you taste and adjust!

Great chefs don't follow recipes blindly. See confusion? Add a connecting question. Students racing ahead? Introduce a challenge extension. The AI gives you ingredients—you create the perfect dish for YOUR diners.

"How do I know if this 'meal' is working?"
Watch your students' faces.

Are they asking follow-up questions? Building on each other's ideas? Saying "Wait, that means..." or "But what if...?" Those are the signs of students who are truly hungry for learning, not just being fed information.

"Can I mix different 'cooking styles' in one unit?"
Absolutely! The best menus have variety.

Start with investigation appetizers, move to skill-building main courses, finish with creative application desserts. Or go full tasting-menu with multiple small discoveries. Your kitchen, your rules.

"What if I'm not good at asking Socratic questions?"
Every great chef started somewhere.

The AI generates not just content but suggested question progressions. Think of them as your "tasting notes"—guides for drawing out the flavors your students are discovering. Practice makes perfect, and your sous chef has your back.

🎭 The Art of Socratic Service
Instead of serving answers, serve questions:
❌ Short-order style: "Leaves change color because chlorophyll breaks down."

✅ Socratic style: "You separated four different colors from this 'green' leaf. What does that tell you about what we call 'green'?"

❌ Fast-food style: "The formula for kinetic energy is KE = ½mv²."

✅ Socratic style: "Your marble went farther when you released it from higher up. What do you think changed about the marble's energy?"
