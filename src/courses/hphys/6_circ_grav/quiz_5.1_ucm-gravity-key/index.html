4. (b) Calculate the magnitude of the centripetal force acting on the stone at the bottom of the swing.

  • At the bottom of the swing, the sum of all forces acting on the mass is:

    \[\Sigma{F_r} = F_t + F_g\]

    To maintain the circle, the object needs to experience a net radial force of $F_c = \frac{m \cdot v^2}{r}$

    3 significant figures $F_r = \frac{mv^2}{r}$
    $m = 0.50 \,kg$
    $v_t = 8.0 \,m/s $
    $r = 1.2 \,m$    		 $F_r = \frac{(0.50 kg)(8.0 m/s)^2}{1.2 m}$
    $F_r = 26.666… N$
    $F_r \approx 26.7 N$

Since the centripetal force is provided through the tension, $F_t$ must provide this much force plus the force required to overcome gravity at the bottom of the swing. Since gravity is working against the centripetal: $\Sigma{F_r} = F_t + F_g \ F_t = \Sigma{F_r} - F_g$ $F_t = \frac{m \cdot v^2}{r} - (- m \cdot a_g) $ $F_t = \frac{0.50 kg \cdot (8.0 \frac{m}{s})^2}{1.2 m} + (0.50 \, kg \cdot 9.80 \frac{m}{s^2})$ $F_t = 31.56666… N$ $F_t = 32 N$

7. (a) If the hammer makes 1 revolution in 0.990 s, calculate the centripetal acceleration of the hammer.

Given:

  • Radius (r) = 1.80 m [cite: 16]
  • Period (T) = 0.990 s [cite: 18]

Formulas:

  • $v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}$
  • $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}$

Calculations:

  • $v = \frac{2\pi (1.80 m)}{0.990 s}$
  • $v = \frac{11.3097}{0.990} m/s$
  • $v = 11.424 m/s$
  • $a_c = \frac{(11.424 m/s)^2}{1.80 m}$
  • $a_c = \frac{130.508}{1.80} m/s^2$
  • $a_c = 72.504 m/s^2$
  • $a_c \approx 72.5 \frac{m}{s^2}$

7. (b) What is the tension in the chain?

Given:

  • Mass (m) = 7.0 kg [cite: 16]
  • $a_c$ = 72.5 $\frac{m}{s^2}$ (calculated above)

Formula:

  • $F_T = F_c = ma_c$

Calculation:

  • $F_T = (7.0 kg)(72.5 \frac{m}{s^2})$
  • $F_T = 507.5 N$
  • $F_T \approx 508 N$

8. A satellite is in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) making 11.25 orbits per Earth day. Its orbit is perfectly circular with an altitude of 650.0 km above Earth’s surface. Ignoring the radius of Earth, calculate the period of the spacecraft (in minutes).

Given:

  • 11.25 orbits per day [cite: 20]

Formula:

  • $Period (T) = \frac{Time}{Number \, of \, orbits}$

Calculation:

  • Convert days to minutes: 1 day * 24 hours/day * 60 minutes/hour = 1440 minutes
  • $T = \frac{1440 \, minutes}{11.25 \, orbits}$
  • $T = 128 \, minutes$

9. Calculate the velocity of the spacecraft. Ignore the radius of Earth.

Given:

  • Period (T) = 128 minutes = 7680 seconds
  • Altitude = 650.0 km = 650,000 m

Formula:

  • $v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}$

Calculation:

  • $v = \frac{2\pi (650,000 m)}{7680 s}$
  • $v = \frac{4084070.45}{7680} m/s$
  • $v = 531.78 m/s$

10. Calculate the centripetal acceleration of the spacecraft. Ignore the radius of Earth.

Given:

  • Velocity (v) = 531.78 m/s
  • Radius (r) = 650,000 m

Formula:

  • $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}$

Calculation:

  • $a_c = \frac{(531.78 m/s)^2}{650,000 m}$
  • $a_c = \frac{282890.8}{650000} m/s^2$
  • $a_c = 0.4352 m/s^2$
  • $a_c \approx 0.435 \frac{m}{s^2}$

11. Calculate the gravitational force between two students sitting 1.50 m apart. Assume each student has a mass of 70.0 kg.

Given:

  • $m_1$ = 70.0 kg [cite: 23]
  • $m_2$ = 70.0 kg [cite: 23]
  • r = 1.50 m [cite: 23]
  • G = $6.674 \times 10^{-11} N \cdot m^2/kg^2$

Formula:

  • $F = G\frac{m_1m_2}{r^2}$

Calculation:

  • $F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11} N \cdot m^2/kg^2) \frac{(70.0 kg)(70.0 kg)}{(1.50 m)^2}$
  • $F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{4900}{2.25} N$
  • $F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11})(2177.78) N$
  • $F = 1.453 \times 10^{-7} N$
  • $F \approx 1.45 \times 10^{-7} N$
