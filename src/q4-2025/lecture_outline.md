# Honors Physics: Revised Sequential Lecture Outline

## Unit 8: Electromagnetism
*Building on recent harmonics and waves concepts*

### Lecture 8.1: Electromagnetic Waves (90 minutes)
- **Learning Objectives:**
  - Describe properties of electromagnetic waves
  - Relate wave concepts from harmonics unit to electromagnetic waves
  - Understand the electromagnetic spectrum

- **Topics:**
  - EM Wave Speed: c = fλ
  - Speed of Light: c = 3×10⁸ m/s
  - Photon Energy: E = hf
  - Electromagnetic Spectrum
  - Connection to previously studied wave phenomena (Unit 7)

- **Activities:**
  - Interactive simulation: Exploring the electromagnetic spectrum
  - Group work: Connecting wave concepts to electromagnetic phenomena

### Lecture 8.2: Magnetic Fields and Forces (90 minutes)
- **Learning Objectives:**
  - Calculate forces on moving charges in magnetic fields
  - Determine magnetic fields created by current-carrying wires
  - Apply the right-hand rule to magnetic force problems

- **Topics:**
  - Magnetic Force on Moving Charge: F = qv×B
  - Magnetic Force on Current: F = I(L×B)
  - Magnetic Field of a Wire: B = μ₀I/(2πr)
  - Right-Hand Rule applications

- **Activities:**
  - Demonstration: Forces on current-carrying wires
  - Lab: Measuring magnetic fields around conductors

### Lecture 8.3: Electromagnetic Induction (90 minutes)
- **Learning Objectives:**
  - Explain Faraday's Law and applications of electromagnetic induction
  - Calculate induced EMF in various scenarios
  - Apply Lenz's Law to determine direction of induced current

- **Topics:**
  - Faraday's Law: ε = -dΦ/dt
  - Lenz's Law and conservation of energy

- **Activities:**
  - Demonstration: Electromagnetic induction with coils and magnets
  - Problem-solving: Calculating induced EMF in various scenarios

## Unit 9: Electricity
*From static to current electricity*

### Lecture 9.1: Electric Charge and Field (90 minutes)
- **Learning Objectives:**
  - Apply Coulomb's Law to calculate electric forces
  - Understand the concept of electric fields and their representation
  - Calculate electric field strength from various charge distributions

- **Topics:**
  - Coulomb's Law: F = k|q₁q₂|/r²
  - Coulomb Constant: k = 1/(4πε₀) = 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²
  - Electric Field: E = F/q
  - Point Charge Field: E = k|q|/r²
  - Conservation and Quantization of Charge

- **Activities:**
  - Demonstrations: Electrostatic attractions and repulsions
  - Field mapping exercise: Plotting field lines around various charge configurations

### Lecture 9.2: Electric Potential and Capacitance (90 minutes)
- **Learning Objectives:**
  - Relate electric field to electric potential
  - Calculate potential differences and electric potential energy
  - Understand capacitance and energy storage in capacitors

- **Topics:**
  - Electric Potential: V = U/q
  - Point Charge Potential: V = k|q|/r
  - Field-Potential Relationship: E = ΔV/d
  - Potential Difference: ΔV = V₂ - V₁
  - Capacitance: C = Q/V
  - Parallel Plate Capacitor: C = ε₀A/d
  - Energy in Capacitors: U = ½CV² = ½QV

- **Activities:**
  - Lab: Measuring capacitance of various capacitors
  - Problem-solving workshop: Electric potential calculations using algebraic formulas

### Lecture 9.3: Current and Resistance (90 minutes)
- **Learning Objectives:**
  - Define electric current and understand its microscopic basis
  - Apply Ohm's Law to simple circuits
  - Calculate resistance of conductors with different geometries

- **Topics:**
  - Current as flow of charge: I = Q/t (aligned with Giancoli Ch. 18)
  - Drift Velocity concept (qualitative understanding)
  - Ohm's Law: R = V/I (Holt Physics Ch. 17)
  - Resistance of a Wire: R = ρL/A
  - Conventional Current vs. Electron Flow

- **Activities:**
  - Demonstration: Current flow visualization
  - Lab: Verifying Ohm's Law experimentally (based on Giancoli Lab Manual)

### Lecture 9.4: Circuit Analysis (90 minutes)
- **Learning Objectives:**
  - Analyze series and parallel circuits
  - Apply Kirchhoff's rules to solve multi-loop circuits
  - Calculate power and energy in electric circuits

- **Topics:**
  - Series Circuits: R_series = R₁ + R₂ + ... (Holt Physics Ch. 18)
  - Parallel Circuits: 1/R_parallel = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + ...
  - Kirchhoff's Current Law: Sum of currents entering a junction equals sum leaving
  - Kirchhoff's Voltage Law: Sum of voltage drops equals sum of voltage rises
  - Electric Power: P = IV = I²R = V²/R (Giancoli Ch. 19)

- **Activities:**
  - Circuit building lab: Series and parallel configurations
  - Problem-solving session: Circuit analysis without calculus

## Unit 10: Optics
*Connecting wave-particle duality to geometric optics*

### Lecture 10.1: Wave-Particle Nature of Light (90 minutes)
- **Learning Objectives:**
  - Connect electromagnetics concepts to optics
  - Explain the dual nature of light
  - Apply wave concepts to understand light behavior

- **Topics:**
  - Light as EM Wave
  - Wave-Particle Duality
  - Photon Energy: E = hf
  - Index of Refraction: n = c/v
  - Connection to Units 8 (Electromagnetism) and previous wave studies

- **Activities:**
  - Demonstration: Photoelectric effect simulation
  - Discussion: Historical development of wave and particle theories of light

### Lecture 10.2: Wave Optics - Interference and Diffraction (90 minutes)
- **Learning Objectives:**
  - Explain the principle of superposition in wave interference
  - Calculate positions of maxima and minima in interference patterns
  - Analyze diffraction patterns from various apertures

- **Topics:**
  - Double-Slit Interference: d sin(θ) = mλ (constructive), d sin(θ) = (m+1/2)λ (destructive)
  - Single Slit Diffraction: sin(θ) = λ/a
  - Diffraction Grating: sin(θ) = mλ/d
  - Interference patterns and applications

- **Activities:**
  - Virtual simulation: Double-slit experiment
  - Lab: Analyzing interference and diffraction patterns

### Lecture 10.3: Wave Optics - Polarization (90 minutes)
- **Learning Objectives:**
  - Understand the concept of polarization as a wave phenomenon
  - Apply Malus' Law to calculate intensity of polarized light
  - Explain applications of polarization in technology and nature

- **Topics:**
  - Linear Polarization
  - Malus' Law: I = I₀cos²(θ)
  - Polarization by reflection
  - Polarizers and wave filtering
  - Applications of polarization

- **Activities:**
  - Demonstration: Polarizing filters and light intensity
  - Investigation: Polarization in LCD screens and 3D glasses

### Lecture 10.4: Geometric Optics - Reflection and Refraction (90 minutes)
- **Learning Objectives:**
  - Explain the law of reflection and apply it to simple optical systems
  - Understand and apply Snell's law to calculate angles of refraction
  - Describe total internal reflection and calculate critical angles

- **Topics:**
  - Law of Reflection: θᵢ = θᵣ
  - Snell's Law: n₁sin(θ₁) = n₂sin(θ₂)
  - Total Internal Reflection: sin(θc) = n₂/n₁
  - Fiber optics and applications

- **Activities:**
  - Demonstration: Laser beam reflection and refraction through different media
  - Problem-solving session: Calculating angles using reflection and refraction laws

### Lecture 10.5: Geometric Optics - Lenses and Mirrors (90 minutes)
- **Learning Objectives:**
  - Apply the thin lens equation to various optical scenarios
  - Calculate magnification for converging and diverging lenses
  - Distinguish between real and virtual images formed by mirrors

- **Topics:**
  - Thin Lens Equation: 1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ
  - Linear Magnification: M = -dᵢ/dₒ
  - Mirror Equation: 1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ
  - Spherical Mirror: f = R/2
  - Types of lenses and mirrors

- **Activities:**
  - Lab: Determining focal length of various lenses
  - Ray diagram workshop: Tracing light paths through optical systems
