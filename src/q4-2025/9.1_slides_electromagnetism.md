## Magnetic Field of a Current-Carrying Wire

<div style="display: flex; justify-content: space-between; align-items: center;">
<div style="width: 50%;">

**Long straight wire:**
- $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$
- $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}$ T·m/A
- Field lines form concentric circles
- Direction determined by right-hand rule

## Forces on Moving Charges

<div style="display: flex; justify-content: space-between; align-items: center;">
<div style="width: 50%;">

**Lorentz Force Law:**
- $\vec{F} = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$
- Force perpendicular to both velocity and field
- Magnitude: $F = qvB\sin\theta$
- No force when parallel to field ($\theta = 0°$)
- Maximum force when perpendicular ($\theta = 90°$)

</div>
<div style="width: 45%;">

![height:250px](https://placeholder.pics/svg/300/DEDEDE/555555/Magnetic%20Force%20on%20Charge)

</div>
</div>

## Motion of Charged Particle in Magnetic Field

<div style="display: flex; justify-content: space-between; align-items: center;">
<div style="width: 50%;">

**Circular Motion:**
- When $\vec{v} \perp \vec{B}$, particle moves in circular path
- Radius: $r = \frac{mv}{qB}$
- Period: $T = \frac{2\pi m}{qB}$
- Applications: mass spectrometers, cyclotrons

</div>
<div style="width: 45%;">

![height:250px](https://placeholder.pics/svg/300/DEDEDE/555555/Circular%20Motion%20in%20B-field)

## Forces on Current-Carrying Wires

<div style="display: flex; justify-content: space-between; align-items: center;">
<div style="width: 50%;">

**Force on a Wire:**
- $\vec{F} = I(\vec{L} \times \vec{B})$
- Magnitude: $F = ILB\sin\theta$
- Current is moving charge: $I = nqvA$
- Applications: electric motors, loudspeakers

</div>

## Right-Hand Rules

<div style="display: flex; justify-content: space-between; align-items: center;">
<div style="width: 50%;">

**Right-Hand Rule #1 (Current → Field)**
- Wrap fingers around wire in direction of current
- Thumb points in field direction

**Right-Hand Rule #2 (Force on Moving Charge)**
- Fingers in direction of B
- Thumb in direction of v
- Palm pushes in direction of F (for positive q)

# Lecture 5B: Electromagnetic Induction and EM Waves

---

## Faraday's Law

<div style="display: flex; justify-content: space-between; align-items: center;">
<div style="width: 50%;">

**Electromagnetic Induction:**
- Changing magnetic flux induces EMF
- $\varepsilon = -\frac{d\Phi}{dt}$
- Magnetic flux: $\Phi = BA\cos\theta$
- For N-turn coil: $\varepsilon = -N\frac{d\Phi}{dt}$

</div>
<div style="width: 45%;">

![height:250px](https://placeholder.pics/svg/300/DEDEDE/555555/Faraday's%20Law)

</div>
</div>

---

## Methods of Inducing EMF

<div style="display: flex; justify-content: space-between; align-items: center;">
<div style="width: 50%;">

1. **Changing magnetic field** (B changing)
2. **Changing area** (A changing)
3. **Changing orientation** (θ changing)
4. **Moving conductor in field**

All cause change in magnetic flux!

</div>
<div style="width: 45%;">

![height:250px](https://placeholder.pics/svg/300/DEDEDE/555555/Induction%20Methods)

## Lenz's Law

<div style="display: flex; justify-content: space-between; align-items: center;">
<div style="width: 50%;">

**The negative sign in Faraday's Law:**
- Induced current creates field that opposes the change
- Conservation of energy principle
- Demonstrations:
  - Falling magnet through copper tube
  - Eddy currents in conductors

## Electromagnetic Waves

<div style="display: flex; justify-content: space-between; align-items: center;">
<div style="width: 50%;">

**Key Properties:**
- Self-propagating oscillations of E and B fields
- Transverse waves
- Travel at speed of light ($c = 3 \times 10^8$ m/s)
- No medium required (can travel in vacuum)
- Relationship: $c = f\lambda$
- Energy transport: $E = hf$ (photon energy)

</div>

## Connecting Optics and Electromagnetism

<div style="display: flex; justify-content: space-between; align-items: center;">
<div style="width: 50%;">

**Light as an EM Wave:**
- Visible light is EM radiation (400-700 nm)
- Refraction explained by EM wave speed: $n = \frac{c}{v}$
- Polarization is orientation of E-field
- Wave-particle duality: $E = hf$
- Colors correspond to different frequencies

<div style="display: flex; justify-content: space-between; align-items: center;">
<div style="width: 100%;">

- Electric and magnetic phenomena are fundamentally connected
- Moving charges create magnetic fields
- Changing magnetic fields create electric fields

- Foundation for modern physics, technology, and communications
