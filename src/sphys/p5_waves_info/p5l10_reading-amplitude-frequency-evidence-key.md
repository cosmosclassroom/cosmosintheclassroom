# Amplitude/Frequency Evidence

### Do EM radiation’s frequency and/or amplitude affect the risk of harm?


|  | High-amplitude (brighter)  EM radiation | Low-amplitude (dimmer) EM radiation |
| -----: | :----- | :----- |
| **Infrared (lower frequency)** | Can break down skin’s collagen, contributing to wrinkles (in very high amplitudes) |  Can help wounds heal, reduce swelling, and treat acne. Does not cause sunburn or skin cancer |
|**Visible light (middle frequency)** | Can cause skin redness in people with very sensitive skin (bright SAD lamps) | Treats symptoms of Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD). Not dangerous and has no negative health effects  |
| **Ultraviolet (high frequency)** | Can make skin darker over time, similar to a suntan or sunburn. Contributes to skin cancer and eyelid cancer |  Used to harden gel nail polish, make bodily fluids glow for crime scene investigation, and harden resin in 3D printers. May increase risk of skin cancer |
|  **X-ray (higher frequency)**  | Used in CT scans and nuclear imaging. Exposure to many scans can increase cancer risk |  Used in medical imaging. Can be harmful, even at low amplitudes |



## Reflection Questions

1. What other measurements might show us more evidence about how different amplitudes and frequencies of EM radiation interact with matter (other than human beings)?

   **Multiple Possible Responses**

- "We could measure how different amplitudes and frequencies of EM radiation affect the temperature of different materials. For example, we could test how quickly various materials heat up when exposed to different types of infrared radiation. This might show us how the energy of the radiation is absorbed and converted into heat, which could be relevant to understanding how it affects human tissue."

- "Another measurement could involve looking at how different EM radiation types affect the growth of plants or microorganisms. We could expose plants to different levels of UV radiation and measure their growth rate or look for signs of damage. Or we could see how different frequencies affect bacteria growth. This could give us more information about how EM radiation interacts with living cells in general, not just human cells."

1. Based on your notes in the table about this evidence, what do you think causes more harm to human beings: high-amplitude EM radiation or high-frequency EM radiation? Why? 

- The text suggests that both high-amplitude and high-frequency EM radiation can cause harm to humans.
- High-amplitude radiation, as seen with infrared and X-rays, can lead to skin damage and increased cancer risk with repeated exposure.   
- High-frequency radiation, particularly UV and X-rays, is strongly associated with cancer and can penetrate deeper into the body, causing more significant harm.   
- It could be argued that high-frequency EM radiation may pose a greater risk due to its ability to cause cancer and penetrate human tissue more effectively.
