Data Interpretation Activity: Wave-Particle Duality of Light

### Introduction

This activity introduces students to the wave-particle duality of light by examining mock data tables and graphs comparing the frequency and amplitude of light waves and their abilities to (a) damage living cells and (b) activate the photoelectric effect.

Graph IdeasYou can have students create the following types of graphs:For Cell Damage:Bar Graph 1: Frequency on the x-axis, Cell Damage Level on the y-axis (grouping by similar frequencies). This should show a trend of higher frequency leading to more damage.Scatter Plot 1: Amplitude on the x-axis, Cell Damage Level on the y-axis (grouping by similar frequencies). This should show that while higher amplitude might increase the extent of damage at damaging frequencies, it doesn't cause damage at lower frequencies.For Photoelectric Effect:Scatter Plot 2: Frequency on the x-axis, Kinetic Energy of Ejected Electrons on the y-axis. This should show a positive correlation, with a threshold frequency below which no electrons are ejected. Amplitude should not affect the kinetic energy.Bar Graph 2: Frequency on the x-axis, Number of Ejected Electrons on the y-axis (comparing different amplitudes at the same frequency above the threshold). This should show that at frequencies above the threshold, higher amplitude leads to more ejected electrons.Bar Graph 3: Amplitude on the x-axis, Number of Ejected Electrons on the y-axis (grouping by similar frequencies above the threshold). This should clearly demonstrate the direct relationship between amplitude and the number of ejected electrons (intensity).Analysis Questions for StudentsRelating to Cell Damage:Observe Table 1 and Graph 1.
