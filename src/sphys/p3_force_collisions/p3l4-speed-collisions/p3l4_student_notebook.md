### Understanding Velocity vs. Speed

**Velocity vs. Speed:**
- Speed = how fast something moves (magnitude only)
- Velocity = speed WITH direction (vector quantity)

In our experiments:
- RIGHT = positive velocity
- LEFT = negative velocity

Example: -5 m/s means 5 meters per second to the LEFT

### Mathematical Relationships

**Collision E Calculation (green cart 2× mass):**

Green cart:
- Initial v = 0.79 m/s
- Final v = 0.33 m/s
- Δv = 0.33 - 0.79 = -0.46 m/s

Blue cart:
- Initial v = 0 m/s
- Final v = 0.92 m/s
- Δv = 0.92 - 0 = 0.92 m/s

Ratio of masses: m₁/m₂ = 2m/m = 2
Ratio of velocity changes: Δv₁/Δv₂ = -0.46/0.92 = -0.5

So: m₁/m₂ = -Δv₂/Δv₁ = 2

THE BIG FORMULA: m₁/m₂ = -Δv₂/Δv₁

This means: The ratio of masses equals the negative reciprocal of the ratio of velocity changes!

### Checkpoint: Mass & Velocity Changes

1. If a 3 kg cart collides with a 1 kg cart, ratio of velocity changes?
   
   m₁/m₂ = 3/1 = 3
   m₁/m₂ = -Δv₂/Δv₁
   3 = -Δv₂/Δv₁
   Δv₂/Δv₁ = -3
   
   The 1 kg cart's velocity change will be 3 times larger than the 3 kg cart's change.

2. How would increasing mass affect velocity change?
   
   Increasing the mass of an object decreases its velocity change in a collision.
   If mass doubles, velocity change is halved.

3. Why do smaller vehicles experience greater velocity changes?
   
   According to our formula m₁/m₂ = -Δv₂/Δv₁, the lighter vehicle (smaller m) will experience a 
   proportionally larger velocity change (larger Δv). This is why collisions between trucks and 
   cars are more dangerous for the car occupants.

4. A 1500 kg car collides with a 3000 kg truck. Truck's Δv = 2 m/s. Calculate car's Δv.
   
   m₁/m₂ = -Δv₂/Δv₁
   1500/3000 = -Δv₁truck/Δvcar
   1/2 = -2m/s/Δvcar
   Δvcar = -(2m/s ÷ 1/2) = -(2m/s × 2) = -4 m/s
   
   Car's velocity change = 4 m/s (twice the truck's change)

### Real-World Applications

Problem: 20,000 kg truck collides with 20 kg shopping cart

1. Mass ratio:
   m₁/m₂ = 20,000/20 = 1,000

2. Expected velocity change ratio:
   m₁/m₂ = -Δv₂/Δv₁
   1,000 = -Δvcart/Δvtruck
   Δvcart/Δvtruck = -1,000

3. If truck's velocity changes by -0.01 m/s:
   Δvcart/(-0.01 m/s) = -1,000
   Δvcart = -0.01 m/s × (-1,000) = 10 m/s
   
   Shopping cart's velocity changes by 10 m/s (22 mph)!
   
   This is why momentum conservation is important for vehicle safety design.

## Exit Ticket

1. Define velocity and explain how it differs from speed:
   
   Velocity is a vector quantity that includes both speed (magnitude) and direction. Speed is just 
   the magnitude portion without directional information. For example, 50 mph north is a velocity, 
   while just 50 mph is a speed.

2. Explain relationship between mass ratios and velocity change ratios:
   
   The ratio of masses equals the negative reciprocal of the ratio of velocity changes:
   m₁/m₂ = -Δv₂/Δv₁
   
   This means the more massive object experiences less velocity change, and the ratio is inversely 
   proportional to the mass ratio.

3. Why do heavier vehicles provide better protection in collisions?
   
   According to our mathematical relationship, a heavier vehicle experiences a smaller velocity change 
   in a collision. Since injuries are related to rapid changes in velocity (acceleration/deceleration), 
   occupants of heavier vehicles experience less violent motion changes, reducing injury risk. This 
   is why SUVs are often considered "safer" than smaller cars in collisions.

4. 1000 kg car collides with 2000 kg SUV. Car's velocity changes by 6 m/s. Calculate SUV's change.
   
   m₁/m₂ = -Δv₂/Δv₁
   1000/2000 = -ΔvSUV/Δvcar
   1/2 = -ΔvSUV/6m/s
   ΔvSUV = -6m/s × (1/2) = -3 m/s
   
   The SUV's velocity changes by 3 m/s, half the car's change.
