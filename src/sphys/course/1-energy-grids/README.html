Unit P.1: The Invisible Currents

Energy Flow from Earth’s Systems (Electricity Unit)

🔌 Our Big Question

How can we design more reliable systems to meet our communities’ energy needs?

📚 Unit Overview

This unit investigates the Texas power crisis of February 2021 as our anchoring phenomenon. Through this real-world lens, students explore electrical energy transfer, system stability, and the engineering trade-offs involved in designing reliable power grids.

Duration: 26 class meetings (85 chunks)
Quarter: 1
Prerequisites: Basic algebra, unit conversions

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

  • Explain how energy transfers through electrical systems using particle and field models
  • Analyze circuit diagrams using Ohm’s Law and power equations
  • Model the relationship between energy supply, demand, and grid stability
  • Evaluate engineering design solutions using criteria, constraints, and trade-offs
  • Argue for community energy solutions based on scientific evidence and stakeholder needs

🧠 Key Physics Concepts

Energy & Circuits

  • Energy vs. Power (Joules vs. Watts)
  • Voltage, Current, and Resistance
  • Ohm’s Law: V = IR
  • Power equations: P = IV, P = I²R
  • Series and parallel circuits

Systems Thinking

  • Electric and magnetic fields
  • Energy transfer and conservation
  • Grid infrastructure modeling
  • System stability and failure modes

Engineering Design

  • Criteria vs. constraints
  • Trade-offs in energy systems
  • Cost, reliability, and environmental impacts
  • Stakeholder perspectives in policy decisions

🔬 Hexagon Labs

Lab 1.1: Analyzing Blackout Data

Students investigate datasets from the February 2021 Texas crisis to identify when demand exceeded supply and which energy sources failed.

Skills: Data analysis, correlation vs. causation, timeline construction

Lab 1.2: Modeling Energy Transfer in Wires

Students analyze simulated data to determine how wire properties (length, thickness, material) affect resistance and energy loss.

Skills: Variable analysis, graphing relationships, efficiency calculations

Lab 1.3: Engineering Design Tradeoffs

Students use decision matrices to evaluate solar, wind, and natural gas based on cost, reliability, environmental impact, and land use.

Skills: Multi-criteria analysis, weighted scoring, evidence-based argumentation

🗣️ Key Vocabulary

Term Simple Definition
Circuit A complete path for electricity to flow
Voltage Electrical “pressure” that pushes current
Current The flow rate of electric charge
Resistance Opposition to electrical flow
Power How fast electrical energy is used
Energy Grid The network that delivers electricity from plants to homes
Trade-off Accepting one cost to gain another benefit
Constraint A limitation that must be satisfied in design
