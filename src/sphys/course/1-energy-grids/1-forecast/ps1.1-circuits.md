# Problem Set 1 — Circuits & Power

Objective: Apply fundamental concepts of energy, power, and circuits to solve quantitative problems. This practice will solidify understanding of how voltage, current, resistance, power, and energy relate.

Reference: Section 1.4 of Unit 1 Flexbook.

---

## Key formulae

- Ohm's law: \(V = I R\)  
- Power (basic): \(P = V I\)  
- Alternate power forms: \(P = I^2 R\) and \(P = \dfrac{V^2}{R}\)  
- Energy and power: \(E = P t\)  (where \(t\) is time in seconds)

## Part 2 — Ohm's Law Practice (\(V = I R\))

1. A standard LED requires a current of \(0.02\ \text{A}\) when \(3\ \text{V}\) is applied. What is the LED's resistance?  
   - Use \(R = \dfrac{V}{I}\).

2. A simple electric motor has resistance \(15\ \Omega\). If it draws \(0.8\ \text{A}\), what is the battery voltage?  
   - Use \(V = I R\).

3. A smartphone charger is plugged into a \(120\ \text{V}\) outlet. If the charger's internal resistance is \(48\ \Omega\), how much current does it draw?  
   - Use \(I = \dfrac{V}{R}\).

(Include units in each answer.)

---

## Part 3 — Power Calculations Practice (\(P = V I\))

1. A large television is plugged into \(120\ \text{V}\) and draws \(2.5\ \text{A}\). What power does it consume (W)?  
   - \(P = V I\)

2. A microwave is rated \(1100\ \text{W}\). If plugged into \(120\ \text{V}\), what current does it draw?  
   - Rearranged: \(I = \dfrac{P}{V}\)

3. An electric car fast-charger delivers \(150{,}000\ \text{W}\). If the current is \(375\ \text{A}\), what is the system voltage?  
   - \(V = \dfrac{P}{I}\)

---

## Part 4 — Synthesis Problems (Multi-step)

1. A toaster has resistance \(12\ \Omega\) and is plugged into \(120\ \text{V}\).
   a) Current drawn? Use \(I = \dfrac{V}{R}\).  
   b) Power consumed? Use \(P = V I\) (or \(P = \dfrac{V^2}{R}\)).

2. A \(60\ \text{W}\) incandescent bulb is plugged into \(120\ \text{V}\).
   a) Current drawn? \(I = \dfrac{P}{V}\).  
   b) Filament resistance? \(R = \dfrac{V}{I}\) or \(R = \dfrac{V^2}{P}\).

3. A \(100\ \text{W}\) bulb is left on for \(3\) hours.
   a) How many seconds is 3 hours? (Compute \(t\) in seconds.)  
   b) How many joules of energy were used? Use \(E = P t\) and show the substitution with \(P=100\ \text{W}\).

## Part 5 — Challenge — Connecting to the Phenomenon

A common portable electric space heater is rated \(1500\ \text{W}\).

a) Using \(P = V I\), calculate the current a single heater draws from a \(120\ \text{V}\) outlet.  
   - \(I = \dfrac{P}{V}\). Show numeric result with units.

b) In your own words, explain why millions of homes simultaneously drawing this extra current would strain the electrical grid. Mention transformers, distribution lines, and thermal limits. (Short answer)
