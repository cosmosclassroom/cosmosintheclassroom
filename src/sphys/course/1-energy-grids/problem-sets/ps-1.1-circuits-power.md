## Part A: Warm-Up (Do These First)

1. **Example:** A hair dryer draws 10 amps of current from a 120-volt outlet.
   
   What power does it use?
   
   Power = voltage × current
   P = V × I
   P = 120 V × 10 A
   P = 1200 W (watts)

2. **Example:** A light bulb with 60 ohms of resistance is connected to a 12-volt battery.
   
   What current flows through the bulb?
   
   Current = voltage ÷ resistance
   I = V ÷ R
   I = 12 V ÷ 60 Ω
   I = 0.2 A (amps)

3. Now you try: A toaster with 20 ohms of resistance is plugged into a 120-volt outlet. Find the current.

## Helpful Reminders

### Formulas to Use
- Ohm's Law: V = I × R
  - V is voltage in volts (V)
  - I is current in amperes (A)
  - R is resistance in ohms (Ω)

- Power Formulas:
  - P = V × I
  - P = I² × R
  - P = V² ÷ R
  - P is power in watts (W)

- Energy Formula:
  - E = P × t
  - E is energy in joules (J) when t is in seconds
  - E is energy in watt-hours (Wh) when t is in hours

### Conversions
- 1 hour = 3600 seconds
- 1 kilowatt (kW) = 1000 watts (W)
- 1 megawatt (MW) = 1,000,000 watts (W)
- 1 kilowatt-hour (kWh) = 3,600,000 joules (J)
