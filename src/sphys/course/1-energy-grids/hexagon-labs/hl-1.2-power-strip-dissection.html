Hexagon Lab 1.2: Power Strip Dissection

Today’s Question: How do power strips distribute electricity, and how does this relate to larger power grid systems?

Complete this lab in your Science Notebook.

What You Need:

  • Your Science Notebook
  • D Battery with holder
  • Alligator Clips (4-6)
  • Dissected Power Strip (with cover removed)
  • “Grain of Wheat” bulbs (1.5V, 100mA)
  • Simple switch
  • Ruler and colored pencils

Background:

Power strips are simple versions of electrical distribution systems. By exploring how a power strip works, we can understand the basic principles of how electricity flows through larger systems like community power grids.

Part 1: Identify the Components

Step 1: In your notebook, create a table with three columns: “Component”, “What it does”, and “Power Grid Equivalent”.

Step 2: For each component, write its function and identify which part of a power grid it represents.

Components to include:

  • D-Battery
  • Alligator Clips
  • Power Strip
  • Light Bulbs
  • Switch

Example: D-Battery: Provides electrical energy → Power plant (energy generation)

Part 2: Build Your Circuit

Step 1: Connect your components following these steps:

  1. Connect one alligator clip to the positive (+) battery terminal
  2. Connect another clip to the negative (-) terminal
  3. Attach these clips to the power strip wires (not the green wire)
  4. Insert a light bulb into one socket of the power strip

Step 2: In your notebook, draw your circuit diagram clearly labeling all components.

Step 3: Test your circuit:

  1. Turn the power strip switch ON and record your observations in your notebook.
  2. Turn the power strip switch OFF and record your observations in your notebook.

Part 3: Explore Different Circuit Types

Series Circuit Test:

  1. Connect two light bulbs in a series (one after another in a single path)
  2. Turn on the power and in your notebook, record the brightness.
  3. Disconnect one bulb. In your notebook, describe what happens to the other bulb.

Parallel Circuit Test:

  1. Connect two light bulbs in parallel (each on a separate socket of the power strip)
  2. Turn on the power and in your notebook, record the brightness.
  3. Remove one bulb. In your notebook, describe what happens to the other bulb.

Make a Claim: In your notebook, complete this sentence: “In a power grid, a _____ circuit is more reliable because _____.”

Your Evidence: In your notebook, list a specific observation from your experiment that supports your claim.

Part 4: Do the Math

Calculate: If your battery provides 1.5 volts and each bulb has a resistance of 15 ohms, what is the current through:

  1. A single bulb circuit: $I = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{1.5\text{ V}}{15\text{ Ω}} = 0.1\text{ A} = 100\text{ mA}$

  2. Two bulbs in series (total resistance = sum of individual resistances) In your notebook, show all steps of your calculation.

  3. Two bulbs in parallel (use the formula: $\frac{1}{R_{total}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}$) In your notebook, show all steps of your calculation.

What does this tell us? In your notebook, explain which circuit arrangement (series or parallel) allows the bulbs to receive more current and why this matters.

Part 5: Connect to the Big Picture

Think About It: In your notebook, write a paragraph explaining how the power strip model helps explain what happened during the Texas power grid failure.

In your notebook, answer these questions:

  1. How is a power strip’s switch similar to switches in the larger power grid?
  2. What happens to homes (light bulbs) when a transmission line (wire) fails in:
    • A series system?
    • A parallel system with multiple pathways?
  3. Why do communities use parallel circuits with multiple pathways for delivering electricity?
