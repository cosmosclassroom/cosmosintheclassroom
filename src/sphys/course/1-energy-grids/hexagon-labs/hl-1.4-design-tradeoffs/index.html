Hexagon Lab: Engineering Design Tradeoffs

Unit P.1: The Invisible Currents

Lab Overview

Objective: Use a decision matrix to quantitatively evaluate different energy sources based on weighted criteria, then construct a claim-evidence-reasoning argument for a community energy recommendation.

Duration: 50 minutes (2.5 chunks)

Materials: Computer/tablet access to data webpage, calculator, lab notebook

The Engineering Challenge

A small Texas town (population 15,000) needs to replace their aging coal power plant. As energy consultants, your job is to analyze three options and make a data-driven recommendation based on what matters most to your assigned community.

Your Task: Use engineering decision-making tools to choose between solar, wind, and natural gas power systems.

Part 1: Data Analysis & Matrix Construction

(25 minutes)

Step 1: Explore the Energy Data (5 minutes)

Open the provided data webpage. You’ll see a table and charts comparing three energy sources across four criteria:

Energy Source Cost per kWh (USD) Land Use (m² per GWh) Reliability (%) CO₂ Emissions (tons per GWh)
Solar Farm $0.08 15,000 85% 50
Wind Farm $0.06 25,000 78% 25
Natural Gas Plant $0.07 2,000 95% 800

Quick Analysis Questions (record in your notebook):

  1. Which source has the lowest cost per kWh?
  2. Which source requires the least land?
  3. Which source is most reliable?
  4. Which source produces the least CO₂?
  5. Key Question: Is any single source “best” in all categories? What does this tell you about engineering decisions?

Step 2: Get Your Community Assignment (5 minutes)

Your teacher will assign your pair one of these community types:

Agricultural Community

  • Priorities: Protecting farmland, keeping costs low for rural families
  • Values: Cost (Weight: 5), Land Use (Weight: 5), Reliability (Weight: 3), Environment (Weight: 2)

Environmental Community

  • Priorities: Fighting climate change, sustainable future
  • Values: Cost (Weight: 2), Land Use (Weight: 3), Reliability (Weight: 3), Environment (Weight: 5)

Industrial Community

  • Priorities: Steady power for factories, economic competitiveness
  • Values: Cost (Weight: 4), Land Use (Weight: 2), Reliability (Weight: 5), Environment (Weight: 2)

Record your community type and weights in your notebook.

Step 3: Build Your Decision Matrix (15 minutes)

Decision Matrix Instructions:

Step 3b: Score each energy source on each criterion (1-5 scale, where 5 = best):

Use the data to assign scores. For example:

  • Lowest cost gets highest score (5)
  • Highest reliability gets highest score (5)
  • Lowest emissions gets highest score (5)
  • Lowest land use gets highest score (5)

Scoring Guide:

  • Cost: Solar = 3, Wind = 5, Gas = 4
  • Land Use: Solar = 3, Wind = 2, Gas = 5
  • Reliability: Solar = 3, Wind = 2, Gas = 5
  • Environment: Solar = 4, Wind = 5, Gas = 1

Step 3c: Calculate weighted scores by multiplying each score by its weight:

Example calculation for Agricultural Community:

  • Solar Cost Score: 3 × 5 (weight) = 15
  • Solar Land Use Score: 3 × 5 (weight) = 15

Step 3d: Add up the total weighted score for each energy source. The highest total wins!

Part 2: Claim-Evidence-Reasoning Task

(20 minutes)

Scientific Argumentation

Now you’ll write a scientific argument defending your community’s energy choice using the Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) framework.

Your Prompt: “Which energy source should your community choose for their new power plant?”

CER Framework

Write your response using these sentence starters:

CLAIM (What is your recommendation?): “Based on our analysis, [your community type] should choose [energy source] for their new power plant.”

EVIDENCE (What data supports your claim?): “Our decision matrix shows that [energy source] scored [total score], which was [higher/lower] than [other sources]. Specifically, this source performed well in [specific criteria] with scores of [specific numbers]. This matters because our community weighted [criteria] as [weight] due to [community priority].”

REASONING (Why does the evidence support your claim?): “This choice makes sense because our community prioritizes [main values]. The data shows that [energy source] best matches these priorities by [explain connection]. While [energy source] may not be perfect in [weaker areas], the trade-offs are acceptable because [explain why].”

Partner Work: Draft your CER argument together. You’ll present this to the class, so make sure both partners understand the reasoning.

Presentation Preparation

Prepare a 1-minute presentation that includes:

  • Your community type and chosen energy source
  • One key piece of evidence from your matrix
  • Why this choice makes sense for your community’s values
