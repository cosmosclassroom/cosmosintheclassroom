Unit P.1 Research Brief: The Invisible Currents

Part 2: BLUEPRINT (During Learning)

System Models of the Grid

What We’ve Learned So Far

From our investigations, we now know:

  • Electricity travels from power plants through high-voltage transmission lines to our homes
  • The Texas crisis happened when power plants couldn’t generate enough electricity to meet demand
  • Grid failures cascade through the system - when generation fails, everything downstream fails
  • The grid must balance supply and demand in real time

Today’s Focus: The Bigger Questions

Now that we understand HOW electricity gets to our homes, we need to ask:

  • Supply: Where does our electricity actually come from?
  • Demand: What affects how much electricity we need?
  • Reliability: Why do some energy sources fail when we need them most?
  • Equity: Why did some communities lose power longer than others?
  • Solutions: How can we design a more reliable system?

Part D: Designing Solutions

Engineers must consider many factors when designing energy systems.

For each design choice, there are trade-offs:

Energy Source Advantages Disadvantages
Natural Gas Cheap, reliable, fast startup Burns fossil fuel, can freeze
Wind Clean, renewable, cheap to run Variable, dependent on weather
Solar Clean, renewable, getting cheaper Only works during day, affected by weather
Nuclear Very reliable, clean, high output Expensive, safety concerns
Coal Reliable, cheap Dirty, contributes to climate change
