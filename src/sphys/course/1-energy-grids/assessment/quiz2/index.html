Quiz 1: Electrical Concepts & Systems

Name: ______ **Date:** ____ **Period:** __

Formulas Provided:

  • Ohm’s Law: $V = IR$
  • Power: $P = IV$
  • Energy: $E = P \times t$

Part A: Core Concepts & Vocabulary (10 pts, ~8 min)

1. Match the Concept (4 pts)
Match each scenario to the correct term.
A) “Electrical pressure” pushing electrons through a wire.
B) How hard it is for electricity to flow through a wire.
C) The total flow of electrons per second.
D) The rate at which energy is used.

2. Vocabulary Check (2 pts)
Define “current” in your own words.

3. Real-World Connection (4 pts)
Why do engineers care about power when designing electrical devices for homes? (1-2 sentences)

Part B: Calculations & Application (15 pts, ~12 min)

4. Ohm’s Law Practice (6 pts)
A lamp has a resistance of $24\,\Omega$ and is plugged into a $120\,\text{V}$ outlet.
a) What is the current? Show your work.
b) What does this current mean for the lamp?

5. Power Calculation (5 pts)
A space heater uses $1,500\,\text{W}$ of power at $120\,\text{V}$.
a) How much current does it draw? Show your work.
b) Why is it important to know this current for safety?

6. Energy Use (4 pts)
A $60\,\text{W}$ bulb runs for $3$ hours.
a) How much energy does it use in Joules?
b) Show your calculation steps.

Part C: Systems & Engineering Thinking (10 pts, ~8 min)

7. System Model (5 pts)
Draw a simple diagram showing how electricity flows from a power plant to a lamp in your house. Label: power plant, wires, lamp, energy flow.

8. Trade-Offs (5 pts)
Why can’t engineers design a “perfect” energy system for everyone? List two trade-offs they must consider.

Part D: Reflection & Self-Assessment (5 pts, ~5 min)

9. Metacognitive Check (5 pts)
a) What was the hardest part of this quiz for you?
b) What strategy did you use to solve a problem you found difficult?

Key Terms Bank:

  • Voltage: The push that moves electric current.
  • Current: The flow of electric charge.
  • Resistance: How much a material slows down current.
  • Power: How fast energy is used.
  • Energy: The ability to do work.

Universal Design Options:

  • You may show your work with words, diagrams, or tables.
  • Ask for help or use a calculator as needed.
