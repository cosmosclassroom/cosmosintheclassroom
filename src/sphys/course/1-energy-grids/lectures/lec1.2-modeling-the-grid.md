## Slide 4: Energy Source Reliability
**Energy Source Reliability**

Not all energy sources respond the same way to extreme conditions.

**Definition:** Reliability = ability to provide power when needed, especially during emergencies

**Key Insight:** Each source has different strengths and vulnerabilities

---

## Slide 5: Natural Gas Performance
**Natural Gas (40% of Texas Power)**

**Normal Operation:** Cheap, fast startup, very reliable
**During February 2021:** Dropped from 40% to 20% of grid supply

**Why it failed:** Gas wells and pipelines froze in extreme cold
**Lesson:** "Reliable" depends on the conditions

---

## Slide 6: Wind Power Performance  
**Wind Power (20% of Texas Power)**

**Normal Operation:** Clean, cheap to run, weather-dependent
**During February 2021:** Dropped from 20% to 12% of grid supply

**Why it struggled:** Turbine blades iced over, reduced wind speeds
**Surprise:** Performed better than expected for the conditions

---

## Slide 7: Coal and Nuclear Performance
**Coal and Nuclear Power**

**Coal (15% of Texas Power):** Stayed at 15% - kept running
**Nuclear (10% of Texas Power):** Stayed at 10% - kept running

**Why they didn't fail:** Designed for continuous operation, less weather-sensitive
**Trade-off:** Higher environmental impact (coal) or safety concerns (nuclear)

---

## Slide 8: The Storage Challenge
**Why Can't We Store Electricity?**

**The Problem:** Electricity must be used the instant it's made
**Analogy:** Like a river - water flows constantly, can't "pause" the flow

**Storage Reality:** Converting electricity to other forms (chemical, mechanical) loses energy and costs money

---

## Slide 9: Real-Time Balancing
**Supply Must Equal Demand Every Second**

**Physics Requirement:** If supply < demand, voltage drops and system fails
**Grid Challenge:** Predict and match electricity use 24/7
**Texas Crisis:** When supply dropped 40%, demand was still 100%

Formula: **Grid Stability = Supply - Demand = 0 (always)**

## Slide 11: Equity in Energy Access
**Who Loses Power First?**

**Data from Houston (Feb 2021):**
- Wealthy areas: 2 days without power
- Middle-class areas: 4 days without power  
- Low-income areas: 7 days without power

**Cause:** Older infrastructure in low-income neighborhoods fails first

---

## Slide 12: Policy Trade-offs
**Engineering vs. Policy Decisions**

**Engineers ask:** What's technically possible?
**Policymakers ask:** What's politically and economically acceptable?

**Example:** Winterizing Texas grid would cost $10 billion but prevent future blackouts
**Trade-off:** Higher electricity bills vs. lower blackout risk

---

**Design Constraints (What limits us):**
