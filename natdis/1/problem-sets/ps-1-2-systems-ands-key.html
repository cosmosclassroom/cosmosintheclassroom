Problem Set 1.1: Forces & Waves

Your Role: The Scientist
What You're Practicing: Analyzing measurement data and understanding fundamental physical relationships

Key Formulas for This Set

$Risk = Hazard \cdot Vulnerability$

$Cost \, Effectiveness = \frac{Total \, Benefit}{Total \, Cost}$

Primary wave relationship: $$Distance = Speed \times Time$$

Wave interval calculation: $$\Delta t_{S-P} = t_{S-wave} - t_{P-wave}$$

Part 1: Measurement Analysis

1. A P-wave travels through rock at a constant speed of $6 \text{ km/s}$. It takes the wave $30 \text{ s}$ to reach a monitoring station.
Calculate the distance from the earthquake's epicenter to the station.

2. Scientists detect a P-wave arriving at 14:23:05 and an S-wave from the same earthquake arriving at 14:23:28.
What is the S-P interval in seconds?

3. An earthquake occurs $1{,}500 \text{ km}$ from a seismic station. P-waves travel at $8 \text{ km/s}$ through this region.
How long will it take for the P-wave to reach the station?

Part 2: Data Interpretation

4. A seismologist analyzes three monitoring stations and finds epicenter distances of $120 \text{ km}$, $85 \text{ km}$, and $95 \text{ km}$ respectively.
Explain how triangulation uses this data to locate the earthquake's epicenter.

5. Two earthquakes have magnitudes of $5.2$ and $6.8$ on the Richter scale.
Which earthquake released more energy, and approximately how much more?

6. In 2-3 sentences, explain how scientific measurements of earthquake magnitude can differ from the intensity experienced by a community, using the concepts of distance and vulnerability.

Problem Set 1.2: Systems & Solutions

Your Role: The Engineer
What You're Practicing: Evaluating design alternatives and quantifying engineering trade-offs

Key Engineering Formulas

Risk assessment: $$Risk = Hazard \times Vulnerability$$

Cost-effectiveness evaluation: $$Cost \, Effectiveness = \frac{Total \, Benefit}{Total \, Cost}$$

Safety margin calculation: $$Factor \, of \, Safety = \frac{Resisting \, Forces}{Driving \, Forces}$$

Part 1: Risk Analysis

1. A community has a seismic hazard rating of $8$ (scale 1-10) and vulnerability rating of $4$ (scale 1-10).
Calculate the overall risk level for this community.

2. After implementing new building codes, the community reduces its vulnerability to $2$.
What is the new risk level? Calculate the percentage reduction in risk.

Part 2: Design Evaluation

3. Three building retrofit options have these characteristics:

Option Cost Safety Factor Annual Maintenance
A $\$2.8$ million $2.1$ $\$45{,}000$
B $\$4.5$ million $2.7$ $\$28{,}000$
C $\$6.2$ million $3.4$ $\$18{,}000$

Which option has the highest safety factor? Which has the lowest 10-year total cost?

4. Option B prevents an estimated $\$12$ million in earthquake damage over its lifetime.
Calculate the cost-effectiveness ratio for Option B, including 10-year maintenance costs.

Part 3: Systems Integration

5. An engineer must choose between retrofitting $100$ older buildings at $\$75{,}000$ each or constructing $20$ new earthquake-resistant buildings at $\$375{,}000$ each.
Which option protects more buildings? Which provides better safety per dollar invested?

6. In 2-3 sentences, explain how engineering decisions must balance multiple systems (economic, safety, environmental) when designing for community resilience.
