1.1 The Phenomenon

On August 23, 2011, a magnitude 5.8 earthquake centered in Virginia was felt up and down the East Coast, including in Branford, Connecticut. While not destructive, it was a surprising and rare event for many. It raised a question: we think of earthquakes as a West Coast problem, but the ground here can move, too. A scientist’s first job isn’t to panic, but to ask questions and analyze the evidence. The evidence from any earthquake arrives as waves traveling through the ground.

1.2 The Science & The Tools

Key Vocabulary:

  • Seismic Waves: Waves of energy that travel through the Earth, caused by an earthquake.
  • Epicenter: The point on the Earth’s surface directly above where an earthquake begins.
  • Plate Tectonics: The scientific theory that the Earth’s outer shell is divided into several large, moving pieces called plates.
  • Magnitude: A measure of the total energy released by an earthquake at its source.

The Core Principle: Elastic Rebound Theory
The movement of tectonic plates is not smooth. The massive, rough edges of plates get stuck against each other. As the plates continue to try to move, energy is stored in the rock, which bends and deforms like a stretched rubber band. When the stress becomes too great, the rock suddenly breaks and snaps back, releasing the stored energy as seismic waves. This process is the fundamental cause of earthquakes.

The Formula:
Finding Distance from a Seismogram
Seismographs record two main waves: fast P-waves and slower S-waves. The time gap between their arrivals (the S-P interval) tells us the distance to the epicenter.

Where ‘d’ is distance, ‘s’ is the speed (or velocity) of the wave, and ‘t’ is the travel time. A more practical formula relates the S-P interval directly to distance, often found on a time-travel graph.

Worked Example:
A seismograph records the first P-wave at 10:00:00 and the first S-wave at 10:00:45. The S-P interval is 45 seconds.

  1. Calculate the S-P Time Interval
    The S-P time interval (Δt) is the difference between the arrival time of the S-wave () and the arrival time of the P-wave (​). Δt = S-wave arrival − P-wave arrival
    Given the arrival times:
    P-wave = 10:00:00
    S-wave = 10:00:45
    The calculation is:
    Δt = 10:00:45 − 10:00:00 = 45 seconds

    Figure 1. P-S wave travel time graph. The farther apart the two arrival times are on the graph, the farther from the epicenter was the station that recorded them.

  2. Step 1: Identify Your S-P Interval on the Time Axis
    First, find your calculated S-P interval on the vertical axis (the y-axis), which represents time. In our example, the interval is 45 seconds. The easiest way to do this is to use a scrap piece of paper or a ruler. Place the edge of the paper along the y-axis and make two marks: one at 0 and one at 45 seconds. This sets the “length” of your time gap.

  3. Step 2: Find Where the Gap Fits Between the Curves
    Now, keeping your paper perfectly vertical, slide it to the right across the graph. You are looking for the one specific place where your top mark (45 s) touches the S-wave curve and your bottom mark (0 s) simultaneously touches the P-wave curve. The vertical distance between the two curves must exactly match your 45-second interval.
    On our example graph, this location is shown by the solid green line. Notice how it fits perfectly between the P- and S-wave curves, representing that 45-second gap.

  4. Step 3: Read the Epicentral Distance on the Distance Axis
    Once you have found the correct spot, you must determine the corresponding distance. From that point on the graph, move your finger straight down to the horizontal axis (the x-axis), which represents the epicentral distance.
    The dashed green line on our graph illustrates this final step, leading from the 45-second gap down to the x-axis. Reading the value on the axis, we can see that a 45-second S-P interval corresponds to an epicentral distance of 380 km.

Using a standard time-travel graph, a seismologist can determine this 45-second gap corresponds to a distance of approximately 380 km.
