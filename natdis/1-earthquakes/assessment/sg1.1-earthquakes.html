Key Concepts to Review

1. Seismic Waves and Timing

P-waves vs. S-waves: - P-waves (Primary): Faster, arrive first - S-waves (Secondary): Slower, arrive second - S-P interval: Time difference between S-wave and P-wave arrivals

Important Formula:

Time = Hours:Minutes:Seconds format
S-P interval = S-wave time - P-wave time

Example: - P-wave arrives at 2:15:30 - S-wave arrives at 2:16:05 - S-P interval = 35 seconds

2. Finding Earthquake Distance

Key Formula:

Distance = Speed × Time

What you need to know: - Use travel-time graphs to find distance from S-P interval - Read graphs carefully (time on y-axis, distance on x-axis) - P-waves typically travel at about 6 km/s in the crust

3. Magnitude vs. Intensity

Magnitude: - What it is: Total energy released by the earthquake - Scale: Richter Scale or Moment Magnitude (numbers like 5.8, 6.2) - Key point: One number for the whole earthquake

Intensity: - What it is: How strong the shaking feels in a specific place - Scale: Modified Mercalli Intensity (I-XII) - Key point: Different locations experience different intensities

Why intensity varies: - Distance from epicenter - Local geology (rock vs. soil) - Building construction quality

Key Vocabulary to Know

Epicenter: Point on Earth's surface directly above where an earthquake starts

Seismograph: Instrument that records earthquake waves

S-P interval: Time difference between S-wave and P-wave arrivals

Triangulation: Using data from multiple stations to locate an epicenter

Magnitude: Measure of total energy released by an earthquake

Intensity: Measure of earthquake effects at a specific location

Modified Mercalli Scale: Scale (I-XII) that describes earthquake intensity based on damage and what people felt
