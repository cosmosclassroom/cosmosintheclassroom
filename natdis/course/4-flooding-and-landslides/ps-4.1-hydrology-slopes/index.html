Problem

This is the basic calculation for quantifying landslide risk. A steeper slope is more likely to fail.

You are reading a topographical map. You see a steep hillside where the change in elevation (“Rise”) is $50 \text{ meters}$ over a horizontal distance (“Run”) of $100 \text{ meters}$.

What is the slope of the hillside?

Given:

  • $Rise = 50 \text{ m}$
  • $Run = 100 \text{ m}$

Find: $Slope$

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

  • $Rise = 50 \text{ m}$
  • $Run = 100 \text{ m}$
  • Unknown: $Slope = ?$

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

We use the slope formula:

\[Slope = \frac{Rise}{Run}\]

Step 3: Substitute Values and Track Units

\[Slope = \frac{50 \text{ m}}{100 \text{ m}}\] \[Slope = 0.5\]

Final Answer: The slope is $0.5$. (This is a very steep slope; a 1.0 slope would be a 45-degree angle).

Problem

This calculation measures the volume of water in a river, which is the key variable in flood forecasting.

During a storm, a hydrologist measures a river.

  • The river channel’s cross-sectional area is $200 \text{ m}^2$.
  • The water’s average velocity is $3.0 \text{ m/s}$.

What is the river’s discharge?

Given:

  • $Area = 200 \text{ m}^2$
  • $Velocity = 3.0 \text{ m/s}$

Find: $Discharge$ (in $\text{m}^3/\text{s}$)

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

  • $Area = 200 \text{ m}^2$
  • $Velocity = 3.0 \text{ m/s}$
  • Unknown: $Discharge = ?$

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

We use the discharge formula:

\[Discharge = Area \cdot Velocity\]

Step 3: Substitute Values and Track Units

\[Discharge = (200 \text{ m}^2) \cdot (3.0 \text{ m/s})\] \[Discharge = 600 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}\]

Final Answer: The river’s discharge is $600 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$. (This means 600 cubic meters of water are passing by every second).

Problem

This problem asks you to work backwards, like a scientist trying to determine *how deep* a flood will get (the "flood stage").

A river channel is $50 \text{ meters}$ wide. During a major flood, its discharge is measured at $1,200 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$, and the water is moving at a velocity of $4.0 \text{ m/s}$.

What is the depth of the river?

Hint: This is a two-part problem. First, find the river’s total cross-sectional Area using the discharge formula. Then, use the area formula ($Area = Width \times Depth$) to find the depth.

Step 1: Find the Cross-Sectional Area

We rearrange the discharge formula ($Discharge = Area \cdot Velocity$) to solve for $Area$.

  • $Discharge = 1,200 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$
  • $Velocity = 4.0 \text{ m/s}$
\[Area = \frac{Discharge}{Velocity}\] \[Area = \frac{1,200 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}}{4.0 \text{ m/s}}\] \[Area = 300 \text{ m}^2\]

Step 2: Find the Depth

Now we use the area formula for a rectangle: $Area = Width \cdot Depth$. We can rearrange this to solve for $Depth$.

  • $Area = 300 \text{ m}^2$
  • $Width = 50 \text{ m}$
\[Depth = \frac{Area}{Width}\] \[Depth = \frac{300 \text{ m}^2}{50 \text{ m}}\] \[Depth = 6.0 \text{ m}\]

Final Answer: The depth of the river during the flood is $6.0 \text{ meters}$.
