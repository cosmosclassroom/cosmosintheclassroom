Problem

This is the most basic calculation an engineer makes to determine if a slope is safe.

An engineer is analyzing a hillside.

  • The Driving Forces (gravity, water weight) are $10,000 \text{ units}$.
  • The Resisting Forces (soil friction) are $15,000 \text{ units}$.

What is the Factor of Safety (FS)? Is the slope stable?

Given:

  • $Resisting \, Forces = 15,000 \text{ units}$
  • $Driving \, Forces = 10,000 \text{ units}$

Find: $Factor \, of \, Safety$ and stability.

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

  • $Resisting \, Forces = 15,000 \text{ units}$
  • $Driving \, Forces = 10,000 \text{ units}$
  • Unknown: $FS = ?$

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

We use the Factor of Safety formula:

\[FS = \frac{Resisting \, Forces}{Driving \, Forces}\]

Step 3: Substitute Values and Track Units

\[FS = \frac{15,000 \text{ units}}{10,000 \text{ units}}\] \[FS = 1.5\]

Final Answer: The Factor of Safety is $1.5$. Since $FS > 1$, the slope is stable.

Problem

This problem models how an engineer would calculate the pressure that floodwater exerts on a floodwall, which is the "driving force" the wall must resist.

During a flood, the water pushes against a $20 \text{ m}^2$ section of a floodwall with a total force of $800,000 \text{ Newtons}$.

What is the pressure on that section of the floodwall? (The unit for pressure is Pascals, where $1 \text{ Pa} = 1 \text{ N/m}^2$).

Given:

  • $Force = 800,000 \text{ N}$
  • $Area = 20 \text{ m}^2$

Find: $Pressure$ (in Pascals)

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

  • $Force = 800,000 \text{ N}$
  • $Area = 20 \text{ m}^2$
  • Unknown: $Pressure = ?$

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

We use the pressure formula:

\[Pressure = \frac{Force}{Area}\]

Step 3: Substitute Values and Track Units

\[Pressure = \frac{800,000 \text{ N}}{20 \text{ m}^2}\] \[Pressure = 40,000 \text{ N/m}^2\]

Final Answer: The pressure is $40,000 \text{ Pascals}$ (Pa).

Problem

This problem asks you to think like an engineer designing a solution. You know the forces, and you know your target safety level. How strong must your solution be?

An engineer is designing a retaining wall to stabilize a slope.

  • The Driving Forces on the slope are $50,000 \text{ units}$.
  • The natural Resisting Forces (from soil) are only $40,000 \text{ units}$.

The engineer’s design must achieve a Factor of Safety of $1.5$.

How much additional resisting force must the new retaining wall provide?

📝 Solution Workspace

Show all work and include units in your final answer.

Complete this problem in your notebook or on separate paper.

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Determine the Goal (Total Resisting Force)

We are given the target $FS$ and the $Driving \, Forces$. We can rearrange the $FS$ formula to find the total resisting force needed.

  • $FS_{target} = 1.5$
  • $Driving \, Forces = 50,000 \text{ units}$

\(FS = \frac{Resisting_{total}}{Driving}\) \(Resisting_{total} = FS \cdot Driving\)

\(Resisting_{total} = (1.5) \cdot (50,000 \text{ units})\) \(Resisting_{total} = 75,000 \text{ units}\)

Step 2: Calculate the Required Additional Force

The design must provide a total of $75,000$ units of resistance. The slope already has $40,000$ units of natural resistance. The retaining wall must provide the rest.

\[Force_{wall} = Resisting_{total} - Resisting_{natural}\] \[Force_{wall} = 75,000 \text{ units} - 40,000 \text{ units}\] \[Force_{wall} = 35,000 \text{ units}\]

Final Answer: The new retaining wall must be engineered to provide $35,000 \text{ units}$ of additional resisting force.
