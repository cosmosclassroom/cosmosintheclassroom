Problem

This is the most basic calculation for any evacuation plan: how long does it take to get to safety?

A coastal town’s designated “Tsunami Safe Zone” is on a hill $1,200 \text{ meters}$ inland from the beach.

Using the standard evacuation speed of $1.5 \text{ m/s}$, how long will it take a person on the beach to evacuate to the safe zone? (Give your answer in minutes).

Given:

  • $Distance = 1,200 \text{ m}$
  • $Average \, Speed = 1.5 \text{ m/s}$

Find: $Evacuation \, Time$ (in minutes)

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

  • $Distance = 1,200 \text{ m}$
  • $Speed = 1.5 \text{ m/s}$
  • Unknown: $Time = ?$

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

We use the evacuation time formula:

\[Evacuation \, Time = \frac{Distance \, to \, Safety}{Average \, Speed}\]

Step 3: Substitute Values and Track Units

\[Time = \frac{1,200 \text{ m}}{1.5 \text{ m/s}}\] \[Time = 800 \text{ s}\]

Step 4: Convert to Minutes

\[Time = 800 \text{ s} \div 60 \text{ s/min} \approx 13.3 \text{ minutes}\]

Final Answer: It will take $13.3 \text{ minutes}$ to evacuate.

Problem

This problem models the central crisis of a tsunami warning: Is the *available* warning time greater than the *required* evacuation time?

A tsunami is generated 150 km offshore. Scientists calculate (using methods from PS 2.1) that it will arrive in 18 minutes.

The town’s safe zone is $2.0 \text{ km}$ inland. Can a person evacuating at $1.5 \text{ m/s}$ reach the safe zone in time?

Hint: Compare the “available time” (18 min) to the “required time” (your calculation).

Step 1: Calculate Required Evacuation Time

First, find out how long it takes to evacuate.

  • $Distance = 2.0 \text{ km} = 2,000 \text{ m}$
  • $Speed = 1.5 \text{ m/s}$

\(Time_{required} = \frac{Distance}{Speed} = \frac{2,000 \text{ m}}{1.5 \text{ m/s}}\) \(Time_{required} \approx 1,333 \text{ s}\)

Step 2: Convert Available Warning Time to Seconds

We need to compare the times in the same unit.

  • $Time_{available} = 18 \text{ minutes}$
\[Time_{available} = 18 \text{ min} \cdot 60 \text{ s/min} = 1,080 \text{ s}\]

Step 3: Compare Times and Conclude

  • $Time_{required} = 1,333 \text{ s}$
  • $Time_{available} = 1,080 \text{ s}$

Final Answer: No, the person cannot make it. It takes 1,333 seconds to evacuate, but they only have 1,080 seconds of warning time. They will be $1,333 - 1,080 = 253$ seconds (or about 4.2 minutes) too late. This community needs a closer safe zone or a faster warning system.

Problem

This problem asks you to think like a policymaker or engineer. If you can't change the warning time, you must change the infrastructure. You will "work backwards" to design a solution.

A coastal community has a reliable warning time of only 12 minutes. The town council wants to build “vertical evacuation towers” (strong, tall buildings) as their safe zones.

To ensure the safety of all residents, including those who move slower, the engineers must plan for an “impaired” evacuation speed of $0.8 \text{ m/s}$.

What is the maximum allowed distance (in meters) that any home can be from an evacuation tower?

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

We are given the time and the speed, and we need to find the distance.

  • $Time = 12 \text{ minutes}$
  • $Speed = 0.8 \text{ m/s}$
  • Unknown: $Distance = ?$

Step 2: Convert Time to Seconds

The speed is in m/s, so our time must be in seconds. \(Time = 12 \text{ min} \cdot 60 \text{ s/min} = 720 \text{ s}\)

Step 3: Select and Rearrange the Parent Equation

The parent equation is $Time = Distance / Speed$. We need to solve for $Distance$.

\[Distance = Speed \cdot Time\]

Step 4: Substitute Values and Track Units

\[Distance = (0.8 \text{ m/s}) \cdot (720 \text{ s})\] \[Distance = 576 \text{ m}\]

Final Answer: The maximum allowed distance is $576 \text{ meters}$. This means the town must build enough towers so that no resident is more than 576 meters away from the nearest one. This is a key engineering and policy constraint.
