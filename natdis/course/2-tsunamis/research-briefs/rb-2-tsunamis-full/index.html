NATDIS Unit 2

Metacognitive Journal: Your Thinking Journey

Throughout this unit, you’ll keep a journal to help you track your thinking, connect your learning, and reflect on your growth as a scientist, engineer, policymaker, and citizen. Use the prompts below to guide your entries at each stage:

(a) Forecast: Your Initial Ideas & Predictions

  • What do you currently believe causes tsunamis? Write down your ideas, even if you’re unsure or think they might be wrong.
  • Make a prediction: Which type of coastal defense (engineered or natural) do you think would work best for Branford, CT? Why?
  • What do you expect will be the biggest challenge for a community facing a tsunami? Explain your reasoning.

(b) Blueprint: Connecting Your Experiences to Big Ideas

  • Choose at least two activities (labs, problem sets, or readings) from your progress tracker. For each:
    • Briefly describe what you did.
    • What did you learn about tsunamis, wave physics, or coastal engineering?
    • Did this experience change or confirm your initial predictions? How?
  • Connect your learning to the unit’s central question: How can we ensure community resilience against a low-frequency, high-impact coastal hazard?
  • Use the geo-literacy framework:
    • Interactions: How did human and natural systems interact in your activities?
    • Interconnections: Did you notice any connections between local actions and broader impacts?
    • Implications: What consequences did you anticipate or observe from different engineering or policy choices?

(c) Reflection: How Has Your Thinking Grown?

  • How has your thinking about tsunamis and coastal resilience changed since the start of the unit?
  • In what ways have your abilities to use math and science to understand and solve natural disaster engineering problems improved?
  • As a deliberate citizen, what responsibilities do you have in understanding, evaluating, and acting on scientific, engineering, and policy information?
  • What ethical or societal considerations now seem most important to you when planning for natural disasters?
