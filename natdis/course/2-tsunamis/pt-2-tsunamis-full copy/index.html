Unit 2: Tsunami Resilience Research Brief — Branford, CT

Goal:
Analyze the risk of a major Atlantic-generated tsunami, propose specific coastal defense engineering, and develop a public policy and warning system for the community of Branford, CT.

Central Theme:
How can we ensure community resilience against a low-frequency, high-impact coastal hazard?

Part 1: The Scientist’s Report — Hydrodynamic Principles & Local Hazard

Role: Scientist
Your task is to provide the Engineer and the Policymaker with the foundational data on the threat: its speed, energy, and predicted time of impact.

Entry 1: Hydrodynamic Principles & Local Hazard

  • Tsunami Mechanism and Speed:
    • Explain the fundamental process by which a seismic event (e.g., a massive subduction zone rupture) generates a full-column tsunami wave.
    • State the Shallow Water Wave Formula:
      [ v = \sqrt{g \cdot d} ] and explain the single, most important factor controlling the wave’s speed.
      (Why is the speed of the wave independent of the earthquake’s magnitude in the deep ocean?)
  • Shoaling and Wave Transformation:
    • Describe the process of shoaling.
    • Explain what happens to the tsunami’s speed, wavelength, and height as it travels from the Deep Ocean Basin (Zone A) to the Continental Shelf (Zone C).
    • Use the data from Hexagon Lab 2.1 to provide a quantitative example:
      Compare the calculated wave speed in the Deep Ocean Basin (~4,500 m depth) versus the speed near the shore (Continental Shelf, ~50 m depth).
  • Predicted Arrival Time:
    • Based on your calculations in Hexagon Lab 2.1 (Total Distance: 5,000,000 m), state the total predicted travel time from the epicenter to the Branford coastline.
    • Crucial Data Point: Provide the total arrival time in hours.

Part 2: The Engineer’s Proposal — Design and Placement of Mitigation Structures

Role: Engineer
Your task is to design a set of protective structures that will use energy dissipation to reduce the tsunami’s impact to manageable levels along the Branford coastline.

Entry 2: Design and Placement of Mitigation Structures

  • Structural Selection:
    • Identify the two primary types of coastal defense structures you recommend (e.g., vertical seawalls, sloped revetments, submerged breakwaters, or man-made reefs).
    • Justify your choices by explaining which structure is best for dissipating energy (reducing the incoming wave’s height and speed) and which is best for deflecting energy (preventing run-up).
  • Structural Placement and Design:
    • Based on your work in Hexagon Lab 2.2, discuss the optimal placement of your primary defense structure (e.g., 100 m offshore at a specific depth, or directly onshore).
    • Explain the physics behind your placement decision.
      How does placing a structure in deeper water versus shallow water affect the run-up height that reaches the community?
  • Material and Systems Thinking:
    • Considering the relentless power of water and salt, what primary construction material (e.g., reinforced concrete, natural stone, or specialized geotextiles) do you specify for the coastal barrier?
      Justify this choice in terms of its durability and cost-effectiveness.
    • Explain one potential cascading failure if your defense system is inadequate (e.g., if the seawall fails, what asset is flooded next, and what service is lost?).

Part 3: The Policymaker’s Brief — Policy on Public Readiness

Role: Policymaker
Your task is to use the Scientist’s time data and the Engineer’s defense plan to create a robust and effective plan for public safety and recovery.

Entry 3: Policy Brief on Public Readiness

  • Public Warning Timeline:
    Using the calculated arrival time (in hours) from Part 1, create a tiered public warning timeline (a set of steps):

    Time Before Arrival Action
    T - [Time] Initial alert issued (Policymaker’s primary decision point)
    T - [Time] Mandatory evacuation order announced
    T - [Time] Final “All Clear” or “Seek Immediate Vertical Shelter” warning
    • Explain why the Policymaker must plan the timeline based on the worst-case scenario (earliest possible arrival time), even if the most probable time is later.
  • Evacuation Strategy and Vulnerability:
    • Based on the data and models from Hexagon Lab 2.3, define the specific criteria for a mandatory evacuation zone (e.g., all areas below X meters above sea level and within Y kilometers of the coast).
    • Address the concept of social vulnerability:
      How does your plan specifically address citizens with limited mobility, or those who require public transportation to evacuate, to ensure all members of the community are included in the safety plan?
  • Policy Defense and Long-Term Resilience:
    • Beyond the physical defense structures, propose two non-structural policy measures (e.g., land-use zoning, public education, emergency funding mechanisms) that the City Council should enact immediately to increase long-term Resilience.
    • Conclude your brief by defining Resilience using the three core pillars of this unit:
      • the Scientist’s prediction
      • the Engineer’s structure
      • the Policymaker’s warning system
