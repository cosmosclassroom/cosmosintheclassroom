Tsunami Travel

NATDIS Unit 2

Difficulty:

Submission Format: paper

Estimated Time: 45 minutes

Problem Set 2.1 (Scientist): Hydrodynamics & Waves

Objective: Apply the principles of wave mechanics and the Shallow Water Wave Formula to predict the characteristics and travel time of a tsunami.

Instructions:

  • Answer all questions below.
  • Show your work clearly for any calculations.
  • Use $g = 9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.

Part 1: Conceptual Hydrodynamics

Full-Column Wave:
A common wind wave only disturbs the top 10 meters of the ocean surface. Explain why a tsunami is called a full-column wave. How does this difference affect the total amount of energy transferred from the earthquake to the water?

Seismic Generation:
A major earthquake occurs in the Atlantic Ocean. A scientist notes that the tectonic plates primarily moved laterally (side-to-side) along a transform fault, rather than vertically (up-and-down). Why is this specific type of fault movement unlikely to cause a destructive, large-scale tsunami?

Wavelength:
Compared to a wind wave, a tsunami has an extremely long wavelength (hundreds of kilometers). Explain how this long wavelength causes a tsunami to behave like a shallow water wave even when traveling across the deep ocean.

Part 2: Calculation — The Shallow Water Wave Formula

A tsunami is generated 3,000 km offshore from the coast of Branford, CT, and must cross two distinct zones of ocean depth.

Formula:

\[v = \sqrt{g \cdot d}\]

Scenario Data:

Zone Ocean Depth ($d$) Distance Traveled ($D$)
Zone 1: Abyssal Plain 5,500 m 2,000 km
Zone 2: Continental Slope 1,200 m 1,000 km

Remember to convert all distances from kilometers (km) to meters (m) before calculating time!

Calculate Speed in Zone 1:

Calculate the wave speed ($v_1$) in meters per second (m/s) across the Abyssal Plain.

$v_1$ = ___________ m/s

Calculate Time in Zone 1:

Calculate the time ($T_1$) in seconds for the wave to cross Zone 1 (2,000 km).

$T_1$ = ___________ seconds

Calculate Speed in Zone 2:

Calculate the wave speed ($v_2$) in meters per second (m/s) across the Continental Slope.

$v_2$ = ___________ m/s

Calculate Time in Zone 2:

Calculate the time ($T_2$) in seconds for the wave to cross Zone 2 (1,000 km).

$T_2$ = ___________ seconds

Total Lead Time:

What is the total travel time ($T_{Total} = T_1 + T_2$) in seconds?

Convert this total time into hours (round to two decimal places). This is the crucial lead time the Policymaker will use.

$T_{Total}$ = ___________ hours

Part 3: Synthesis and Interconnections

Shoaling and Energy Transformation:
Based on your calculated speeds, the tsunami slows down dramatically as it enters Zone 2.

Explain, in terms of energy conservation, why this slowdown causes the tsunami’s wave height (amplitude) to increase rapidly, transforming it into a destructive wave near the coast.

Scientist to Policymaker:
The Policymaker asks you for the single, most critical piece of information you can provide from this Problem Set.

State your answer, and explain why providing the accurate Total Lead Time is a question of public policy and logistics, not just physics. (Hint: Think about what happens if your time calculation is off by one hour.)
