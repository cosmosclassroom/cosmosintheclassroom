Problem

This first problem asks you to calculate the raw speed of a tsunami wave in the deep, open ocean.

A tsunami is generated in the Pacific Ocean where the average water depth is $d = 4,200 \text{ m}$. What is the speed of the tsunami wave in m/s?

Given:

  • Water depth: $d = 4,200 \text{ m}$
  • Acceleration due to gravity: $g = 9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$

Find: Speed of the wave ($Speed$)

🔍 View Solution

Step 1: Identify Givens and Unknowns

  • $g = 9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$
  • $d = 4,200 \text{ m}$
  • Unknown: $Speed = ?$

Step 2: Select Parent Equation

We use the shallow-water wave speed formula:

\[Speed = \sqrt{g \cdot d}\]

Step 3: Substitute Values and Track Units

\[Speed = \sqrt{(9.80 \text{ m/s}^2) \cdot (4,200 \text{ m})}\] \[Speed = \sqrt{41,160 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}^2}\] \[Speed \approx 202.9 \text{ m/s}\]

Final Answer: The tsunami travels at $203 \text{ m/s}$. (For reference, that’s over 730 km/hr, the speed of a jetliner).

Problem

This two-step problem models the exact task of a tsunami warning center: predicting *when* a wave will arrive at a distant coast.

An earthquake off the coast of Japan generates a tsunami. The city of Honolulu, Hawaii, is $6,200 \text{ km}$ away. Assume the average ocean depth between them is $d = 4,500 \text{ m}$.

How long will it take for the tsunami to reach Honolulu? (Give your answer in hours).

Hint: This is a two-part problem. First, find the speed of the wave. Then, use that speed to find the travel time.

🔍 View Solution

Part 1: Calculate Tsunami Speed

Given:

  • $g = 9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$
  • $d = 4,500 \text{ m}$

\(Speed = \sqrt{g \cdot d} = \sqrt{(9.80 \text{ m/s}^2) \cdot (4,500 \text{ m})}\) \(Speed = \sqrt{44,100 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}^2} = 210 \text{ m/s}\)

Part 2: Calculate Travel Time

Given:

  • $Speed = 210 \text{ m/s}$
  • $Distance = 6,200 \text{ km}$

First, convert distance to meters to match the speed units: \(Distance = 6,200 \text{ km} \cdot 1,000 \text{ m/km} = 6,200,000 \text{ m}\)

Now, use the travel time formula: \(Travel \, Time = \frac{Distance}{Speed}\) \(Travel \, Time = \frac{6,200,000 \text{ m}}{210 \text{ m/s}} \approx 29,524 \text{ s}\)

Finally, convert seconds to hours: \(Travel \, Time = 29,524 \text{ s} \div 3,600 \text{ s/hr} \approx 8.20 \text{ hours}\)

Final Answer: It will take approximately $8.20 \text{ hours}$ for the tsunami to reach Honolulu.

Problem

This problem explores **shoaling**. It asks you to analyze *why* a tsunami that is harmless in the deep ocean becomes a towering wall of water at the coast.

A tsunami wave is approaching the coast. Calculate its speed at two points:

  1. Deep Water: In the Aleutian Trench, with a depth of $d = 7,000 \text{ m}$.
  2. Shallow Water: At the continental shelf, with a depth of $d = 100 \text{ m}$.

After calculating both, explain what this dramatic change in speed implies for the wave’s height.

Part 1: Speed in Deep Water ($d = 7,000 \text{ m}$)

\(Speed_1 = \sqrt{g \cdot d_1} = \sqrt{(9.80 \text{ m/s}^2) \cdot (7,000 \text{ m})}\) \(Speed_1 = \sqrt{68,600 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}^2} \approx 262 \text{ m/s}\)

Part 2: Speed in Shallow Water ($d = 100 \text{ m}$)

\(Speed_2 = \sqrt{g \cdot d_2} = \sqrt{(9.80 \text{ m/s}^2) \cdot (100 \text{ m})}\) \(Speed_2 = \sqrt{980 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}^2} \approx 31.3 \text{ m/s}\)

Part 3: Analysis (Shoaling)

The wave’s speed decreases from $262 \text{ m/s}$ (over 940 km/hr) to $31.3 \text{ m/s}$ (about 113 km/hr).

As the front of the massive, long-wavelength wave enters the shallow water, it slows down dramatically. The back of the wave, still in deeper water, is moving faster and “catches up.” This causes the wave’s energy to compress into a much shorter wavelength.

Because the total energy must be conserved, the energy that was once spread out as speed is converted into wave height (amplitude). This is shoaling, and it is why a 1-meter wave in the deep ocean can become a 20-meter wave at the coast.
