Tsunami Speed

Course: NATDIS
Unit: 2

Hexagon Lab 2.1: Tsunami Arrival Times (The Scientist) Objective: To use the shallow water wave formula to accurately calculate the speed of a tsunami across different oceanic zones and predict the earliest possible arrival time for specific locations along the Connecticut coastline.

Central Question: How does the Earth’s bathymetry (underwater topography) transform a uniform speed wave in the deep ocean into a predictable, time-constrained threat near the shore?

Introduction: The Race Against Time As the Scientist responsible for early warning, your most important job is to give the Policymaker as much lead time as possible. The National Tsunami Warning Center detects a massive seismic event (magnitude 9.0) in the Atlantic that will generate a tsunami impacting the East Coast.

The key to your prediction is the depth of the ocean floor, as defined by the Shallow Water Wave Formula:

v= g⋅d ​

Where:

v is the wave speed (in meters per second, m/s)

g is the acceleration due to gravity (9.8 m/s 2 )

d is the depth of the ocean (in meters, m)

You will calculate the speed across three distinct zones to find the total travel time from the epicenter, which is 5,000,000 meters away from Branford’s coast.

Synthesis and Communication Implications (Shoaling): Compare the wave speed in Zone A (Deep Ocean) to Zone C (Continental Shelf). Despite traveling five times the distance in Zone A, why does the wave spend so much time crossing Zone C? What happens to the wave’s physical properties (specifically, its height and energy) as it slows down in Zone C? (Hint: Review the concept of Shoaling from Chapter 4).
