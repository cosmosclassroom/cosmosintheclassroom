Problem Set: Foundations Instructions: After reading “Unit 1, Chapter 0: An Introduction to a Shifting Earth,” answer the following questions. The goal of this first problem set is to practice applying the core concepts that will guide our entire course. Part 1: Geo-Literacy in Action

Interconnections: A large volcanic eruption in Iceland releases a massive ash cloud into the atmosphere. This causes airlines in Europe, thousands of miles away, to cancel all flights for a week. Which component of geo-literacy does this story best demonstrate? Explain your reasoning in one or two sentences.

Implications: A town council is considering a proposal to build a new shopping mall on a large area of wetland near a river. A geo-literate citizen understands that paving over the wetland (an interaction between a human system and a natural system) could lead to more severe flooding in downstream neighborhoods during heavy rains. What is this understanding of potential future consequences called?

Part 2: The Consequence Engine Scenario: A city is considering two different plans to protect a neighborhood from flooding. Use the data in the table below and the “Consequence Engine” framework to answer the questions that follow. Plan Resilience Predictability Community Trust Plan A: Build a large concrete flood wall High High Low Plan B: Restore the natural wetland Medium Low High

Tradeoffs: Plan A has high Resilience, but low Community Trust. What do you think might be the reasons for this tradeoff? (Why might the public dislike a plan that offers a high level of protection?)

Making a Choice: Imagine you are the policymaker. If your primary goal is to make a decision that the community will support and trust, which plan would you choose and why? If your primary goal is to have the most effective and predictable flood protection, which plan would you choose and why?

Part 3: Place & The Citizen

Synthesis: The introduction argues that a deliberate citizen must understand the difference between a space and a place. Why is it important for a citizen to see their town not just as a physical space, but as a meaningful place when evaluating a proposal from an engineer or a policymaker? Explain your answer in three to four sentences.
