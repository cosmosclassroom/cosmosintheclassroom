NATDIS Unit 1

1.3 The Policymaker

The Price of Safety

Core Idea

Public policy is a balancing act.
It uses scientific and engineering data to make the best possible decision for a community by weighing:

  • Costs
  • Benefits
  • Competing values

Key Concepts

  • Trade-offs:
    • Choosing one option means giving up the benefits of another.
    • There is no “perfect” answer.
  • Cost-Benefit Analysis:
    • A systematic process for calculating and comparing the benefits and costs of a project or decision.

Key Concepts Continued

  • Stakeholders:
    • Groups of people affected by a decision (e.g., students, parents, taxpayers, business owners).
  • Justification:
    • Using evidence and data to defend a choice as the most responsible one, even if it isn’t the most popular or cheapest.

The Dilemma: No Perfect Answers

Thinking Lens: Cause and Effect

    • Perfect Safety:
      • Thickest steel
      • Most advanced base isolators
      • Biggest dampers
    • Reality:
      • Every decision comes with a trade-off.
      • Choosing one thing requires giving up something else.

The Toolkit: Making the Best Bad Choice

So how do we choose? We can’t just go with our gut feeling. We need a logical way to compare plans that are wildly different. This is where we use Cost-Benefit Analysis.

You’ve already seen the formula:
\(\text{Cost Effectiveness} = \frac{\text{Total Benefit}}{\text{Total Cost}}\)

The Analysis

Let’s break down what this really means:

    • “Benefit” is our goal—in this case, reducing potential damage and saving lives.
    • “Cost” is everything we have to give up to get that benefit—money, time, and disruption.
    • This formula gives us a powerful tool: a single number that tells us how much safety we “buy” for every million dollars we spend.

Example: Comparing Two Plans

Plan Cost Benefit Disruption
Plan A $50 million Reduces damage by 75% Closes school for 8 months
Plan B $90 million Reduces damage by 90% Completed over one summer

How to Calculate Cost-Benefit

To compare the two plans, use the formula:

\[\text{Cost Effectiveness} = \frac{\text{Total Benefit}}{\text{Total Cost}}\]

Example Calculation:

    • Plan A:
      \(\frac{75\%}{\$50\,\text{million}} = 1.5\% \text{ benefit per million dollars}\)
    • Plan B:
      \(\frac{90\%}{\$90\,\text{million}} = 1.0\% \text{ benefit per million dollars}\)
  • Interpretation:

  • Plan A gives you more safety per dollar spent, but Plan B delivers higher total safety with less disruption.
    Which matters more for your community?

  • Discussion Prompt

  • Turn to your partner and discuss:
    • Which one is “better”?
    • Is the most effective one always the best one?
    • What other information would you need to make a responsible choice?

The Justification: Defending Your Decision

  • A policymaker’s final product is a recommendation.

  • A clear, defensible argument supported by evidence.

  • You must:
    • Acknowledge the downsides.
    • Show that you listened to stakeholders.
    • Prove that your choice is prudent, responsible, and ethical.
