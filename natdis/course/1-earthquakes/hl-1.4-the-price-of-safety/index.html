Overview

Abstract

In this lab, you will step into the role of a policymaker for the town of Branford. Presented with several engineering proposals to improve seismic safety, you must conduct a formal cost-benefit analysis. Your goal is not to find a perfect solution, but to choose the most prudent and defensible course of action given a limited budget and competing priorities. This exercise simulates the real-world trade-offs leaders face when allocating public funds for disaster resilience.

Introduction

The scientists have identified the seismic risk. The engineers have proposed solutions. Now, the decision rests with you. The town of Branford has a limited budget for infrastructure projects, and every dollar spent on earthquake safety is a dollar not spent on schools, roads, or parks. Your task is to analyze the data, weigh the difficult trade-offs, and make a formal recommendation that you can defend to your fellow citizens.

Investigation : Investigation 1: Cost-Benefit Analysis of Seismic Policies

Analyze the provided data for three different seismic safety proposals for the town of Branford. You will calculate key metrics to compare their efficiency and help you make an informed decision.

Synthesis: Claim-Evidence-Reasoning

Connection to Unit Phenomenon

This lab simulates the difficult, real-world decisions that community leaders, city planners, and politicians must make. It is rare that a solution is obviously the best in all categories. More often, leaders must weigh competing values—economic vitality versus immediate safety, individual preparedness versus public works, the desires of the public versus the recommendations of experts. By using a structured, evidence-based approach like cost-benefit analysis, policymakers can make these difficult choices in a way that is transparent, logical, and defensible to the stakeholders they represent.
