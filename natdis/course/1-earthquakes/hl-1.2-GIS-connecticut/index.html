Hexagon Lab 2: Assessing Local Seismic Hazard Objective: In your role as a Geoscientist, you will collect and analyze real-world seismic data to determine the level of earthquake hazard for the town of Branford, Connecticut. This will help you answer our essential question: What is the hazard?

Background: While Connecticut is not as famous for earthquakes as California, it does experience seismic activity, most notably from the Moodus seismic area. Our task is to investigate the historical record to understand the frequency and magnitude of these events. To do this, we will use the Earthquake Catalog from the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS), the primary government agency responsible for monitoring earthquakes. Modality: Complete this assignment in your Science Notebook

Procedure: Navigate to the USGS Database: Open a web browser and go to the USGS Earthquake Catalog search page: https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/search/

Set Your Search Parameters: We need to tell the database what to look for. In the “Basic Options” section, enter the following settings:

Magnitude: Set the range from 2.0 to 10. This will filter out very small tremors and show us events significant enough to be felt. Date & Time: Set the “Start” date to 1900-01-01. This will give us over a century of historical data. Geographic Region: Choose the “Custom” option. You will draw a rectangle on the map that covers Connecticut and the surrounding area. A good rectangle would have these approximate corner coordinates: North: 42.5 South: 40.5 West: -74.0 East: -71.5

Run the Search: Click the “Search” button. The website will generate a list and a map of all the earthquakes in that region and time period that fit your criteria.

Data Collection: Review the list of earthquakes generated by your search. Identify the three most significant earthquakes (i.e., the ones with the highest magnitude). Record their details in the table below.

Date Magnitude General Location (City/State)

Analysis and Conclusion: Answer the following questions based on the data you collected.

Frequency: Looking at the full list of earthquakes from your search, would you describe the seismic activity in this region as frequent or infrequent? Explain your reasoning. Magnitude: Consider the magnitudes of the earthquakes you found. Were they generally high-magnitude or low-magnitude events?
