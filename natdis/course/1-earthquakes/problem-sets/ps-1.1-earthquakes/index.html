Problem Set 1.1: Forces & Waves

Your Role: The Scientist
Your Mission: Apply simple mathematical concepts to analyze seismic data and understand the difference between an earthquake’s strength (Magnitude) and its real-world effects (Intensity).

Key Formulas for This Set

To find the distance a wave travels:

\[Distance = Speed \times Time\]

To find the time gap between wave arrivals:

\[\Delta t_{S-P} = t_{S-wave} - t_{P-wave}\]

Part 1: Wave Calculations

Instructions: Use the formulas above to answer the following questions. Show your work in the space provided.

1. A P-wave travels through rock at a constant speed of $6 \text{ km/s}$. It takes the wave $30 \text{ s}$ to reach a city.
How far away was the earthquake’s epicenter?

2. A seismograph at a research station detects a P-wave arriving at 10:05:15 AM. It detects the slower S-wave from the same earthquake arriving at 10:05:40 AM.
What is the $S-P$ interval in seconds?

3. An earthquake happens $1{,}200 \text{ km}$ away from a seismic station. The P-wave from the quake travels at a constant speed of $8 \text{ km/s}$.
How long will it take for the P-wave to reach the station?
(Hint: You will need to rearrange the distance formula to solve for time.)
