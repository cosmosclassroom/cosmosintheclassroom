Problem Set 1.2 (Engineer): How Buildings Stay Safe

Goal: To learn how engineers choose materials and designs to protect buildings from earthquakes.

Instructions: Answer the questions below on a separate sheet of paper. Show your work for math problems.

Part 1 — Why Buildings Shake

Background: Remember inertia. Inertia is the rule that says an object wants to stay still. During a quake, the ground moves, but the top of a building tries to stay in one place.

Question Prompt
1 During an earthquake, which building’s roof (A or B) will shake back and forth more? (Building A: short, heavy brick; Building B: tall, flexible steel)
Answer (write on separate sheet)  
Question Prompt
2 In one sentence, explain why tall buildings need to be flexible.
Answer (write on separate sheet)  

Part 2 — Choosing the Right Materials

Scenario: You are an engineer helping to build a new part of the Yale New Haven Health Shoreline Medical Center. You must choose the safest material.

Material data:

Material Strength Flexibility Cost (per sq ft)
Plain Concrete High Very Low $150
Steel Very High High $300
Wood Medium Medium $225
Question Prompt
3 Based on the table, which material is the best choice for a hospital in a place with earthquakes?
Answer (write on separate sheet)  
Question Prompt
4 Explain your choice in one sentence. Why is it the safest?
Answer (write on separate sheet)  
Question Prompt
5 Why is it extra important to use the safest (and most expensive) materials for a hospital, but maybe not for a small store?
Answer (write on separate sheet)  

Part 3 — Calculating an Earthquake’s Push

Background: The push an earthquake gives a building is called seismic force. We can find it with this simple formula:

Force = Mass × Acceleration

(Acceleration is how fast the ground is shaking.)

Assume the ground acceleration = 3 m/s².

Given Value
Building X mass 500,000 kg
Building Y mass 300,000 kg
Ground acceleration 3 m/s²
Question Prompt
6 Calculate the seismic force on Building X and Building Y. Show your math. Which building gets pushed harder? (Use F = m × a)
Answer (show your work on a separate sheet)
