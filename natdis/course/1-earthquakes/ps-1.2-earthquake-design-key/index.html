Earthquake Systems Key

NATDIS Unit 1

Difficulty:

Submission Format: paper

Estimated Time: 45 minutes

Problem Set 1.2 (Engineer): Structural Forces & Materials

Objective:
Apply principles of physics and material science to solve common earthquake engineering challenges.

Instructions

Answer the following questions on paper. Show your work for any calculations.

Part 1: The Problem of Inertia

Scenario:
An earthquake causes the ground to move back and forth rapidly. Two buildings are located in the area:

  • Building A: A tall, 30-story skyscraper, designed to be flexible and sway.
  • Building B: A short, 3-story brick building, which is very stiff and rigid.

Conceptual Question:
Remember the “glass of water” analogy from your reading. The water’s tendency to stay still is inertia.
During an earthquake, which building’s roof will experience a greater whipping motion due to inertia?
Explain why a tall, flexible building must be specially designed to handle these inertial forces.

Part 2: Choosing the Right Materials

Scenario:
As the lead engineer, you must choose the primary material for constructing a new hospital in a known earthquake zone. Your team has provided the following data on three options:

Material Compressive Strength Ductility Cost (Per sq ft)
Unreinforced Concrete High Very Low $150
Structural Steel Very High High $300
Reinforced Wood Medium Medium $225

Analysis Question:
Based on the table, which material would you choose?
In 2–3 sentences, justify your decision by explaining why its specific properties make it the safest choice for a hospital in an earthquake zone, even if it isn’t the cheapest.

Part 3: Calculating Seismic Force (Simplified)

Scenario:
The force an earthquake exerts on a building depends on the building’s mass and the ground’s acceleration.
We can model this with a simplified formula:

[\text{Seismic Force} = \text{Building Mass} \times \text{Ground Acceleration}]

Let’s assume the ground acceleration during a moderate earthquake is 3 m/s².

  • Building X: A heavy, concrete library with a mass of 500,000 kg
  • Building Y: A modern, steel-frame office building with a mass of 300,000 kg

Calculation:
Calculate the seismic force that would act on Building X and Building Y. Show your work.

Part 4: Synthesis

Putting It All Together:
Look at your answers from all three parts.
In 2–3 sentences, explain the engineering “big idea”:
Why do modern earthquake-resistant buildings often use lighter materials and more flexible designs?
Connect your answer to both inertia and seismic force.
