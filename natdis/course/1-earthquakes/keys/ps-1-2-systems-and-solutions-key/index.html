Part 1: Risk Analysis

1. A community has a seismic hazard rating of $7$ (on a scale of 1-10) and a vulnerability rating of $6$ (on a scale of 1-10).
Calculate the overall risk level for this community.

Solution: \(\begin{align} \text{Given: } &\text{Hazard} = 7, \text{Vulnerability} = 6 \\ \text{Formula: } &Risk = Hazard \times Vulnerability \\ \text{Calculate: } &Risk = 7 \times 6 = 42 \\ \text{Answer: } &\text{Risk level} = 42 \text{ (on a scale of 1-100)} \end{align}\)

2. After implementing earthquake-resistant building codes, the same community reduces its vulnerability to $3$.
What is the new risk level? How much did the risk decrease?

Solution: \(\begin{align} \text{New Risk: } &Risk_{new} = 7 \times 3 = 21 \\ \text{Risk Reduction: } &\Delta Risk = 42 - 21 = 21 \\ \text{Percent Reduction: } &\frac{21}{42} \times 100\% = 50\% \\ \text{Answer: } &\text{New risk} = 21, \text{Risk decreased by } 21 \text{ points (50\%)} \end{align}\)

Part 2: Design Trade-offs

3. Three building designs have the following characteristics:

Design Construction Cost Safety Factor Maintenance Cost/Year
A $2.5 million 1.8 $50,000
B $4.2 million 2.5 $30,000
C $6.0 million 3.2 $15,000

Which design offers the highest safety factor? Which has the lowest total cost over 10 years?

Solution:

Highest Safety Factor: Design C has the highest safety factor at $3.2$.

10-Year Total Costs: \(\begin{align} \text{Design A: } &\$2.5M + (10 \times \$50K) = \$2.5M + \$0.5M = \$3.0M \\ \text{Design B: } &\$4.2M + (10 \times \$30K) = \$4.2M + \$0.3M = \$4.5M \\ \text{Design C: } &\$6.0M + (10 \times \$15K) = \$6.0M + \$0.15M = \$6.15M \end{align}\)

Answer: Design C has the highest safety factor (3.2). Design A has the lowest total cost over 10 years ($3.0 million).

4. Using the cost-effectiveness formula, if Design B prevents $8 million in potential earthquake damage:
Calculate the cost-effectiveness ratio for Design B (including 10-year maintenance costs).

Solution: \(\begin{align} \text{Total Cost (Design B): } &\$4.5M \text{ (from Problem 3)} \\ \text{Total Benefit: } &\$8.0M \text{ (damage prevented)} \\ \text{Formula: } &Cost \, Effectiveness = \frac{Total \, Benefit}{Total \, Cost} \\ \text{Calculate: } &Cost \, Effectiveness = \frac{\$8.0M}{\$4.5M} = 1.78 \\ \text{Answer: } &\text{Cost-effectiveness ratio} = 1.78 \text{ (or } \$1.78 \text{ benefit per } \$1.00 \text{ spent)} \end{align}\)

Part 3: Systems Analysis

5. An engineer must choose between retrofitting 50 older buildings at $$100{,}000$ each or building 10 new earthquake-resistant structures at $$500{,}000$ each.
Which option protects more buildings? Which option provides better long-term safety per dollar spent?

Solution:

Buildings Protected:

  • Option 1 (Retrofit): 50 buildings protected
  • Option 2 (New Construction): 10 buildings protected

Cost Comparison: \(\begin{align} \text{Option 1 Total Cost: } &50 \times \$100{,}000 = \$5{,}000{,}000 \\ \text{Option 2 Total Cost: } &10 \times \$500{,}000 = \$5{,}000{,}000 \end{align}\)

Cost per Building Protected: \(\begin{align} \text{Option 1: } &\frac{\$5{,}000{,}000}{50} = \$100{,}000 \text{ per building} \\ \text{Option 2: } &\frac{\$5{,}000{,}000}{10} = \$500{,}000 \text{ per building} \end{align}\)

Answer: Option 1 (retrofitting) protects more buildings (50 vs. 10). Option 1 also provides better long-term safety per dollar spent ($100,000 vs. $500,000 per building protected), assuming similar levels of protection.

6. In 2-3 sentences, explain how engineering decisions must balance multiple systems (economic, safety, environmental) when designing for earthquake resilience.

Sample Answer: Engineering decisions for earthquake resilience must consider the economic system (budget constraints and cost-effectiveness), the safety system (protecting lives and property), and environmental systems (sustainable materials and construction impacts). These systems often conflict—for example, the safest design may be too expensive, or the most environmentally friendly option may provide less protection. Engineers must find optimal solutions that provide acceptable performance across all three systems while prioritizing public safety within realistic budget constraints.

  • Award full credit for answers that mention balancing multiple systems
  • Look for specific examples of trade-offs between systems
  • Accept various reasonable explanations of how systems interact
  • Partial credit for answers that address only two of the three systems
