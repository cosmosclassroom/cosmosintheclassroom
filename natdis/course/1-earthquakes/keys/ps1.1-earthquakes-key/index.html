Problem Set 1.1 Answer Key: Forces & Waves

Instructor Notes: This answer key provides complete solutions with step-by-step calculations. Partial credit should be awarded for correct setup and reasoning even if final answers differ.

Part 1: Wave Calculations

1. A P-wave travels through rock at a constant speed of $6 \text{ km/s}$. It takes the wave $30 \text{ s}$ to reach a city.
How far away was the earthquake’s epicenter?

Solution: \(\begin{align} \text{Given: } &\text{Speed} = 6 \text{ km/s}, \text{Time} = 30 \text{ s} \\ \text{Formula: } &\text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time} \\ \text{Calculate: } &\text{Distance} = 6 \times 30 = 180 \\ \text{Answer: } &\text{Distance} = 180 \text{ km} \end{align}\)

2. A seismograph at a research station detects a P-wave arriving at 10:05:15 AM. It detects the slower S-wave from the same earthquake arriving at 10:05:40 AM.
What is the S−P interval in seconds?

Solution: \(\begin{align} \text{Given: } &t_{P-wave} = 10:05:15, t_{S-wave} = 10:05:40 \\ \text{Formula: } &\Delta t_{S-P} = t_{S-wave} - t_{P-wave} \\ \text{Calculate: } &\Delta t_{S-P} = 40 \text{ s} - 15 \text{ s} = 25 \text{ s} \\ \text{Answer: } &\text{S-P interval} = 25 \text{ seconds} \end{align}\)

3. An earthquake happens $1{,}200 \text{ km}$ away from a seismic station. The P-wave from the quake travels at a constant speed of $8 \text{ km/s}$.
How long will it take for the P-wave to reach the station?

Solution: \(\begin{align} \text{Given: } &\text{Distance} = 1{,}200 \text{ km}, \text{Speed} = 8 \text{ km/s} \\ \text{Rearranged Formula: } &\text{Time} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}} \\ \text{Calculate: } &\text{Time} = \frac{1{,}200}{8} = 150 \\ \text{Answer: } &\text{Time} = 150 \text{ seconds (or 2 minutes 30 seconds)} \end{align}\)

Part 2: Applying Magnitude and Intensity

4. Looking only at the Magnitude column, which earthquake was stronger (released more energy)?

Answer: Quake Beta (Magnitude 7.0)

Explanation: Magnitude measures the actual energy released at the earthquake’s source. The higher the magnitude number, the stronger the earthquake.

5. Based on the Observed Effects, which earthquake produced a higher Intensity where people lived?

Answer:

  • Quake Alpha Intensity: High (VII-VIII) - widespread damage, cracked walls, 500 buildings damaged
  • Quake Beta Intensity: Low (I-II) - barely felt, no damage
  • Quake Gamma Intensity: Moderate (IV-V) - felt by most, minor items fell, no structural damage

Quake Alpha produced the highest intensity in populated areas despite having a lower magnitude.

6. In 2-3 sentences, use the specific terms Magnitude, Intensity, and Vulnerability to explain how a lower-magnitude earthquake can result in a higher-intensity experience for a community.

Sample Answer: Magnitude measures the energy released at the earthquake’s source, but Intensity measures the actual effects people experience. Quake Alpha had a lower magnitude than Quake Beta, but it occurred in a densely populated city with high vulnerability (many buildings and people). Quake Beta, despite its higher magnitude, occurred in a remote desert with low vulnerability (few people or structures), resulting in lower intensity effects where people actually lived.

  • Look for understanding that location and population density affect intensity
  • Accept various reasonable explanations of the magnitude-intensity relationship
  • Partial credit for answers that address only two of the three required terms
