Unit 1 Performance Task: Earthquake Resilience in Branford, CT

Performance Task: Earthquake Resilience in Branford, CT

Task Overview

You are a member of Branford’s community resilience task force. Your assignment is to conduct a scientific analysis of local earthquake hazards, interpret engineering data to evaluate structural safety, and synthesize policy recommendations that balance cost, benefit, and equity. This task integrates advanced concepts and skills from Unit 1. You will have up to 1 hour to complete all sections. Responses should demonstrate clear reasoning, precise use of evidence, and thoughtful synthesis.

Part 1: Seismic Impact Analysis — One Quake, Three Towns (Scientist)

Scenario: A magnitude 4.5 earthquake occurs in Connecticut. The epicenter is located near Branford. Three towns experience the quake:

Town Distance from Epicenter (km) Population Infrastructure Quality Observed Effects (Intensity)
Branford 10 28,000 Modern, retrofitted Pictures fall, minor cracks
New Haven 35 130,000 Mixed, some old Cracked walls, moderate panic
Guilford 60 22,000 Older, few upgrades Felt by most, some items fall

Mercalli Intensity Reference Table:

Intensity (Roman Numeral) Description (Summary)
I-II Not felt except by a few
III-IV Felt indoors, hanging objects swing
V-VI Felt by most, some items fall, cracks
VII-VIII Damage to buildings, panic, injuries
IX+ Major destruction

Use the observed effects and this table to assign a Mercalli Intensity rating to each town.

  • Calculate the expected P-wave travel time to each town if the wave speed is 6 km/s.
  • Assign a Mercalli Intensity rating to each town based on observed effects and local vulnerability.
  • Compare how distance, population, and infrastructure affect the intensity experienced in each town, even though the magnitude is the same.

Part 2: Building Safety Data (Engineer)

Shake-Table Results:

Building Design Low Shaking Medium Shaking High Shaking
A (No bracing) Stable Damaged Collapsed
B (Bracing) Stable Stable Damaged
C (Base isol.) Stable Stable Stable

Selected Response:

  1. Which building design remained stable at all shaking levels?
    • A (No bracing)
    • B (Bracing)
    • C (Base isolation)
  2. Base isolation helps buildings by:
    • Making the building heavier
    • Decoupling the building from ground motion
    • Adding more windows

Short Answer:

  1. In 1-2 sentences, explain why you would recommend your chosen design for Branford’s public buildings.

Part 3: Policy & Community Safety (Policymaker)

Safety Options Table:

Option Cost ($) Safety Benefit (1-10) People Helped (%)
Retrofit old buildings 50,000 9 40
Teach safety in schools 10,000 6 60
Run emergency drills 20,000 8 90

Selected Response:

  1. Which option helps the most people?
    • Retrofit old buildings
    • Teach safety in schools
    • Run emergency drills
  2. Which policy best supports vulnerable populations?
    • Retrofit old buildings
    • Teach safety in schools
    • Run emergency drills

Short Answer:

  1. Write 2-3 sentences justifying your policy choice for Branford. Explain your reasoning using data from the table.

Submission Requirements

  • Show your calculations and answers for each part.
  • Write your explanations in clear, simple sentences.
  • You do not need to make a model or presentation—just answer the questions and explain your thinking.

Assessment Criteria

  • Correct use of data and calculations
  • Clear explanations and reasoning
  • Answers show understanding of science, engineering, and safety
  • Thoughtful recommendations for community needs
  • Consideration of community needs and ethical issues
