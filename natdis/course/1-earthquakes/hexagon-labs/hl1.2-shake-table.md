
# Hexagon Lab 1.2: Shake-Table Engineering




## Objective
Build a model tower, test its ability to withstand simulated shaking, analyze its weak points, and redesign it to be more resilient.

## Introduction
Earthquakes create sideways forces that are dangerous to buildings. Engineers use solutions like cross-bracing and shear walls to help structures resist these forces. In this lab, you'll test these principles yourself.

## Materials
- 20 plastic straws
- 1 meter masking tape
- 1 small piece of cardboard (about 15 cm x 15 cm)
- 1 small weight (e.g., bag of 20 pennies, small beanbag)
- Shake table (or a flat, stiff textbook you can shake by hand)

---

## Part 1: Initial Design & Build
1. In your science notebook, sketch a simple, rectangular tower at least 20 cm tall.
2. Build your tower using straws and tape on the cardboard base. Make it as tall and stable as possible. **Do not use diagonal (cross-bracing) supports yet.**
3. Tape the weight securely to the top of your tower (simulates a real building's mass).

---

## Part 2: Testing Procedure
1. Place your tower on the shake table.
2. Test at each level (Low, Medium, High) for about 10 seconds each.
3. After each test, record your observations and rate stability from 1 (collapsed) to 5 (perfectly stable).

**Data Table Example:**

| Shake Level | Observations                | Stability Rating (1-5) |
|-------------|----------------------------|------------------------|
| Low         |                            |                        |
| Medium      |                            |                        |
| High        |                            |                        |

---

## Part 3: Analysis & Redesign
1. Review your data. Where was your tower weakest? Why did it fail or bend?
2. In your notebook, sketch a new, improved design. Add at least one engineering principle (e.g., cross-bracing, wider base) and label it.
3. Rebuild your tower using your improved design.

---

## Part 4: Final Test
1. Test your redesigned tower using the same procedure.
2. Record your results in a new data table.
3. Compare the stability ratings of your first and second towers, especially during the high-level shake.

---

## Part 5: Conclusion & Reflection
Answer these in your science notebook (use complete sentences):

1. **Effectiveness:** Which design was more successful at resisting the simulated earthquake? Use your data to support your answer.
2. **Engineering Principles:** What specific engineering principle did you add? How did it help your tower handle sideways forces?
3. **Real-World Connection:** How does this experiment help you understand why some buildings collapse during earthquakes while others remain standing? Connect your answer to resilience and vulnerability.
