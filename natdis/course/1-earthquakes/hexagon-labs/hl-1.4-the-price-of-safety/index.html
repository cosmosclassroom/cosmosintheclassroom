The Price Of Safety

Course: NATDIS
Unit: 1

Hexagon Lab 1.4: The Price of Safety (Policymaker)

Objective: To conduct a quantitative Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA) to recommend the most cost-effective and socially responsible seismic retrofitting policy for the community of Branford, CT.

Central Question: Given a fixed budget, which policy choice maximizes public safety and long-term economic recovery (Resilience)?

Introduction: The Budgetary Straitjacket

As the Policymaker, you have received the Engineer’s detailed proposals. They are technically sound, but the total cost is $150 Million. The total available budget for this year’s seismic infrastructure bill is only $50 Million. Your task is to select a combination of projects that stay within this $50 Million Constraint while maximizing the Total Benefit to the community.

You must apply the key formula from Chapter 3:

\[\text{Cost} \cdot \text{Effectiveness} = \frac{\text{Total Benefit (Value of lives, property saved)}}{\text{Total Cost (Investment)}}\]

Dataset: Branford, CT Proposed Seismic Retrofit Projects

The following table summarizes the four primary project options, providing the engineer’s estimated cost and the policymaker’s projected benefit (value of avoided loss and protected lives).

Project ID Infrastructure Type Engineer’s Solution Cost ($) Projected Benefit ($) Criticality Score (1–5) Exposure (People Affected)
P-1 E.O.C. & Hospital (New) Full Base Isolation $25,000,000 $150,000,000 5 (MAX Critical) 1,500
P-2 Old Stone Bridge (I-95) Tuned Mass Dampers $20,000,000 $40,000,000 4 (High Economic) 40,000
P-3 3 Schools (Existing) Advanced Cross-Bracing $10,000,000 $60,000,000 4 (High Social) 3,000
P-4 Residential Homes (200 Units) Basic Shear Walls Retrofit $5,000,000 $15,000,000 2 (Low Critical) 500

Table 1. Summary of proposed seismic retrofit projects for Branford, CT. Each row details the infrastructure type, engineering solution, estimated cost, projected benefit (in avoided losses and protected lives), criticality score, and population exposure. This table provides the technical basis for cost-benefit analysis and policy prioritization in the lab.

Investigation

  1. Calculate Cost-Effectiveness: For all four projects (P-1 through P-4), calculate the Cost-Effectiveness Ratio (Total Benefit / Total Cost). Show your work for the two projects with the highest and lowest ratios.
  2. Initial Prioritization (The Engineer’s View): Rank the projects 1–4 based only on the Projected Benefit (highest dollar value first). If you were only focused on maximizing the value of assets saved, which projects would you choose? What is the total cost of this selection?
  3. Policy Prioritization (The Policymaker’s View): As the Policymaker with a $50 Million budget, you must create a final policy brief recommending a specific combination of projects. Your choice must be justified by maximizing a combination of Criticality, Exposure, and Cost-Effectiveness.
    • Proposal: State the combination of project IDs you will fund (e.g., P-1 + P-3).
    • Budget Check: Verify the total cost of your combination does not exceed $50,000,000.
    • Total Outcome: Calculate the total Projected Benefit delivered by your chosen combination.
  4. Tradeoff Justification: Compare your final policy choice (from Question 3) with the Engineer’s purely benefit-driven choice (from Question 2).
    • Identify the Tradeoff: Which project did you have to cut to meet the budget?
    • Justify: Explain, using the concepts of Vulnerability and Strategic Resource Allocation, why your chosen combination (which likely has a lower overall dollar benefit than the engineer’s full list) is the most responsible use of public funds.

Synthesis

Systems and System Models: The total risk to Branford’s community is a system with many moving parts. Explain how a failure to fund P-1 (Hospital) creates a cascading failure within the social system after the earthquake, even if the building itself only affects 1,500 people. (Hint: Consider the effect on emergency medical response and general community stability.)

Implications: You present your final decision to the city council. The public is upset that you chose to spend $25M on the hospital (P-1, max Criticality) instead of the $20M bridge (P-2, max Exposure). How do you use the Criticality Score to explain and defend your decision?

This lab requires the students to perform simple division (CBA) and then engage in a complex analytical and ethical justification, perfectly setting them up for the final written Research Brief entry.
