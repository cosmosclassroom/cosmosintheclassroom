Unit 1: Earthquakes

Course: Natural Disasters (natdis)
Duration: 3 weeks
Prerequisites: None required; basic understanding of physical science recommended

Unit Overview

In this unit, students explore the science and engineering behind earthquakes. They investigate how seismic forces shape communities, analyze real seismic data, and design solutions for earthquake resilience. The unit centers on the challenge of building safe infrastructure in a seismically active region like Branford, CT.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

  • Explain the causes of earthquakes and the principles of plate tectonics
  • Analyze seismic data to forecast hazards and locate epicenters
  • Apply structural engineering concepts to design earthquake-resistant buildings
  • Evaluate policy and cost-benefit tradeoffs in public safety decisions
  • Model systems and communicate solutions using scientific and engineering practices

Unit Structure

Reading Materials (Flexbook)

  • fb-1.1-scientist-earthquakes.md - Investigating Plate Tectonics and Seismic Forces
  • fb-1.2-engineer-earthquakes.md - Principles of Structural Engineering for Seismic Forces
  • fb-1.3-policymaker-earthquakes.md - Balancing Public Safety and Economic Constraints

Lectures

  • lec-1.1-seismic-forces.md - Introduction to seismic forces and earthquake phenomena
  • lec-1.2-engineering-resilience.md - Engineering solutions for earthquake resilience

Laboratory Activities

  • hl-1.1-epicenter-lab.md - Locating an Epicenter using seismic wave data
  • hl-1.2-shake-table.md - Shake-Table Engineering: Testing building designs for earthquake resistance

Problem Sets

  • ps-1.1-forces-waves.md - Calculating seismic wave speeds and epicenter triangulation
  • ps-1.2-systems-solutions.md - Risk assessment and cost-benefit analysis for engineering and policy solutions

Research Activities

  • rb-forecast.md - Scientist’s Report: Forecasting the Hazard
  • rb-blueprint.md - Engineer’s Proposal: Designing for Resilience
  • rb-reflection.md - Policymaker’s Brief: The Tradeoffs of Resilience

Assessments

  • quiz_1_1.md - Quiz on seismic forces and earthquake basics
  • Final Assessment - Cumulative assessment: hazard analysis, engineering design, and policy proposal

Suggested Learning Sequence

  1. Week 1: Introduction to earthquakes, plate tectonics, and seismic data; complete Flexbook readings and Hexagon Lab 1.1
  2. Week 2: Engineering principles for earthquake resistance; Shake-Table Lab and Problem Set 1.1
  3. Week 3: Policy analysis, cost-benefit activities, and completion of Research Brief and final assessment

Resources and Materials

Required Resources

  • Seismic data sets and maps
  • Shake-table simulation materials (or digital simulation)
  • Flexbook chapters and worksheets

Optional Enrichment

  • USGS Earthquake Database exploration
  • Videos on earthquake engineering and real-world case studies
  • Advanced readings on seismic risk modeling

Student Progress Tracking

Students should complete the progress-tracker.md as they work through unit activities to reflect on learning and monitor progress.

Instructor Notes

  • Emphasize the difference between seismic hazard and risk
  • Address misconceptions about earthquake predictability
  • Encourage hands-on engineering design and data analysis
  • Use local context (Branford, CT) to make learning relevant
