# Quiz 1.1: Decoding Seismic Signals

Segment 1 of 3: Seismic Wave Analysis (7 minutes)

Context: On August 23, 2011, a magnitude 5.8 earthquake centered in Virginia was felt throughout the Northeast, including in Branford, Connecticut.

1. A seismograph in Hartford, CT recorded P-wave arrival at 2:51:32 PM and S-wave arrival at 2:52:17 PM. Calculate the S-P interval in seconds.

Equation: S-P interval = S-wave arrival time - P-wave arrival time

Variables: Δt = tS - tP

2. Using the S-P interval from Problem 1 and the standard travel-time graph, determine the distance from Hartford to the earthquake epicenter.
3. If the P-wave traveled at an average speed of 6.0 km/s through the Earth's crust, calculate how long it took the P-wave to travel from the Virginia epicenter to Hartford.

Equation: Time = Distance ÷ Speed

Variables: t = d / v

4. The 2011 Virginia earthquake had a magnitude of 5.8, but it was felt from Georgia to Maine. Explain why a single earthquake (cause) can have very different intensity experiences (effects) in different locations.
5. As a scientist, what would be your biggest concern about the seismic monitoring network in the Northeast based on this 2011 event?
