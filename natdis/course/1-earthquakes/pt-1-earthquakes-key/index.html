Answer Key (Matches Student Version)

Part 1: Seismic Impact Analysis — One Quake, Three Towns (Scientist)

  1. Calculate the expected P-wave travel time to each town if the wave speed is 6 km/s.
    • Branford: $10\,\text{km} \div 6\,\text{km/s} = 1.67\,\text{s}$
    • New Haven: $35\,\text{km} \div 6\,\text{km/s} = 5.83\,\text{s}$
    • Guilford: $60\,\text{km} \div 6\,\text{km/s} = 10.0\,\text{s}$
  2. Assign a Mercalli Intensity rating to each town based on observed effects and local vulnerability.
    • Branford: V–VI (Felt by most, some items fall, cracks)
    • New Haven: VI–VII (Cracked walls, moderate panic)
    • Guilford: V (Felt by most, some items fall)
  3. Compare how distance, population, and infrastructure affect the intensity experienced in each town, even though the magnitude is the same.
    • Closer towns (Branford) experience stronger shaking, but modern infrastructure reduces damage. Larger populations (New Haven) may have more panic and damage if infrastructure is older. Guilford is farthest, so intensity is lower, but older buildings are more vulnerable.

Part 2: Building Safety Data (Engineer)

  1. Which building design remained stable at all shaking levels?
    • C (Base isolation)
  2. Base isolation helps buildings by:
    • Decoupling the building from ground motion
  3. In 1-2 sentences, explain why you would recommend your chosen design for Branford’s public buildings.
    • Sample: “Base isolation keeps buildings stable during all levels of shaking by reducing the force transferred from the ground. This design is ideal for Branford’s public buildings to maximize safety and minimize damage.”

Part 3: Policy & Community Safety (Policymaker)

  1. Which option helps the most people?
    • Run emergency drills (90%)
  2. Which policy best supports vulnerable populations?
    • Run emergency drills (includes all residents, especially those needing assistance)
  3. Write 2-3 sentences justifying your policy choice for Branford. Explain your reasoning using data from the table.

Part 1: Hazard Analysis (Scientist)

S-P Interval Data:

  • S-P intervals: Branford (4.0s), New Haven (6.5s), Guilford (2.5s)
  • The shortest S-P interval (Guilford) is closest to the epicenter; the longest (New Haven) is farthest. Triangulation uses all three to plot circles on a map, with the intersection point as the epicenter. Students should show calculations for distance using travel-time graphs (not provided here, but typically: Distance = S-P interval × average wave speed).
  • Triangulation increases confidence by using multiple data sources and reducing error from any single station.

Branford Earthquake Table:

  • 2012 had the strongest earthquake (Magnitude 4.5, “Strongest on record”).
  • Community risk is highest in years with greater magnitude. Students should discuss how magnitude, frequency, and infrastructure affect risk (e.g., older buildings may be more vulnerable).

Part 2: Building Safety Data (Engineer)

Shake-Table Results: | Building Design | Low Shaking | Medium Shaking | High Shaking | |—————–|————-|—————|————–| | A (No bracing) | Stable | Damaged | Collapsed | | B (Bracing) | Stable | Stable | Damaged | | C (Base isol.) | Stable | Stable | Stable |

  • Building C (Base isolation) performed best at all shaking levels. Building B (Bracing) is also resilient, but less so at high shaking.
  • Cross-bracing increases lateral stability; base isolation decouples the building from ground motion, reducing force transfer.
  • Recommended design: Base isolation for critical infrastructure (hospitals, schools); cross-bracing for cost-effective upgrades to existing buildings.
  • Modification: Combine base isolation with cross-bracing for maximum resilience, or add shear walls for further stability.

Part 3: Policy & Community Safety (Policymaker)

Safety Options Table: | Option | Cost ($) | Safety Benefit (1-10) | People Helped (%) | |——————————-|———-|———————-|——————-| | Retrofit old buildings | 50,000 | 9 | 40 | | Teach safety in schools | 10,000 | 6 | 60 | | Run emergency drills | 20,000 | 8 | 90 |

  • Cost-benefit analysis: Emergency drills offer high safety benefit (8/10) and help the most people (90%), with moderate cost. Retrofitting is most effective per building, but helps fewer people and is expensive. Teaching safety in schools is low cost, moderate benefit, and reaches many children.
  • Best option for equity: Emergency drills, as they include all residents, especially vulnerable groups.
  • Sample recommendation: “I recommend Branford prioritize emergency drills for all residents. This option provides a high safety benefit and reaches the largest percentage of the population, including those who may need extra assistance. Retrofitting old buildings should be considered for critical infrastructure as funds allow, and safety education in schools should supplement community preparedness.”
