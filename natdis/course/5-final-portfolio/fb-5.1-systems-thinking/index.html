  • type: reading title: | The Rules of the Game: Systems Thinking content: | To build a real plan, you must move beyond single-hazard thinking and adopt a Systems Thinking approach.

    ### 1. Systems Thinking This means you analyze the community as a complex system of interconnected parts. Ask yourself: How do these systems interact?

    • Interconnections: The same rainstorm that causes flooding also saturates soil, causing landslides. The systems are linked.
    • Cascading Failures: An earthquake (Hazard 1) damages a power grid, which causes the water pumps (Infrastructure) to fail, which means firefighters have no water to fight fires (Hazard 2). This is a cascading failure.

    ### 2. The Composite Risk Map This is your most important tool. It’s a map that overlays all the different hazard zones (flood, fire, seismic, etc.) on top of each other. The areas where multiple high-risk zones overlap are your “hotspots.” This map allows you to prioritize your efforts.

    ### 3. Prioritizing Risks You can’t fix everything at once. You must prioritize using a simple formula: $Risk \, Priority = Probability \cdot Impact$. A high-probability event (like a yearly flood) and a low-probability event (like a major earthquake) might both be high priorities if their impact is large enough. # ————————————————————————– # SECTION 4: Application & Practice # ————————————————————————–
