# Complete Vocabulary List
## Natural Disasters: Keeping Our Community Safe

---

## Unit 1: Earthquakes - When the Ground Shakes

### Chapter 1: What Scientists Do - Finding Where Earthquakes Start

**Earthquake** - When the ground shakes because big pieces of rock underground move suddenly.

**Epicenter** - The place on the ground right above where an earthquake starts.

**P-wave** - The first, faster wave that comes from an earthquake. P stands for "primary."

**S-wave** - The second, slower wave that comes from an earthquake. S stands for "secondary."

**Seismograph** - A machine that detects and records earthquake waves.

**Data** - Information and numbers that scientists collect to learn about something.

**Triangulation** - A method that uses information from three different places to find where something happened.

### Chapter 2: What Scientists Do - Measuring How Strong Earthquakes Are

**Magnitude** - A number that tells us how strong an earthquake is. Higher numbers mean stronger earthquakes.

**Intensity** - How much damage an earthquake causes and how scary it feels to people.

**Richter Scale** - A system of numbers used to measure earthquake magnitude from 1 to 10.

**Mercalli Scale** - A system that describes earthquake intensity using words like "light" or "severe."

**Energy** - The power that is released when an earthquake happens.

**Fault** - A crack in the ground where pieces of rock move during an earthquake.

### Chapter 3: What Engineers Do - Building Safe Houses

**Engineer** - A person who designs and builds things like buildings, bridges, and roads.

**Safe Building** - A building that is built strong enough to not fall down during an earthquake.

**Foundation** - The bottom part of a building that sits on the ground.

**Cross-bracing** - Strong supports that go diagonally across a building to make it stronger.

**Base isolation** - A way of building where the bottom of a building can move separately from the shaking ground.

**Building code** - Rules that tell people how to build safely.

**Retrofit** - To add safety features to an old building to make it stronger.

### Chapter 4: What Town Leaders Do - Deciding How Much Safety Costs

**Cost** - How much money something costs to buy or build.

**Benefit** - Something good that happens as a result of spending money or taking action.

**Cost-benefit analysis** - Comparing how much something costs to how much good it will do.

**Budget** - The amount of money that is available to spend.

**Policy** - A rule or plan that a government makes.

**Risk** - The chance that something bad might happen.

**Vulnerability** - How easily something can be hurt or damaged.

**Community** - All the people who live in the same town or area.

## Unit 2: Tsunamis - When the Ocean Gets Angry

### Chapter 5: What Scientists Do - Understanding Big Waves

**Tsunami** - A very big wave in the ocean that can flood the land near the coast.

**Underwater earthquake** - An earthquake that happens under the ocean floor.

**Wave height** - How tall a wave is from bottom to top.

**Wave length** - The distance from one wave to the next wave.

**Wave speed** - How fast a wave moves through the water.

**Ocean depth** - How deep the water is from the surface to the bottom.

**Bathymetry** - The measurement and mapping of how deep the ocean floor is.

**Energy transfer** - When energy moves from one place to another.

### Chapter 6: What Scientists Do - Predicting Where Tsunamis Will Hit

**Coast** - The place where the land meets the ocean.

**Coastline** - The edge of the land along the ocean.

**Elevation** - How high above sea level something is.

**Sea level** - The average height of the ocean's surface.

**Topography** - The shape of the land, including hills and valleys.

**Warning system** - A way to tell people about danger before it happens.

**Evacuation route** - A safe path for people to leave a dangerous area.

### Chapter 7: What Engineers Do - Protecting the Coast

**Coastal engineering** - Designing and building things to protect the coast from waves.

**Sea wall** - A big, strong wall built near the ocean to stop big waves.

**Hard protection** - Using concrete, rocks, or metal to protect the coast.

**Soft protection** - Using sand, plants, or natural materials to protect the coast.

**Natural barrier** - Plants like trees or sand dunes that help protect the coast from big waves.

**Mangrove** - A type of tree that grows in salty water near the coast.

**Breakwater** - A wall built in the water to break up big waves before they reach shore.

### Chapter 8: What Town Leaders Do - Planning for Tsunamis

**Zoning** - Rules about what can be built in different areas of a town.

**Land use** - How different areas of land are used (for houses, parks, businesses, etc.).

**Building restriction** - A rule that says certain things cannot be built in dangerous places.

**Emergency plan** - A plan that tells people what to do when a disaster happens.

**Early warning** - Telling people about danger before it happens so they can get safe.

**International cooperation** - When different countries work together to solve problems.

## Unit 3: Wildfires - When Fire Spreads Too Fast

### Chapter 9: What Scientists Do - Understanding How Fire Works

**Wildfire** - A big fire that spreads through forests or grasslands and is hard to control.

**Fire triangle** - The three things fire needs: heat (to start it), fuel (things to burn), and air (oxygen).

**Heat** - Warmth or high temperature that can start a fire.

**Fuel** - Anything that can burn, like trees, grass, or wooden houses.

**Oxygen** - A gas in the air that fire needs to keep burning.

**Ignition** - When something starts to burn or catch fire.

**Combustion** - The process of burning.

### Chapter 10: What Scientists Do - Studying Fire Weather

**Fire weather** - Hot, dry, windy weather that makes fires spread faster and be more dangerous.

**Temperature** - How hot or cold something is.

**Humidity** - How much water is in the air. Low humidity means dry air.

**Wind speed** - How fast the wind is blowing.

**Drought** - A long time without rain when everything gets very dry.

**Fire season** - The time of year when wildfires are most likely to happen.

**Rate of spread** - How fast a fire moves across the land.

### Chapter 11: What Engineers Do - Building Fire-Safe Communities

**Defensible space** - An area around a house where plants and trees are cleared away so fire can't reach the house easily.

**Fire-resistant materials** - Building materials that don't burn easily, like metal or concrete.

**Firebreak** - A strip of land with no plants that can stop a fire from spreading.

**Vegetation management** - Controlling and removing plants that could help fire spread.

**Building design** - How a house or building is planned and built.

**Fire safety zone** - An area that is kept clear of things that can burn.

**Sprinkler system** - Pipes and water sprayers that can put out fires automatically.

### Chapter 12: What Town Leaders Do - Making Fire Rules

**Wildland-urban interface (WUI)** - The area where houses and wild land with trees and plants meet.

**Building permit** - Official permission needed before building a house or building.

**Fire code** - Rules about how to build safely to prevent fires.

**Evacuation** - When people leave their homes quickly to get away from danger.

**Fire department** - The group of people whose job is to put out fires and help in emergencies.

**Prevention** - Doing things ahead of time to stop bad things from happening.

**Public education** - Teaching people in the community about important safety information.

## Unit 4: Floods and Landslides - When Water and Land Move

### Chapter 13: What Scientists Do - Understanding Water Flow

**Flood** - When water covers land that is usually dry.

**Watershed** - An area of land where all the rain flows to the same river or lake.

**River** - A large stream of water that flows toward the ocean or a lake.

**Stream** - A small river or flow of water.

**Runoff** - Water from rain that flows over the ground instead of soaking in.

**Absorption** - When water soaks into the ground.

**Stream flow** - How much water is moving down a river or stream.

**Peak flow** - The highest amount of water flowing in a river during a flood.

### Chapter 14: What Scientists Do - Understanding Why Hills Slide

**Landslide** - When rocks, dirt, and trees slide down a hill or mountain.

**Slope** - How steep a hill is.

**Soil** - The dirt and small rocks that plants grow in.

**Erosion** - When wind or water wears away soil and rocks.

**Gravity** - The force that pulls things down toward the ground.

**Saturation** - When soil is completely full of water and can't hold any more.

**Trigger** - Something that causes a landslide to happen, like heavy rain.

**Unstable slope** - A hillside that is likely to slide down.

### Chapter 15: What Engineers Do - Controlling Water and Stabilizing Hills

**Dam** - A big wall built across a river to control the flow of water.

**Levee** - A wall built along a river to keep flood water from spreading to nearby land.

**Drainage** - A system of pipes or ditches that carries water away from an area.

**Retaining wall** - A strong wall built to hold soil and rocks in place on a hillside.

**Storm drain** - Pipes that carry rainwater away from streets and buildings.

**Flood control** - Methods used to prevent or reduce flooding.

**Slope stabilization** - Making a hillside stronger so it won't slide down.

**Engineered solution** - Something that is carefully designed and built to solve a problem.

### Chapter 16: What Town Leaders Do - Planning Safe Development

**Floodplain** - The flat area next to a river that gets covered with water during floods.

**Flood zone** - An area that is likely to flood during big storms.

**Development** - Building new houses, roads, or businesses in an area.

**Permeable surface** - Ground that lets water soak through it, like grass or gravel.

**Impermeable surface** - Ground that doesn't let water soak through it, like concrete or asphalt.

**Stormwater management** - Planning how to handle rainwater in a town or city.

**Green infrastructure** - Using plants and natural areas to help control water flow.

**Sustainable development** - Building in ways that don't harm the environment or create new problems.

## Unit 5: Our Town's Safety Plan - Putting It All Together

### Chapter 17: Combining All the Dangers

**Multi-hazard** - When several different types of disasters could happen in the same place.

**Hazard map** - A map that shows where different dangers are located.

**Risk assessment** - Looking at all the dangers in an area and deciding which ones are most important to worry about.

**Cumulative risk** - When multiple dangers add up to create an even bigger problem.

**Interconnected** - When different things are connected to each other and affect each other.

**Cascade effect** - When one disaster causes another disaster to happen.

### Chapter 18: Making a Complete Safety Plan

**Emergency management** - The process of preparing for, responding to, and recovering from disasters.

**Mitigation** - Things we do ahead of time to make disasters less harmful.

**Preparedness** - Getting ready for disasters before they happen.

**Response** - What people do during and right after a disaster happens.

**Recovery** - The process of fixing damage and getting back to normal after a disaster.

**Resilience** - The ability of a community to bounce back quickly after a disaster.

**Stakeholder** - A person who cares about what happens because it affects them.

### Chapter 19: Talking to the Community

**Public meeting** - A gathering where community members can learn about and discuss important issues.

**Communication plan** - A plan for how to share important information with the community.

**Community input** - Ideas and opinions from people who live in the area.

**Consensus** - When most people agree on something.

**Priority** - Something that is more important than other things.

**Trade-off** - When you have to give up one thing to get something else.

**Public participation** - When community members take part in making decisions that affect them.

**Civic engagement** - When people get involved in their community and local government.
