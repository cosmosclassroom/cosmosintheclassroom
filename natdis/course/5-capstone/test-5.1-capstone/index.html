Q1: Scientist’s Synthesis (Composite Risk Score - Hexagon Lab 5.1)

  • id: q1 type: numeric prompt: | The table below shows the calculated risk scores (out of 10, where 10 is highest risk) for three different locations in the community of Branford, CT. The Composite Risk Score is the average of the four hazard scores.

    | Location | Seismic Risk (10) | Tsunami Risk (10) | Wildfire Risk (10) | Flood/Landslide Risk (10) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Location A (Downtown)** | $2$ | $4$ | $1$ | $9$ |
| **Location B (Coastal Beach)** | $3$ | $10$ | $2$ | $5$ |
| **Location C (Inland Hills)** | $5$ | $0$ | $8$ | $3$ |

Calculate the **Composite Risk Score** for **Location C**.   points: 10   answer: 4.0   units: "score"   tolerance: 0.01   explanation: "Composite Risk Score = $\\frac{5+0+8+3}{4} = \\frac{16}{4} = 4.0 \\, \\text{score}$"

Q3: Engineer’s Integrated Proposal (Cross-Unit Application)

  • id: q3 type: essay prompt: | You are tasked with protecting Location B (Coastal Beach) from its two highest risks: Tsunami Risk (10) and Flood/Landslide Risk (5).

    Propose one specific **Engineering Solution** from the Tsunami unit (Unit 2) AND one specific **Engineering Solution** from the Flooding/Landslide unit (Unit 4) that would work together to mitigate the risk in this coastal area.

Explain how these two solutions **integrate** to form a better, single system.   points: 35   rows: 10   explanation: "A strong answer proposes two compatible solutions, such as: **Tsunami Solution:** An early warning system (DART buoys and sirens). **Flood Solution:** Restoration of coastal wetlands/mangroves. **Integration:** Wetlands/mangroves provide soft coastal defense against small-to-moderate waves/flooding, while the early warning system protects against the high-impact Tsunami event."

Q4: Policymaker’s Communication (Stakeholder Defense)

  • id: q4 type: essay prompt: | Your final task is to act as the Resilience Planner. You must present your $$10$ million integrated resilience plan to the Town Council. One council member says the cost is too high and suggests only building seawalls (a ‘hard’ solution) and doing nothing else.

    Write a persuasive policy defense that addresses this criticism. Use the concepts of **long-term resilience** and **cost-benefit analysis** to argue that your integrated plan (which should include 'soft' or 'green' solutions like wetlands or defensible space) is the more prudent long-term investment than relying on a single, expensive 'hard' structure.   points: 40   rows: 15   explanation: "The response must argue that the single seawall is vulnerable to cascading failure (it only addresses tsunami/coastal flood, not wildfire or inland flood/landslide) and requires expensive maintenance (a long-term cost burden). The integrated plan, while having higher upfront costs, provides multiple benefits (e.g., green infrastructure provides flood defense AND ecological benefits) and reduces long-term maintenance/reconstruction costs by making the community more intrinsically resilient to diverse hazards."
