# Franklin’s Contributions: The Law of Charges

![](assets\images\9_electricity\0_Lecture_10_Electrostatics_3.png)

__Charges__  \( _q_ \) exist in two forms: positives and negatives

__Like__  charges repel;  __opposite __ charges attract

Charge is  __conserved__ ; the net amount of electric charge produced in any \(natural\) process is zero

# Charges in Atoms

![](assets\images\9_electricity\0_Lecture_10_Electrostatics_4.png)

In their default state\, atoms are electrically  __neutral__

The number of protons is  __balanced__  by the number of electrons

Electrons are easily  __transferred__  between some atoms

# Materials and Charge

![](assets\images\9_electricity\0_Lecture_10_Electrostatics_5.png)

When one atom has a greater affinity for electrons\, it can “ __steal__ ” them from another

The buildup of electrons on one material and absence on the other results in  __unequal__  charges

The interaction between positive and negative charges results in an  __attractive__   __force__

# “Quantum” charge

Careful experiments in the 19th and 20th centuries showed that charge is  __quantized__

__Charge__  always exists in pairs and is always in multiples of a fundamental unit of charge

This fundamental unit is symbolized by the letter  _e _ and represents the precise charge of a single particle

# The elementary charge

A  __proton__  is said to carry a charge of \+ _e_  and an  __electron__  is said to carry a charge of \- _e_

The value of  _e _ has been determined  __experimentally__  to be

1\.602176 x 10\-19 C\, or Coulombs

We approximate this value\, called  _elementary charge_ \, as  __1\.6 x 10__  __\-19  __  __C__

# Conductors transfer charge

Materials in which charge flows freely are called  __conductors__

![](assets\images\9_electricity\0_Lecture_10_Electrostatics_7.png)

_Examples: metals\, some liquids_

Conductors  __distribute__  charge through the material \(and into others\, if able\)

# Insulators do not transfer charge

Materials through which charge does not flow are called  __insulators__

![](assets\images\9_electricity\0_Lecture_10_Electrostatics_8.png)

_Examples: glass\, rubber\, silk\, plastic_

Insulators keep charges  __local__

# Semi- and superconductors

__Semiconductors__  are materials which transfer charge at intermediate rates\, depending on their chemical composition

__Superconductors__  are materials which transfer charge indefinitely when kept at very low temperatures

# Charge can transfer by contact

![](assets\images\9_electricity\0_Lecture_10_Electrostatics_9.png)

__Insulators__  can be charged by contact and the charges hold because the material does not transfer it readily to anything else

__Conductors__  require being handled with an insulator to charge\, otherwise they just transfer the charge to the human body \(which is a conductor\)

# Ground

![](assets\images\9_electricity\0_Lecture_10_Electrostatics_10.png)

Connecting a conductor to the Earth\, called  _grounding_ \, is a way of  __redirecting__  excess charge into a basically infinite  _sink_  for electrons

Grounding is done in both  __electrostatics__  and  __electric__   __currents__  to protect people and devices from unexpected/unwanted flows of charge

# Charge by induction

One charged\, insulating sphere imbalances the charge in another

![](assets\images\9_electricity\0_Lecture_10_Electrostatics_11.png)

A ground is attached to the second sphere\, allowing the electrons to leave the sphere and travel to earth

The ground is removed\, leaving an excess of positive charge in the second sphere

# The Electric Force

Coulomb observed that two charged objects near each other experience  __acceleration__

This acceleration is said to be due to the  __Electric Force__

Coulomb observed that:

The  __closer__  two objects are together\, the  __greater__  is the force between them

The  __stronger__  the charge of the two objects\, the  __greater__  is the force between them

# Measuring the force

Coulomb carefully  __measured__  the attractive force using a torsion balance

![](assets\images\9_electricity\0_Lecture_10_Electrostatics_13.png)

Two charges are  __separated__ \, one an insulating rod suspended by silk

The attraction of the two is measured when the rod  __twists__ \, and a reflected light wave measures the  __angle__  of the  __displacement__

# Coulomb’s Law

__Coulomb’s Law__  describes the relationship between the electric force\, charges\, and distances between the charges

The force is proportionate to the  __charges__  and inversely proportional to the  __square__  of the  __distance__  between them

# Electric Fields

The fact that induction works without actually touching the two charged spheres together indicates that the electric force is a  __field force__

The balloon\-on\-hair demonstration shows that the electric force is  __much__   __stronger__  than the gravitational force

The concept of a field helps explain how  __forces __ can be exerted on objects at a  __distance__

Even Newton was hesitant about this concept in his maths

Electric fields can be thought as regions of  __space__  around a charged object

When another charged object  __enters__  the field\,  __forces__  of an electrical nature arise

The electric field strength can be calculated using a test charge\,  _q_  _0_  placed near a second object with larger charge\,  _Q_

Large test charges could redistribute charge in the source\, causing measurement errors

Test charges are assumed to be  __positive__  and relatively small in charge

# The strength of a field
