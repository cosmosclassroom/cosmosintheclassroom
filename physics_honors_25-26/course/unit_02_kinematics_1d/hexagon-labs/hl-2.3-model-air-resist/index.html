Hexagon Lab 2.3: Modeling the Effects of Air Resistance

Complete the following investigations in your Science Notebook following the format of a scientific paper.

Abstract

This investigation uses a projectile motion simulation to compare the trajectory of an object in a perfect vacuum with its path in a realistic environment that includes air resistance. By analyzing the differences in maximum height, horizontal range, and path shape, we will develop a more complete model for the flight of real-world objects, such as the sounding rocket.

Introduction

Background and Context

In the real world, a fired cannonball or a coasting rocket doesn’t follow the perfect, symmetrical parabolic path that our kinematic equations predict. Air resistance, a complex form of friction, acts on the object, stealing its energy and altering its journey. While our idealized vacuum models are powerful tools for understanding the fundamental principles of gravity, it’s crucial to understand their limitations. This investigation explores how significant the effect of air resistance can be and helps us refine our model of the sounding rocket’s flight.

Research Question

How does air resistance alter the maximum height and range of a projectile compared to its ideal path in a vacuum?

Methods

Materials

  • Interactive Projectile Motion Simulation
  • Calculator
  • Research Log/Science Notebook

Dataset

This investigation uses an interactive simulation that fires a projectile and plots its trajectory.

  • Controls: You can set the initial speed, launch angle, and mass of the projectile.
  • Environment: A toggle switch allows for air resistance to be turned ON or OFF.
  • Analysis: The simulation displays the two paths (vacuum vs. air) on the same graph and provides data for maximum height and horizontal range.

Experimental Parameters:

  • Object: Cannonball
  • Initial Speed: $50.0 \text{ m/s}$
  • Launch Angle: $45.0^\circ$

Variable Interpretation & Calibration

First, we will establish a baseline by analyzing the projectile’s motion in an idealized vacuum, where gravity is the only force acting on it after launch.

Investigation 2: The “Real World” - Motion with Air Resistance

Variable Interpretation & Calibration

Now, we will introduce air resistance to see how it changes the motion. We will then quantify these changes by comparing them to our baseline data.

Guiding Questions:

  1. Keeping all other settings identical, turn Air Resistance ON and launch the projectile again. The simulation will overlay this new trajectory with the one from Investigation 1.
  2. Visually compare the two paths. How does the shape of the trajectory with air resistance differ from the vacuum path? Is it still symmetrical?
  3. Record the new Maximum Height and Horizontal Range.
  4. Calculate the percentage decrease for both height and range using the formula: \(\text{Percent Decrease} = \frac{|\text{Ideal Value} - \text{Actual Value}|}{\text{Ideal Value}} \times 100\%\)
