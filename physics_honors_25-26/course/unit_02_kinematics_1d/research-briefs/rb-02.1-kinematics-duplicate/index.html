Unit 2 Research Brief: The Art of Observation

The Multi-Stage Launch of a Sounding Rocket

The launch of a rocket is a story told in chapters of immense power and silent coasting. For a few brief moments, the engine wages a fiery battle against gravity. Once its fuel is spent, the rocket continues its journey upward, slowing steadily until it reaches a graceful, weightless peak—its apogee—before beginning its descent. Every moment of this dramatic flight is captured in a stream of data, a language we can learn to read.

The Scenario

You are a mission analysis intern at NASA’s Wallops Flight Facility, home of the sounding rocket program. Your team has just completed a successful launch, and the raw telemetry data from the rocket’s vertical flight has been downloaded. The lead engineer has provided you with the velocity-time graph of the ascent and has tasked you with creating a complete “flight narrative.” Your job is to transform this single graph into a comprehensive story of the rocket’s journey to its highest point.

Your Central Question

How can the patterns within a single velocity-time graph be used to tell the complete, quantitative story of a rocket’s multi-stage journey from launch to its highest point?

This brief will guide you through forecasting, modeling, and reflecting on the rocket’s flight.

Assessment Criteria

Identical to the assessment criteria in the hphys-research-brief-template.md.

Forecast (25 points)

  • Initial Model (10 points): Clear, detailed prediction with logical reasoning.
  • Variable Identification (8 points): Identifies relevant variables with thoughtful predictions.
  • Prior Knowledge (4 points): Makes appropriate connections to previous learning.
  • Research Questions (3 points): Poses investigable questions.

Blueprint (35 points)

  • Model Refinement (10 points): Shows clear evolution in thinking based on evidence.
  • Mathematical Relationships (10 points): Accurately identifies and expresses relationships.
  • Scientific Explanation (10 points): Provides mechanism-based explanation using scientific principles.
  • Application (5 points): Thoughtful application to practical situations.

Reflection (40 points)

  • Final Model (12 points): Comprehensive, accurate, well-integrated understanding.
  • Evidence Evaluation (8 points): Thoughtful analysis of evidence quality and support.
  • Limitations (6 points): Honest assessment of model limitations and uncertainties.
  • Real-World Connections (6 points): Meaningful connections to applications.
  • Metacognition (4 points): Thoughtful reflection on learning process.
  • Future Questions (4 points): Generates meaningful questions for further investigation.
