Problem Set 2.2: The Rules of Constant Acceleration Complete this problem set on separate paper to be collected by your instructor.

Introduction In this problem set, you will apply the powerful kinematic equations to solve problems involving constant acceleration. This mathematical model allows us to predict the future state of a moving object with incredible precision. Remember to use the standard problem-solving strategy: list knowns and unknowns, select the right equation, solve algebraically, and then substitute numbers.

Apprentice Level: Foundational Skills Complete the following problems, showing all work, including the equation used.

  1. Basic Acceleration A sprinter accelerates from rest to a top speed of 11.2 m/s in 2.50 s. What is the magnitude of her average acceleration?

  2. Distance and Deceleration A train traveling at an initial speed of 35.0 m/s applies its brakes, causing a uniform deceleration of 1.50 m/s 2 . How far does the train travel before it comes to a stop?

  3. Freefall from Rest A construction worker accidentally drops a wrench from a tall building. a) What is the velocity of the wrench after falling for 4.00 s? b) How far has the wrench fallen in that time?

Journeyman Level: Applied Problem-Solving 4. Vertical Launch A model rocket is launched straight upward with an initial velocity of 55.0 m/s. a) How long does it take for the rocket to reach its maximum altitude (apogee)? b) What is the maximum altitude the rocket reaches? c) What is the total time of flight from launch until it returns to the ground (assuming it lands at the same height)?

  1. Multi-Step Stopping Problem A driver is traveling at 28.0 m/s when she sees a deer in the road 110.0 m ahead. Her reaction time before applying the brakes is 0.50 s. Once she applies the brakes, her car decelerates at a constant rate of 4.50 m/s 2 . a) How far does the car travel during her reaction time? b) After applying the brakes, how far does the car travel before stopping? c) Does she hit the deer? Justify your answer with your calculations.

Master Level: Complex Analysis & Synthesis 6. Connecting to the Phenomenon Let's apply the kinematic equations to our sounding rocket. From Hexagon Lab 2.1, we know the engine cuts out at t=8.0 s, at which point the rocket has a velocity of 280.0 m/s and has reached an altitude of 1120 m. From that moment on, it is in freefall. a) Calculate the additional time it takes for the rocket to reach its apogee after the engine cuts out. b) Calculate the additional height the rocket gains during this "coast" phase. c) What is the rocket's total maximum altitude? How does this value compare to the one you found graphically in Problem Set 2.1?
